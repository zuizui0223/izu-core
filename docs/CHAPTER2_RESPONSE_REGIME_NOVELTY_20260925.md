# Island syndrome as a response regime: focused novelty audit

Date: 2026-09-25. Scope: ecological framing only; no code edits or model runs. This is a targeted nearest-literature audit, not a systematic review or proof that an idea is absent. C/I/S factor rankings are not the novelty criterion. The proposed emphasis is the distribution of floral/community responses, including opposing directions retained after richness matching.

## Assessment

**The framing is scientifically promising, but context dependence, mixed directional island responses, multiple island syndromes, and richness-controlled functional distributions already have close precedents.** The defensible prospective contribution is to operationalize a specified community-reassembly intervention as a conditional distribution of responses, explain when its mean conceals biologically distinct directions, and test those predictions against appropriate baselines. A new term alone would not supply that contribution.

Most directly, floral comparative work already reports smaller, larger, and unchanged island flowers depending on family and archipelago [1]. A recent island-syndrome synthesis explicitly proposes multiple trait-space focal points and grouping island types by environmental conditions [2]. An empirical island-flora study measures functional dispersion and evenness while controlling richness [3]. These overlap with the proposed framing and must appear in its positioning.

## Eight close sources: overlap and remaining distinction

### 1. Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson (2020): direct floral counterexample to a single mean direction

[Floral Trait Evolution of Angiosperms on Pacific Islands](https://doi.org/10.1086/709018), American Naturalist 196:87–100; [author abstract indexed by PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32552102/).

Primary comparative analysis of 556 species and 136 independent island–mainland contrasts. Flowers were not smaller on average; direction varied among archipelagos and families, including smaller, larger, and unchanged outcomes. The authors distinguish ecological filtering from postcolonization evolution. **Overlap:** mixed directions and a misleading universal floral syndrome are already explicit. **Distinction:** this does not by itself identify the distribution of causal responses to a controlled pollinator-community intervention, or a richness-matched model response map. Do not recast its lack of a global mean as a lack of biological structure.

### 2. Ciarle & Burns (online 2024; volume 2025): closest conceptual precedent

[The island syndrome in plants on New Zealand's outlying islands: a review](https://doi.org/10.1080/0028825X.2024.2377418), New Zealand Journal of Botany 63:2300–2324.

This is a synthesis, cited as the authors' original conceptual proposal rather than primary empirical proof. Its final synthesis proposes multidimensional trait space, multiple island-syndrome focal points, non-monotonic directions, and grouping islands by biotic/abiotic conditions to test trait-space modularity. **Overlap:** plural, condition-dependent island syndromes are already proposed. **Distinction:** an intervention-defined distribution of paired response contrasts would be an operational implementation/extension; it is not the first move beyond one universal syndrome. Static positions or clusters in trait space and distributions of changes under specified interventions remain distinct estimands.

### 3. Barajas Barbosa et al. (2023): distributional island functional ecology with richness controls

[Assembly of functional diversity in an oceanic island flora](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06305-z), Nature 619:545–550.

Primary Tenerife analysis maps trait-space density and functional diversity, relating species groups to biogeographical and diversification histories. It distinguishes divergence/trait-space expansion from convergence/dense packing. Its figure descriptions explicitly report functional richness, dispersion, and evenness controlling species richness, including complete-case sensitivity analyses. **Overlap:** island functional distributions, convergence versus divergence, and richness control are established. **Distinction:** these are mainly vegetative/life-history trait-space and assembly comparisons, not a floral response distribution generated by an experimentally specified pollination perturbation. Richness matching alone cannot establish novelty here.

### 4. Biddick, Hendriks & Burns (2019): mean cancellation versus conditional structure

[Plants obey (and disobey) the island rule](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1907424116), PNAS 116:17632–17634; [primary article in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731657/).

Primary plant analysis finds size-dependent shifts in stature and leaf area, despite no consistent increase/decrease in paired averages for these traits. Seed size behaves differently. **Overlap:** a weak aggregate mean can coexist with structured positive and negative responses; conclusions also differ among functional traits. **Distinction:** the conditioning variable is mainland trait size and the response is an island–mainland comparison, rather than the specific floral community-reassembly response sought here. This is direct prior art for the statistical motivation, not a floral mechanism calibration.

### 5. Benitez-Lopez et al. (2021): context-dependent island rule at broad scale

[The island rule explains consistent patterns of body size evolution in terrestrial vertebrates](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-021-01426-y), Nature Ecology & Evolution.

Primary phylogenetic meta-analysis tests island–mainland size changes across terrestrial vertebrates. Mainland size, taxonomic group, climate, island area, and isolation structure the outcomes. **Overlap:** opposite signs can be coherent predictions of one conditional rule rather than exceptions to a mean tendency. **Distinction:** animal body size and its covariates do not supply floral-response mechanisms. Avoid claiming that island-syndrome research generally assumes a uniform sign; the island rule is itself explicitly conditional.

### 6. Traveset et al. (2016): island pollination-network composition and structure

[Global patterns of mainland and insular pollination networks](https://doi.org/10.1111/geb.12362), Global Ecology and Biogeography.

Primary comparative network study reports that oceanic-island networks have fewer species/links, lower interaction diversity, and greater plant niche overlap than comparison networks, potentially related to pollinator richness. **Overlap:** community composition, diversity, and island context already frame pollination-network differences. **Distinction:** network structure is not the same as the direction/distribution of floral trait responses or reproductive consequences. A richness-matched response analysis needs to establish what remains beyond network-size differences, rather than use a network metric as an evolutionary endpoint.

### 7. Wang et al. (2020): functional pollinator groups do not map cleanly to floral syndromes

[Plants are visited by more pollinator species than pollination syndromes predicted in an oceanic island community](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-70954-7), Scientific Reports 10:13918.

Primary Yongxing Island study links floral traits and potential pollinator functional groups; overall trait-based prediction was limited, while floral restrictiveness related to specialization. It explicitly acknowledges that body pollen carriage does not establish deposition efficiency or seed-set contribution. **Overlap:** nonuniform trait–guild associations and limitations of categorical syndromes are known. **Distinction:** observations of visitor groups do not identify a distribution of effective reproductive responses. A new functional response regime needs either reproductive validation or an explicit model-conditional label.

### 8. Tur, Castro-Urgal & Traveset (2013): ecological function and context, not topology alone

[Linking Plant Specialization to Dependence in Interactions for Seed Set in Pollination Networks](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0078294), PLOS ONE 8:e78294.

Primary field study measures dependence using seed set with/without pollinator access, alongside network specialization. The relationship between dependence and generalization differs between coastal and mountain communities. **Overlap:** functional reproductive dependence is context-dependent and need not follow simple network specialization. **Distinction:** this is not a test of the island floral response-regime hypothesis. It shows why a regime called functional needs a measurable function rather than just trait displacement or a network rank.

## What an operational response-regime contribution would require

The following is a proposed design/interpretation, not a literature finding or a certified result from the present project.

Define each response contrast before aggregation: `Delta_r = endpoint(intervention, r) - endpoint(matched baseline, r)`, with the sign convention, biological endpoint, matching unit, observation horizon, and eligible cases declared. A response regime is then a **conditional distribution** `P(Delta | specified community change, baseline state, declared design)`, not merely a rank of inputs or a post-hoc label attached to positive/negative results.

Report the mean together with the median, quantiles/spread, and fractions below a negative tolerance, within an equivalence band, and above a positive tolerance. Set a scientifically meaningful tolerance prospectively where possible. Retain mixed directions and failed/unevaluable cases with their denominators. Do not call an exactly nonzero numerical value a biological sign reversal without checking magnitude and stochastic uncertainty.

Distinguish four levels that can otherwise be conflated:

- **Across-scenario response heterogeneity:** different community configurations produce different paired contrasts.
- **Within-scenario stochastic variation:** replicate outcomes differ at fixed conditions.
- **Within-community trait diversity:** species/individuals occupy a spread of trait values.
- **Between-region mean differences:** different regions occupy different average positions.

The first is the proposed response-regime target; none of the other three is automatically its estimator. A multimodal distribution of scenario responses is also not automatically a multistable dynamical system: alternate attractors require a separate dynamical test.

If mixed signs persist after richness matching, the justified conclusion is that **richness alone does not account for the observed directional heterogeneity under that matching design**. It does not prove independence from all diversity effects, or identify composition as the sole cause. Matching can change the target population; document common support, exclusions, and retained scenario weights. Separate abundance, identity, trait composition, initial state, and richness interventions where the design supports them.

Simulation design frequencies are not natural-island frequencies. For example, the fraction of grid cells with negative responses depends on the chosen ranges and grid weights. Report it as a design-conditional fraction unless an independently justified natural sampling distribution is available. A stronger biological contribution would predict which measured community conditions place a new case in each response class, with held-out evaluation or a prospective test.

A useful formulation is: **we operationalize island floral responses as intervention-conditioned distributions and test when opposing directions persist after richness matching, rather than treating the pooled mean as the full syndrome.** Position this as an extension of context-dependent island evolution and functional trait-space research [1–5], with a pollination-specific mechanism and measurement bridge [6–8]. Avoid claiming the first distributional or plural island-syndrome framework.

## Existing evidence labels must retain their scope

`natural42` and `literature0/25` are project evidence labels supplied in the task context; their underlying artifacts were not re-audited in this literature-only subtask.

**natural42 does not calibrate a model response regime.** A set of natural communities/records can motivate realistic heterogeneity or provide a comparison domain, but cannot identify the model's regime boundaries or natural regime prevalence without an explicit observation-to-model mapping, comparable response estimands, measurement-error treatment, and independent calibration/validation. Observational variation in natural traits is not itself an intervention response.

**literature0/25 is not evidence that papers failed to report variance.** Unless those 25 papers were explicitly coded for a preregistered variance/distribution-reporting criterion, the existing count retains only the criterion actually audited. A zero count for joint parameter perturbations or sensitivity analysis cannot be relabeled as zero distributional analyses, zero context-dependent findings, or an absence of this ecological idea. This audit identifies close counterexamples to a broad absence claim.

## Access and claim boundaries

Searches were focused on island floral trait evolution, conditional island rules, functional trait distributions, island pollination networks, and reproductive dependence. Publisher text for sources 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, and 8 was retrieved; source 4's indexed primary-article methods/results and PubMed metadata were inspected; source 1 was assessed from its author abstract indexed by PubMed. Some PMC direct opens returned access checks. The exact project contrast statistics and richness-matched mixed-sign findings are left to the main analysis; this note reports none as established.

No first-ever claim is supported. The present audit narrows the candidate contribution to an explicit, reproducible, biologically interpretable response-distribution analysis and its explanatory/predictive value, independent of C/I/S rankings.
