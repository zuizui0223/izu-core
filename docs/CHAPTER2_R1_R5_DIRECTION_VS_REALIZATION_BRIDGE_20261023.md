## Check A — exactly what the source model predicts with no visitors

For *this conditional comparison* take two artificial plant states with the same M=0.5, expressed A=0.5, mating timing and resource settings, varying only investment I from 0.3 to 0.7; set investment cost c_I=0.4, positive selfed-seed viability and **zero visitors**. Source `reproduction.py` then returns zero outcrossing and positive selfed offspring. Its expected selfed reproductive return is proportional to

`exp(-c_I I²)`, holding the other traits, mating rule and assay environment fixed.

Consequently, the finite-state log ratio and the local invasion gradient at I=0.5 obey

```text
log[W(I=0.7)/W(I=0.3)] = -c_I*(0.7² - 0.3²) = -0.16
d log(W)/dI |_(I=0.5) = -2*c_I*0.5 = -0.4
```

This arithmetic is independently checked in the existing **full reproduction kernel** and **fixed-resident rare-mutant selection kernel**, for both `prior` and `delayed` selfing and for `fixed` and `evolving` A expression (four combinations). The equality is a source-model conditional payoff identity, **not a numerical estimate of population-level selection, historical evolution or a matched intervention on the archived transfer**. It does not hold without additional qualifications when visitors or non-trivial A/M–I associations are present. This check advances no population, produces no offspring history and changes no model.

The **original R5 no-visitor** transfer nevertheless stores these finite realized exact signs:

| Historical A policy | Allocated nested futures | Occupied | I positive | I negative | I zero | Extinct (I undefined) | Cohorts with any I change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fixed | 1,024 | 704 | **27** | 37 | 640 | 320 | 2/32 |
| Evolving | 1,024 | 1,024 | **29** | 99 | 896 | 0 | 4/32 |

The sign counts use **exact numeric comparison**, including potentially near-zero changes; they do not establish a meaningful magnitude or that the positive trajectories were selected for increasing I. Nor is this contradiction between an experimental treatment and its finite outcome: the local equation fixes resident A/M and reproductive state, whereas transfer starts with heritable variation, possibly correlated loci, distinct historical states and finite genetic transmission. A positive finite change can occur without a positive local `d log(W)/dI`; determining how much arose from stochastic sampling, associations or within-population state change requires a different, controlled analysis. The existing conditional finding is that **directional payoff, inherited possibility and realized change answer different questions**.

## Check B — why R4's total-policy effect changes sign without new mediation claims

Use the first-event mutually exclusive categories already admitted in the source-sealed R4 audit:

- G: founder-relative high-I support is lost *while occupied* before extinction;
- E: extinction precedes G;
- X: occupied at census 80 while retaining the specific high-I target support.

For every allocated founder×visitor crossing, `G+E+X=1`; for the all-start rates,

```text
delta(X) = -delta(G) - delta(E)
```

| Budget | Evolving minus fixed first-G | Evolving minus fixed first-E | Evolving minus fixed X | X policy difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **B8** (complete survival) | +2,132 | 0 | −2,132 | **−8.328125 pp** |
| **B4.5** (harsh resource) | +3,122 | −12,227 | +9,105 | **+35.56640625 pp** |

The difference between these exploratory X contrasts is **+43.89453125 pp**, matching the earlier source-sealed crossed audit. This is an exact arithmetic decomposition over **25,600 paired crosses**, whose independent sampling axes are 160 founders and 160 visitor histories. It is **not** a decomposition into direct genetic and mediated survival effects. G is a cumulative *first-event* indicator, not a cause-specific hazard. Among-survivor support is post-treatment selected and must not be reported as a genetic maintenance effect. Two resource budgets show different total effects; they do not locate a continuous tipping threshold.

## Check C — R1/R2/R3 source identities remain distinct

- Registered original shared-founder R1/R2: four fixed-A low-service I changes are negative and four attenuation effects are positive. This fact **does not reverse the prospectively registered independent fresh-founder overall inconclusive R2 verdict**.
- Exploratory baseline pollen factorial: under both initial A values (0.3, 0.5), the additional visitor decline has a **negative** investment contrast relative to maintained activity under the strongly limited baseline, but a **positive** contrast under the weakly limited baseline. That is not an absolute increase in I versus the founder, nor a controlled equal-absolute-pollen historical-memory test.
- Exploratory known-source held-out skill, evolving-A B8: I-change founder-only **−0.459%**, I-change visitor-history-only **+0.359%**, M-change history-only **+40.146%**. The sum-of-squares **97.622%** I interaction-plus-realization fraction is not an estimate of drift. The matched endpoint-repeatability analysis is another cohort.

## New cross-result insight and its inferential rank

Three levels should not be treated as one response: **(1) attainable allelic support**, **(2) the reproductive return that favors a direction in a specified state**, and **(3) realized changes and their demographic persistence**. R1 and the source payoff establish the non-necessity of evolving A for I decline and a conditional negative selection direction; R2 shows context-dependent coupling; R3 limits known-source predictive skill; R4 distinguishes genetic and demographic outcomes; R5 shows conditional transfer paths can change in a direction not justified as adaptation by local payoff alone.

This conceptual bridge is a *synthesis* of separate designs and readbacks, **not a verified serial mediation path**. No result here proves selection-vs-drift percentages, generally lost evolvability, permanent irreversibility, universal selfing syndrome, speciation, natural island arrival filtering, or actual return to ancestral flowers.

## Most discriminating follow-up experiments (not executed here)

1. **First: R2 matched-pollen test.** Preserve identical founder genotypes and visitor-history identities, then manipulate A expression/evolution while holding realized pollen receipt constant at matched checkpoints. Distinguish absolute q, male export and ovule compensation; prespecify whether the target is a local payoff gradient or a late inherited I response. The existing 384 cases do not isolate this mechanism.
2. **R3 crossed repeated realizations.** At multiple independently sampled founder×visitor cells, repeat demographic stochastic streams while keeping both source axes fixed. Only this can separate the F×V interaction from stochastic realization for each trait, subject to the scope of the new sources and model.
3. **R4 prospectively registered resource gradient.** Freeze multiple B values, identical target/competing-event rules, follow G/E/X over time, and use fresh independently sampled source axes. This can test whether the net X effect changes sign more than once, without relabeling the already observed two-budget reversal as a discovered universal threshold.
4. **R5 same-entry-genome transplant.** Starting from identical viable inherited genotypes, vary visitor return and destination environments, define a biologically meaningful I-change tolerance in advance, and separately measure genetic target support, actual change, persistence, reproductive return and genealogical persistence. Repeat donor times before/after fixation; do not infer A-only effects by comparing unmatched historical cohorts.
5. **R1 optional zero-selfing intervention.** Only if the stronger statement that self-fertilization itself is unnecessary is desired, add a separate true zero-selfing arm. It is **not required** to preserve or strengthen the existing non-necessity-of-A-*evolution* result.
