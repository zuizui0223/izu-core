## Figure 1

The first panel group establishes the exact logical boundary before showing any simulation. Along fixed `k_eff`, the variance factor is fixed while third and fourth cumulant factors change. The analytical mixed-response result then gives the direction of higher-order change in `I/C` as `sign(e²b²−c²d²)`. The alignment line `e²b²=c²d²` is therefore the rank-transport sufficiency boundary at quadratic order.

## Figure 2

The second figure moves from existence to generality to mechanism. The plant–pollinator model shows the concrete `C>I` to `I>C` crossing. The consumer–resource grid prevents the argument from resting on one illustrative parameter set: 18/54 settings show a `C/I` reversal in at least four of six seeds. The prespecified scalar-curvature prediction fails (reversal counts 41, 38, 32 as `H4` rises), after which the fresh intervention isolates the phase-shaping feedback (0/108 versus 52/108 reversals).

## Figure 3

The natural-data figure has two jobs and no more. Panel a demonstrates that breadth and synchrony are separately occupied in 42 admitted systems. Panel b exposes the replication limitation that a scatter plot alone can hide: England removal reduces `phi` span to 0.162 and Martinique removal reduces interior occupancy to 0.188. The appropriate natural conclusion is broad two-dimensional occupancy with source complementarity, not leave-any-source-out invariance.

## Figure 4

The final figure explains why the natural regime map does not itself validate determinant-rank transport. The frozen audit is outcome-rich (21/25 comparable responses) but transition-process-poor (2/25 direct arrival/replacement), with 0/25 complete outcome-independent contracts. Hence formal external prediction remains `not_evaluable`.
