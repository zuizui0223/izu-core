## The key Chapter 1 paradox that Chapter 2 now resolves

Chapter 1 can be summarized as:

```text
greater isolation
    -> stronger pollen limitation
    -> recurrent assurance/accessibility

but

pollinator-facing colour / architecture
    -> different directions among regions
```

Without Chapter 2 this can look like an inconsistent or weak island syndrome.

Unified Model 3 provides a coherent explanation:

```text
common functional problem
        ↓
plant starting state × visitor functional composition
        ↓
different reproductive-selection directions
        ↓
deterministic inheritance preserves those differences
        ↓
assurance / demography / history / connectivity
        ↓
different realized phenotypes and persistence outcomes
```

Thus **functional recurrence and phenotypic non-convergence are not competing conclusions**. They are expected at different levels of the same response chain.

## The H3/H4 connection

Chapter 1's H3 and H4 can now be separated cleanly:

- H3 asks whether isolation is associated with a harsher pollination environment.
- H4 asks whether particular traits are associated with reduced pollen limitation within that environment.

Model 3 shows why both can be true. Reproductive assurance and accessibility can buffer reproductive loss or permit persistence without erasing the upstream pollination problem. Therefore a trait associated with lower pollen limitation among island taxa need not imply that isolation itself is benign or that the trait completely reverses the original ecological constraint.

## What Chapter 2 still cannot claim about Chapter 1

Chapter 2 does not establish:

- that a specific Chapter 1 regional colour/architecture pattern was generated by a particular Model 3 parameter regime;
- that historical *Bombus* loss caused any Chapter 1 regional pattern;
- that the four geographic strata are four evolutionary replicates of one transition;
- that cross-sectional Chapter 1 trait composition is equivalent to the Model 3 B-layer inherited trajectory;
- that reproductive assurance causally mediates the isolation effect in Chapter 1;
- that the Model 3 synthetic trait coordinate is one named colour, tube-depth or nectar-guide trait.

## Real-island evidence closes part of the bridge

The reprojected Chapter 2 real-island evidence gives natural examples of the response vocabulary needed to make the Chapter 1 interpretation biologically credible:

- Izu: common upstream matching decline with downstream floral and pollen branching;
- Ogasawara / Xisha: access or effectiveness differences can propagate toward reproduction;
- Hawaii / Puerto Rico–Mona: altered interaction context can be buffered;
- Dominica: a simple signed-position projection can fail;
- Surtsey / Tiritiri / direct partner-loss systems: founding and history matter.

What is still largely absent is a natural B-layer dataset that follows inherited trait/genotype change through a measured pollinator transition while also tracking demography.
