**Key new falsification pressure:** The sign of the resemblance to a simple A/I-association advantage **reversed in all four exposed synthetic group panels** after removing direct expressed M (in addition to I) from mating/reproductive returns. This does **not** rule out A-mediated genetic hitchhiking in general; it shows the initial positive four-panel result was **not stable under removal of an important M-related reproductive channel in this original Model3 source family**. The stronger control retains separate inherited M variation and mutation, but makes M phenotypically identical in the reproductive ledger; artificial annual I reassignment breaks M–I relationships even when they have no direct payoffs, and can change drift/family distributions. These interactions are **not a pure causal mediation estimate for A**.

In the stronger M/I-neutral A mutation OFF control, all A genes remain the original 0.5, so genetically differential parental reproductive returns have been eliminated by construction; I can still change through finite drift, segregation and mutation. With A mutation ON, A-associated parental differences can arise, but the 24-update I endpoint reflects stochastic trajectory divergence as well as any source mating-weight effect. The source had no per-birth A mutation-origin I ancestry causally manipulated under these policy arms.

## Conclusion and next gate

The existing Model3 mechanism **allows** indirect transmission of otherwise directly neutralized I through dynamically selected multi-locus parent backgrounds. But the evidence to **specifically attribute** that result to positive A–I hitchhiking, to a universal "genetic origin of floral response", or to the specific 1000-update founder sign changes from PR #569 is **not established**. The missing critical test is a **new independent genetic/visitor cohort with explicit source-born A mutant I backgrounds**, an M fitness-neutral control (now technically feasible), a pre-committed full-history target and enough independent founder/history units to evaluate demographic variance.
