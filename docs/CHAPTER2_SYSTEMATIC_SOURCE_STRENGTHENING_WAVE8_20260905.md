## Cleared targets

### Christmas Island

Primary anchor: `10.1186/s40462-022-00315-8`.

Todd et al. tracked Christmas Island flying foxes with GPS telemetry and collected facial/body pollen swabs from 216 animals, alongside direct foraging observations. Pollen identification links the animals to floral resources and supports an island-scale pollen-vector role.

This clears the earlier process-evidence gap. It does not establish a source-state → community-transition → plant-response trajectory.

Decision: `eligible_new_group` for later manuscript-value review.

### Solomon Islands

Primary anchor: `10.2307/2388592`.

Kress (1985) directly observed *Melonycteris woodfordi* pollinating *Heliconia solomonensis* in the Solomon Islands, documented pollen placement and foraging behaviour, and used hand self-pollinations consistent with probable self-incompatibility.

This is strong direct pollination plus breeding-system evidence. Source work is cleared, but manuscript promotion is held until the exact geography of the unresolved Pacific multi-system morphology layer is recovered.

Decision: `hold_overlap_unresolved_pacific_multi_system`.

### Palau

Primary source: *Threatened Endemic Plants of Palau*, Craig Costion / Critical Ecosystem Partnership Fund and Conservation International Pacific Islands Program.

A targeted field study visited flowering *Parkia parvifoliola* trees over a two-week period. It directly observed the Micronesian honeyeater, *Apis mellifera*, *Chrysomya* and additional insects at flowers. The study was explicitly motivated by a historical lost-pollinator hypothesis and concluded that current multiple visitors make simple pollinator-loss limitation unlikely.

This replaces the first-pass syndrome/hypothesis-only state with direct field evidence. Because the source is an authoritative conservation research report rather than a journal article, its provenance is kept explicit. Promotion is also held pending Pacific geographic de-duplication.

Decision: `hold_overlap_unresolved_pacific_multi_system`.

### Comoros and Mayotte

Primary anchors:

- Trewhella et al. 2001, *Acta Chiropterologica* 3(2):135–147;
- `10.1016/j.sajb.2016.02.205` for Mayotte *Vanilla humblotii*.

The Comoros fruit-bat study directly documents flower-feeding interactions on Anjouan and Mohéli, including use of kapok flowers by *Pteropus livingstonii* and *P. seychellensis comorensis*. The Mayotte study directly measures breeding system, flower visitors and natural fruit set in *Vanilla humblotii*.

Together these sources broaden evidence beyond the earlier Mayotte-only record. However, the broad Comoros source does not measure pollination effectiveness. The target is therefore cleared from source-work with an explicit effectiveness caveat rather than treated as a complete process transition.

Decision: `eligible_new_group_with_effectiveness_caveat`.

## Open targets

Regional Caribbean *Zamia–Rhopalotria* sources establish the specialized mutualism and Cayman-associated taxa, but a Cayman-local direct plant-pollination effectiveness or reproductive-outcome observation has not been recovered.

Decision: `hold_local_pollination_directness`.

### Chatham Islands

The geography is already represented in the Southwest Pacific morphology layer, but targeted searching still does not recover a qualifying Chatham-local pollination or breeding-system primary experiment.

Decision: `hold_direct_pollination_unrecovered`.

### Norfolk Island

Authoritative taxonomy describes *Elatostema montanum* as monoecious with male and female inflorescences, whereas the Parks Australia conservation account describes rare male and female plants whose close placement enabled pollination and seed production. A direct local experimental pollination study resolving this wording/mechanism discrepancy has not been recovered.

Decision: `hold_pollination_mechanism_unresolved`.
