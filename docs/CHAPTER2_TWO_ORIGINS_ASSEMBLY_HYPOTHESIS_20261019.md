# Two origins of island reproductive function and floral form — a prospective synthesis hypothesis

**2026-10-19 | Scientific status: NEW CROSS-CHAPTER HYPOTHESIS, NOT A CONFIRMED ISLAND-ASSEMBLY RESULT.** Source readout and literature comparison only; no biological updates, colonization simulations, or changes to any preregistration. This belongs to a **future island assembly study / dissertation synthesis**, not completed Q1–Q4 Results in the single ongoing PR #609 Article. No proof that arrival filtering is deterministic or post-establishment flower evolution random is implied.

## Central hypothesis, revised

**Question:** Does recurrent self-compatible reproductive function among island floras arise more from sorting of arriving mainland lineages, whereas context-dependent floral differentiation arises more from evolution following establishment? Are those two processes differently predictable at lineage and repeated-archipelago levels?

**Proposed asymmetric prediction (to falsify):**
- Establishment/survival sorting is predicted to enrich source-lineage **self-compatibility/autofertility** conditional on arrival and ecological context.
- Conditional post-establishment floral investment **I** may show stronger accumulated responses to changing visitors, with more variation among independently replicated inherited histories; this is a question, not proof of stochasticity or a universally negative response.
- Reproductive capacity **A** may already be high or genetically constrained in some starting states, yet other mating regimes can evolve A after island settlement. Sorting and evolution can both contribute to both traits; estimate their fractions rather than assigning an exclusive origin in advance.

**Non-equivalences:** Ch1 self-compatibility is not identical to model A (continuous autonomous assurance expression/capacity), floral colour/architecture is not identical to model I (net floral investment with maternal+paternal consequences), and apparent mainland versus island differences are not literal pre-/post-pollinator-decline stages of Model3.

## Prior art — a hard constraint on novelty

This is **not** the first colonization-filter hypothesis:
- [Schueller 2004, *Nicotiana glauca*](https://doi.org/10.3732/ajb.91.5.672) directly compared mainland and two newly colonized Channel Islands and concluded that higher self-pollination ability may reflect establishment filtering rather than current selection in established populations.
- [Grossenbacher et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.14534), [Razanajatovo et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/geb.12854) and [Zell et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.20234) report an island self-compatibility/colonization association. Zell et al. explicitly find **arrival opportunity** important, and breeding system alone explained relatively little marginal occurrence variance (approx. 3–4% in one main model).
- [Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson 2020](https://doi.org/10.1086/709018) compared 136 independent island/mainland sister-taxon contrasts and found **no mean island-wide reduction in floral size**, with regional and family differences. Thus “flowers consistently get smaller within lineages” is not an established global fact.

**Potential novelty:** a *single provenance-aware design* identifying how **arrival chance**, **establishment**, **post-establishment persistence**, **within-lineage evolution**, and **their predictability** combine differently for reproductive function versus floral display, with an explicit mapping to corrected macro-flora data. This must beat simple generic sorting and generic finite-population evolution as explanations, not merely reproduce Baker's law.

## Exact staged accounting

For each lineage i, use original-source reproductive/flower traits z_i^S and sampled arrival traits z_i^A (possibly founder-genotype selected from a source distribution). At island-specific standardized censuses define a finite **candidate source pool**, an **arrived pool**, **newly established** lineages, and **survivors** at census T. Trait averages at each stage must use the same species/presence weighting as the final island floral estimand (or predeclare a clearly different abundance-weighted target). Every surviving lineage has an ancestral *arriving* and *established* genotype reference and current z_i^T.

When each stage is nonempty and all weights are normalized under a consistent convention, the telescoping descriptive decomposition is exact:

`zbar_survivors,T − zbar_source,S =
 [zbar_arrived,A − zbar_source,S]                 (dispersal/arrival + source/founder sampling)
+[zbar_established,initial − zbar_arrived,A]      (establishment filter)
+[zbar_survivors,initial − zbar_established,initial] (later lineage persistence filter)
+[zbar_survivors,T − zbar_survivors,initial]       (within-survivor change)`.

This **does not claim mutually exclusive causal mechanisms**. If post-establishment evolution changes extinction risk, the third term partly reflects evolutionary history through survivor identity; within-survivor changes are conditional on surviving. Counterfactual variants are needed to separate that interaction. For empty islands, do NOT compute undefined means as zero: analyze probability of empty island separately. If introduced lineages/immigration enter after establishment, add a corresponding explicit term rather than silently changing the denominator.

To isolate how selection versus within-lineage change affects flora composition, use matched **frozen-trait versus evolving-trait** policies and **staged arrival/establishment survival policies** in a predeclared factorial. Because changing evolution may itself alter survival composition, report policy-dependent counterfactual assemblages rather than pretending a unique additive mediated effect. A symmetrical Shapley-style allocation could describe factorial interactions, but not establish causal mediation unless interventions are well-defined and invariance assumptions justified.

## Minimum new island-assembly experiment — NOT EXECUTED

This is a *new* model layer with explicit arrivals/lineages, not a relabelling of the 160×160 test. Keep Chapter 2 native reproductive operators when biologically appropriate, but validate that source trait SC/auto-A and investment I are comparable before feeding Chapter 1 bins.

1. **Candidate mainland lineages:** independently sample source-genotype populations over a biologically justified range of **A0 and I0**, including different loci/support/variance and at least two reproductive timing/cost modes. The old .3/.5 strata are *initial phenotype design values*, **not** independent field source lineages. Fix an arrival opportunity per lineage and island (distance/propagule pressure), and make it either uncorrelated with A or deliberately correlated in a prospective sensitivity.
2. **Arrival → establishment → persistence:** draw arrival opportunities and propagule counts separately from initial reproductive viability, then record establishment before any within-lineage evolution can occur. After establishment, run existing native Model3 finite reproduction in specified island visitor/resource regimes, recording each unique lineage's occupancy/genotype/trait each census, **including failed arrivals and extinction**. Multiple lineages may occupy the same island under an explicit competition/resource sharing rule, or be independently grown and aggregated as a *no-interaction* island assemblage control; never call the no-interaction pool a real interacting community.
3. **Counterfactuals:** same original source draw, island environment and declared random master streams for arrival; manipulate establishment filter and A/I evolvability separately in 2×2 interventions (no need to assume synchronous RNG consumption after histories diverge). Record original A0/I0, establishment identity, per-lineage genetic change and later fitness. Keep loss of A alleles distinct from expressed selfing and absent SC.
4. **Replicate full source→archipelago draws** and independent reproductive-demographic streams. Repeatedly replaying only the *same* generated founders can estimate conditional noise but cannot test new-lineage generality. Cross source lineage pool, archipelago environment and random demography, with held-out validation of both source lineages and islands. State preregistered stop and budget conditions. Do not start before scientific design authorization.
5. **Outcome and negative controls:** original all-candidate denominators: P(arrive|candidate), P(establish|arrived), P(persist|established) and taxon-richness/failure. Island-flora (species-weighted) A/SC and I trait composition with credible source-state mapping. Force trait-neutral establishment; freeze evolution only; allow evolution only; both natural. Reject the asymmetry if within-lineage A changes dominate SC enrichment, if I composition shifts mainly by establishment/persistence, or if held-out trait-based establishment prediction offers minimal gain over arrival-opportunity, lineage taxonomy and generic ecological baselines.

**Prediction tests:** For both initial-A establishment and I evolution, score strictly held-out individual lineages on Brier/log-loss versus honest prior/arrival models, with calibration, precision and proper support. Also estimate prediction of **archipelago trait composition under independent full resets**, rather than equating a precisely known large-sample average with near-deterministic single lineage outcomes. Compare distributional forecasting on commensurable standardized scales and include repeat-level variance at fixed founder and island.

## External empirical prediction: same species versus total flora

Proposed test, **not yet available from Ch1's species-level trait database**: For established mainland–island populations of the same species, is source-corrected change in **directly measured autonomous selfing/self-compatibility** on average smaller than change in **directly measured display/investment**? Does the among-species flora contrast exceed the corresponding within-lineage change for assurance but not for display? **Do not assume the answer is yes**. Same-species contrasts retain founder-genotype filtering, drift and plasticity, not pure evolutionary selection; a common garden, earliest possible colonist samples, pedigree/genetic history or historical herbarium comparison would help.

A single species-wide trait label in the Ch1 database cannot identify geographic variation within that species. The Chapter1 H1 'within' analysis is not a paired population-level measurement of different flowers in the same species. Record species×island vs species×mainland provenance and distinguish colonist prevalence from evolutionary phenotypic differentiation before meta-analysis.
