## Single authoritative story

**Question.** Does allowing reproductive assurance to evolve under pollination limitation affect population persistence and the remaining founder-relative genetic options for floral investment in the same way?

**Answered within the fixed finite-diploid Model 3.** Floral investment can decline while assurance expression is fixed at A=0.5 (selfing is still possible). In the registered 160-founder × 160-visitor crossed design, all populations survive under B8, while permitting A evolution increases loss of founder-relative high-I allele support by **8.328125 percentage points** (marginal 97.5% interval **6.693910–9.962340**). Under B4.5, allowing A evolution sharply improves census-80 survival, producing the separately registered policy-by-budget survival interaction **92.707031 points** (marginal 97.5% interval **92.049592–93.364471**). These endpoints identify neither a genetic-loss-mediated survival pathway nor an irreversible future failure to adapt. In the late transfer, retained allele support, I polymorphism and phenotypic response are distinct.

**Biological extension, not an observed conclusion.** Descendants may encounter new pollination environments by dispersing from islands into new regions, including hypothetical island-to-mainland re-entry. Adapting at the destination need not mean restoring ancestral floral investment. Existing exploratory 240-path secondary-colonisation findings and pedigree readback demonstrate why destination occupancy, selected marker persistence and genealogical descent must be kept separate; they do **not** establish an A-specific adaptive advantage.
