## Mauritius — introduced competitor to pollination efficiency

Hansen, Olesen & Jones (2002; `10.1046/j.1365-2699.2002.00720.x`) studied introduced *Apis mellifera*, two endemic white-eyes and two endemic *Sideroxylon* trees on Mauritius. The study directly quantified visitor rates, nectar standing crop and nectar production, then used bagging/caging experiments to compare pollination efficiency.

The strongest supported chain is:

`introduced honeybee presence → rapid nectar depletion → reduced temporal access by endemic birds → lower honeybee pollination efficiency than endemic white-eyes`

This is unusually close to the Chapter 2 local-realization/effectiveness stages. It directly shows that partner identity can alter both realized resource access and the quality of pollination service.

The boundary matters. The study lacked a neutral honeybee-free control site, so exploitative competition is strongly supported but not isolated as a before/after causal introduction experiment. Exact honeybee arrival timing, source plant functional state and a matched historical community transition are unavailable. Olesen et al. (2002; `10.1046/j.1472-4642.2002.00148.x`) independently places introduced species within a Mauritian pollination-network context, but it does not close those temporal gaps.

## Ogasawara — community restructuring to asymmetric reproduction

Abe (2006; `10.1093/aob/mcl117`) surveyed flower visitors and floral traits across 12 Ogasawara islands. Solitary endemic small bees dominated preserved satellite-island networks, whereas introduced honeybees dominated the two main islands and had spread to nearby satellite islands.

Watanabe et al. (2018; `10.1111/1442-1984.12183`) then studied the endemic distylous tree *Psychotria homalosperma*. The introduced honeybee was the most frequent visitor, but its morphology and foraging caused pollen transfer mainly from short- to long-styled morphs. Hand/open pollination and pollen-tube observations linked this interaction asymmetry to reproduction: long-styled flowers showed much greater fruit set than short-styled flowers.

Together the source package supports:

`introduced honeybee dominance/spread → island-scale pollinator-community restructuring → focal asymmetric pollen transfer → asymmetric fruit set`

This is a strong empirical analogue of the paper's claim that community realization and organismal state jointly determine response. It is still not one matched transition contract: the island-wide network survey and focal reproductive study differ in taxon, time and unit, and the original long-proboscis pollinator of *P. homalosperma* is inferred rather than historically observed.

## Fiji — recent arrival to realized interaction network

Fiji provides the cleanest arrival chronology among the current process packages. Groom et al. (2015; `10.1111/1744-7917.12136`) documented *Braunsapis puangensis*, first recorded in Fiji in 2007, and its rapid spread. Da Silva et al. (2016; `10.1111/aen.12149`) expanded its distributional and foraging record across major Fijian islands.

Groutsch et al. (2019; `10.1111/aen.12346`) examined three introduced bees and one native bee at 16 sites on Viti Levu, covering 27 plant species and 165 bee–plant interactions. Exotic bees collectively accounted for more visits than the native bee on many plants, while the newly introduced buzz-pollinator *Amegilla pulchra* did not show the expected preference for solanaceous weeds.

The supported chain is therefore:

`recent anthropogenic bee arrival → geographic spread → realized occupation of plant–pollinator interaction networks`

This directly strengthens the model's arrival axis and shows that arrival need not map to a simple uniformly harmful response. The chain stops before plant-specific pollination effectiveness or reproductive output, so Fiji remains process-rich but outcome-incomplete for the full Chapter 2 contract.

## What this changes

The world confrontation is no longer described only as a broad list of heterogeneous outcomes. It now contains three source-verified process packages that resolve different sections of the theoretical mechanism chain:

| Geographic system | Deepest directly supported stage | Main unresolved link |
|---|---|---|
| Mauritius | interaction competition → pollination efficiency | no neutral pre/post or honeybee-free transition |
| Ogasawara | community restructuring → pollen transfer → fruit-set asymmetry | cross-study/taxon linkage; original pollinator inferred |
| Fiji | recent arrival → spread → realized interaction network | no linked plant reproductive response |

These packages strengthen mechanistic plausibility and measurement design. They do not validate the synthetic response surface and do not change any denominator.

## Manuscript implication

The strongest defensible external statement is now:

> Source-verified island studies resolve complementary segments of the proposed mechanism chain: recent pollinator arrival can be followed into network occupation in Fiji; introduced honeybees can alter realized nectar access and provide lower pollination efficiency than endemic birds in Mauritius; and introduced honeybee dominance in Ogasawara co-occurs with focal asymmetric pollen transfer and fruit set. No system yet measures all source-state, transition, local-realization and plant-response coordinates on one outcome-independent matched transition, so formal external prediction remains not evaluable.

This wording improves the world-to-mechanism connection without converting retrospective source packages into validation replicates.
