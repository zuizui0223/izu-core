## 2. The paper's question and answer

**Question:** Does evolution of reproductive assurance under pollination limitation preserve population persistence and the genetic options for subsequent floral evolution to the same extent?

**Supported answer:** No single direction applies to these distinct outcomes. Under the tested native model, assurance evolution changes founder-targeted floral allele support even when all populations survive, while its demographic effect depends strongly on resource conditions. Retaining a target-compatible allele is also different from retaining segregating variation or realizing a subsequent trait response.

**日本語の中心文：** 送粉制約下での生殖保証の進化は、集団の存続と、将来の花形質変化に必要な遺伝的選択肢を同じようには維持しない。全個体群が生存する条件でも高い花投資へのアレル支持範囲を失う確率が変化し、厳しい資源条件では存続が大きく改善する。ただし、これらは異なる評価項目・条件の比較であり、遺伝的損失が存続を媒介した証拠ではない。

The novelty is not the generic proposition that selfing can support reproduction while altering genetic diversity. That broad relationship already has theoretical and experimental precedents. The contribution here is the matched policy comparison of a specified directional floral target and persistence, with explicit tests distinguishing support, polymorphism, sequence and realized response.

## 4. Main-text flow and treatment of the secondary-colonization idea

Use one argument rather than the chronology of every repository experiment:

1. Explain the separable reproductive returns of I and A, and establish that evolving A is not required for investment decline (Figure 1).
2. Establish conditional responses and retain sequence/finite-realization controls (Figures 2–3); do not promote observed A-first events into randomized causal evidence.
3. Give the registered genetic/persistence comparison the greatest interpretive weight, followed by the distinct variation-duration readback (Figures 4–5).
4. Close with the bounded new-environment transfer (Figure 6), distinguishing genetic support, variation and realized response.

The Introduction and Discussion should describe **entry into another region or pollination environment**, not only local pollinator restoration. Island-to-mainland movement is one motivating scenario, not a requirement that ancestral floral investment must be recovered for adaptation to occur. Preserve the user's distribution-change framing without converting a synthetic visitor environment into observed geography.

Suggested connective wording for the next main-text copyedit:

> A population can encounter a new pollination regime because its interaction partners change locally, or because its descendants colonize a different region. The relevant question is therefore not simply whether an ancestral flower reappears, but whether the population retains the variation needed to respond to its destination. We use secondary colonization as a motivation for distinguishing persistence, genetic target support and realized response, without assuming that adaptation requires a return to the ancestral phenotype.

Keep the 240-path secondary-colonization pilot in the supplementary evidence map and a short Discussion passage. The corrected pedigree replay is completed: in the distant resident recipient at t25, fixed/evolving histories retain selected source markers in 2/8 versus 4/8 paths, but genealogical descendants in 4/8 versus 6/8. These are nested futures from **two founder levels crossed with two visitor-history levels**, not eight independent population origins. They illustrate that marker disappearance need not imply descendant extinction; they do not establish an adaptive advantage of island-evolved assurance. [Reviewed integration and resolved findings](CHAPTER2_HITCHHIKING_SECONDARY_EXPANSION_REVIEW_20261020.md).
