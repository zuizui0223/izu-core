# Journal of Ecology cover letter — draft

Dear Editors of *Journal of Ecology*,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Evolution of reproductive assurance can precede floral attraction loss without being required for it under pollinator isolation.”**

Pollinator limitation is often expected to promote reproductive assurance and reduced floral attraction as components of a selfing syndrome. A common interpretation is sequential: reproductive assurance evolves first and subsequently relaxes selection on attraction. Our study separates that temporal observation from the stronger causal claim.

Using one explicit plant–pollinator eco-evolutionary model, we combine fixed-plant reproductive-return assays, maintained-isolation evolutionary trajectories and a matched intervention that blocks evolution of reproductive-assurance capacity. In the focal delayed-selfing setting, visitor limitation reduces the outcross return to floral investment before plant traits evolve. Reproductive assurance then reaches a predeclared change threshold before floral investment in 51 of 64 visitor histories. Importantly, investment still declines when assurance capacity is held fixed.

Both components were independently tested under a prospectively frozen replication using 64 new visitor histories and eight nested demographic repeats. The assurance-first sequence was reproduced in 51/64 histories (95% history-bootstrap 0.688–0.891), while the fixed-assurance intervention again produced a negative far-population investment change and a negative far-minus-near investment contrast. The sequence is not universal: under prior selfing with positive mutation, only 30/64 histories were assurance-first at the same threshold. We therefore make a bounded claim rather than a universal selfing-syndrome law.

We believe the study fits *Journal of Ecology* because the central contribution is a plant ecological mechanism linking pollinator replenishment, reproductive return, mating strategy and floral evolution. The result changes how a familiar ecological pattern should be interpreted: **temporal precedence is not causal necessity**. It also yields a direct empirical prediction—longitudinal studies should measure pollinator exposure, mating route and inherited floral change on the same populations rather than inferring a serial pathway from cross-sectional trait associations.

The manuscript is intentionally concise. Numerical convergence diagnostics, broader parameter surfaces, repeatability analyses and detailed natural-island confrontation are retained in Supporting Information so that the main article remains focused on the confirmed ecological mechanism and its scope boundary.

The confirmatory raw-data archive has been prepared with a frozen checksum and will be cited through a public DOI-backed record before submission. The manuscript does not assign named islands to synthetic model cells, estimate natural evolutionary rates, or claim that reproductive assurance itself is unnecessary.

Thank you for considering this work.

Sincerely,
