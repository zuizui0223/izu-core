# Chapter 2 journal-fit audit: a theory-first submission funnel

**Audit date:** 2026-08-28
**Object audited:** the current Chapter 2 architecture in `docs/ISLAND_ECOLOGY_RESEARCH_ARTICLE_ACTIVE_DRAFT_V2_20260827.md`, `docs/CHAPTER2_CANONICAL_STORY_20260827.md` and the frozen Chapter 2 results
**Constraint:** no new simulation, no new empirical data, no post-hoc projection of known island outcomes into synthetic regimes

## Executive decision

The current Chapter 2 does **not** justify an upgrade from *Journal of Ecology* to *Ecology Letters* by reframing alone. Its strongest publishable contribution is a model-conditional theory of **response geometry**: a common perturbation can generate different response signs because turnover regime, starting state, realized community and local filtering act at different levels. That is a real conceptual contribution, but its present empirical layer is triangulation rather than independent validation, and its generality beyond plant–pollinator systems is argued rather than demonstrated.

The reconstructed paper reaches **project Tier B**: a general conditional-response framework with an empirical reality constraint and a mechanistic-resolution funnel. It does not reach project Tier C because transportability is not demonstrated beyond the focal model class and formal external prediction is not evaluable.

The ranked submission recommendation is:

| Rank | Journal | Route status | Recommended route | Decision under the no-new-results constraint |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Oikos** | **Recommended** | Research paper; Forum only if rewritten as a genuinely synthetic concept paper | Best fit for a project-Tier-B reconstruction around state × realized community × filtering |
| 2 | **Journal of Ecology** | **Strong fallback** | Research article | Safest route if plant reproduction and the Izu zoom remain central |
| 3 | **Ecology** | **Stretch** | Article | Credible but at higher desk risk because the deduction is demonstrated in one model class |
| 4 | **Ecology Letters** | **Closed** | Letter is not currently justified; Synthesis would be a different project | Likely desk rejection; the central objection is not repairable by manuscript restructuring alone |
| 5 | **The American Naturalist** | **Closed** | Major Article is not currently justified | Likely desk rejection for insufficient conceptual unification beyond the focal ecological class |

These route labels are journal-fit decisions, not substitutes for the project Tier definitions.

No sixth journal is added. A specialist theoretical outlet would be technically compatible, but *Oikos* and *Ecology* already cover the defensible general-theory space while *Journal of Ecology* covers the plant-centred route. Adding another title would not resolve the actual decision.

## What the current paper can legitimately contribute

The strongest general object already present is not an island syndrome and not an externally validated predictor. It is this hierarchical architecture:

```text
perturbation composition / turnover balance
    -> response regime
        × starting functional state
        × realized community
        + state-by-community non-additivity
            -> branch identity
                -> local filtering reallocates branches
                    -> downstream assurance mainly changes magnitude
```

The frozen evidence is unusually useful for this argument because it separates roles that are often conflated:

- partner loss and arrival organize transitions among all-positive, mixed and all-negative synthetic regimes;
- starting position organizes mean response geometry but explains only a small share of cell-level variation;
- realized community is the dominant cell-level component, with a substantial non-additive remainder;
- local filtering changes branch identity bidirectionally but asymmetrically;
- assurance attenuates declines without sign rescue in the declared envelope;
- Izu raw realized matching echoes source-state/community-composition geometry, while null-corrected matching does not support non-random partner sorting;
- the 25-entry external audit supplies no outcome-independent full prediction challenge, so formal external model comparison remains `not_evaluable`.

This is a **Level-2 explanatory theory**: it explains how divergent post-establishment responses are possible within a declared model and identifies separable control levels. It is not a field-calibrated transition surface, a frequency estimate for nature, or an ultimate explanation of island assembly and history.

## Journal-by-journal evidence and fit

### 1. Oikos — recommended, ranked first

#### Current scope and recent publication pattern

*Oikos* explicitly prioritizes original theoretical and empirical work aimed at generalization and synthesis across taxa, systems and ecological disciplines. Its Forum format is reserved for high-novelty conceptual unification that serves as a departure point for future work, while ordinary Research papers may report novel theory or critical empirical results ([official journal information](https://www.oikosjournal.org/about-journal/journal-information); [official author guidance](https://nso-journals.org/oikos-for-authors)).

Representative recent publications show both relevant routes. A 2024 Forum paper transferred biodiversity–functioning logic across biological hierarchies by making the cross-level abstraction, rather than one dataset, the contribution ([Mayor et al. 2024](https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/oik.10225)). A 2024 Research paper used a food-web model to integrate sorting and within-group trait adaptation as distinct sources of adaptive capacity ([Wojcik et al. 2024](https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/oik.10544)). A 2025 model paper developed a simplified theoretical route for diagnosing how temperature changes consumer–resource stability ([Synodinos et al. 2025](https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/oik.10761)). These examples do not imply that model-only papers are routinely accepted; they show that model-led work is in scope when the abstraction clarifies a general ecological mechanism.

#### Fit assessment

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Conceptual contribution | **Strong.** Branch generation, branch allocation and downstream magnitude modification can be presented as distinct levels of ecological response. |
| Empirical depth | **Adequate as illustration, not validation.** Izu can show how the abstract variables meet a real data boundary, including the null-corrected failure. |
| Theoretical novelty | **Moderate to strong if reconstructed.** The novelty is the hierarchical response-geometry decomposition, not the existence of context dependence by itself. |
| Generality beyond island pollination | **Plausible but currently latent.** The architecture can apply to consumer–resource turnover, host–pathogen spillover, restoration filters or mutualistic rewiring, but the manuscript must derive the mapping rather than list analogies. |
| Likely desk-reject objection | “This is an island pollination ABM whose broader relevance is asserted after the fact.” |
| Fixable by restructuring alone? | **Yes.** Reframe the paper around a general response map; retain island pollination as the worked biological realization; use Izu as a boundary probe; keep all frozen claim ceilings. |

#### Required reconstruction

1. Replace “island response” as the primary object with **response geometry under community reorganization**.
2. Define four roles before introducing the model: regime control, starting state, realized community and local filtering. Treat assurance as a downstream operator, not another sign-regime axis.
3. Make the main theoretical contrast explicit: a state-only response function versus a state-relative-to-realized-community response function.
4. Present the 80.17% community component and 17.64% non-additivity as the reason that trait-only prediction fails, not as a side result.
5. Use islands and pollination to constrain biological meaning; do not project other systems into the synthetic phase plane from known outcomes.

The Research-paper route is safer than Forum because the current contribution includes new simulation results and a focal empirical analysis. Forum would require a much shorter, broader synthesis whose conceptual unification stands independently of the Chapter 2 result package.

### 2. Journal of Ecology — strong fallback, ranked second

#### Current scope and recent publication pattern

*Journal of Ecology* publishes plant ecology, including plant interactions with animals, fungi and bacteria, and explicitly accepts theoretical studies when they provide general ecological insight beyond a case study ([British Ecological Society scope](https://www.britishecologicalsociety.org/content/journal-of-ecology/); [journal overview](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/13652745)).

Recent publication demonstrates that new plant-centred mechanism theory can stand as a Research Article without a new field dataset: Simon et al. developed a within-host plant disease model to explain when growth differences create pathogen spillover and derived biologically explicit predictions for multi-host plant communities ([Simon et al. 2025](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2745.70002)). The journal also publishes model-plus-large-data work when a new model is advanced as a predictor of broad plant-community patterns, as in a global forest analysis of plant beta diversity ([Xiao et al. 2025](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2745.14487)). Recent plant–pollinator work typically anchors mechanism in observed individual networks, disturbance and plant fitness ([Alves-de-Lima et al. 2026](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1365-2745.70348)). Together, these examples make plant-centred theoretical modeling clearly admissible while showing why empirical anchoring strengthens disturbance-and-interaction papers.

#### Fit assessment

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Conceptual contribution | **Strong within plant ecology.** The paper explains why plants sharing a broad pollinator perturbation need not share a response direction. |
| Empirical depth | **Moderate.** Izu provides source-locked plant and pollinator trait structure, but not an independent test of the synthetic sign surface. |
| Theoretical novelty | **Moderate.** The contribution is a structured separation of turnover regime, starting state, realized community, filtering and assurance. |
| Generality beyond island pollination | **Not required to the same degree as at the generalist journals.** Generality across plant–biotic interaction contexts is sufficient if stated carefully. |
| Likely desk-reject objection | “The empirical layer does not validate the synthetic coordinate, and the manuscript sometimes promises a broader island mechanism than it tests.” |
| Fixable by restructuring alone? | **Yes.** Keep plant reproduction central, call the model a synthetic mechanism experiment, and make the Izu negative/null-corrected result part of the inference boundary. |

This remains the safest route if the desired article is recognizably about island plant ecology rather than a general theory paper. It is ranked below *Oikos* only because the user-requested funnel asks whether the existing architecture can be elevated conceptually without new results; *Oikos* provides the clearer home for that reconstruction.

### 3. Ecology — stretch

#### Current scope and recent publication pattern

*Ecology* accepts theoretical, analytical, experimental and empirical approaches, with emphasis on new concepts, tests of ecological theory and generalizations transferable to other species, populations, communities or ecosystems ([official aims and scope](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/hub/journal/19399170/aims-and-scope/read-full-aims-and-scope)).

Recent Articles show a genuine place for model-led general ecology. Pichon et al. used a spatial plant-community model to connect mixed interaction types with emergent multistability ([Pichon et al. 2024](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ecy.4369)). Medeiros and Saavedra developed a decomposition of state-dependent community sensitivity and checked model patterns against controlled community data ([Medeiros & Saavedra 2023](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ecy.4115)). A 2024 Statistical Report paired simulation validation of a hierarchical community model with an 11-species application ([Gilbert et al. 2024](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ecy.4326)). The relevant pattern is that abstraction is welcomed, but it normally resolves a recognizable general-theory problem and often demonstrates applicability beyond the generating simulation.

#### Fit assessment

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Conceptual contribution | **Potentially strong.** Response geometry and branch reallocation are general ecological ideas. |
| Empirical depth | **Borderline.** The focal empirical analysis is not a validation of predicted response regimes. |
| Theoretical novelty | **Moderate.** State-dependent perturbation response already has an active literature; the paper must show what branch generation versus branch allocation adds. |
| Generality beyond island pollination | **Necessary and not yet demonstrated.** A formal mapping to a generic interaction-reorganization problem is needed. |
| Likely desk-reject objection | “The parameter-surface diagnosis is informative for this model, but it does not yet yield a sufficiently general ecological deduction.” |
| Fixable by restructuring alone? | **Partly.** A theory-first manuscript could become reviewable, but the absence of a second model class, analytical result or independent response test remains a material risk. |

*Ecology* is therefore a credible stretch, not the default. It should follow *Oikos* in the funnel only if the manuscript can state a precise general result that survives removal of island terminology from the title, abstract and conceptual figure.

### 4. Ecology Letters — closed under the current evidence state

#### Current scope and recent publication pattern

*Ecology Letters* describes itself as a venue for rapid publication of the most novel ecological research and prioritizes concise papers of originality, general interest and contribution to new developments; it discourages descriptive confirmation or extension. Its Perspective, Synthesis and Method formats are invitation- or proposal-mediated rather than ordinary unsolicited full-paper routes ([official aims and scope](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14610248/homepage/productinformation.html)).

Recent papers illustrate two demanding but different routes. A 2025 Letter directly subjected coexistence-theory forecasts to a highly replicated multigenerational experiment and reported both predictive overlap and low precision ([Terry 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ele.70047)). A 2025 Synthesis combined theoretical simulations with a curated literature dataset to argue that global change asymmetrically rewires habitat coupling across ecosystem types ([Ward et al. 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ele.70174)). The journal also publishes theory-first Letters when the theoretical object itself is broad, as in a community-level measure of collective integration ([Zelnik et al. 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ele.14358)). These examples show that empirical data are not an absolute requirement; they also show that model-only entry requires a field-level theoretical object rather than a broad claim attached to a focal architecture.

#### Fit assessment

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Conceptual contribution | **Interesting but below the current threshold.** Conditional response geometry is useful, yet context dependence and state dependence are not themselves new developments. |
| Empirical depth | **Insufficient for the Letter route.** Izu triangulates raw matching but rejects the stronger null-corrected interpretation; zero external systems meet the frozen full prediction contract. |
| Theoretical novelty | **Insufficiently general.** The transition surface is a diagnostic of one declared synthetic design, not an analytical or model-class invariant. |
| Generality beyond island pollination | **Claimed, not established.** No second interaction class or independent cross-system response challenge demonstrates transportability. |
| Likely desk-reject objection | “The manuscript extrapolates a focal synthetic model into a general ecological principle without commensurate independent prediction, broad synthesis or general theory.” |
| Fixable by restructuring alone? | **No for a Letter.** Stronger framing cannot create external validation or a model-invariant result. A Synthesis would require a proposal and a new structured evidence synthesis, making it a different project rather than a rewrite. |

Submission to *Ecology Letters* now would spend the strongest result—the protected Level-2 explanation—on a venue whose likely objection is already documented by the frozen readiness audit. The correct stop rule is to retain this journal as a future route only after a genuinely new gate: independent outcome-blind response prediction, a general mathematical result, or a broad predeclared synthesis across interaction classes.

### 5. The American Naturalist — closed under the current evidence state

#### Current scope and recent publication pattern

*The American Naturalist* seeks conceptual unification across ecology, evolution, behavior and organismal biology and prioritizes work that poses significant problems or changes how a broad biological readership thinks. Major Articles may contain new theory, data or analysis, but the broad conceptual issue must remain central ([official aims, scope and article types](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/journals/an/instruct)). The journal introduced an “Inspiring Perspectives” format in 2025 for short, potentially paradigm-changing conceptual arguments rather than empirical reports or balanced syntheses ([editor's announcement](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/735689)).

Representative recent papers are correspondingly organismal and unifying. A 2024 article combined network and evolutionary theory to derive how changes along the mutualism–antagonism continuum alter community coevolution ([Pedraza et al. 2024](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/727472)). Kao et al. combined a conceptual framework, theoretical model and cross-taxon literature to explain why social organisms show opposing responses to resource scarcity ([Kao et al. 2023](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/725426)). A 2025 study used long-term song-sparrow information to parameterize simulations integrating evolutionary rescue, inbreeding and migration load ([Reid et al. 2025](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/737752)). These examples place the bar at biological unification, eco-evolutionary mechanism or cross-organism explanatory reach, not merely technical model novelty.

#### Fit assessment

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Conceptual contribution | **Promising but too ecologically local.** The distinction among regime, starting state, realization and filtering is conceptually clean. |
| Empirical depth | **Too narrow for the present Major Article claim.** The focal Izu test does not establish the theory across organisms or mechanisms. |
| Theoretical novelty | **Below the journal's unification bar.** The ABM exposes conditionality but does not derive a new general biological law or integrate ecological and evolutionary dynamics. |
| Generality beyond island pollination | **Not demonstrated.** The ultimate-WHY and evolutionary layers are explicitly outside the model. |
| Likely desk-reject objection | “This is a strong domain-specific ecological model, not a conceptual unification of organismal biology.” |
| Fixable by restructuring alone? | **No as a Major Article.** A separate short Inspiring Perspective could use the architecture to propose a general hypothesis, but that would abandon the current result paper and would need a genuinely provocative cross-biological thesis. |

The American Naturalist should therefore not be treated as an adjacent fallback after *Ecology Letters*. Its criterion is different, not simply a lower or higher empirical threshold.

## Recommended theory-first manuscript funnel

### Recommended route: Oikos research paper

Use a system-general title such as:

> **Response geometry under community reorganization: how starting state, realized community and local filtering generate divergent outcomes**

The abstract should lead with the general inverse problem: the same directional environmental change can produce opposite outcomes, so mean perturbation effects do not identify response architecture. The island plant–pollinator model becomes a fully specified test case. The central novelty statement should be:

> A perturbation can determine the response regime without determining branch identity; starting state organizes mean geometry, realized community dominates individual outcomes, and local filtering reallocates branches after the regional perturbation has occurred.

The Izu analysis should be retained because its partial success and null-corrected failure demonstrate the distinction between raw matching geometry and non-random interaction sorting. The 25-entry readiness failure belongs in the inference-boundary section or Supplementary Information, not as a pseudo-validation panel.

### Strong fallback: Journal of Ecology research article

Keep the present plant-centred title family and make the biological question narrower:

> Why do plant lineages exposed to pollinator-community reorganization show different reproductive response directions?

Lead with plant functional matching, community realization and reproductive propagation. Retain the island context as the biological source of the perturbation and retain Izu as empirical triangulation. Do not promise taxon-general theory; promise a general principle for plant responses to changing biotic interaction environments.

### Stretch route: Ecology article

Attempt only if the manuscript can meet three writing tests without new results:

1. the theory is intelligible without island-specific terminology;
2. the novelty relative to existing state-dependent community-response theory is stated as a formal branch-generation/branch-allocation distinction;
3. every general claim is tied to a result already present, while cross-system analogies are labelled as predictions rather than demonstrations.

Failure of any test returns the paper to the plant-centred Journal of Ecology route rather than authorizing additional post-hoc simulation.

## Non-negotiable claim ceilings across all routes

- Do not interpret synthetic regime counts, filtering transition rates or assurance frequencies as frequencies in nature.
- Do not call the 10-parameter regression a field-causal driver model.
- Do not map observed island systems into synthetic regimes from their known outcomes.
- Do not describe Izu raw-match support as validation of null-corrected sorting, floral evolution or the synthetic sign surface.
- Do not make assurance a sign-rescue mechanism; in the frozen envelope it is a magnitude modifier.
- Do not turn geographic overlap labels into an independent archipelago denominator.
- Do not claim ultimate WHY: assembly, colonization history and the origins of starting state remain outside Chapter 2.
- Do not repair the failed external-prediction gate through outcome-derived proxies, system deletion or response-scale pooling.

## Final recommendation

The best no-new-results strategy is **Oikos first, Journal of Ecology second**. The Oikos version should be a genuine theory-first reconstruction, not the current island manuscript with a broader title. If preserving the empirical plant-island narrative is more important than maximizing conceptual breadth, retain *Journal of Ecology* as the first target.

*Ecology* is a defensible stretch after the general response architecture is made explicit. *Ecology Letters* and *The American Naturalist* should remain closed under the current evidence state: their likely objections concern missing independent support or missing field-level conceptual unification, neither of which can be supplied by stronger rhetoric.

This audit uses representative recent papers to characterize demonstrated editorial fit; it is not an exhaustive sample and does not estimate acceptance probabilities.
