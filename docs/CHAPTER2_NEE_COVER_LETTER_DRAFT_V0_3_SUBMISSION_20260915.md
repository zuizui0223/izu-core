# Cover letter — Nature Ecology & Evolution

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Determinant rankings in nonlinear ecology depend on breadth and synchrony,”** as an Article for *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.

Ecologists routinely ask which factor matters most and often treat the resulting ranking as a portable property of the system. Our central result is that this interpretation can fail: in nonlinear ecological responses, determinant order can be a property of the ecological regime in which a system is observed.

The manuscript combines four evidence layers that were kept analytically separated.

First, we derive an exact sufficiency boundary. Under a bilinear state–community response, a variance-equivalent effective number gives community and state-by-community contributions the same second-moment multiplier, so they cannot reverse rank. Higher-order analysis shows why that sufficiency is not generic: equal second moments leave higher cumulants unconstrained, and an exact quadratic response identifies the mixed-curvature condition under which those omitted moments alter determinant ordering.

Second, two structurally distinct nonlinear ecological models cross this boundary. We retain a prespecified scalar-curvature prediction that failed rather than optimizing it away. Guided by that failure and the exact mixed-response result, we froze a feedback intervention before running six unused simulation seeds. Active state adjustment produced 52 community-to-interaction rank reversals in 108 paired comparisons; the feedback knockout produced none. The intervention changes phase topology rather than merely increasing one variance component.

Third, before extracting natural coordinates we froze a result-blind public-data admission and measurement protocol together with numerical safeguards against interpreting a narrow or one-dimensional natural sample as broad regime occupancy. The final regime plane contains 42 island interaction systems from six studies and five island or archipelago groups. Source-balanced partner breadth and temporal synchrony span both dimensions, are not strongly collinear and occupy the interior of the plane. The pattern remains broad after removing all 19 systems from Mallorca, the largest study, after which source searching was stopped according to the prespecified rule.

We also report a stricter leave-one-study-out sensitivity analysis performed after the primary natural analysis was fixed. Removing England STEP reduces synchrony dispersion, and removing Martinique reduces interior occupancy. We therefore do not describe every region of the natural plane as redundantly supported by multiple sources. The natural result is narrower and more informative: the context space required by the theory is real and broadly occupied, while its present under-replicated regions identify clear targets for prospective study.

Finally, a separate frozen audit of 25 island research entries finds 21/25 with comparable plant responses, only 2/25 with direct partner arrival or replacement, and 0/25 with the complete outcome-independent determinant–response contract. We therefore do not claim that the synthetic determinant crossover has already been observed in nature. Instead, the natural regime analysis establishes external occupancy of the relevant context dimensions, while the audit explains why existing studies cannot yet test transport of determinant rankings across those regimes.

We believe the manuscript is suited to *Nature Ecology & Evolution* because the advance is not specific to island pollination or to a single modelling architecture. It concerns a broad inferential practice across ecology: compressing structured context and then treating a determinant ranking as globally meaningful. The paper provides an exact mathematical boundary, two nonlinear demonstrations, a failed prediction retained as evidence, a prospective mechanism intervention, a result-blind external regime test and a concrete measurement agenda for comparative ecology.

The manuscript does not equate natural Hill diversity with the synthetic effective-number parameter, infer a universal natural threshold, use natural data to retune the models, or claim a natural `C/I` crossover. Great Britain retains a source compilation’s pre-existing island-study classification; it was not reclassified after inspecting its position in the natural regime plane. Source-admission decisions, effort normalization and natural coordinates are versioned with source locks and checksums.

This work is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. Earlier manuscript surfaces developed during the project remain archived provenance and are not simultaneously submitted versions of this manuscript. Any future companion submission reusing frozen model or island-evidence material will disclose overlap explicitly.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
