## Short scope enquiry (draft)

Dear Editors,

We would welcome your advice on the suitability of a modelling study entitled “Founding constraints and the predictability of reproductive evolution in an island plant model”. The study asks how inherited variation and pollinator limitation shape reproductive assurance, floral investment and subsequent evolutionary options after colonization.

The contribution is a set of interventions connecting questions usually considered separately: whether floral investment can decline without assurance evolution, how assurance changes environmental investment contrasts, which trait responses are predictable, and whether demographic persistence coincides with retention of a specified floral option. Across archived finite-population experiments, investment declined with assurance expression fixed. In separate registered comparisons, assurance evolution strongly improved persistence under harsh resources and increased loss of alleles compatible with a founder-relative investment target where all populations survived. Exploratory prediction and transfer analyses distinguish these intervention results from hypotheses about their mechanisms and future colonization.

We frame the island implications as model-based predictions, with autonomous assurance, investment allocation and visitor matching distinguished from self-compatibility, flower size and speciation. The manuscript uses five Results sections and six main figures. Its abstract follows below.

Island colonists must reproduce with available partners while retaining options for later environmental change. Using a finite diploid model with separately inherited reproductive assurance, pollinator matching and floral investment, we asked which responses to pollinator limitation are predictable and which genetic options persist. Founding variation constrained accessible changes, while exploratory known-source prediction from founder identity provided little skill for investment change. Investment declined with assurance expression fixed, and allowing assurance evolution modified environmental investment contrasts. Repeated-history experiments showed comparatively repeatable assurance endpoints. In a separate crossed cohort, visitor histories predicted matching change better than investment change. A preregistered comparison found a 92.71-percentage-point survival policy-by-resource interaction and, under a separate condition with complete survival, an 8.33-point increase in loss of alleles compatible with a founder-relative investment target. Bounded transfers further distinguished persistence, ancestral recovery and response through other traits. Reproductive assurance thus connects immediate reproduction with distinct demographic and genetic consequences: it changes both which populations persist and which floral options remain available to their descendants.

## Formal cover letter (draft)

Dear Editors,

Please consider “Founding constraints and the predictability of reproductive evolution in an island plant model” for publication as an Article in Nature Ecology & Evolution.

Island colonists face both an immediate reproductive challenge and a subsequent evolutionary one: successful reproduction need not preserve the variation needed for a later floral response. We use a finite diploid model to connect those outcomes while separating reproductive assurance, investment in attraction and visitor matching. The study combines intervention comparisons, registered demographic and genetic endpoints, and explicitly exploratory analyses of predictability and transfer.

Our main contribution is the distinction between mechanisms and outcomes that a single selfing-syndrome narrative can conflate. Investment decline does not require assurance evolution, yet evolving assurance changes environmental investment contrasts. Assurance also changes persistence and retention of a specified floral option under distinct resource conditions. This connects reproductive ecology to evolutionary predictability without equating low predictive skill with drift, or target-allele retention with general evolvability. The island setting supplies testable ecological questions; speciation and mainland recolonization remain implications for future work.

All findings derive from existing experiments and saved outputs. The manuscript retains null and inconclusive outcomes, separates registered from exploratory results, and provides source-linked figures and reproducibility records.
