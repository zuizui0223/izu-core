Dear Editors of *Evolution Letters*,

Please consider our Letter, **“Directional similarity can mask opposite changes in historical repeatability in a generative island-floral model,”** for publication in *Evolution Letters*.

The manuscript asks why evolutionary responses can become more similar in direction while differing in how strongly ecological history remains reproducible. Using one explicit eco-evolutionary visitor-transfer, reproduction and inheritance operator, we separate immediate selection, deterministic genotype propagation, genetic accessibility and finite-population realization. The central result is that larger plant populations and visitor-history pooling can both increase directional sign uniformity while having opposite effects on reproducible history structure.

A post-hoc theoretical reduction provides the mechanistic interpretation. Additive trait means obey an exact Price closure under the declared reduction conditions, while higher-order inheritance structure does not admit the same closure. Floral investment has an analytic marginal pollination-benefit versus investment-cost threshold. A separately frozen rare-mutant extension, including maternal, paternal and selfed transmission, showed that the same near–far visitor histories rotate joint selection toward lower floral investment and greater reproductive assurance across the frozen state grid. The finite two-trait follow-up did **not** support common alternative selfing/outcrossing endpoint classes. Importantly, the structural delayed-selfing control itself reached a 16.4% maximum syndrome-endpoint frequency; prior selfing reached 10.2%, pollen discounting 21.3%, and assurance cost 43.0%. We therefore interpret endpoint occurrence as non-specific to trade-offs, with the clearest trade-off-specific amplification under assurance cost.

We believe the work fits *Evolution Letters* because it connects a broad problem in evolutionary repeatability to a mechanistically explicit model and prospective validation. The model is not calibrated to named islands and is used to establish sufficiency and separation rather than natural prevalence. The manuscript also explicitly distinguishes the exact nonlocal continuous sexual-genetic system from the reduced phenotype equation; it does not describe Model 3 itself as a PDE.
