# Response modes to pollinator-regime simplification

## Why a response-mode layer is needed

The comparative Izu programme should not assume that every plant lineage responds to altered pollination by changing the same continuous floral trait. The current evidence already contains qualitatively different outcomes that would be biologically distorted if forced into one effect-size scale.

`data/predictive_meta/regime_response_mode_registry.csv` therefore records **what kind of response is observed** separately from the quantitative within-lineage synthesis.

## Current modes

### 1. Within-lineage continuous erosion

The source-locked *Campanula microdonta* calibration contains continuous island-series erosion in flower size and multilocus outcrossing. These are quantitative response shapes, but the continuous axis is not mechanistically identified because population/colonisation history is also ordered across the archipelago.

### 2. Threshold reproductive assurance

Autonomous reproductive capacity in the same *Campanula* calibration shows the distinct Oshima-to-Toshima transition. The tested climate PC1, mainland distance, island area/connectivity, and pre-1986 volcanic-recency axes do not reproduce this response shape as well as the predeclared step.

This makes the autonomous-capacity channel the strongest current breakpoint leverage, but not a historical causal estimate of Bombus loss.

### 3. Hybrid replacement and interaction rewiring

Suetsugu et al. (2024; DOI `10.1111/nph.19325`) provide a different response mode on Kozu Island. The specimens initially identified as the mainland Bombus-dependent orchid *Goodyera henryi* were genetically identified as hybrids with *G. similis*; pure *G. henryi* was not recovered on Kozu. The hybrids are pollinated by the scoliid wasp also pollinating *G. similis* on the island.

The source additionally reports a shorter viscidium in the Kozu hybrid population (2.2 ± 0.2 mm, n=56) than in mainland *G. henryi* (3.1 ± 0.3 mm, n=60). These values are retained in `goodyera_suetsugu_2024_source_extraction.csv`, but the identity change means the contrast is **not** eligible as a within-lineage geographic effect.

The correct response label is therefore not “pure *G. henryi* generalized its pollination system.” It is **hybrid replacement accompanied by interaction rewiring**.

### 4. Matched dependency control

In the same Goodyera study, *G. similis* uses scoliid wasps on both mainland and Kozu, and the source reports no discernible regional difference in its floral morphological traits. This is a useful within-study dependency control because it shares genus and much of the sampling context without sharing the mainland *G. henryi* Bombus dependency.

The published statement is not an equivalence test; until trait-level uncertainty is used in a predeclared equivalence framework, it remains qualitative control evidence.

### 5. Alternative-guild specialization

The *Lilium auratum* source remains an alternative mechanism example: island var. *platyphyllum* shows stronger crepuscular/nocturnal hawkmoth-associated scheduling, whereas the mainland variety uses a broader day/night Lepidoptera context. Because variety and geography are confounded, it does not enter the staged-Bombus holdout.

## Comparative interpretation

The emerging programme therefore distinguishes at least four biologically different outcomes when a lineage encounters a changed pollination environment:

1. persist with gradual within-lineage trait or mating-system change;
2. cross a threshold into reproductive assurance;
3. rewire interactions through hybridization or another lineage-level transition;
4. fail to show the shared response expected for a dependency-matched contrast.

A fifth ecological outcome—failure to establish or persist across a regime boundary—belongs to the separate occupancy/filtering analysis.

These outcomes must not be pooled as if they were noisy measurements of one “island adaptation score.” The quantitative meta-analysis remains restricted to genuinely compatible within-lineage observations; the response-mode registry is a parallel categorical/mechanistic synthesis.

## Falsification value

This broader architecture makes the pollinator hypothesis harder, not easier, to satisfy. A convincing result must explain why:

- a specialist lineage can show a shared breakpoint or a biologically coherent alternative response;
- a matched nondependent lineage does not repeatedly show the same breakpoint;
- static geography and measured history competitors do not reproduce the pattern;
- lineage replacement, hybridization, and occupancy filtering are not mislabelled as within-lineage evolution.

The present evidence is sufficient to define these response classes, but not yet to estimate their frequencies across the Izu flora. That requires additional independent lineages and a preregistered eligibility rule.
