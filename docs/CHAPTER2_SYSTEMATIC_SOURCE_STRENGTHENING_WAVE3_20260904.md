## Cleared targets

### Virgin Islands

Primary anchor: `10.1017/S0030605320001234`.

The Oryx study of the British Virgin Islands endemic tree *Vachellia anegadensis* directly measured flower visitation and pollinator dependency on Anegada and Fallen Jerusalem. Animal visitation increased seed-pod production, with Lepidoptera particularly important. This replaces the first-pass regional alien-flora trait compilation with direct endemic-plant pollination and reproductive evidence.

The study still lacks a matched historical community transition, so it does not pass the full Chapter 2 contract.

### Guadeloupe

Primary-data anchor: `10.5772/64674`.

Wild *Vanilla mexicana* populations were sampled across Guadeloupe. Reproductive-biology experiments compared insect-exclusion bagging with natural fruit set; bagged and open fruit set were approximately equal, and population-genetic evidence supported predominant selfing/autogamy. This gives direct local reproductive evidence rather than only an applied controlled-pollination record.

The source is retained as a primary-data book chapter, not relabelled as a journal article.

### Marquesas Islands

Primary anchor: `10.1098/rsbl.2011.0771`.

The Pacific *Glochidion–Epicephala* study surveyed specialized pollination mutualists across remote islands and includes source-native Marquesas evidence. Female *Epicephala* specimens from Fatu Hiva and Nuku Hiva carried pollen, and the broader 17-island survey directly established persistence/reassembly of the specialized pollination mutualism across southeastern Polynesia.

The Marquesas evidence supports direct source-native mutualist presence and pollen carriage, but not a matched source-state → community-transition → plant-response trajectory.

### Pitcairn Islands

Anchors: `10.1111/j.1467-8748.2010.01715.x` plus the National Botanic Gardens of Ireland *Abutilon pitcairnense* conservation-project results.

The conservation project reports five days of flowering-season observations on Pitcairn in 2010: ants visited early in flower life and bees visited more frequently, especially in the afternoons. The species history, locality and ex-situ conservation context are independently documented in Curtis's Botanical Magazine.

This is sufficient to clear the first-pass claim that no local pollinator observation had been recovered. It is **not** sufficient for manuscript promotion: the observation window is short and no natural reproductive-effect experiment links those visitors to plant fitness. Pitcairn is therefore retained as a direct search record but not promoted as a global-confrontation candidate.

## Targets that remain open

### Comoros and Mayotte

Primary Mayotte anchor: `10.1016/j.sajb.2016.02.205`.

The *Vanilla humblotii* study provides strong direct Mayotte reproductive and visitor evidence. However, the broad search target is `Comoros and Mayotte`; equivalent source-native coverage for Grande Comore, Anjouan and Mohéli has not been recovered. Strong evidence from one subtarget is not treated as complete archipelago coverage.

The target remains a breadth candidate with an explicit subtarget limit, but source strengthening remains open.

### Cayman Islands

Anchors include `10.11646/zootaxa.3970.1.1`, `10.3389/fpls.2021.639368` and the Cayman hawkmoth/ghost-orchid study (`10.5281/zenodo.7167943`).

Cayman cycad-associated *Rhopalotria* are directly documented and Caribbean *Zamia–Rhopalotria* pollination is strongly supported regionally. The ghost-orchid work recovers plausible hawkmoth pollinators but explicitly lacks a direct observed orchid-pollination event. Host association and pollinator plausibility are therefore not converted into a direct Cayman plant-pollination result.

### Chatham Islands

The Chatham geography is already represented within the current Southwest Pacific morphology layer, and the flora is well documented. The recovered *Phormium tenax* review and Chatham flora sources do not isolate a Chatham-specific visitor or reproductive experiment. Process-level source work therefore remains open.
