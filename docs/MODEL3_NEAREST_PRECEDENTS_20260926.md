## Verified sources and overlap

### Peterson & Kay (2015), American Naturalist, 10.1086/679107

Primary full text, including methods:
https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086%2F679107

Their diploid individual-based source-sink model includes colonization, gene flow, quantitative adaptation, pollen limitation and genetic load. A plastic shift in prior selfing differs from the archived delayed-selfing mechanism. This substantially overlaps proposed rescue and variation-retention arguments. Adding seed immigration and selfing is not itself a new contribution. Visitor-mediated investment/matching and interruption/recovery experiments are possible extensions, not established novel results.

### Xu (2023), American Naturalist, 10.1086/725425

Primary author PDF, model section pp. 338–340:
https://www.zoology.ubc.ca/edg/pdfs/Xu2023.pdf

The model distinguishes plastic and evolutionary selfing increases after pollen limitation, includes deleterious mutations and modifier alleles, and combines deterministic population genetics with stochastic demography. It separates ovule-based selfing from effective selfing among fertilized seeds. Its focal mechanism competes with outcross opportunities. This closely precedes rescue timing and population-size comparisons. Changing selfing implementation alone is not sufficient novelty.

### Degottex-Fery & Cheptou (2023), Evolutionary Ecology, 10.1007/s10682-023-10266-0

Primary publisher abstract; full methods not accessible:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10682-023-10266-0

This paper models mating-system evolution after a pollinator crash under infinite and finite population size, including stochastic pollination and inbreeding-depression effects. It directly precedes the proposed broad finite-versus-deterministic headline. Equation-level differences remain unverified. The abstract rejects a claim that this broad comparison is new, but does not establish that every Model 3 mechanism is already covered.

### Zell et al. (2025; online 2024), New Phytologist, 10.1111/nph.20234

Primary publisher methods:
https://nph.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/nph.20234

This analysis combines island occurrence, breeding system, lifespan, floral symmetry and arrival opportunity. Distance runs from mainland occurrences to island coastlines and is an opportunity proxy, not a measured dispersal rate. Oceanic and continental island occurrence are also compared. Global trait/arrival/history comparisons therefore already exist. Model 3 can investigate dynamic mechanisms, but cannot claim that this entire global problem was unstudied. Their geological classification is not a direct map of founding versus vicariance trajectories.

## Consequences for the claim

Reject as standalone novelty: finite populations differ from deterministic models; selfing supplies reproductive assurance; colonization, immigration or lifespan affects adaptation; island flowers show mixed directions; changing regimes changes variance-component rankings.

Retain existing attenuation as model-conditional evidence worth explaining. The best current candidate is **separating direct selection on costly attraction from assurance-mediated change, then explaining when interruption and restoration of effective pollination produce recoverable versus persistent floral differences across repeated islands**. Finite variation and seed immigration are testable explanations for different recovery outcomes. Selection must emerge from visitor effectiveness and pollen transfer rather than an assigned optimum guaranteeing the answer.

This candidate is unverified and not certified absent from prior literature. If only a familiar source-sink/selfing-rescue result survives the controls, report that narrower result rather than rename it a new regime.

## Discriminating evidence required

1. Fixed-state reproductive assays identify changed marginal investment returns; assurance capacity and realized selfing remain distinct.
2. Exposure-matched visitor chronology alters trajectories under a common recovery environment, or the history hypothesis is rejected for that regime.
3. Visitor restoration and demographic/genetic replenishment have separately measured effects; immigrant replacement is not resident adaptation.
4. Finite/density differences are re-evaluated after pollen, recruitment-expectation and grid controls; interpretation follows the surviving contrast.
5. Occupancy, extinct trajectories and survivor-conditioned traits retain explicit denominators.

These predictions may fail. Failure is not permission for post-outcome retuning.
