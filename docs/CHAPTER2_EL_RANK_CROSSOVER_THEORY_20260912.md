# General sufficient conditions for determinant-rank crossover

Updated: 2026-09-12

## Goal

Abstract the Chapter 2 system-size result away from pollination-specific `k` and identify when ecological determinant rankings should or should not reverse under community averaging.

The object is a two-way response surface

`Y_k = f(X, Zbar_k)`

where `X` is starting state and `Zbar_k` is an average of `k` community/environment realizations. The Chapter 2 two-way decomposition corresponds to a starting-state main effect, a community-realization main effect and a state-by-community non-additive remainder.

## Smooth-response sufficient condition

Let `Z_1,...,Z_k` be iid community-state vectors with mean `mu` and covariance `Sigma`, and let

`Zbar_k = k^-1 sum_i Z_i`.

Assume `f(x,z)` is twice continuously differentiable near `z=mu` and the required moments are finite. Define population analogues of the two-way components:

- `S_k = Var_X( E_Z[f(X,Zbar_k) | X] )`,
- `C_k = Var_Z( E_X[f(X,Zbar_k) | Zbar_k] )`,
- `I_k = Var(f(X,Zbar_k)) - S_k - C_k`.

A Taylor expansion around the mean community state gives

`E_Z[f(x,Zbar_k)] = f(x,mu) + O(k^-1)`.

Hence

`S_k = S_inf + O(k^-1)`,

where

`S_inf = Var_X[f(X,mu)]`.

Let `g(x)=grad_z f(x,mu)` and `gbar=E_X[g(X)]`. Because

`Var(Zbar_k)=Sigma/k`,

the community main effect has first-order coefficient

`C_k = (gbar^T Sigma gbar)/k + o(k^-1)`.

The first-order state-by-community remainder is generated by variation in sensitivity across starting states:

`I_k = E_X[(g(X)-gbar)^T Sigma (g(X)-gbar)]/k + o(k^-1)`.

Therefore, if `S_inf>0`, the starting-state component remains order one while the community main effect averages down at order `1/k`. There must then exist a sufficiently large finite `k` for which `S_k > C_k`.

If additionally `C_1 > S_1`, the determinant ranking must reverse somewhere along the sequence from the small stochastic regime to the large-community regime. This is a sufficient-condition statement; it does not imply a universal crossover location.

An approximate crossover scale when higher-order terms are small is

`k_star approx (gbar^T Sigma gbar) / S_inf`.

This is an effective stochasticity-to-state-contrast ratio, not a richness threshold.

## Exact bilinear special case

For centered independent `X` and `Zbar_k`, let

`f(x,z)=a*x + b*z + c*x*z`.

Then the two-way decomposition is exact:

- `S_k = a^2 Var(X)`,
- `C_k = b^2 Var(Zbar_k)`,
- `I_k = c^2 Var(X) Var(Zbar_k)`.

For independent community copies with `Var(Z)=sigma^2`,

`Var(Zbar_k)=sigma^2/k`.

Thus

- starting-state variance remains constant,
- community-realization variance decays exactly as `1/k`,
- interaction remains positive at every finite `k` when `c != 0`, while also decaying as `1/k` asymptotically.

The exact crossover criterion is

`k > b^2 sigma^2 / (a^2 Var(X))`.

This minimal case captures the qualitative Chapter 2 result without any pollinator-specific parameter.

## When crossover need not occur

Independent averaging is not guaranteed in ecological communities. If the `k` community copies are exchangeable with pairwise correlation `rho`, then

`Var(Zbar_k)=sigma^2 [rho + (1-rho)/k]`.

The community component therefore has a nonzero asymptotic floor

`C_inf = b^2 sigma^2 rho`.

In the bilinear case, a large-k starting-state dominance requires

`a^2 Var(X) > b^2 sigma^2 rho`.

If the shared stochasticity floor is larger than the starting-state component, averaging more community units does not force a rank reversal.

This gives an ecological countercondition: synchronous forcing, spatial covariance or other shared environmental variation can preserve community-realization dominance even when nominal community size is large.

The corresponding effective number of independent community units is

`k_eff = k / [1 + (k-1) rho]`.

The theory therefore predicts that determinant ordering should track effective stochastic independence more closely than raw species richness or raw community size.

## Interpretation for Chapter 2

The admitted Chapter 2 result is one realization of this broader architecture:

1. small stochastic systems expose strong among-realization community variance;
2. independent pooling suppresses that additive community component;
3. a persistent mean-community contrast among starting states becomes relatively more visible;
4. state-by-community interaction can remain large through the finite-k crossover even though smooth independent-copy theory predicts eventual asymptotic decay;
5. the numerical Chapter 2 crossover near `k=4` is model-specific and is not a natural ecological threshold.

The general claim suitable for a broader ecology audience is therefore:

> Community averaging can reverse the ranking of ecological response determinants when realization-driven variance averages down faster than persistent state-dependent differences, without requiring interaction structure to disappear at the finite-community crossover.

The failure condition is equally important:

> Rank crossover is not expected when shared or correlated community stochasticity leaves a realization-variance floor that remains larger than the state-dependent component.

## Claim boundary

This derivation gives sufficient conditions, not necessary conditions. It does not establish that every ecological community must show rank crossover, that natural `k` maps to pollinator richness, or that the Chapter 2 numerical crossover can be exported as a field threshold.
