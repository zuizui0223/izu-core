## Abstract

Pollinator limitation can favor reproductive assurance and reduced floral investment, but expected reproductive returns need not determine the order or realization of inherited responses. We studied finite diploid plant populations with explicit floral-investment and assurance loci. Across four preregistered reproductive settings, investment declined under reduced visitor replenishment even when assurance evolution was blocked; permitting it compressed investment divergence (+0.101–0.220). Both regimes remained pollen-limited. In delayed selfing with assurance cost, assurance changed first in 51/64 visitor histories, but sequence depended on reproductive setting. In separate comparisons, deterministic genotype-density propagation predicted assurance-first, whereas only 3/32 and 2/32 finite histories realized that category despite persistence. In another source cohort, 703/2,304 histories lost genetic access to a specified further investment decline before fixation. An independent stress experiment found a conditional +10.84-percentage-point occupancy interaction but not order-mediated rescue. Selection, inherited accessibility, realized order and persistence are distinct outcomes.

# Introduction

Pollinator limitation alters both the return to attracting outcross pollen and the advantage of autonomous reproduction. Increased reproductive assurance and reduced floral investment can accompany the same environmental change without forming a necessary evolutionary sequence. A predicted reproductive advantage, a trait crossing a threshold first, retention of alleles needed for further change, and population persistence are different outcomes. Observing reduced floral display alongside increased selfing does not establish that selfing evolution was required for that reduction.

The coupling of floral allocation and mating-system evolution has long been modelled (Sakai 1995; Morgan & Wilson 2005). Pollinator exclusion can produce rapid autogamy and trait-specific responses (Bodbyl Roels & Kelly 2011; Busch et al. 2022), while reproductive assurance can weaken field-measured selection on flower size (Teixido & Aizen 2019). Lepers et al. (2014) used deterministic analysis and stochastic individual-based simulations of pollination and selfing. Founder size, selfing and persistence have been linked to adaptation in individual-based models (Peterson & Kay 2015; Sachdeva 2019), and selfing's effects on polygenic adaptive potential have been modelled (Hartfield & Glémin 2024). Neither floral–selfing coupling, stochastic modelling nor erosion of genetic variation is therefore a novelty claim here.

We test how closely finite populations reproduce predicted joint changes in independently inherited floral investment (I) and reproductive assurance (A). Separately, we test whether standing alleles retain access to a specified further response following Mendelian transmission. A genotype-density approximation may predict assurance-first even when occupied finite populations rarely meet that threshold-defined order. Our density closure is not the exact stochastic expectation of the finite model; disagreement establishes a conditional predictive gap, not its cause or an attributable genetic-drift fraction. Genetic-access loss in a separate cohort offers a possible route, not an identified explanation of that gap.

This matters for islands, where breeding-system enrichment and floral phenotypic convergence need not coincide (Grossenbacher et al. 2017; Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson 2020; Hiraiwa & Ushimaru 2017, 2024). Colonization and establishment filtering also shape observed island floras (Zell et al. 2025). Our higher- and lower-replenishment treatments are synthetic visitor regimes, not reconstructed mainland and island histories.

We distinguish four questions: **Q1, selection:** when do pollen-mediated returns favor lower investment? **Q2, sequence:** which inherited response crosses a sustained-change criterion first, and is that precedence necessary? **Q3, finite realization:** when does genotype support for a further response disappear, or finite order depart from deterministic propagation despite occupancy? **Q4, persistence:** how do separately assigned reproductive-expression and demographic histories affect survival? Q2–Q3 provide the central test, Q1 a registered counterfactual baseline and Q4 an independent demographic consequence. Each question uses its own registered experiments, with units, states and horizons. This is not a natural mediation analysis: the visitor regimes neither identify historical isolation effects nor assign persistence differences to an evolved sequence.

# Materials and Methods

## Reproductive model and invasion accounting

Model 3 follows a finite population of diploid plants with inherited visitor-matching, floral-investment and reproductive-assurance loci. Visitor functional types arrive and disappear stochastically; their optimum, breadth and effectiveness determine pollen removal and receipt. Floral investment increases affinity and pollen export but reduces the available ovule budget. Outcross fertilization saturates with pollen receipt; autonomous selfing can occur before or after outcrossing, and selfed offspring experience inbreeding depression. Separate maternal and paternal gametes, Mendelian segregation, adult survival, density-limited recruitment and seed immigration govern finite inheritance.

We contrasted higher ('near') with lower ('far') visitor replenishment while holding plant capacity and the other ecological rules fixed. These labels are **synthetic replenishment conditions, not mainland/island distances or pollen-sufficiency classes**. Both environments began with the same visitor source state. The four predefined reproductive settings were cost-free delayed selfing, prior selfing, pollen discount (lost male outcross export as assurance increases) and a direct assurance allocation cost. Cost-free delayed assurance is structurally beneficial if unfilled ovules and viable selfed offspring remain; it is a permissive control rather than an equally demanding test of the direct costs of assurance.

For a rare mutant in a held-fixed resident pollen environment, expected parental-genome contribution is `W=F/2+P/2+S`: maternal outcross `F`, paternal outcross `P`, and viable selfing `S`. We used corrected rare-mutant rather than whole-population investment derivatives and partitioned selection into maternal outcross, paternal export, selfing displacement and ovule allocation cost. Fixed-state local gradients are mechanistic diagnostics, not estimators of dynamic genetic mediation.

## Preregistered evolutionary intervention

The primary frozen experiment used **64 independent new visitor histories**, eight nested demographic repeats per history, 1,000 reproductive updates, near/far replenishment, fixed/evolving assurance and all four reproductive settings: **8,192 trajectories** under positive mutation probability 0.01. Plants began with the identical 48-founder matching/investment distribution, and both assurance alleles were set to 0.5. In fixed mode, assurance capacity remained 0.5 while matching and investment inherited and mutated; in evolving mode assurance also inherited and mutated. Blocking *capacity evolution* does **not** block realized selfing.

The two preregistered estimands were (i) fixed-assurance terminal far-minus-near investment and (ii) the matched four-cell interaction `[(far−near)_evolving−(far−near)_fixed]`, using demographic repeats occupied in all four cells. Each setting passed only if the first history-bootstrap 95% interval was wholly negative and the second wholly positive; the global rule required all four settings, at least 60/64 admissible visitor histories and 90% occupancy per arm. The eight repeats were averaged *within* visitor histories, which were the independent resampling units. Extinct phenotypes were missing, never zero. A zero-mutation control required fixed and evolving matched traces to be exactly identical: 128/128 pairs passed.

After confirmation, we decomposed the interaction algebraically into the evolving-minus-fixed near and far effects, without claiming natural mediation fractions. At snapshot 400, a separate matched-history fixed-resident diagnostic changed assurance from 0.25 to 0.75 and recomputed the four investment-gradient components. A distinct frozen temporal-order cohort defined trait change as a founder-relative threshold crossing sustained 20 updates; temporal precedence was not treated as a necessity test. Separate fixed-state assays compared fractional pollen deficit with absolute viable maternal seeds.

## Supplemental visitor-tempo experiment

A separately frozen experiment supplied descriptive supporting evidence from 1,024 native diploid trajectories over 400 updates: 16 synthetic visitor profiles, four nested demographic repeats, eight selfing-timing/cost/mutation settings and two visitor schedules. Abrupt or gradual changes in the optima of four functional visitor types altered trait matching, without removing insect individuals. First-100-update cumulative pollen delivery was matched **only in a fixed clonal N48, X=I=A=0.5 calibration population**; actual pollen delivery in evolving populations remained an outcome. Both schedules shared the terminal visitor composition from updates 100–399. Assurance increases and investment decreases were defined relative to actual founder means by a 0.05 change sustained for 20 consecutive censuses, with confirmation by update 100 for the crossing primary. Paired differences used **16 independent profile clusters**, with demographic repeats averaged within profiles, and 9,999 two-sided descriptive percentile-bootstrap draws. These frozen endpoints do not add another confirmatory primary to the paper. Complete settings, sequence definitions and verification limits are retained in Supporting Information S5.

## Independent finite-population experiment

The demographic study uses authenticated diploid states after 400 prehistory updates, followed by **80 updates** under separately assigned, equal-cumulative-dose *phenotypic expression schedules*: assurance first (A-first) or investment first (I-first). These schedules do **not** impose the sequence in which inherited alleles arose. Each future branch records whether any plants remain. A postzygotic intervention halves viable selfed-seed contribution while leaving outcross production unchanged at the intervention point.

The original population capacity parameter `K` also appeared in a pollen-recipient background denominator, confounding demographic space with pollination. An independently checked control therefore replaced that denominator by a distinct fixed pollen-background `B`, allowing `K8` versus `K48` comparisons at **B48** and identical up-to-eight diploid founder genotypes. Define `D(K,B,g)` as the A-first minus I-first 80-update occupancy contrast under viable-selfed-seed treatment `g`; `tau(K,B)=D(K,B,baseline)−D(K,B,half-selfed)`. The registered primary estimand was `tau(K8,B48)−tau(K48,B48)`.

A separately preregistered cohort used 64 previously unused visitor-history identities, with branches and nested repeats averaged within history, and 9,999 paired history-bootstrap draws. Support required an absolute effect at least 0.005 and a two-sided percentile 95% interval excluding zero. The earlier inconclusive K or B primaries, near/far assigned-order equivalence, resource-window failure and later inconclusive early-versus-late timing test were retained. Two other cohorts' similar K contrasts are **secondary/descriptive**, not additional confirmatory successes or a pooled meta-analysis. The demographic experiments share a reproductive framework but differ in endpoints, intervention assignments, sample identities and some K/B biology from the 1,000-update evolutionary experiment. Neither identifies an evolutionary-investment-to-persistence mediator. The original four-setting campaign had terminal occupancy 1.0 in every arm.

## Prospective conditional-resolution studies

Separately frozen studies used new demographic streams and two fixed founder backgrounds, without pooling with the visitor-history primary. A6,144-trajectory study compared two assurance allele-width endpoints at identical founder phenotypes in three low-supply contexts. A3,072-preparation study compared paired mutation-access schedules, then branched each archived end state into two common200-update K8 challenges. All-start response and occupancy estimands, simultaneous paired-bootstrap intervals and conservative distribution-free bounds were fixed before execution. The complete designs and identification limits appear in Supporting Information S8.

A prospectively frozen follow-up reused1,024 paired bottleneck states under two200-update challenges and five lifelong expression policies: native, assurance-fixed, investment-fixed, both-fixed or all-traits-fixed. Alleles retained native inheritance/mutation. The20,480 futures included4,096 native replays. Four attenuation contrasts used19,999 block-stratified paired bootstrap draws with simultaneous intervals; distribution-free bounds and full genotype/parentage verification were retained(S9).


## Minimal state and temporal decomposition

A further frozen intervention equalized initial assurance means to0.5 while preserving variance, cross-trait covariance and other state fields. Its4,096 futures and authenticated native/assurance-fixed references supported four initial-mean/residual contrasts with19,999 within-founder bootstrap draws and simultaneous intervals(S10). Retrospective accounting separated selection, parental sampling, transmission and mutation in64,000 archived updates, and decomposed96 finite-state kernels into survival and genotype-class occupied-time contributions(S11–S12). No ecological conditions were added.

A separately frozen ecological-order assay fixed founder genetic access and varied nine static visitor-supply/function contexts, with128 paired histories in each of two founder blocks (2,304 traces;200 updates). Only assurance mutated. All-start assurance-first minus investment-first proportions retained single-event and censored histories. Thirty-six exact marginal binomial intervals yielded18 simultaneous conservative difference bounds; reversal required consistent opposite signs in both founders. No post-outcome expansion was permitted (S14).

After initial-mean equalization, separate4,096-future probes equalized all parent weights or assigned current mean assurance only in within-class parent-weight calculations. Native reproductive class totals, inheritance and mutation remained. Each probe retained its own four-contrast family and independent verification(S16,S22).

A separate registered Q3 follow-up retained61 original fixation states,64 fresh pairs/state and three policy-zero slots. Three simultaneous bounded intervals required survival in every pair; original horizons, one-time restoration and native operators remained unchanged(S23).

# Results

## Q1 — Thresholds: reproductive returns and genetic access


### Visitor limitation reduces the reproductive return to attraction before plant evolution
At the same fixed plant state, low visitor replenishment sharply reduced the return to floral investment (Fig. 1). In the delayed-selfing setting at snapshot 400, the total investment-contribution derivative changed from +0.579 under near exposure to −0.700 under far exposure. The outcross contribution fell from +1.652 to +0.085, whereas the viable-selfed component partly offset rather than generated the decline. The intrinsic investment-cost coefficient was unchanged across environments. Thus visitor limitation can make reduced attraction favourable before any evolution of reproductive assurance.

### Pollen-deficit and viable-output responses are not equivalent
High seed production need not translate into viable offspring when selfing and inbreeding depression interact, as shown empirically by Van Etten et al. (2015). The reproductive-output intervention here showed that apparent compensation in a fractional pollen-deficit metric need not correspond to higher viable offspring production (Supplementary Fig. S1). In the delayed/far assay at snapshot 400, increasing investment from 0.25 to 0.75 at assurance 0.5 reduced fractional viable pollen deficit by 0.0104 while reducing viable maternal offspring by 15.72 per 48 plants. Reproductive assurance, pollen deficit and absolute viable production are therefore distinct quantities.

### A conditional genetic-access boundary
A separately registered confirmation localized an order-score sign reversal to assurance allele half-widths[0.046875,0.078125] in both fixed founders and all three low-supply contexts. The band has width0.03125; it is neither a continuous root nor a visitor-supply threshold. At the primary0.05 response, the lower endpoint cannot reach the assurance event from standing alleles alone. Larger0.10 response definitions instead favour investment-first at both endpoints (Supporting Information S8).

### Assurance evolution is not required for investment decline
The prospective four-setting test passed its frozen non-necessity criterion in every setting (Fig. 2A). For the cost-free delayed-selfing control, direct assurance benefits are structurally positive whenever ovules remain unfilled; passing this control does not provide the same test of assurance trade-offs as the cost, prior-selfing and pollen-discount regimes. With assurance held fixed at 0.5, far-minus-near investment was −0.4413 [−0.4564, −0.4262] under delayed control, −0.3018 [−0.3172, −0.2866] under prior selfing, −0.3305 [−0.3461, −0.3151] with pollen discount and −0.4337 [−0.4501, −0.4171] with direct assurance cost. All four near/far arms retained terminal occupancy 1.0 and all 64 histories were eligible.

Reduced investment under low replenishment therefore did not require assurance-capacity evolution under any of the four declared reproductive rules. Because fixed capacity still permits realized selfing, this result excludes a requirement for assurance evolution, not all reproductive consequences of selfing.

### Assurance evolution consistently compresses environmental divergence
Allowing assurance to evolve reduced the magnitude of the near–far investment contrast in all four settings (Fig. 2B). These settings test a broader range of **investment responses** to assurance evolution, not four equally discriminating tests of whether assurance can invade: cost-free delayed selfing already favours assurance when ovules remain unfilled. Evolving-minus-fixed interactions were +0.1605 [0.1418, 0.1789] under delayed control, +0.2200 [0.2025, 0.2383] under prior selfing, +0.1915 [0.1762, 0.2069] with pollen discount and +0.1007 [0.0842, 0.1177] with assurance cost. All four settings therefore passed the preregistered attenuation criterion.

The arm decomposition showed that this attenuation was not primarily rescue of far-side investment (Fig. 2C). Relative to fixed assurance, allowing assurance to evolve changed near-side investment by −0.1253 [−0.1395, −0.1112], −0.1985 [−0.2137, −0.1838], −0.1699 [−0.1831, −0.1563] and −0.0789 [−0.0931, −0.0646] across delayed control, prior selfing, pollen discount and assurance cost. Far-side effects were much smaller and positive: +0.0352 [0.0233, 0.0471], +0.0215 [0.0116, 0.0315], +0.0216 [0.0110, 0.0327] and +0.0218 [0.0120, 0.0312]. Consequently, 78.1%, 90.2%, 88.7% and 78.3% of the interaction, respectively, was localized to additional investment decline in the high-replenishment arm.

### The direct selection effect is stronger where outcross returns remain available
The corrected rare-mutant diagnostic resolved the same environmental asymmetry at a fixed plant state (Fig. 2D). The near label denotes **higher visitor replenishment**, not pollen sufficiency. The independent fixed-plant snapshot-400 assay found mean raw deficit 0.3901 near versus 0.4948 far, and mean viable-seed deficit 0.5852 near versus 0.7423 far, in its delayed-timing assurance-cost setting across 64 visitor histories and fixed 48-plant states. Here the *raw* deficit is measured **after** autonomous selfing: with delayed assurance fixed at a=0.5, the source equation is d_raw=(1−a)(1−F/O), so mean outcross fertilization fractions F/O are approximately 0.2197 near and 0.0103 far. The 39% near deficit is NOT a claim that only 39% of ovules remain without outcross pollen. These fixed-state fractions are not terminal unfertilized-ovule fractions in evolved populations. Increasing assurance from 0.25 to 0.75 made the investment gradient more negative in both environments, but the effect was much stronger near than far. At snapshot 400, the near-minus-far assurance effect on the investment gradient was −0.3794 [−0.4376, −0.3170] under delayed control, −0.5231 [−0.6041, −0.4361] under prior selfing, −0.4661 [−0.5389, −0.3876] with pollen discount and −0.3794 [−0.4369, −0.3177] with assurance cost.

Maternal outcross and paternal pollen-export components supplied most of these differences. In delayed control and assurance cost, their near-minus-far contributions were −0.125 and −0.135, respectively; under prior selfing they were −0.214 and −0.235; with pollen discount they were −0.179 and −0.198. Selfing-displacement and allocation-cost components acted in the same direction, except that selfing displacement is structurally zero under prior selfing.

Under low replenishment, attraction-dependent outcross return was already small. Increasing assurance therefore removed relatively little additional attraction value. Under high replenishment, substantial maternal and paternal return remained, so the same assurance increase more strongly reduced selection for investment.

## Q2 — Sequence: temporal precedence is conditional
The independent temporal-order replication recovered an assurance-first sequence in the delayed-selfing, direct-cost setting: 51 of 64 histories were assurance-first at the primary 0.05 threshold and 13 were near-simultaneous (proportion 0.797; 95% history-bootstrap interval 0.688–0.891). The same criterion did not generalize to prior selfing, where 30 of 64 histories were assurance-first and the interval spanned 0.5.

Temporal precedence is neither universal nor required for investment reduction. Standing genetic variation can accelerate adaptation generally (Barrett & Schluter 2008); this principle is not a novelty claim. In the exploratory 1,536-history A-width intervention, expressed founder phenotypes were matched while assurance-allele half-width changed from 0.046875 to 0.078125. The all-start assurance-first-minus-investment-first score increased by +0.6693, whereas assurance-event incidence increased by only +0.0339 (S20). This is a model- and threshold-specific shift in relative order, not proof that segregating variance alone determines speed: allele support and threshold reachability also change, the fixed 0.05/20-census rule matters, and there were only two founder-genotype backgrounds, with no new independent-founder confirmation.

An additional post hoc, single-founder F1 mechanism audit (S29) distinguished **segregational access** from **instantaneous reproductive return**. Under an original-I-preserving but artificially reconstructed A-locus control, four signed A/I first-child double-threshold outcomes were equally accessible. Separately, placing each possible signed phenotype as one hypothetical adult into the unchanged source community gave the highest immediate parental-genome contribution to A-increased/I-decreased across all nine M-by-visitor setting cells. These source-conditioned diagnostics do **not** establish realized recruitment, sustained evolutionary order, an adaptive selection coefficient or an effect of naturally occurring precedence on persistence.

In the separate visitor-tempo cohort, abrupt-minus-gradual differences in assurance-increase crossings confirmed by update 100 ranged from 0 to +42.1875 percentage points across all eight settings; investment-decrease differences ranged from −6.25 to +21.875 points (Supplementary Fig. S2; Supporting Information S5). In all four direct-cost-0.5 settings, assurance differences were +21.875 to +42.1875 points. These are descriptive profile-cluster contrasts. An assurance-only outcome is not an assurance-first sequence: in delayed selfing with cost 0.5 and no mutation, the 53 abrupt-arm assurance crossers comprised eight assurance-first and 45 assurance-only trajectories. Every setting and schedule retained occupancy 64/64 at both updates 100 and 400. This ceiling supplies no identified persistence benefit, harm or practical-equivalence result; visitor-tempo assignment does not identify a causal natural mutation-order or inherited-order-to-survival mechanism.

With fixed genetic access, the ecological-order assay identified no replicated supply or composition reversal: all18 simultaneous intervals included zero. At matched supplies0.5/1, investment-decrease events occurred in3/1 of128 main and4/0 of128 confirmation histories. Many missing events were structurally unreachable from archived fixed investment alleles under the same no-investment-mutation/no-immigration law. This conditional future bound does not establish why earlier responses were absent, natural irreversibility or an ecological threshold (S14).

An initial-state audit found multiple order outcomes within every one of18 identical-start cells. A post-outcome six-class majority lookup classified only1,117/2,304 histories. Initial assurance response was zero despite segregation potential. Thus initial expected response alone does not determine realized order; no prospective clock or natural-order benefit was established (S18).

Among335 assurance-first histories, investment-decline expected response began earlier in299; requiring20 consecutive response updates retained216. This rejects a universal start-time-only explanation, not a role for timing. These on-path inherited-return covariances are not invasion gradients (S19).

## Q3 — Realization: deterministic response and finite inheritance

We compared conditional deterministic genotype-density propagation (without demographic sampling) with a finite diploid ABM using the same reproductive and inheritance operator. In a separately frozen zero-mutation, 128-visitor-history near–far bridge, investment contrasts were −0.4510 (density) and −0.1446 (ABM); mixed-sign history labels occurred in 0/128 and 12/128 cases at zero deadband. Matching annual visitor counts reversed both mean contrasts (+0.0338 and +0.0333), but also changed visitor identity persistence and left 16/128 versus 68/128 mixed histories. Increasing plant capacity from 48 to 192 reduced finite mixed labels from 12/128 to 1/128 (Fig. S3).

A distinct original-birth-mutation genotype-density implementation passed both registered numerical-grid comparisons in ten founder/visitor contexts (S7). In two low-supply, functionally matched contexts, density predicted assurance-first; only 3/32 and 2/32 ABM histories realized that category at the 0.05/20-census threshold (Fig. 3). All remained occupied at 200 updates; assurance-only events were not classified as assurance-first. Full-genome and variation-restoration audits showed sampling/transmission offsets and loss of accessible investment alleles, but did not isolate drift or a unique causal allocation (S11–S24). Controlled visitor composition can permit opposing deterministic directions, although the original isolation bridge's deterministic response was one-directional. **Density is not the stochastic expectation of the ABM**: these differences do not quantify drift or natural lineage frequencies.

A separate source-locked audit of **2,304 original fixed-access finite histories** identified loss of access to a predeclared investment decline. With no investment mutation or immigration, the minimum surviving investment allele cannot decrease; in **703 histories**, a founder-relative 0.05 investment decline became structurally unreachable **before single-allele fixation**, even while investment variance remained. Among **1,238 first-exclusion transitions** (a different denominator), **315** lost the required low-investment alleles although the preceding state's conditional expected frequency response favoured those alleles. Thus positive expected parental weighting did not guarantee retention after finite parent sampling and Mendelian transmission. These are outcome-selected source-accounting records, **not** a causal drift fraction, natural-island frequency, or a complete explanation of the density–ABM discrepancy (S14).

A registered three-policy follow-up separated same-support and same-variance comparisons across26,352 futures. No directional effect replicated across both fixed-state blocks; equal-variance terminal means were equivalent within the specified0.01 margin. Endpoint fixation also precludes restoring variance while preserving both mean and support, limiting interpretation of a pure maintenance intervention(S26).

## Q4 — Persistence: consequences of reproductive preparation

An independent, pilot-informed but prospectively frozen 32-history trial compared fixed versus evolvable A under 400-update near/far histories, then transferred eight plants to an 80-update stress grid (four ovule budgets × two future visitor regimes). The registered treatment-by-past-pollination interaction in mean terminal occupancy was **+0.1084 [0.0654,0.1514]** (S33). A evolvability **continued during the future**, and the shock changed carrying capacity, fecundity and pollen transfer together. This is not an isolated genetic legacy, naturally evolved A-first benefit or island rescue.

Separate mutation-access preparations produced conditional terminal occupancy contrasts of +2.83 [0.68,4.98] and +4.59 [1.86,7.32] percentage points. Equalizing mean assurance attenuated them by 3.13 and 4.49 points, but lifelong-expression and parent-weighting mechanisms remained unresolved; the lower-growth challenge was floor-limited (S8–S10,S16,S22). Unrestricted-order associations were founder/challenge dependent (S25,S28), and some exploratory kernels gained endpoint occupancy while losing cumulative persistence (S12).

### Conditional demographic consequences of assigned reproductive expression history

The following 80-update experiments **imposed phenotypic expression schedules**, not spontaneous mutation order. They are separate from the evolving-assurance experiments above and retain their own independent visitor histories and preregistered nulls.

#### 1. Order effects were small and not specific to geographic visitor history

The original near–far A-first versus I-first assigned-order contrast was **practically equivalent** within its predeclared ±0.05 margin. The independent restricted ovule-budget-window confirmation **failed**. This does not establish a general order effect or a far-environment rescue.

#### 2. A controlled selfed-seed intervention changed the small schedule advantage

At eight founders and K8, halving viable selfed seeds reduced the A-first-minus-I-first occupancy contrast from +0.01209 to +0.00394. The change identifies a controlled viable-selfed-seed sensitivity, not the survival effect of natural inherited sequence (S3).

#### 3. The first capacity contrasts were either confounded or inconclusive

Earlier founder-number/K contrasts combined multiple mechanisms. Independently tested matched-founder K and B-at-K8 primaries were inconclusive; their original estimates and intervals remain in Supporting Information S3.

#### 4. An independent predeclared test supported demographic K moderation at fixed B48

With 64 fresh visitor-history clusters and matched founders, the preregistered K8-versus-K48 moderator at constant pollen background B48 was **+0.007746** (95% paired history-bootstrap **[+0.002497,+0.013015]**). This is approximately 0.775 occupancy percentage points of interaction sensitivity, not an overall K or naturally evolved-order benefit. **Figure 4:** [Original K-at-B48 cohort estimates and unpooled intervals](../figures/chapter2_k_at_B48_three_cohort_original_intervals_20261010.svg), generated without refitting by `scripts/render_chapter2_capacity_companion_evidence_figure.py`. Its square marks the **only prospectively preregistered supported primary**; the two other 64-history estimates (+0.013430 and +0.008580) are secondary/descriptive, with **No pooled uncertainty** or threefold confirmatory success claimed. See Supporting Information S3 and Figure 4.

#### 5. A later prospective timing contrast did not resolve early versus late importance

The separate preregistered late-versus-early selfed-seed viability moderator was −0.002965 [−0.007263,+0.001317], **inconclusive**. These assigned-expression, demographic and preparation-policy tests do not identify a natural genetic-order-to-persistence mediator.

# Discussion

Pollinator limitation changed reproductive returns and evolved floral investment, but **selection, order and genetic realization were not interchangeable**. Under fixed assurance, low visitor replenishment reduced floral investment across all four prospectively defined mating settings. Allowing assurance evolution **compressed** rather than necessarily amplified investment divergence. These are confirmed Model 3 counterfactuals, but they do not determine which inherited trait changes first or whether a particular further response remains accessible in a finite population.

Teixido & Aizen (2019) already demonstrated selection attenuation by assurance across three field populations; Lepers et al. (2014) modelled mating-system and pollinator dynamics using both adaptive dynamics and stochastic individuals. We therefore do **not** claim discovery of coupled selection or the first individual-based model. Q1 tests the more specific **evolving-versus-fixed assurance intervention** under four regimes. Q2 and Q3 separately ask when those expected selection directions appear as inherited changes, with explicit diploid allele support and finite parent sampling. The results remain model-specific and finite-horizon, not an inferred island history.

The asymmetry is consistent with the corrected maternal/paternal invasion accounting. Severe visitor limitation removes much of the opportunity for outcross mating before assurance changes. Where some outcross returns remain, assurance evolution has more investment value to displace. Crucially, the higher-replenishment arm was **not well pollinated**: a separate static, fixed-plant snapshot-400 assay estimated outcross fertilization of about 22% of ovules there, compared with about 1% in the lower-replenishment arm. Its 39% 'raw deficit' is the shortfall **after** autonomous selfing, not the fraction lacking outcross pollen. Even an exploratory 16-fold pollen-budget feasibility test failed the defined near-sufficiency criterion. We therefore cannot generalize this interaction to a saturated mainland comparator.

The four settings are mechanistically distinct but not four equally difficult demonstrations that assurance itself is favoured. Delayed selfing without cost or pollen discount has a structural assurance advantage whenever ovules remain unfilled and viable selfing is possible. The assurance-cost setting was more discriminating and had the smallest attenuation (+0.1007). Nor does blocking assurance evolution remove the possibility of *realized* autonomous selfing. The 78–90% near-side localization is a **post-confirmation arithmetic decomposition**, not a direct/indirect causal fraction.

The genetic results illuminate why **temporal order is not necessity**. The focal assurance-first frequency changed with mating rule, while the fixed-assurance intervention still allowed investment decline. In a separate mutation-restricted cohort, finite inheritance sometimes eliminated alleles necessary for a specified future investment decrease **before fixation**, including transitions with favourable conditional parental weighting. In other contexts, deterministic density predictions of assurance-first were rarely realized by occupied finite populations. These cohorts do not identify a common causal mediator, pure drift share or universal order law. Visitor pooling and higher plant capacity changed realized heterogeneity without isolating an autonomous stochastic contribution.

A separate, pilot-informed stress study found a positive interaction of assurance evolvability with past visitor replenishment on terminal occupancy. Because evolvability continued during the challenge and K, fecundity and pollen transfer changed together, that interaction does not identify a legacy effect of inherited assurance alleles.

The supported demographic-capacity interaction provides an additional qualification, not an explanation that can be read directly from trait divergence. In the separate assigned-order experiment, the original geographic-order contrast was practically equivalent within its registered margin, and the restricted-resource hypothesis failed. Only after separately controlling pollen-background dilution did an independent registered study detect a small K-dependent moderation of the selfed-seed viability sensitivity of the order contrast. This is an interaction of **imposed expression history, engineered seed viability and demographic capacity**, not proof that autonomous evolution follows the prescribed order, that floral reduction causes extinction, or that smaller islands uniformly benefit from insurance. The later test did not resolve whether early versus late viable selfed-seed availability was responsible.

The new expression intervention connects preparation policy to subsequent use of reproductive assurance without isolating a pure-order advantage. Holding assurance expression at a common reference throughout the challenge reduced the preparation contrast, whereas the investment-only contrasts remained unresolved. This result is conditional on the two registered challenges and founder backgrounds; the lower challenge has an extinction floor. It identifies a controlled policy response, not necessity, a unique genetic mediator, or natural historical causation.

Initial mean assurance contributed to this preparation difference; both parent-weighting probes were inconclusive. Residual expression and finite selection lack uniquely identified causal shares.

A complementary mechanism remains of interest but is not established as a survival pathway. In post-discovery source-ledger experiments, increasing a focal plant's investment sometimes benefited other mothers' viable seeds even when its own finite parental genetic return was negative. Replaying authentic evolved diploid source states confirmed a nonfocal pollen-mediated benefit, yet whole-group seed effects were not consistently positive. A static two-factor maternal-seed accounting showed that floral-investment changes simultaneously alter **recipient-specific pollen receipt and ovule allocation**; resource savings can offset reduced pollen service. These exploratory history-bootstrap intervals often spanned zero. Moreover, the original 48-plant demographic regime was nearly saturated with viable seeds, so one-generation expected census differences were negligible. These results motivate a sharper future test of whether an evolved loss of shared pollen-transfer benefits can survive resource compensation and density regulation; **we do not identify a causal chain** from evolving assurance through floral investment to lower population persistence.

Our results concern one explicitly specified model class, not a measured historical transition on real islands. Visitor arrival is imposed externally, not caused by floral-investment evolution. The original evolutionary contrast is a **finite 1,000-update outcome**, not an established long-term stationary state; a separate deterministic long-horizon diagnostic failed stationarity but did not re-test the four-setting attenuation. Island trait associations may reflect lineage sorting, establishment, local evolution and persistence, which these experimental manipulations cannot partition historically. An ecological test of the broader hypothesis would need matched natural pollination and genetic-parentage data, or genuinely independent evolutionary and demographic models with falsifying regimes rather than further outcome-selected parameter tuning.

# Conclusion

Selection under pollinator limitation did not ensure a single sequence or genetically realizable floral response. Assurance-first order was context dependent, and finite transmission could remove the allele support required for further investment decrease even while plants persisted. As a confirmed reproductive baseline, investment declined without assurance evolution, while permitting that evolution compressed divergence across four prespecified settings. A separate demographic experiment identified conditional effects on occupancy but not naturally evolved order-mediated rescue. Thus reproductive selection, response sequence, inherited realization and population persistence are distinct filters within this explicitly specified model.

## Evidence hierarchy and study-origin record

The four-setting 1,000-update intervention and its prespecified all-four passing rule are the **primary evolutionary result**. The high- versus low-replenishment gradient decomposition and fixed-plant pollen-saturation assessment are separately specified post-confirmation or earlier fixed-state supporting diagnostics. The cost-free delayed-selfing control is structurally favourable to assurance when viable selfing and unfilled ovules exist; the smallest interaction occurs with direct assurance cost (+0.1007).

The demographic experiment is a **second, nonexchangeable question within the same paper**, with its *own* registered primary and multiple negative or inconclusive antecedents: original near/far order DID practically equivalent; independent resource-window hypothesis failed; fixed-founder and B-at-K8 primary contrasts inconclusive; newly registered fixed-B48 K8-vs-K48 contrast +0.0077457 supported; original early-versus-late primary inconclusive. The three independent source cohorts are not three confirmatory model architectures. Original K/B intervention replaces one recipient pollen-dilution denominator solely for the demographic control; this is not the same exact frozen biology as the canonical four-setting campaign for all K/B comparisons.

The visitor-tempo experiment was separately frozen before execution and admitted after raw-data verification. Its registered crossing endpoints and descriptive profile-bootstrap intervals are **supplemental, model-specific evidence**, not a replacement for the original four-setting primary or another confirmation of the demographic K result. Its 16 profile clusters are not pooled with the separate 64-history cohorts. The original event/order/occupancy audit independently recomputed 128 setting/outcome comparisons; that audit did not independently recompute the 156 secondary fields per setting (Supporting Information S5). A later retrospective expected-to-realized accounting of the same cohort has its own record in `docs/CHAPTER2_TEMPO_REALIZATION_ACCOUNTING_20261011.md`; it does not change the registered endpoints or establish genetic mediation.

This integration adds the separately frozen4,096 initial-mean futures and8 technical clamp controls, with their own four-contrast bootstrap family. Separate4,096-future all-parent and within-class assurance-parent probes each retain their own four-contrast family, all unresolved; original full raw records and independent birth/bootstrap checks are preserved. The64-pair target-access audit adds no history or primary inference. The existing320-ABM and96-kernel analyses are retrospective accounting; no cohorts or uncertainty intervals are pooled. The earlier `docs/CHAPTER2_MANUSCRIPT_ECOLOGY_LETTERS_20261006.md` and `docs/CHAPTER2_CAPACITY_PERSISTENCE_COMPANION_MANUSCRIPT_20261010.md` remain **source-provenance manuscripts**, not separate active submissions under the one-paper route.
