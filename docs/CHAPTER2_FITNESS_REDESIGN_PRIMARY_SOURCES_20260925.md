# Chapter 2 fitness redesign: primary-source design note

Date: 2026-09-25. Status: research proposal only; no implementation or simulation. This note does not certify the present model or alter frozen outcomes.

## Preserve the scientific question

**When does island community reassembly make floral evolution converge across regions, and when does it create region-specific divergence?** Keep this ambitious question, but make reproductive success the intervening mechanism. An island label, weighted pollinator centroid, or reduction in trait distance cannot by itself supply a selection gradient.

The proposed causal sequence is: community composition and abundance → visit sequences → compatible pollen transfer → maternal offspring and outcross siring → inherited trait response. The equations below are proposed modeling choices and original mathematical deductions, not equations claimed to have been copied from the cited papers.

## Minimum defensible fitness module

Start with one interpretable floral trait and, only if needed, a second trait such as display/reward investment. For focal individual or morph `i`, donor `k`, pollinator guild `j`, and community state `C`:

1. Define encounter supply and attraction separately. A possible visit allocation is `v_ij = B_j a_j(z_i)/sum_l n_l a_j(z_l)`, with `n_l` included consistently for morph versus individual units. Define the zero-denominator rule explicitly. Absolute `B_j` must matter for pollen limitation; normalized probabilities alone erase it.
2. Construct a donor–recipient transfer ledger `T_ki`: expected compatible outcross pollen reaching `i` from `k`, combining visit sequence probabilities, removal, carryover/deposition, and compatibility. Conspecific abundance and constancy enter here. Count self pollen separately. Visitation, pollen removal, deposition, and siring are distinct variables.
3. Map total compatible receipt `P_i = sum_k T_ki` to bounded outcross fertilization. One proposed phenomenological response is `q_i = 1-exp(-P_i/K_i)`, with explicit units and sensitivity alternatives. With available ovules `O_i`, outcross seeds are `X_i = O_i q_i` before selfing competition. The exponential is an assumption, not a known universal relationship.
4. Allocate each recipient's outcross offspring among donors by their compatible pollen shares (or an explicitly specified pollen-competition function). Then each viable outcross offspring has exactly one maternal and one paternal contribution. Male fitness is realized siring, not merely pollen exported.
5. Let resource investment change `O_i`, pollen supply, survival, or a declared budget. Avoid a costless trait that monotonically improves all components. Costs must be specified before outcome inspection; if plausible signs/strengths are unknown, map conditional regimes rather than selecting a cost that produces the desired endpoint.

This module allows saturation of female fitness while male siring remains responsive, and allows attraction increases to raise visits without proportionally raising compatible fertilization. Those are model capabilities to test, not results already demonstrated here.

## Selfing: biological and bookkeeping requirements

For a **delayed-selfing limiting case**, let `Y_i = s_i (O_i-X_i)` fill some otherwise unfertilized ovules. Expected viable maternal offspring are `F_i = X_i + (1-delta_i)Y_i`, assuming outcross offspring survival is the reference and the chosen `delta` covers all remaining survival stages. Define `M_i` as viable outcross offspring sired elsewhere. Gene-copy contribution, up to a common scale, is then `W_i = X_i + 2(1-delta_i)Y_i + M_i`. If survival is modeled separately, do not apply the same depression twice.

The factor two is transmission accounting; it is not a doubling of demography. Population recruitment uses actual offspring numbers. This ledger follows the conceptual distinction in [Lloyd 1992](https://doi.org/10.1086/297041); the formula here is a proposed simplified implementation.

Delayed selfing that only uses unfilled ovules has zero seed discounting by construction. It is therefore a useful favorable bound, not an adequate universal default. Prior/competing selfing must remove or contest ovules that could otherwise outcross. Pollen discounting must reduce export or siring through the same transfer ledger. Changes in display/herkogamy may alter both assurance and outcross performance; specify the coupling rather than insert an independent fitness bonus. Explicitly exclude self-incompatible species from autonomous-selfing scenarios unless compatibility evolution is itself modeled.

Do not evolve `delta` downward by an arbitrary rule called purging. That claim needs a genetic-load model; [Porcher & Lande 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1420-9101.2005.00905.x) supplies prior art for the additional machinery.

## Heritable evolution: two defensible levels

**A. Minimum quantitative-genetic approximation.** For a declared phenotype distribution with covariance `P`, calculate relative reproductive fitness from the ledger, the selection differential `S = Cov(z, W)/mean(W)`, and `beta = P^{-1}S` where identifiable. Use `Delta zbar = G beta` under the stated additive quantitative-genetic approximation; `G` is a specified positive-semidefinite additive genetic covariance matrix, not an arbitrary convergence-rate constant. The framework is grounded in [Lande 1979](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1979.tb04694.x) and [Lande & Arnold 1983](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1983.tb00236.x). With a narrow distribution and smooth fitness, a local derivative approximation may be useful, but calculate or verify it rather than equate it to a pollinator centroid.

For frequency dependence, perturb the focal phenotype against a held-fixed resident community when evaluating selection. Recompute community state between generations; differentiating a landscape in which every resident changes together is a different derivative. Retain plausible `G` sensitivities and report that constant `G` omits its own evolution. A species represented by one mean may conceal within-species polymorphism or disruptive selection.

Use an outcrossing baseline as the clean first evolutionary model. Selfing can first be a fixed ecological scenario with a clearly labeled response approximation. Do not claim that standard random-mating `G beta` plus the transmission factor automatically derives partial-selfing inheritance. Selfing changes genetic associations and covariance; [Lande & Porcher 2015, Maintenance of Quantitative Genetic Variance Under Partial Self-Fertilization](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4512550/) is relevant primary theory for that extension (indexed abstract inspected).

**B. Explicit inheritance.** Sample maternal and paternal contributions from the same ledger; transmit specified diploid alleles through Mendelian segregation, recombination if modeled, and mutation. For selfed offspring both gametes come from the same parent. State genotype→phenotype and environmental variation. This is necessary if claims concern allele-frequency trajectories, evolving selfing/inbreeding, drift, linkage, genetic variance loss, or genetic load. It is not necessary simply to call an explicitly qualified quantitative-genetic response evolutionary. Explicit inheritance also cannot rescue an unjustified ecological fitness function.

**Recommended sequence when selfing is central:** first verify a fixed-mating fitness landscape and the female/male ledger; then make a small explicit parentage/inheritance model the primary evolutionary analysis. Keep the quantitative-genetic model as an analytical baseline or approximation, rather than silently extending its random-mating response equation to mixed mating. Explicit Mendelian floral inheritance with fixed inbreeding depression still does not model purging; genetic load is a further extension.

## Why the weighted centroid is only a special case

Original algebraic check: if `W(z) = W0 - 0.5 sum_j a_j ||z-theta_j||^2`, all `a_j` are positive constants independent of `z` and community, and `W` remains positive on the domain, then `grad W = sum_j a_j(theta_j-z)`. Its stationary point is the weighted centroid. Thus a centroid rule corresponds to a particular common-curvature quadratic landscape; it is not a general consequence of pollination.

For `W(z)=sum_j a_j exp(-||z-theta_j||^2/(2 sigma_j^2))`, the gradient weights depend on `z` and kernel width, and multiple peaks are possible. Attraction-dependent visitation, floral costs, ovule saturation, selfing, compatibility, and male competition add further terms. Aigner's marginal-tradeoff argument motivates testing these derivatives; it does not authorize assuming a centroid.

## Experimental design and contribution boundary

Freeze paired community interventions before inspecting outcomes: hold total pollinator supply constant when replacing guild composition, and separately vary total supply at fixed composition. Independently vary conspecific abundance/constancy, plausible cost curvature, pollen limitation, and selfing mode/cost regimes. Use common initial trait distributions to isolate environmental effects; then cross regional initial states to test history dependence. Separate evolutionary shifts within lineages from apparent convergence caused by compositional replacement or extinction.

Include a **sorting-only baseline**: freeze each lineage's inherited trait while allowing the same declared colonization, establishment, and loss processes. Compare against an evolution-enabled treatment with matched external forcing. A question about floristic composition concerns which lineages occur and persist; it is not equivalent to a question about within-lineage floral evolution. If assembly is prescribed rather than modeled, say so and restrict the claim to evolutionary responses conditional on those assembled communities. Decomposing community trait changes into turnover and within-lineage change prevents an invalid transfer between these questions.

Predeclare convergence as reduction in a between-region trait-distance statistic relative to matched initial/baseline states, and divergence as persistent or increased separation with an explicit horizon and persistence rule. Also report viable recruitment, male/female fitness components, selection gradients, and loss of lineages. Similar endpoints without reproductive success are not evidence of adaptive convergence. Small trait changes caused by low `G` or low reproduction are not necessarily common adaptive optima.

The prospective contribution is a conditional regime map: which reassembly changes move regions toward shared versus distinct reproductive fitness attractors, and through which measured ledger components? Specialization, abundance effects, floral fitness trade-offs, and reproductive assurance themselves are established mechanisms. Novelty would require demonstrating their joint, island-reassembly consequences and falsifiable boundaries, with no claim that those old mechanisms were newly discovered. External historical claims require external observations; this model alone yields model-conditional predictions.

Before scientific runs, require identity checks: outcross maternal totals equal outcross paternal totals; each selfed recruit is one individual but two parental contributions; no compatible pollen gives no outcross seeds; saturation respects ovule/resource bounds; costly selfing can lose despite raising seed counts; zero genetic variance gives no heritable response; analytical special cases agree with computed gradients. These are future admission gates, not checks performed by this research note.
