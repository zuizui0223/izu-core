# Investment can reduce pollen deficit yet reduce viable offspring

The predeclared exploratory whole-population factorial completed all 6,912 conditions. Eight boundary/nonmutation tests passed. The runner verified source archives, exact coverage, initial near/far equality and reproduction-ledger consistency. A separate raw-record reconstruction verified all 6,912 deficit formulas and all 84 contrast means and history-bootstrap intervals. The numerical ABM/deterministic/PDE closeout remains incomplete.

All 48 founder plants retain their matching alleles. Investment and capacity are assigned independently to 0.25, 0.5 or 0.75 in every plant. The same frozen visitor histories supply snapshots 0, 200 and 400; plants do not evolve in this assay. Snapshot400 is a visitor-history index, not 400 updates of evolution of these manipulated plants.

These are descriptive 95 percent paired-history bootstrap intervals, not multiplicity-adjusted confirmation. All snapshots and factorial cells are retained in the summary JSON.

Under strong isolation, extra investment slightly reduces the fractional pollen deficit but produces fewer viable offspring. A lower deficit is therefore not sufficient evidence of greater reproductive success: the saturation denominator and allocation budget change with investment. The investment cost coefficient itself is unchanged. This is a community-wide instantaneous intervention, including altered donor and recipient contributions, not an individual invasion gradient or realized evolutionary response.

At investment0.5, capacity0.75 minus0.25 reduces viable pollen deficit in the far exposure by0.2474 in the delayed setting and0.4524 in the prior setting, while increasing expected viable maternal offspring by53.56 and82.98 respectively. This is a model-conditional compensation mechanism. Differences between settings cannot be attributed to timing alone because capacity cost also differs and the saturation counterfactual respects prior selfing.

Q1 connection: H3 concerns environmental pollen shortage; H4 concerns how plant traits alter the remaining deficit. Neither establishes that all traits lowering pollen deficit will be favoured. This assay supports a reproductive-assurance pathway, but it does not implement measured open-flower accessibility or colour. The fixed/evolving-capacity evolutionary campaign is still required for H2's realized-evolution claim.
