# Figure captions — island ecology manuscript

Updated: 2026-08-24

These captions follow the ecology-first H1–H5 manuscript architecture. State-separability diagnostics are Supplementary rather than a main biological result.

## Figure 1. Island ecological response architecture

**A common pollinator-functional perturbation can generate different post-establishment plant responses because lineages enter the change from different functional starting states and because downstream interaction context modifies propagation.** (A) The model applies a shared mainland-like to island-like change in pollinator functional opportunity rather than fitting a different perturbation to each external island system. (B) Plant lineages occupy different pre-existing positions in a standardized functional trait space; this heterogeneity creates the potential for opposite response signs. (C) Local support/network context and partner effectiveness modify which branch a lineage realizes, while autonomous reproductive assurance acts further downstream. (D) The resulting ecological states include branching, same-direction propagation and buffering or alternative responses. Frozen intervention results identify pre-existing functional-position heterogeneity as the minimal tested branch generator, local interaction context as a bidirectional branch allocator with buffering capacity, and assurance as a robust magnitude attenuator rather than a robust sign-rescue mechanism. The lower panel places this mechanism sequence against the strict external island challenge: 13 systems comprise three branching, six same-direction propagation, two buffering/alternative, one reproductive-axis-decoupling constraint and one retained falsification. The challenge set is not a prevalence sample and does not imply one shared empirical mechanism.

## Figure 2. Pre-existing functional position is the replicated minimal generator of branching

**Within-run mixed-sign branching collapses only when pre-existing lineage functional-position heterogeneity is removed.** Bars show the mixed-sign run fraction in the original frozen residual experiment. The full residual model produced mixed-sign branching in 0.4167 of matched runs. Removing initial trait-position heterogeneity reduced mixed-sign frequency and mean within-run branching balance to zero and changed 37 paired lineage response signs, whereas removing trait-adjustment heterogeneity or assurance-ceiling heterogeneity retained branching. The same boundary was reproduced in an independently seeded frozen block: full-model mixed-sign frequency was again 0.4167, whereas initial-trait removal reduced branching to zero and changed 44 paired lineage signs. Thus, among the residual factors tested, pre-existing functional position is the replicated minimal synthetic generator of same-environment lineage response-sign branching. The standardized coordinate is an abstract functional position and is not equated post hoc with any single empirical floral trait.

## Figure 3. Local interaction context reallocates branches, whereas assurance mainly attenuates propagation

**Branch generation and downstream propagation are mechanistically distinct.** (A) In the downstream factorial, removing local support changed 105/288 paired lineage response signs, compared with 13/288 for partner effectiveness and none for dependency heterogeneity, even though two-sided branching remained possible with all tested downstream modifiers off. (B) In the independent network-context experiment, local support attenuated 85/96 eligible negative reproductive responses, crossed the sign boundary in 16/96, and worsened 11/96. Network context is therefore a bidirectional branch allocator with buffering capacity rather than universal protection. Autonomous assurance occupied a different synthetic regime: it attenuated 207/216 reproductive declines but produced 0/216 sign rescues in the independent block. (C) Assurance attenuation remained frequent across saturation values 1, 2 and 3, whereas sign rescue remained absent; a broadened 525-contrast support envelope likewise contained no strong sign rescue. Together these results support a hierarchy in which starting state generates branch potential, interaction context reallocates propagation, and assurance mainly dampens downstream magnitude.

## Figure 4. Cross-island recurrence of branching, propagation, buffering and protected exceptions

**Independent island plant–pollinator systems repeatedly occupy the broad response states generated by the already-frozen model.** (A) The 13 strict external systems comprise three branching systems, six same-direction propagation systems, two buffering or alternative systems, one reproductive-axis-decoupling constraint and one retained falsification. (B) Branching systems are the Izu multi-taxon Hiraiwa–Ushimaru system, Caribbean Gesneriaceae and the Canary Islands Teide honeybee-network experiment. Same-direction propagation systems are Ogasawara *Psychotria homalosperma*, New Zealand *Rhabdothamnus solandri*, the Guam–Saipan bird-loss natural experiment, Seychelles invasive-ant disruption, Mauritius *Roussea simplex* invasive-ant disruption and Bahamas *Pavonia bahamensis* following hurricane-associated pollination loss. Buffering or alternative systems are Hawaiian lobelioids after bird extinction and California Channel Islands *Nicotiana glauca*. Puerto Rico–Mona *Guaiacum sanctum* is retained as a reproductive-axis-decoupling constraint, and Dominica *Heliconia* as a failed frozen signed-position projection. All 11 generative challenges are covered or sign-compatible with states that existed before the final external challenge. This supports recurrence of a conditional island-response architecture, not a shared empirical causal mechanism or a global frequency estimate of response classes.

## Supplementary Figure S1. Observation-to-mechanism state-separability diagnostics

**Response-state compatibility is not equivalent to one-to-one mechanism identification.** Sensitivity, false-positive rate and specificity are computed from frozen intervention frequencies. Mixed-sign branching is highly specific but insensitive for pre-existing trait-position heterogeneity within the declared intervention family; same-direction response therefore does not imply uniform starting states. Strong sign rescue is specific but rare for network context relative to the tested assurance route, whereas magnitude attenuation occurs frequently under both network context and assurance and is consequently weak for inverse discrimination. These diagnostics are used only as an inference guard on the ecological interpretation and are not the primary biological contribution.
