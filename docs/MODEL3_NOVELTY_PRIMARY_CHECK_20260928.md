# Model 3: primary-literature novelty check

Date: 2026-09-28. Scope: targeted nearest-precedent search, not a systematic review or proof of priority. Current model evidence was checked against `MODEL3_CH2_BRIDGE_ECOLOGICAL_RESULTS_20260928.md`. No simulation or model change was made.

## Judgment

Model 3 has a defensible **combination and causal-comparison contribution**, but pollinator loss causing floral change, reproductive assurance, genetic impoverishment, and context-dependent responses are established topics. The best island-ecology question is: **How does isolation-mediated assembly of pollinator communities translate into floral evolution, and how much of that response is realized in finite plant populations?**

The current results support changes in mean inherited floral investment under paired ecological interventions. They do not yet establish a universal response-regime sequence, persistent latent sign branching, a pure genetic-drift effect, or a quantitative prediction for particular natural islands.

## Island-ecology position

The contribution joins island community assembly (arrival, loss and continuity of mutualists), reproductive ecology (pollen receipt, assurance and costs), and evolutionary realization in finite resident populations. Pollination is the mechanism; isolation and repeated island histories supply the biogeographic question. The mechanism can also operate in habitat fragments and is not exclusive to oceanic islands.

The strongest currently evidenced contrast is the mean isolation response: -0.1446 under natural histories, +0.0333 under annual response-blind visitor-count matching, and -0.2716 with larger plant populations. The positive matched difference means the non-isolated side declined more; it does not mean isolated flowers increased their investment. This is a test of intervention-dependent isolation effects, not a demonstration that species richness alone explains evolution.

The finite-population contribution is promising but bounded: capacity 48 versus 192 changes multiple finite processes. It supports different realized evolutionary magnitudes in the model, not a universal adaptive deficit or a separately identified effect of drift. Stable mixed-sign branching and S/C/I reversals remain unresolved under the existing precision diagnostics.

## Projection to natural islands

A process-level projection is feasible now as a measurement framework; direct forecasts require new independent data. Use at least two axes: **pollination opportunity and continuity** (effective visits, pollen deposition, years/seasons without service, community persistence and functional matching), and **plant population/evolutionary capacity** (flowering population size, effective population size if estimable, genetic variation, selfing and inbreeding depression). Island distance and origin describe how these processes arise rather than directly supplying calibrated model parameters.

The Izu study above gives a particularly relevant starting point for functional matching and reproduction. Its two survey years and cross-sectional networks do not by themselves recover arrival/extinction probabilities, long-term absence histories, population genetic parameters or 200-generation evolution. A 42-system network compilation could be projected onto observed ecological coordinates, with missing dimensions explicitly unavailable, but cannot validate inherited investment trajectories merely by occupying a model-defined region.

In situ evolution must also be distinguished from colonization filtering. [Ciarle, Burns & Mologni (2025), *Flower size evolution in the Southwest Pacific*, 10.1093/aob/mcaf005](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcaf005), explicitly addresses island floral trajectories across lineages and pollination modes. Therefore the gap should not be written as an absence of all island macro-comparative floral research.

## Defensible novelty wording

> We connect isolation-driven pollinator assembly to inherited floral investment and use paired counterfactuals to distinguish changes associated with visitor-community conditions from departures associated with finite plant populations. Within this model, the direction of the isolation contrast depends on community interventions, while its magnitude depends strongly on plant population size.

This is an evidence-backed framing of this study, not a world-first assertion. Mechanism-specific interventions, independent empirical calibration and out-of-sample island predictions would strengthen it; they are not completed results.
