# Chapter 2 — specific novelty after nearest-precedent review (2026-10-15)

**Scope:** updated journal positioning of the **single active unified Model 3 manuscript**, not a new biological analysis, priority certification, systematic review, or alteration of historical 2026-09-26–10-06 audits. This dated addendum corrects the active literature bridge while preserving those original audit bytes.

## Ranked positive findings and evidence ceiling

1. **Sequence is not causal necessity (primary argument).** Bodbyl Roels & Kelly (2011) experimentally removed pollinators from *Mimulus guttatus* for five generations and interpreted the trait response as favoring a sequential selfing-syndrome model. Model 3 instead couples **51/64** assurance-first responses in the independently confirmed delayed-selfing/cost setting with an explicit fixed-assurance evolutionary intervention: floral investment falls under limited replenishment **even when assurance-capacity evolution is disabled in four preregistered reproductive regimes**. It isolates the *necessity of capacity evolution*, **not** the absence of realized selfing: capacity is fixed at 0.5 and selfing is still allowed. A targeted literature audit did not find the same necessity comparison, but does **not** certify that no earlier model ever asked it.

2. **Evolutionary divergence compression (second strongest tested result).** With evolving versus fixed assurance capacity, near–far floral-investment contrasts attenuate by **+0.1007 to +0.2200** under all four registered settings. Approximately **78–90%** of the interaction is localized algebraically to greater investment decline under the higher-replenishment arm, **not** a causally isolated fraction. **Teixido & Aizen (2019) already compared *three* differently visited *Tuberaria guttata* field populations**, using pollination treatments and phenotypic flower-size selection. They establish antecedents for **assurance weakening floral selection and ecological variation in that weakening**. A novelty claim based on being the *first* to compare pollination environments is therefore false. Our distinct demonstration is the model-based, genetic evolutionary counterfactual of **allowing rather than blocking assurance evolution**, and its effect on the **difference of the final evolved investment outcomes between environments**. This finite-horizon model result does not show an actual mainland-island history, a pollinator-saturated control or a universal sign under all regimes.

3. **Inherited genetic access modifies threshold-defined response order (high-upside, not equal-priority confirmation).** The separately registered **6,144-case** two-founder, three-ecology contrast brackets a conditional original Model 3 order-score sign reversal between A-allele half-widths **0.046875 and 0.078125**. The *different* source-replayed, paired **1,536-history pilot** with equal initial expressed phenotypes reports a descriptive all-start order-score shift **+0.6692708333** and A-event incidence difference **+0.0338541667**. In the source's 0.05/20-census event definition, the *lower* width **cannot** cross a +0.05 A response from standing alleles alone; hence the width manipulation changes **allele support/reachability and realized timing**, not an isolated variance-mediated speed. Only **two original founder-genotype backgrounds**, common paired future histories and a single event threshold support the pilot; no independently regenerated founder experiment, generalizable effect size or Q4 mediation is established. Barrett & Schluter (2008) already review fast adaptation from standing genetic variation; the potential narrower novelty is **trait response ordering under a fixed reproductive system**, not “more standing variance can speed adaptation.”

## Editorial hierarchy and non-promotions

**Headline:** Same constrained pollination process can yield **assurance-first observations without an evolutionary causal requirement for assurance**, while evolving assurance **compresses** the difference in floral investment between otherwise compared visitor regimes. These are supported *within the specified Model 3*, not a universal island-syndrome law.

**Supporting/SI:** Strongly conditional allelic-access order effects and finite genotype realization; initial-state and historical order effects on persistence remain distinct; registered failures and nulls stay fixed. No claim of a first universal selfing-first sequence, a universal non-necessity theorem, independently confirmed standing-variance mechanism, a pure genetic drift share, or naturally caused A-first population rescue.

**Manuscript revision:** Adds Teixido & Aizen, Van Etten et al. (corrected bibliography), Zell et al., Barrett & Schluter, and Morgan & Wilson to the **one active Ecology Letters paper**. Historical Journal of Ecology papers remain archival provenance. Abstract, Q1/Q4 numerics, actual data, registered decision rules, source model, original trial results and DOI/deposit status remain unchanged.
