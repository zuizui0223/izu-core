# Chapter 1 unresolved problems → unified Model 3 answers

Updated: 2026-09-27

## Purpose

This document states exactly which unresolved problems remain after Chapter 1 and which of them Chapter 2 can answer mechanistically. It does not assign Chapter 1 regions to Model 3 parameter cells and does not turn comparative associations into reconstructed evolutionary histories.

## The Chapter 1 result

Chapter 1 leaves a deliberately asymmetric result:

1. **A recurrent functional core exists.** Reproductive assurance and floral accessibility/generalization recur in the classic-positive direction across all four predeclared geographic strata.
2. **Isolation is associated with stronger pollen limitation.** Independent GloPL evidence supplies a broad ecological pressure consistent with pollination constraint.
3. **Detailed pollinator-facing phenotype does not recur in one direction.** Colour and colour × architecture combinations differ among northern mid-latitudes, northern high latitudes, tropics and southern extratropics.
4. **Selfing does not absorb every display association.** Several regional display/architecture signals remain after the declared selfing adjustment.

The unresolved question is therefore not simply whether an island syndrome exists. It is:

> **Why can a recurrent pollination problem and recurrent functional insurance coexist with different, sometimes opposing, detailed floral outcomes?**

## Open problem 1 — Why does a common functional pressure not produce one detailed phenotype?

### Chapter 1 limitation

Chapter 1 shows `same functional core + different regional display`, but its comparative design cannot identify the mechanism generating that non-uniformity.

### Chapter 2 answer

The unified Model 3 fixed-state assay shows that the same controlled visitor composition can favour opposite floral-investment directions depending on starting functional position. In the prospective reduction audit:

- start access 0.20 under left4: gradient `+1.5048`;
- start access 0.80 under left4: gradient `-0.8720`;
- the signs reverse under right4.

The first branch therefore appears before inheritance or demographic stochasticity.

### Cross-chapter interpretation

> **A recurrent pressure need not imply recurrent phenotype because functional matching has the capacity to make selection state dependent. Whether isolation-driven assembly preserves that branching is a separate, regime-dependent question.**

This is the direct mechanistic answer to Chapter 1's recurrent-core / non-uniform-display tension.

## Open problem 2 — Are the residual floral responses merely a consequence of selfing?

### Chapter 1 limitation

Chapter 1 conditions regional display associations on the measured selfing core, but that statistical adjustment cannot identify a separate pollinator-mediated evolutionary pathway.

### Chapter 2 answer

The unified reduction audit holds reproductive assurance fixed while starting floral state and visitor composition are crossed, yet reproductive-selection direction still changes sign. The separate Model 3 reproductive assays also show that investment return depends on matching and activity at fixed assurance.

Thus evolution of assurance is **not required** to generate non-uniform floral selection under the controlled-composition operator.

At the same time, the full Model 3 shows that assurance can determine whether a population persists under severe visitor loss.

### Cross-chapter interpretation

> **Assurance can be a recurrent insurance function without being the sole cause of pollinator-facing floral divergence.**

This directly resolves why Chapter 1 can retain a selfing-adjusted display signal while assurance itself recurs globally.

## Open problem 3 — Why can traits associated with island function coexist with stronger pollen limitation?

### Chapter 1 limitation

Chapter 1 finds stronger experimental pollen limitation with isolation, yet autonomous selfing is associated with lower current pollen limitation and some island-associated functional states are compatible with compensation. A comparative analysis cannot tell whether such traits erase the pressure or merely buffer its consequences.

### Chapter 2 answer

Model 3 separates upstream pollination environment from downstream persistence. Reproductive assurance need not reverse the upstream ecological mismatch; under severe visitor absence it can instead determine whether an evolutionary endpoint exists at all.

### Cross-chapter interpretation

> **A stressful island pollination regime and a recurrent buffering trait can coexist because buffering changes realized consequences without eliminating the upstream constraint.**

This converts the apparent Chapter 1 tension into a staged mechanism.

## Open problem 4 — Why do present-day pollinator environments fail to map cleanly onto present-day floral traits?

### Chapter 1 limitation

The regional Chapter 1 patterns are cross-sectional. They cannot distinguish current ecological sorting from inherited effects of earlier pollinator loss, recovery or colonization history.

### Chapter 2 answer

Model 3 chronology holds the final 120-year environment common but yields different inherited investment endpoints:

- uninterrupted: `+0.2115`;
- early visitor loss: `-0.1603`;
- late visitor loss: `+0.0322`.

### Cross-chapter interpretation

> **Current environment is not a sufficient statistic for current phenotype.**

This is especially relevant to Chapter 1 regional comparisons: two floras exposed to similar current conditions can retain different trait distributions because their histories differ.

## Open problem 5 — Is geographic isolation one biological axis?

### Chapter 1 limitation

Chapter 1 necessarily uses geographic isolation as a broad comparative coordinate. That coordinate can combine several biological processes: plant colonization, seed immigration, pollinator arrival, pollinator loss and interaction reorganization.

### Chapter 2 answer

Model 3 separates seed and pollinator connectivity. Changing pollinator distance can reverse investment direction while seed distance is fixed; changing seed distance under the same pollinator regime produces a different response. The two channels alter different parts of the eco-evolutionary process.

### Cross-chapter interpretation

> **One geographic isolation measure can represent multiple mechanistically distinct connectivity channels.**

Chapter 1 estimates the broad macroecological association; Chapter 2 explains why its biological realization need not be one-dimensional.

## Open problem 6 — Do oceanic founding and continental separation create different outcomes simply because of island type?

### Chapter 1 limitation

Comparative island categories can mix assembly history with present ecology, making it tempting to treat oceanic versus continental origin as a direct mechanism.

### Chapter 2 answer

In Model 3, matched founding/separation labels with the same inherited plants, visitors and future history do not produce different outcomes simply because the label changes. Differences require biological state/history differences such as founder composition, inherited variation, visitor community or elapsed history.

### Cross-chapter interpretation

> **Island history matters through inherited ecological and genetic state, not through an island-type label by itself.**

This helps keep Chapter 1 biogeographic categories from being overinterpreted causally.

## Open problem 7 — Why can attraction and access traits reorganize semi-independently?

### Chapter 1 limitation

Chapter 1 shows that colour and architecture do not move as one package. Northern high-latitude and southern-extratropical patterns in particular imply that attraction and access components can reorganize separately.

### Chapter 2 answer and limit

Model 3 directly represents a functional access/matching coordinate and costly floral investment, not literal colour classes or a multichannel visual model. It demonstrates how partner matching can generate conditional selection on a floral investment coordinate, but it does **not** prove why red/pink, blue/purple or yellow/orange changes in a specific Chapter 1 region.

### Cross-chapter interpretation

This Chapter 1 problem is only **partially** resolved. Chapter 2 supplies the general reason that distinct floral coordinates need not share one selection direction, but trait-specific colour/access mapping remains outside the current model.

## Open problem 8 — Does the old Chapter 2 richness/finite-community result survive inside Model 3?

### Current status

Answered prospectively inside Model 3.

The 24,576-case bridge separates three quantities that the old Chapter 2 response-geometry model partly conflated:

1. **visitor amount/richness:** annual response-blind richness matching reverses the mean far-minus-near inherited-investment effect from negative to positive in both finite ABM (`-0.1446 → +0.0333`) and deterministic density (`-0.4510 → +0.0338`);
2. **finite visitor-environment realization:** pooling eight independent visitor histories removes mixed history-level labels in both model forms, without identifying a pure latent-branch mechanism;
3. **finite plant demography:** increasing plant capacity from 48 to 192 reduces finite-ABM mixed histories from `12/128` to `1/128` at epsilon 0 and moves the mean toward the deterministic result.

Richness matching does not eliminate realized heterogeneity: finite-ABM mixed histories rise to `68/128` at epsilon 0. Deterministic mixed branching after matching is much weaker (`16/128` at epsilon 0, `1/128` at 0.01, `0/128` at 0.05).

### Cross-chapter interpretation

The old Chapter 2 claim is therefore refined rather than simply reproduced. **Visitor amount strongly positions the coarse island-response regime, while visitor composition/history and finite plant demography are distinct axes that strongly modify realized directional heterogeneity; exact latent branch prevalence remains unresolved.** This provides a compatible mechanism for how Chapter 1 can show a recurrent broad functional pressure without one detailed phenotypic response.

Legacy Model 2 is no longer needed as an active control gate; its exact-richness, synthetic-`k`, response-rule and S/C/I analyses remain historical provenance under `legacy/model2/` and are excluded from current Supporting Information.

## Dissertation-level result

The chapter connection is now stronger than `Chapter 1 observations → Chapter 2 possible mechanism`.

It is:

```text
CH1
isolation -> stronger pollination constraint
       -> recurrent assurance/accessibility core
       -> but non-uniform detailed display
       -> selfing does not explain every display association
             |
             v
CH2 unified Model 3
functional matching makes selection state-dependent
       -> controlled functional matching has deterministic branch capacity
       -> isolation-driven visitor amount sets a coarse deterministic regime
       -> visitor histories + finite plant demography modify observed directional heterogeneity
       -> assurance/history/connectivity alter persistence and final outcome
             |
             v
recurrent function + non-convergent phenotype
```

The strongest thesis statement is:

> **Island isolation can generate recurrent functional solutions without recurrent phenotypic solutions because ecological matching permits alternative selection directions, while community assembly, reproductive insurance and finite demographic history determine whether those alternatives remain distinct and which persist.**

## Remaining dissertation-scale gap

Chapter 2 does not reconstruct the historical cause of the four Chapter 1 regional patterns. The decisive missing natural evidence is a same-unit A → B → C transition: pre-response plant/genetic state, measured visitor regime and functional match, reproductive selection, inherited trait/genotype change and demographic history.

This remaining gap should be presented as the next empirical test, not filled by assigning Chapter 1 regions to synthetic Model 3 cells.
