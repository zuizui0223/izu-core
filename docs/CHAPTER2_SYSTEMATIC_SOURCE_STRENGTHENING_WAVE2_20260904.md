## Cleared targets

### Cabo Verde

Primary anchor: `10.1016/j.ppees.2012.01.003`.

The comparative oceanic-island Campanulaceae study directly investigates pollination biology across Azores, Madeira, Canary Islands, Cape Verde, Mauritius and Réunion and explicitly includes the Cape Verde endemic *Campanula jacobaea*. This upgrades Cabo Verde from the first-pass trait-only flower-morphology record to direct pollination-process evidence.

Boundary: there is still no matched historical source-state → community-transition → local-realization → plant-response trajectory.

### Corsica

Primary anchor: `10.1046/j.1365-2540.2001.00897.x`.

The Heredity study on the rare Corsican endemic *Anchusa crispa* uses field-based self- and outcross-pollination treatments and follows reproductive/fitness outcomes across two generations. It therefore supplies direct breeding-system/reproductive evidence for Corsica rather than only a Mediterranean umbrella review.

Boundary: no matched pollinator-community transition is observed.

### Cyprus

Primary anchor: Mavrovounioti et al. 2024, *Cypricola* 24:1–16; NERC Open Research Archive record 537738.

Five Red Data Book plants on the Akrotiri Peninsula were surveyed for distribution/population state together with direct observations of insect flower visitors. This is local Cyprus pollination evidence and replaces the first-pass `initial_search_inconclusive` state.

Boundary: no historical community transition or matched reproductive response.

### Maltese Islands

Primary anchor: `10.1016/j.gecco.2025.e03765`.

The study analyses four years of standardized plant–wild bee interaction monitoring across 204 sites in the Maltese Islands, including bee abundance, species richness and functional diversity in relation to local/landscape habitat factors and honeybee abundance. This supplies direct archipelago-scale pollinator-community evidence.

Boundary: the study does not measure a matched source-state transition and plant reproductive outcome.

### Tasmania

Primary anchor: David Keith, revised by Mick Ilowski, *Epacris stuartii Recovery Plan*, Environment Australia / Tasmanian DPIWE, 1999.

The authored recovery report contains original 1995–96 field observations and experiments, not merely a secondary conservation summary. It reports direct *Calliphora* flower visitation/pollination observations and self-pollen, cross-pollen and pollinator-exclusion treatments. Treatment effects on fruit set were not significant, consistent with self-compatibility, while cross-pollinated plants had slightly higher fruit set.

This is retained as a **primary government research report** rather than mislabeled as a peer-reviewed journal article.

Boundary: no matched island community-transition trajectory.

## Norfolk remains unresolved

Two authoritative sources currently conflict on *Elatostema montanum* reproductive biology.

- the Commonwealth threatened-plant recovery synthesis describes the species as wind-pollinated and monoecious with male and female inflorescences;
- the 2026 Parks Australia nursery account describes rare male and female plants whose close placement enabled successful pollination, seed collection and germination.

These descriptions are not silently reconciled. Possible explanations include taxonomic/terminological differences, oversimplification in one source, or a distinction between floral sex expression and individual-level sex that requires primary botanical verification.

Norfolk therefore stays on `hold_mechanism_conflict` and remains in the source-strengthening queue.
