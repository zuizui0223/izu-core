# Q1 regional floral patterns → Q2 functional response

## Background, gap and answer-first poster text

**Background:** Classical island-syndrome explanations link restricted pollinator supply to reproductive assurance and reduced investment in pollinator attraction. Regional floras, however, begin with different floral combinations and interaction contexts.

**Question:** Across a global island dataset, which floral shifts recur, which differ among regions, and are they accounted for by selfing alone?

**Answer:** Regional floral responses differ, and selfing does not account for every association. A single change toward less conspicuous, more accessible flowers does not describe all four regions.

**Next question (Q2):** How can changing visitor communities produce different functional outcomes for different starting floral states?

This framing does not require claiming that no global macroanalysis has ever existed. The specific gap is a joint, region-resolved test of the declared traits, selfing adjustment and functional associations in this dataset. Q1 supplies observations; Q2 tests conditional possibilities, not the historical cause of those observations.

| Southern extratropics | Plain-colour categories increase while shallow/open-tube categories decline | Colour and access shift in different directions: reduced conspicuous-colour representation can coexist with restricted access. Plain includes white and green/brown/inconspicuous categories; it is not a measurement of visual signal strength. | **Plainer colours, but not uniformly open flowers** |

These interpretations are alternatives to test, not four proven regional pollinator mechanisms. Pollination syndromes concern combinations of detection, access and effectiveness; human colour classes cannot identify a visitor guild on their own. In a factorial field experiment, colour and shape affected bee and fly visitation differently ([Campbell et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/jeb.12295)). This supports separating attraction from access, but does not assign that experiment's visitors to the four global regions. Across 40 coastal networks, trait matching and pollination function were linked to pollinator functional diversity ([Hiraiwa & Ushimaru 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.14527)); that is a measurable functional bridge, not a calibration of Q2.

Dispersal belongs upstream: source pools, colonization opportunities and local persistence help determine which partners occur. Local foraging range is not automatically ocean-crossing colonization ability. Thus the poster should not use bee presence/absence, latitude or a flight-distance cartoon as a sufficient explanation of a regional floral pattern.

## H2 and H4 make the connection informative

H2 asks whether isolation–trait associations remain after accounting for selfing. A remaining association is compatible with additional pathways, including visitor-mediated filtering or selection, but is not an estimate of direct pollinator selection. Show colour and structure separately: their regional residual associations differ. Do not describe selfing adjustment as a causal mediation experiment.

H3 and H4 ask different questions. Greater isolation can be associated with stronger pollen limitation overall, while species bearing some island-associated functional traits have lower pollen limitation within the H4 comparison. A stressful setting and traits associated with better performance within that setting are compatible. H4 motivates a compensation hypothesis; it does not establish that those traits evolved because of isolation or that they completely offset its effects. Retain the treatment-sensitivity results in the numerical figure captions.

## Explain S, C and I using the regional discussion

Use the same flower and visitor drawings from the Q1 ending, but label the Q2 grid **Conceptual test — not four fitted regional models**.

- **S: Does the starting flower matter across visitor histories?** Show several starting flowers as rows. In the actual model these are 21 positions on one abstract matching axis, not literal colour/tube-length syndromes. S measures differences among row means of the island-minus-mainland endpoint service.
- **C: Does the visitor history affect starting flowers similarly?** Show independently generated histories as columns. C measures differences among column means, averaged over starting states. A column is not a region.
- **I: Does the effect of a visitor history depend on the starting flower?** Highlight two flowers that respond differently to the same pair of histories. I is the remainder after row and column effects, not a third organism or a separate ecological force.

Every cell compares two modeled contexts after both have run: final island service minus final mainland service. It is not change from a plant's starting service, a measured floral trait shift, or fitness. Region labels must not replace the crossed starting-state/history design. Four regional differences alone cannot estimate S/C/I or show that I dominates in nature.

The ecology is: different starting floral combinations encounter different realized sets of functionally suitable partners; the same visitor change may therefore have different consequences. Q2 demonstrates conditional instances of this principle. Its factorial also shows that the way plant states respond matters: changing the response target can alter component rankings. The original C→I→S sequence is not a universal ecological law.

## k, natural systems and Izu

Illustrate k with several independent visitor-history strips being combined at each simulation step. The implementation concatenates visitor lists, so histories containing more visitor types receive more weight. It is not a literal count of years, generations, species or islands. Repeated, weakly correlated ecological exposures motivate this aggregation experiment, but a field value of k requires a sampling/response model; Hill diversity does not supply it directly.

The 42 interaction systems from six studies locate observed partner breadth and temporal synchrony. They show ecological variation relevant to the question; they do not validate regional S/C/I shares. Keep this natural-system panel between simulation and Izu, rather than jumping directly from simulation to a local example.

Izu is the prospective, measurement-rich bridge: sample repeated visitor histories for identified plants, record floral access traits, measure visitation effort and pollen delivery, and connect these to reproductive outcomes and pollination treatments. Its value lies in the existing spatial/seasonal design and ability to link these measurements, not merely proximity to Atami or an untested claim to be the world's best island system.
