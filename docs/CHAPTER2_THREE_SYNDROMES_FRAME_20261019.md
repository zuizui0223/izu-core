# Pollination, selfing and island floral syndromes: distinct explanatory levels

User steering,2026-10-19: first organize the relationship among these three
concepts. Subsequent environmental change is interpreted primarily as
readaptation after entry into a different pollination environment, not a
requirement to restore the ancestral phenotype. No running endpoint is changed.

| Concept | Biological meaning | What Model3 represents | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pollination syndrome | Covariation among floral traits associated with pollen-vector function | Matching trait M and investment I affect native pollen receipt/export | A particular bee/bird/fly syndrome, colour/scent evolution, or visitor identity inferred from flower size |
| Selfing syndrome | Floral changes associated with increased self-fertilization | Evolvable assurance capacity A, investment I, selfed/outcrossed output | A equals realized selfing rate, self-compatibility evolution, or all floral traits of a natural selfer |
| Island floral patterns | Recurrent, context-dependent floral differences in insular assemblages or lineages | Prescribed visitor histories, finite populations and inherited variation provide mechanistic scenarios | Every island has few visits, all island flowers shrink, or a generic model proves a natural island origin/history |

The explanatory question is whether similar reduced floral phenotypes reflect
the same causal route. Environmental change can alter both the return on floral
investment and the return on assurance. A common ecological cause can produce
correlated responses without assurance evolution being necessary for the onset
of investment decline. Conversely, assurance may subsequently modify selection,
inheritance and persistence. The two possibilities are not mutually exclusive.

## Primary-source constraints

- Hiraiwa & Ushimaru(2017), [doi:10.1098/rspb.2016.2218](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2016.2218),
  compared coastal continental/oceanic island networks. Lower functional
  diversity was associated with interaction niche changes; visitation frequency
  and reproductive effectiveness are not interchangeable. Do not equate an
  island's fewer functional groups with uniformly fewer visits.
- Teixido & Aizen(2019), [doi:10.1093/aob/mcz014](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcz014),
  examined delayed selfing, assurance and flower-size selection in a mixed-mating
  annual. It supports considering assurance as a modifier of floral selection,
  not a universal necessary first step in floral reduction.
- [Floral Trait Evolution of Angiosperms on Pacific Islands(2020)](https://doi.org/10.1086/709018)
  found context dependence rather than a general reduction rule and identified
  ecological filtering as an alternative explanation for floristic patterns.
- [Flower size evolution in the Southwest Pacific(2025)](https://academic.oup.com/aob/article/136/2/287/7960047)
  reported evidence for an island-rule pattern. This is not equivalent to all
  flowers becoming smaller: ancestral size, lineages, pollination modes and
  geographic scope matter. Retain both studies rather than choosing the one
  most favorable to the proposed story.
- Zell et al.(2025), [doi:10.1111/nph.20234](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.20234),
  explicitly separates breeding system, lifespan, floral symmetry and arrival
  opportunity in island colonization. Establishment filtering and evolution
  after establishment must not be collapsed into a single island mechanism.

## Central question and evidence layers

**Under island-relevant changes in pollination, does a selfing-like reduction
of floral investment require assurance evolution, or can ecological selection
generate reduction independently, with assurance subsequently changing its
genetic and demographic consequences?**

1. **Origin of the phenotype.** Existing fixed-A contrasts test necessity of
   assurance evolution for I decline; fixedA still permits selfing. They do not
   show that selfing itself or assurance capacity is unnecessary for persistence.
2. **Coupling of responses.** Changes in A and I can share ecological causes;
   observed order or covariance alone does not identify A causing I decline.
3. **Inheritance and demography.** The ongoing160x160 tests strict I-support
   closure G under B8 and a policy-by-budget survival interaction S. They are
   separate environmental contrasts. Even joint positivity is not a mediation
   result or proof of a survival-versus-adaptation tradeoff in the same lineage.
4. **Subsequent readaptation.** The user's chosen next question is whether an
   assurance-bearing population can respond after entry into a different
   pollination environment. This is a proposed extension, not a result of160x160.

The three syndromes are not successive mandatory stages, nor three mutually
exclusive labels. Similar traits can arise through different ecological,
mating-system and colonization histories. Natural observations would need
independent mating-system, pollen-service and demographic evidence to distinguish
them; flower morphology alone cannot assign the causal route.
