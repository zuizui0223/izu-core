## Common research problem

**Why can reproductive constraint and functional response recur across islands while the evolution or composition of visible floral traits remains heterogeneous?** Chapter 1 supplies the macroecological observational pattern and current functional associations. Chapter 2 uses *one explicitly specified* Model 3 to dissect potential processes between the reproductive return to an allele/trait and its realized inherited and demographic outcome. Neither chapter, alone or juxtaposed, identifies the actual historical cause of Chapter 1's four regional associations.

|---|---|---|

The Chapter 1 universe includes **8,264 island units**, **4,379 broad-H1 islands**, **106,295 accepted taxa**, and **2,969 GloPL experiments**. **83.65% of island-by-species origin statuses are unresolved**. Thus contemporary composition is not an evolutionary time series, and correcting geography is not an evolutionary intervention.

### Reverse Q-to-H view: what would go wrong with a one-to-one alignment?

- **Q1 (selection/thresholds)** is needed to *interpret H3, H2 and H4 together*, not only H1. Four-setting primary: far-minus-near investment remains negative with A fixed (−0.3018 to −0.4413), and evolving A compresses that environment contrast (+0.1007 to +0.2200). This is within Model 3; **near is still strongly pollen limited**, not a pollen-saturated mainland.
- **Q2 (order)** addresses a historical chronology **not observed in any of H1–H4**. A-first was 51/64 for delayed costly selfing versus 30/64 for prior selfing, under a defined sustained 0.05 threshold. It cannot be inferred from H2 adjusting for `selfing_core`, and it does not identify an evolutionary order caused by isolation in Ch1.
- **Q3 (realization)** supplies the sharpest *mechanistic explanation candidate* for H1's heterogeneity and H2's conditional floral responses. In a separate **no-I-mutation/no-immigration** 2,304-history cohort, 703 histories lost access to a *new* I-decrease threshold before I fixation. At 315 of 1,238 selected first-loss transitions, conditional reproductive weighting favoured the needed low-I allele class yet the finite offspring inherited zero copies. These denominators overlap and are outcome-selected; this is neither a pure drift estimate nor a proof of Chapter 1 geographic mechanisms. A different matched density/finite comparison had A-first in only 3/32 and 2/32 histories where its conditional density model predicted A-first; all finite populations stayed occupied.
- **Q4 (persistence)** is conceptually relevant to assemblage filtering behind H1 and possible population-level consequences beyond H4. Its independent registered K-at-fixed-B interaction was small but positive (+0.0077457 occupancy-probability units, 95% history-bootstrap [+0.0024972,+0.0130155]), for **assigned phenotype-expression order × selfed-seed viability**. Separately, history/preparation experiments show other conditional persistence contrasts. They do not establish a benefit of *spontaneous genetic A-first order*, real-island survival, or an evolutionary demographic mechanism underlying H1/H4.

## One dissertation story, with no false causal arrows

**Ch1, observational:** geographic isolation is associated with stronger pollen limitation (**H3**) and recurrent self-compatibility (**H1**); floral morphology has heterogeneous regional associations (**H1/H2**), and some trait combinations correlate with lower contemporaneous pollen limitation (**H4**).

**Ch2, mechanism candidates within Model 3:** changing visitor return can directly shift selection on attraction (**Q1**) without a required selfing-first sequence (**Q2**). Standing genetic access and finite realized parent/homolog transmission determine whether the specified response remains possible (**Q3**). Given an inherited/prepared state, later persistence can depend on mating and demographic conditions (**Q4**) but no complete natural Q1→Q4 causal mediation has been confirmed.

This should be narrated as **"recurrent reproductive constraint does not imply a single realized floral evolutionary route"**, not as "the Chapter 1 geographic result is explained by the Model 3 four-question pipeline".

### Most useful passage for thesis Introduction or Chapter-link discussion (editorial proposed wording)

> Global island floras show a recurring association between isolation and reproductive assurance, but not a uniformly repeated floral form; pollen-addition evidence suggests a related reproductive constraint. To understand how a similar constraint could generate heterogeneous floral outcomes, Chapter 2 distinguishes four processes: selection acting through maternal and paternal reproduction, the timing and genetic accessibility of possible responses, the finite transmission of response-enabling alleles, and conditional demographic persistence. The key distinction is between a trait change being favoured, being genetically reachable, being realized, and changing persistence. These mechanistic possibilities are consistent with, but not identified as the historical causes of, the Chapter 1 observations.

## Non-negotiable cross-chapter inferential firewall

1. **Geography and ecology differ:** Ch1 uses corrected continental isolation distance and measured pollen-addition LRR, whereas Ch2 manipulates synthetic visitor functional-type replenishment. One cannot assign model near/far to real continental/island pollen-sufficient categories.
2. **Trait meanings differ:** H1 self-compatibility is categorical reproductive compatibility, not Model 3 evolving autonomous assurance A; Ch1 colour, symmetry, tube depth and accessibility are not Model 3 I by definition. No numeric regression coefficient is transferable.
3. **Study units differ:** Ch1's island–species and published supplementation studies are distinct observational samples. Ch2's independent visitor histories, nested demographic repeats, source checkpoints and conditioned allele-loss transitions cannot be pooled with them.
4. **Inference differs:** H2 selfing adjustment is statistical, H4 is explicitly post hoc and H3 is a geographic association. Neither proves temporal order, selection, lineage evolution or population persistence; Q2/Q3/Q4 do not retrospectively identify them.
5. **Publication remains separate:** both chapters can share a dissertation-wide research question and cross-reference the other as motivation, not represent Chapter 2 as a confirmation or fit of Chapter 1, nor Chapter 1 as the natural validation of Chapter 2.

**Result:** conceptual correspondence is strong and nonordinal; direct quantitative or historical causal correspondence remains untested. The within-Model-3 positive primary and separate bounded mechanistic diagnostics keep their existing evidence ranks.
