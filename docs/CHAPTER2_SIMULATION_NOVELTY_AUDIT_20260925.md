# Chapter 2 simulation novelty audit — 2026-09-25

## Assessment

The broad ingredients are established: functional variance decomposition, distribution-dependent sensitivity rankings, nonlinear trait matching, and community-dependent floral adaptation are not new. The defensible candidate contribution is their particular application to **when a community compression preserves a state/community/non-additivity ranking**, coupled to explicit analytical nulls and intervention-based demonstrations with disclosed response-rule limits. This is a bounded theoretical/ecological contribution, not presently a new general sensitivity-analysis theory or a demonstrated explanation of regional floral evolution. No first-ever claim is supported by this targeted search.

## What the analytical result does and does not add

For independent centered X and Z, the manuscript's bilinear calculation follows directly from orthogonality: C = b² Var(Z), I = c² Var(X) Var(Z), hence I/C = c² Var(X)/b² when C is nonzero. It is a useful explicit null and teaching result. Exactness alone does not establish mathematical novelty; it is an elementary consequence of the established decomposition, and this search did not establish priority for its particular ecological formulation.

The centered quadratic extension is more specific: at fixed Var(Z), changing third/fourth moments changes the two components through different coefficient combinations. Under symmetry, its derivative sign is controlled by e²b² − c²d². That is a concrete candidate analytical contribution to the ecological argument. However:

- A change in I/C is not necessarily a crossing of I/C = 1; coefficient values and the admissible distribution range matter.
- Bilinearity is a sufficient class, not a necessary boundary for invariance. For example, with centered g(Z), Y = aX + bg(Z) + cXg(Z) retains I/C = c²Var(X)/b² whenever the variances exist, even if g is nonlinear. This is a direct audit derivation, not a claim that a source specifically published this example.
- Quadratic alignment describes the displayed response class; it is not a general necessary-and-sufficient theorem for every nonlinear operator or every family of input distributions.
- Population functional ANOVA and finite-grid sums of squares have related algebra but different weighting/distribution contracts. These contracts must be explicit before treating numerical shares as transferable objects.
- The actual plant model concatenates independently generated histories at each step and weights by the current number of visitor types. Its k intervention is not literally an equal-weight mean of scalar community variables. The bilinear pooled-mean theorem is therefore an analytical comparator unless a formal reduction to that scalar construction is demonstrated.

Safer framing is “an exact invariant null and explicit mechanisms for its failure,” rather than an unqualified “exact sufficiency boundary” suggesting an exhaustive classification. A formal sufficient condition plus counterexamples can be valuable without being a universal boundary theorem.

## Contribution after the additional factorial

The local report preserves a useful discrimination: best-to-centroid target changes lower S at k=16 in all 24 conditional comparisons, while threshold removal alone preserves S dominance. These 24 contrasts are four settings within six seeds, not 24 independent experimental replicates. Fixed state retains traits yet remains I-dominated; therefore initial-state memory does not by itself explain S share. The continuous centroid rule without damping remains C-dominated by component medians at all audited scales.

Consequently neither C→I→S nor C/I reversal is a response-rule-independent law. The specific new result, if retained in publication, is the map of **which declared interventions change component ordering and which do not**, alongside the failed scalar-curvature prediction and the separately frozen consumer–resource feedback test. Those are study-specific findings whose importance rests on biological interpretation and an honest model envelope. Protocol freezes and comprehensive artifacts improve credibility; they are not substitutes for scientific novelty.

Suggested claim: “We use established variance decomposition to identify an invariant bilinear comparator and show, in two declared ecological model classes, how changes to community aggregation and state–community response geometry can alter component rankings. A factorial response-rule challenge identifies limits to that behavior.”

## Connection to Q1 and four regional floral patterns

Q1 can supply observed regional floral differences and motivate hypotheses. Chapter 2 supplies possible response geometries that could make similar community changes affect different floral states differently. That is a mechanistic bridge at the hypothesis level, not an explanation already identified from regional labels. The next identifying evidence is jointly measured pre-response floral state, effort-standardized visitor history, visitor functional fit/effectiveness, and linked pollen/reproductive outcomes, with replication over common state/community support.

Do not assign four regions to synthetic S-, C- or I-dominated regimes, infer their effective k, or attribute their flower colour/form to historical pollinator selection from this simulation. No regional S/C/I calibration was found in the inspected local evidence. Trait-matching/pollination-success data such as Hiraiwa & Ushimaru illustrate a stronger empirical bridge, while a longitudinal or experimental design is needed to distinguish plastic adjustment from genetic evolution. These requirements do not imply adding uncalibrated mechanisms now simply to make the simulation appear more realistic.
