# Chapter 2 2026-10-05 novelty and literature position — 2026-10-06

Status: post-confirmation literature-position note. The literature boundaries were
frozen before the new-history outcome and are unchanged by the result. The
prospectively frozen replication subsequently confirmed the primary delayed-selfing/
costly positive-mutation claim; this document does not broaden that scope.

## Novelty claim to keep

Do **not** claim that pollinator loss can cause selfing evolution, floral
reduction, or a sequential selfing syndrome. Those have direct precedents.

The defensible paper-level contribution is narrower:

> **Observed evolutionary order does not establish causal necessity. In the same
> explicit pollination–reproduction model, reproductive assurance can reach a
> declared change threshold before attraction investment declines, yet blocking
> assurance evolution does not prevent the investment decline because isolation
> can reduce the outcross return to attraction directly.**

A secondary consequence is that permitting assurance evolution can reduce the
near–far investment contrast even when both environments undergo substantial
within-population evolution.

## Closest empirical precedents

### Bodbyl Roels & Kelly 2011 — experimental pollinator loss

Bodbyl Roels, S.A. & Kelly, J.K. 2011. *Rapid evolution caused by pollinator
loss in Mimulus guttatus*. Evolution 65:2541–2552.
DOI: 10.1111/j.1558-5646.2011.01326.x.

The experiment compared bumblebee-present versus no-pollinator populations for
five generations. No-pollinator populations evolved greater autonomous
self-fertilization, and the authors explicitly favored a **sequential** model of
selfing-syndrome evolution in which traits important for selfing efficiency can
change before other traits such as flower size.

Consequence for this paper: **capacity-first order is not itself novel**.
The new test must be whether that temporal order is causally required for the
decline in attraction investment.

### Gervasi & Schiestl 2017 — pollinator-driven divergent evolution

Gervasi, D.D.L. & Schiestl, F.P. 2017. *Real-time divergent evolution in plants
driven by pollinators*. Nature Communications 8:14691.
DOI: 10.1038/ncomms14691.

Experimental evolution with bumblebees versus hoverflies produced divergent
floral signals and mating-system responses; hoverfly-pollinated plants increased
autonomous self-pollination while several attraction-related traits changed.

Consequence for this paper: pollinator identity can jointly alter mating system
and floral display, so joint evolution is not novel by itself. The present model
adds a **necessity intervention**: it asks whether attraction decline remains
when assurance capacity cannot evolve.

## Pollen-limitation measurement precedents

### Ashman et al. 2004 and Knight et al. 2005

Ashman et al. (Ecology 85:2408–2421; DOI 10.1890/03-8024) emphasized that the
causes and consequences of pollen limitation cannot be inferred from a single
supplementation response without considering ecology, resources and evolutionary
responses.

Knight et al. (Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics 36:467–497;
DOI 10.1146/annurev.ecolsys.36.102403.115320) reviewed pollen limitation as a
context-dependent process influenced by historical and contemporary ecological
factors.

Consequence: the Chapter 2 result should not be advertised as the first warning
that pollen limitation is an imperfect fitness proxy.

### Knight, Steets & Ashman 2006 — response-variable and allocation bias

Knight et al. 2006, *American Journal of Botany* 93:271–277,
DOI 10.3732/ajb.93.2.271, showed that pollen-supplementation estimates depend
strongly on experimental design and response variable, and that resource
reallocation can inflate apparent pollen limitation.

Consequence: the model result should be framed as a controlled demonstration of
why **fractional pollen deficit and absolute viable reproductive return can move
differently**, not as a general methodological discovery.

### Van Etten et al. 2015 — viable offspring versus seed production

Van Etten et al. 2015, *Annals of Botany* 116:409–418,
DOI 10.1093/aob/mcv118, combined pollen limitation, selfing and inbreeding
depression in New Zealand *Sophora*. They showed that high seed production can
substantially overstate effective viable offspring when selfed progeny suffer
strong inbreeding depression.

Consequence: the model's pollen-deficit/viable-offspring mismatch has strong
empirical precedent and is best used to connect the process paper to the broader
reproductive-fitness literature.

## Result hierarchy after this audit

1. **Primary novelty:** sequence versus causal necessity under one explicit
   island-replenishment process.
2. **Mechanistic anchor:** same-plant-state isolation contrast in the outcross
   return to attraction.
3. **Unexpected secondary result:** assurance evolution can attenuate geographic
   divergence even when within-environment evolution is large.
4. **Reproductive consequence:** pollen-deficit reduction and viable output can
   disagree.
5. **Supporting only:** mutation/history memory, finite-versus-deterministic
   realization, PDE/numerical diagnostics and natural-island confrontation.

## Wording to avoid

- "first demonstration that selfing evolves before floral reduction";
- "selfing causes floral reduction";
- "pollen limitation is not fitness" as a standalone novelty claim;
- "isolation distance" as calibrated geography;
- "universal assurance-first sequence": the confirmed primary cell passed, but
  prior-selfing with positive mutation did not show an assurance-first majority
  at the primary 0.05 threshold.
