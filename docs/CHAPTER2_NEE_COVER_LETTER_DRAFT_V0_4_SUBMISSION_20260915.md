# Cover letter — Nature Ecology & Evolution

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Nonlinear response limits transport of determinant rankings across ecological regimes,”** as an Article for *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.

Ecologists increasingly recognize that the relative importance of environmental and biotic drivers can depend on context. Recent conditional variance-partitioning methods formalize that dependence, and empirical macro-moth analyses demonstrate it across environmental and functional settings. Our manuscript addresses the next inferential problem: **when can a determinant ranking estimated in one ecological context be transported to another after ecological structure has been compressed into a scalar summary?**

We answer that question with four linked evidence layers.

First, we derive an exact sufficiency boundary. Under a bilinear state–community response, a variance-equivalent effective number gives community and state-by-community contributions the same second-moment multiplier, so they cannot reverse rank. Higher-order analysis then shows why that sufficiency is not generic: equal second moments leave higher cumulants unconstrained, and an exact quadratic response identifies the mixed-curvature condition under which omitted information changes determinant order. The advance is therefore not the observation that importance can be context dependent, but an explicit criterion for when a compressed context can or cannot support rank transport.

Second, two structurally distinct nonlinear ecological models cross this analytical boundary. We retain a prespecified scalar-curvature prediction that failed rather than optimizing it away. Guided by that failure and the exact mixed-response result, we froze a feedback intervention before running six unused simulation seeds. Active state adjustment produced **52 community-to-interaction rank reversals in 108 paired comparisons; the feedback knockout produced none**. The intervention changes phase topology rather than simply increasing one variance component.

Third, before extracting natural coordinates we froze a result-blind public-data admission and measurement protocol. The final regime plane contains **42 island interaction systems from six studies and five island or archipelago groups**. Source-balanced partner breadth and temporal synchrony span both dimensions, are not strongly collinear and occupy the interior of the plane. The coverage remains broad after removing all 19 systems from Mallorca, the largest source study, after which the primary source search stopped according to the prespecified rule.

We additionally report stricter post-primary sensitivity analyses. Under the original source-native schedules, removing England STEP reduces synchrony dispersion and removing Martinique reduces interior occupancy. A subsequently frozen equal-depth sensitivity rarefied every admitted system to six distinct source-native time bins for 1,000 iterations. England itself remained highly synchronous, but the England-excluded synchrony-span criterion passed in 93.3% of valid equal-depth rarefactions and all four dispersion criteria passed in 89.1%, showing that the apparent uniqueness of the England high-synchrony edge is partly sampling-depth contingent. We therefore do not claim that every region of the natural plane is redundantly supported under the observed schedules, nor do we treat six-bin rarefaction as a replacement estimator. To test whether source leverage could be reduced by an additional independent dataset without open-ended source shopping, we prospectively froze a separate four-candidate robustness challenge after the primary route was fixed. All four candidates closed before coordinate extraction, so no new natural coordinates were opened. The natural result is deliberately bounded: the context space required by the theory is real and broadly occupied, while source leverage depends in part on temporal sampling design and remaining under-replicated regions identify targets for prospective study.

Finally, a separate frozen audit of 25 island research entries finds **21/25** with comparable plant responses, only **2/25** with direct partner arrival or replacement, and **0/25** with the complete outcome-independent determinant–response contract. We therefore do not claim that the synthetic determinant crossover has already been observed in nature. Instead, the natural regime analysis establishes external occupancy of the relevant context dimensions, while the audit explains why existing studies cannot yet test transport of determinant rankings across those regimes.

We believe the manuscript is suited to *Nature Ecology & Evolution* because it links a widespread inferential practice—compressing structured ecological context—to a precise transportability problem. The paper supplies an exact mathematical boundary, nonlinear violations in two model classes, a failed prediction retained as evidence, a prospectively frozen mechanism intervention, a result-blind external regime test and a concrete prospective measurement contract.

The manuscript does not equate natural Hill diversity with the synthetic effective-number parameter, infer a universal natural threshold, use natural data to retune the models, or claim a natural `C/I` crossover. Great Britain retains EuPPollNet’s pre-existing island-study classification; it was not reclassified after inspecting its location in the regime plane. Source-admission decisions, effort normalization and natural coordinates are versioned with source locks and checksums.
