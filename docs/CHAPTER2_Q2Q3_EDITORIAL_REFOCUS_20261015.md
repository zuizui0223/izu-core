## The scientific decision

**Conceptual emphasis:** Knowing which reproductive strategy a pollinator-limited environment favours does *not* tell us which heritable floral response occurs first, whether that order is required, or whether a finite diploid population can realize it. The paper's four distinct questions are retained:

- **Q1 (verified selection baseline, not first theory):** across four preregistered mating rules, A-fixed far investment declines and A-evolvable treatment compresses far/near I differences. Near and far both remain pollen-limited. Blocking *A evolution* does not eliminate selfing, which remains fixed at A=0.5. No mediation fraction.
- **Q2 (conditional temporal order):** in the focal delayed-selfing plus assurance-cost context, A crosses a sustained trait threshold first in 51/64 histories. Prior-selfing yields 30/64 in a distinct context; this is neither a universal order law nor evidence that A evolution is necessary for subsequent I decline. The fixed-capacity counterfactual remains separately defined.
- **Q3 (principal candidate for nontrivial mechanism, not a new generic stochastic method):** in two specific low-supply/matching contexts, density predicted A-first while finite ABM realized the same response label in 3/32 and 2/32 histories **even though all populations remained occupied through update 200**. The density closure is *not* the finite ABM's stochastic expectation. In another original no-I-mutation/no-immigration cohort, 703/2,304 paths lose genetic support for a specified additional I decrease before single-allele fixation; 315 of 1,238 retrospectively selected first-loss transitions had a positive expected parental frequency response for needed alleles. These datasets are different and cannot be combined into a causal mediation count, universal drift share or natural-island prevalence.
- **Q4 (separate demographic consequence):** +0.1084 [0.0654, 0.1514] in the independently frozen 32-history stress test; A evolvability remained active during future challenge. A separate fixed-pollen-background K interaction +0.0077457 is small. Neither result identifies naturally evolved assurance-first order as an extinction-rescue mediator.

### Correct closest theory

Sakai (1995), Teixido & Aizen (2019), and **Lepers et al. (2014)** are substantive antecedents. Lepers' full original article, recovered in the author's Lille thesis, **also contains individual-based stochastic simulations**, not only adaptive dynamics. Thus, method novelty cannot be 'first finite IBM'. The architecture-specific candidate difference here concerns explicit independently inherited diploid A and I, genotype accessibility and source-matched realized order. See [full-text priority correction](CHAPTER2_LEPERS_FULLTEXT_PRIORITY_CORRECTION_20261015.md) and [novelty audit](CHAPTER2_NOVELTY_ASYMMETRY_GATE_20261015.md).

### What the new 2,048-path visitor-removal test does and does not add

[Original 64-new-history/two-founder zero-visitor intervention](CHAPTER2_FAR_EARLY_INDEPENDENT_VISITOR_KNOCKOUT_RESULTS_20261015.md) uses two founder genotype backgrounds across 2,048 trajectories and shows that in these two founder backgrounds, allowing A to evolve can **relatively preserve** I even when all visitors are absent. It **does not** prove I increases absolutely, that no-pollen I selection vanishes, a general founder-independent law, or the mechanism of the old 1000-update far E−F sign. It has not been promoted into the Letter's registered primary result. The no-pollen local I log-gradient remains `−2c_I I`.
