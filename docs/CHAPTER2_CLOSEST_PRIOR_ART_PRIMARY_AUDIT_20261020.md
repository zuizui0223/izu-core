# Closest primary literature: contribution and conclusion boundaries

**Read-only literature audit, 2026-10-20.** No simulation, outcome reclassification, new significance test or claim of worldwide priority. This audit supplements the [publication decision](CHAPTER2_PUBLICATION_NOVELTY_DECISION_20261019.md) and [active Article extract](CHAPTER2_NEE_SUPPORTED_RESULTS_ARTICLE_EXTRACT_20261019.md). The conclusion below is an editorial inference from the retrieved primary literature and existing repository results.

## Main decision

The strongest defensible contribution is a **target-specific counterfactual**, not the general proposition that selfing can preserve reproduction while eroding diversity. In the registered comparison, permitting A evolution rather than fixing its expression, with initially matched expressed A, increased loss of founder-relative high-I allele support by **8.328125 percentage points** at B8, where every population survived. Under B4.5, the same policy comparison strongly altered persistence. These are separate genetic and demographic outcomes; full occupancy rules out differential extinction as the explanation of the B8 comparison, but **does not control population-size histories or identify the genetic mediator**. [Registered results](CHAPTER2_CONFIRM160_RESULTS_20261019.md)

Describe this as a result within the specified finite diploid model: **reproductive assurance affects persistence and retention of alleles needed for a specified floral target differently, depending on resource conditions**. Loss of that support is an impossibility bound under the model's closed, mutation-free conditions; retention is not a demonstrated probability of successful recovery. The retrospective three-state presentation is useful, but its across-resource contrast remains exploratory. [Publication decision and three-state source](CHAPTER2_PUBLICATION_NOVELTY_DECISION_20261019.md)

## Retrieval depth and competing explanations

### Acoca-Pidolle (2025): selection-based irreversibility

**Depth:** full nine-page preprint retrieved directly from EcoEvoRxiv and extracted in memory; model and results, pp. 2–6, inspected. The author ORCID still lists the item as a preprint. A live Crossref exact-title query returned DOI `10.32942/X2R93P` as `posted-content`, without a publication relation. No matching journal version was located; this is not proof that none exists. [Primary PDF](https://ecoevorxiv.org/repository/object/10067/download/18650/), [author record](https://orcid.org/0009-0002-8456-5588), [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.32942/X2R93P)

The model combines floral attractiveness and herkogamy in **one bounded quantitative trait**, with delayed selfing, fixed trait standard deviation for threshold analysis, and a mixture of absolute and relative visitor choice. Sufficiently hard selection can prevent return towards outcrossing even after maximal pollinator restoration. This is a selection-landscape mechanism, not explicit tracking of lost alleles. [Primary PDF, pp. 2–6](https://ecoevorxiv.org/repository/object/10067/download/18650/)

**Difference:** izu-core separates inherited A and I and tests A-policy effects on finite-population target support. It does not supersede this preprint's restoration test, establish its tipping point, or demonstrate mutation-enabled permanent irreversibility. Avoid leading with “irreversible evolution after pollinator decline.”

### Lepers, Dufay & Billiard (2014): selfing–pollinator demography

**Depth:** published article reproduced in the author's institutional thesis; PDF pp. 58 and 60–65 inspected, especially article pp. 3–7. This goes beyond the abstract. [Institutional primary copy](https://pepite-depot.univ-lille.fr/LIBRE/EDSMRE/2015/50376-2015-Lepers.pdf), [article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/evo.12533)

The model couples plant and pollinator demography to prior selfing. Pollination costs and rewards decrease with selfing through the imposed function F[s]. It uses adaptive dynamics **and individual-based simulations**, permits different mutants to coexist, and examines fixed or selfing-dependent inbreeding depression. Its authors explicitly state that the latter is not an explicit genetic model. Outcomes include selfing, outcrossing, mixed mating and evolutionary suicide. [Article pp. 3–7 in institutional copy](https://pepite-depot.univ-lille.fr/LIBRE/EDSMRE/2015/50376-2015-Lepers.pdf)

**Difference:** independently inherited investment and assurance allow their relation to be tested rather than imposed through F[s]. The targeted allele-support and matched-expression A-policy endpoints are more specific. Neither stochasticity nor coupling floral investment, reproduction and demography is new here. Lepers also models reciprocal pollinator demography, so an externally imposed visitor-history experiment is not uniformly a more complete ecological model.

### Busch et al. (2022): genetic erosion in persisting populations

**Depth:** primary abstract and indexed publisher Methods/Figure 1 and discussion passages retrieved; direct whole-page access was blocked. Do not describe this as a complete full-text or supplement audit. [Publisher article](https://academic.oup.com/evolut/article/76/9/2130/6966265), [primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35852008/), [PMC primary copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9543508/)

Four Mimulus populations experienced bee/no-bee treatments, two replicates each, for nine generations. Seed input was adjusted to yield approximately 800 adults per population. No-bee populations adapted towards more autonomous seed production and lost substantial genomic polymorphism; the paper explicitly considers genetic draft rather than demographic contraction alone. [Publisher Methods](https://academic.oup.com/evolut/article/76/9/2130/6966265)

**Difference:** “diversity loss despite population persistence” is already established. izu-core adds an intervention on A evolvability, a specified floral target's allele support, and resource-dependent demographic outcomes. It does not establish that its support losses arose through the same draft mechanism, nor that they prevented an observed recovery. “Survival-independent” must mean **not explained by differential extinction in B8**, not independence from all demography.

### Hartfield & Glémin (2024): adaptation under high selfing

**Depth:** publisher full text, including Methods and discussion, retrieved. Analytical models accompany multilocus stochastic SLiM simulations; no simulation code was rerun. [Full primary article](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1011312)

Their simulations use explicit chromosomes, ongoing mutation, prescribed selfing rates and an altered quantitative-trait optimum. High selfing can produce higher long-term fitness despite selection interference. Pleiotropy changes the genetic architecture of adaptation and the prevalence of hitchhikers. The authors discuss demographic interactions as an outstanding issue. [Methods and discussion](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1011312)

**Difference:** izu-core links evolving reproductive assurance, visitor-mediated reproduction and variable population persistence to a target-specific allele boundary. Its mutation-free support-loss result is not a contradiction of mutation-enabled polygenic adaptation, and its three independently segregating loci cannot substantiate a chromosome-scale hitchhiking explanation. Avoid “selfing destroys evolvability” or an inevitable general trade-off; the current evidence concerns a particular target under specified inheritance.

### Tusuubira & Kelly (2024): floral shrinkage need not be adaptive syndrome evolution

**Depth:** full eleven-page publisher PDF retrieved; Results and Discussion on p. 7 inspected together with the primary abstract. [Primary PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2024.1378568/pdf), [article](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2024.1378568)

Twenty Mimulus populations evolved for ten generations under outcrossing, mixed mating or selfing. An additional outbred generation separated homozygosity effects from inherited differences. Flower-size differences largely disappeared after outbreeding, implicating inbreeding depression; improved autogamy was instead obscured by depression and became evident after it was removed. [Primary Results/Discussion, p. 7](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2024.1378568/pdf)

**Difference:** this supplies a strong empirical reason to distinguish flower phenotype, adaptive assurance and their genetic basis. izu-core I is an allocation trait, not a direct measurement of corolla size, and the model does not establish the mechanism behind these empirical floral changes. Cite this as a competing explanation and a boundary on natural-system extrapolation, not as confirmation of the model's I response.

### Peterson & Kay (2015): persistence and adaptation after colonization

**Depth:** publisher abstract and indexed Introduction/Discussion passages retrieved, cross-checked against the institutional abstract. Complete Methods and supplements were not audited. [Primary publisher record](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/679107), [institutional record](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/6444s301)

Individual-based simulations already jointly examined colonization, extinction, quantitative-trait adaptation, inbreeding depression and genetic load. Plastic shifts towards mixed mating could reduce extinction, limit maladaptive gene flow and temporarily increase trait variance; obligate selfing could reduce adaptive potential. The paper explicitly asks when selfing promotes or inhibits niche evolution. [Primary abstract and discussion](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/679107)

**Difference:** neither “selfing enables colonization” nor “selfing has opposing demographic and adaptive effects” is a new organizing principle. izu-core's registered result concerns an evolving-versus-fixed assurance policy and a target-specific floral genetic endpoint after a prescribed visitor history. Its bounded secondary-expansion experiment remains a separate exploratory test, not a stronger demonstration of niche evolution than this prior study. Do not frame secondary expansion as a newly discovered connection between assurance and adaptation.

## Story closure with existing evidence

1. **Opening question:** Does reproductive buffering preserve the inherited options needed for a later floral response? Define the narrower target-support endpoint immediately; do not promise measurement of general adaptive capacity.
2. **Mechanistic setup:** Independently inherited A and I show why temporal precedence and their association need not imply one obligatory sequence. Fixed A still allows selfing.
3. **Decisive answer:** Lead the Results/abstract with the registered B8 target-support effect and the separate resource-dependent survival result. The crossed design improves precision; the number of paths is not the novelty.
4. **Bounded extension:** Saved variation and transfer results show that target support, segregating variation, actual response and persistence are distinct observations. They do not complete a causal mediation chain.
5. **Ending:** State the measured conditional dissociation positively. Keep the unresolved mediator, mutation/immigration-enabled responses and natural island assembly as explicit limits. A-specific hitchhiking and successful adaptation after secondary expansion remain exploratory.

**Recommended contribution sentence:** “By intervening on the evolvability of reproductive assurance while matching its initial expression, we quantify its effects on population persistence and on retention of alleles compatible with a specified floral target; these outcomes need not move together across resource conditions.”

This closes a model-based paper from existing evidence. It does not require adding experiments merely to rescue a broader premise. Do not claim the first selfing–floral model, the first genetic erosion in persisting populations, a general irreversible trap, or an exhaustive literature-priority determination.
