## Result

All2,304 pairs/all36 arm-by-cell rows were read. Original no-I-mutation histories had1,238 ever-deficits and no reopening; renewal had1,214 ever-deficits,914 histories with at least one reopening and4,868 reopening transitions. All4,608 trajectories remain occupied at update200. A standing-support deficit means no currently present I homolog can by itself reach the original founder-relative0.05 target; under future mutation this is not permanent evolutionary impossibility.

Among the914 renewal histories with reopened support: **758 did not complete the original20-census mean-investment event by update200;64 had already completed it before their first deficit;92 completed it after their first reopening.** Reopening is not necessarily a durable allele, an increase in its frequency, a sufficient selective incentive or enough time to cross the population-mean criterion. The92 are temporal follow-up counts, not a causal rescue fraction.

Paired event table:968 present in both arms,278 renewal-only,170 original-only,888 neither. The previously reported1,138→1,246 incidence increase hides both gains and losses. Changing the mutation policy also shifts downstream random streams and genetic selection histories; these paired counts do not isolate a variance-only intervention and receive no new p-value or interval.
