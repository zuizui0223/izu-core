## Why this is separate from the 25 and 36

The frozen 25-entry audit asks whether existing studies jointly measure the coordinates needed for formal external mechanism identification. Its denominator remains frozen.

The 36-entry confrontation is the currently admitted descriptive evidence layer. It remains unchanged until an explicit promotion step decides that a newly source-gated research entry improves the manuscript after geographic de-duplication.

The 111-target universe asks a different question: **which named island geographies have actually been searched, what source quality was recovered, and where are the remaining source gaps?**

Search completion is therefore not evidence admission, and evidence admission is not formal external prediction.

## Source-native overlap correction

Ciarle et al. 2025 (`10.1093/aob/mcaf005`) explicitly sampled ten Southwest Pacific archipelagos:

- Lord Howe;
- Norfolk;
- Kermadec;
- Three Kings;
- Chatham;
- Snares;
- Antipodes;
- Auckland;
- Campbell;
- Macquarie.

Because the Ciarle study is already one of the current 36 research entries, these archipelagos are already represented geographically by that research layer even if a separate process-oriented source is later added. Three Kings was absent from the seed 110-target frame and was therefore added as the 111th effective search target.

This correction also changed an earlier interpretation: **Lord Howe is not a new geographic group relative to the current 36**. First-wave confirmed new higher-level geography is therefore Aegean, Ryukyu and Yakushima.

## Umbrella sources

The programme uses global or multi-archipelago sources as indexes, then returns to original sources before making local claims.

### Global quantitative pollination networks

Traveset et al. 2016 (`10.1111/geb.12362`) compiled 52 quantitative plant-pollinator networks, including continental- and oceanic-island networks. Trøjelsgaard & Olesen 2013 (`10.1111/j.1466-8238.2012.00777.x`) compiled 54 community-wide networks including multiple island sites. The Web of Life database remains a recovery route. None of these sources is expanded into pseudo-independent validation events.

### Southern Ocean

Lord 2015 (`10.1093/aobpla/plv095`) covers 11 Southern Ocean island groups and 321 flowering plant species. It supplies broad floral-trait and breeding-system coverage, not a matched historical community-transition contract.

### Southwest Pacific morphology

Ciarle et al. 2025 supplies 129 phylogenetically independent island-mainland colonisation events across the ten source-native archipelagos listed above. It strengthens geographic and response-shape coverage but does not supply the matched interaction-transition chain required for a full Chapter 2 contract.

## Second-wave search gate

Nineteen high-priority targets were explicitly searched.

Direct or strong evidence was recovered for:

- **Mayotte / Comoros** — *Vanilla humblotii* reproductive biology; pollinator-dependent, self-compatible, very low natural fruit set despite bee and sunbird visits;
- **Socotra** — endemic gecko pollen carriage across *Dracaena cinnabari* populations;
- **Samoa** — field observations, experiments and pollen evidence for flying-fox and starling pollination of *Freycinetia reineckei*;
- **Cook Islands** — *Ficus prolixa* retained its fig-wasp pollinators despite other mutualist extinctions, with habitat loss providing a stronger alternative explanation;
- **Kermadec** — historical primary observations of pollen-bearing tui and lacewing flower visitation.

Other targets produced trait-only, grey-literature, insufficient or inconclusive results. All 19 still fail the full Chapter 2 contract.

Second-wave geographic de-duplication confirmed **Comoros/Mayotte** and **Socotra** as new higher-level geography candidates, treated **Kermadec** as existing current-36 geography, and held **Samoa** and **Cook Islands** until the source-native geography of the unresolved Pacific multi-system morphology layer is recovered.

## Third-wave completion of the remaining search frame

The third wave explicitly searched the **42 targets that remained never-reviewed/prior-uncovered after the first two waves**. The set exactly closes the effective 111-target frame.

Third-wave outcomes:

- **23** `initial_search_inconclusive`;
- **5** crop/germplasm reproductive-strategy sources that are not promoted as natural-island mechanism evidence;
- **2** direct pollination-network sources;
- the remaining 12 rows each recovered one of several specific evidence types, including direct breeding systems, direct brood-pollination networks, direct pollination plus breeding, regional mutualisms, trait compilations, authoritative breeding evidence or biogeography-only evidence.

Seven rows were strong enough to enter an explicit geographic de-duplication review:

2. Sicily;
3. Madagascar;

### Third-wave direct evidence highlights

- **Sardinia** — direct wild-bee/plant interaction networks across Sardinian/Asinara sites (`10.1002/ece3.73394`).
- **Sicily** — direct *Bombus*–plant bipartite networks (`10.1007/s13592-025-01153-4`).
- **Madagascar** — direct lemur pollination of *Ravenala madagascariensis* plus a native-forest flower-visitation web (`10.1002/j.1537-2197.1994.tb15483.x`; `10.26786/1920-7603(2010)1`).
- **Andaman and Nicobar Islands** — direct reproductive-biology/cross-pollination study lead for endemic *Bentinckia nicobarica*; retained on hold until the primary article identity/DOI is locked.
- **Sri Lanka** — detailed pollination ecology and breeding system of endemic *Xylopia championii* (`10.1086/521689`).
- **Taiwan / Lanyu** — hand-pollination and functional distyly in *Psychotria cephalophora* (`10.1186/s40529-015-0091-9`).
- **Society Islands** — quantitative *Glochidion–Epicephala* brood-pollination networks on Tahiti, Moorea and Huahine (`10.1111/1365-2656.12841`; Dryad `10.5061/dryad.1224pr2`).

## What remains after first-pass completion

The next phase is **source strengthening, not geographic discovery by convenience**.

Priority tasks are:

1. improve the 49 targets whose first-pass source is incomplete or inconclusive;
2. recover exact source-native geography for the seven systems in Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson 2020 to resolve Samoa/Cook overlap;
3. lock the primary source for Andaman/Nicobar;
4. recover primary mechanism sources behind authoritative/grey leads such as Norfolk and Tasmania;
5. only after source strengthening, perform a separate manuscript-value promotion review for new evidence entries.

If source-native datasets expose additional named island systems not in the 111-target frame, those systems are added transparently rather than hidden by a fixed denominator.

## Per-target evidence gate

Every target is reviewed against the same fields:

1. source functional state;
2. partner loss;
3. partner arrival/replacement;
4. realized community or functional shift;
5. local filtering;
6. reproductive assurance / breeding-system state;
7. plant response outcome;
8. source accessibility and directness;
9. geographic overlap with existing Chapter 2 entries;
10. full source-state → transition → realization → response contract.

A source can be useful for global confrontation while still failing the full contract. Full-contract failure is never hidden.

## Promotion rule

No target enters the current 36-entry descriptive confrontation merely because a source was found. Promotion requires:

- primary or authoritative source verification;
- an explicit evidence role;
- geographic overlap/de-duplication review;
- a recorded full-contract pass/fail state;
- a separate manuscript-value promotion decision;
- preservation of the frozen 25-entry identifiability denominator.

## Claim boundary

Do not describe the 111-target search universe as 111 independent archipelagos, a complete census of every island on Earth, or 111 empirical tests of the synthetic model.

The defensible statement is:

> **The current 111-target systematic search frame has completed one documented search/coverage pass. All targets have a recorded coverage state, but 49 still require stronger or broader sources. The frozen 25-entry identifiability audit and current 36-entry descriptive confrontation remain unchanged.**
