# Selection conditions, stochastic realization and future accessibility

This is a source-model interpretation of existing evidence, not a new registered
experiment or a revision of the running 160 x 160 design.

## Two local selection conditions

`scripts/model3_island/selection.py` defines separate local investment and
assurance gradients. At a specified interior resident state, the investment
cost threshold determines the sign of the investment gradient, while the
assurance cost threshold determines the sign of the assurance gradient.
These are conditional selection boundaries, not two universal ecological
thresholds with an invariant chronological order. They do not by themselves
establish a fixed allocation tradeoff or instantaneous evolutionary decisions.

The existing `scripts/audit_model3_joint_thresholds.py:run_audit` was rechecked
on 2026-10-19: all 900 local comparisons passed, with maximum absolute
analytic/numerical gradient discrepancy 1.2486083278417937e-09. This verifies
local algebra against the declared numerical comparator, not ecological
generality or realized order in finite populations.

Cost-free delayed selfing without pollen discount can provide a positive
assurance gradient whenever pollen failure remains and inbreeding depression
is below one. Costs, prior selfing and discounting alter that condition. The
running confirmation uses costly assurance and fixed inbreeding depression;
the cost-free statement must not be substituted for its treatment mechanism.

## Individual return and collective output

Individual return includes maternal outcross, paternal outcross and viable
selfed contributions. It cannot be represented by pollen receipt alone.
The existing [finite focal versus collective seed audit](CHAPTER2_BETA_GAMMA_SEED_ENGINEERING_RESULTS_20261010.md)
found 14 negative-individual/positive-collective investment gradients among
384 synthetic static cells, with 362 aligned, zero reverse and eight
inconclusive cells. All six discordances in the compatible K48/B48 subset
persisted under its analytic rare-mutant comparison.

This is model-conditional evidence that selection to reduce investment can
coexist with a positive response of total viable seeds to a collective
investment increase. It does not identify a population optimum, persistence
loss, actual evolutionary spread, or evolutionary suicide. In the documented
example both focal outcross components are positive; a negative selfed
component outweighs them. Therefore "benefits leak to others" alone is not a
complete demonstrated mechanism. These post-outcome static grids contain
zero independently sampled ecological histories.

## Deterministic conditions and probabilistic realization

| Quantity | Deterministic conditional statement | Finite-population uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Selection | Sign of a local expected reproductive gradient | Whether a favorable allele is present and transmitted |
| Order | A gradient can favor A increase and I decrease at the same state | Which change occurs first and how quickly |
| Accessibility | Without mutation or immigration, the remaining allele bounds constrain attainable trait means | When requisite alleles disappear, possibly before extinction |
| Readaptation | A destination environment defines new reproductive returns | Establishment and evolutionary response after transfer |

A genotype-density trajectory is not generally the expectation of the finite
stochastic process. Neither disagreement with that trajectory nor a single
finite realization identifies a numerical fraction of evolution caused by
drift. Recombination can reorganize retained alleles but cannot escape their
trait bounds in this additive model without replenishment.

### Which outcomes repeat more consistently than expected?

The biological question is comparative predictability, rather than assigning
assurance to determinism and floral investment to chance in advance. Separate
repeatability of direction, order, attained phenotype and demographic/genetic
fate. Consistent decline of investment does not imply a consistent A-first
sequence, identical final investment, or identical future accessibility.

Two levels of repetition must be distinguished: repeated random realizations
from the same founder and visitor context, and repeated outcomes across new
founders and visitor contexts. The 160 x 160 design primarily addresses the
second level. Each crossed cell has one paired stochastic realization, so a
founder-by-visitor interaction cannot be separated from within-context
stochastic variability using those endpoints alone. Residual variation must
not be relabeled as measured drift. A narrow interval on an average policy
effect does not imply an individual population's fate is predictable.

An exploratory comparison of predictability must use comparable endpoints and
time horizons, retain extinction among original starts, and distinguish
out-of-context prediction from descriptive fit to the same cells. No new
confirmatory claim about which trait is "more deterministic" follows from the
registered G/S tests. A claim that an outcome is *more* repeatable than expected
also needs an explicit comparator; the phrase cannot substitute for one.

## What the running confirmation can resolve

The fixed 160 founder x 160 visitor design estimates assurance-policy effects
on genetic closure before extinction at B8 and a survival policy-by-budget
interaction across B8/B4.5. It uses crossed-source inference, not 25,600 iid
island replicates. Its primary endpoints do not establish mediation between
closure and survival. Epsilon sensitivity remains descriptive.

The experiment contains no environmental-transfer evolutionary futures.
Readaptation of assurance-bearing populations after entering a different
pollination environment remains a distinct prospective question. Neither
evolving deleterious load, obligate outcrossing nor island-flora assembly is
represented by the current experiment. A fixed assurance policy still permits
selfing. These boundaries remain even if both primary tests support their
registered directions.
