## Bibliography and claim scope

### Bodbyl Roels & Kelly (2011)

Bodbyl Roels, S. A., & Kelly, J. K. (2011). Rapid evolution caused by pollinator loss in *Mimulus guttatus*. *Evolution*, **65**(9), 2541–2552. [DOI: 10.1111/j.1558-5646.2011.01326.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.2011.01326.x).

**Supported:** Five-generation bee/no-pollinator experimental evolution showed improved autonomous reproduction following early fitness loss. The authors explicitly interpret the pattern as sequential syndrome evolution: selfing-efficiency traits first, other floral characters later. This establishes prior publication of the sequence interpretation. [Publisher abstract](https://academic.oup.com/evolut/article-abstract/65/9/2541/6854748); [PMC Summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5958604/).

**Unsupported extension:** A universal measured lag between selfing and flower-size decline; proof that selfing evolution is necessary for attraction reduction; a visitor-replenishment or geographic-isolation experiment. The Summary's later-flower-change statement is the authors' interpretation, not evidence here of a subsequently observed reduction. Preserve the publisher's 2011 author form, “Bodbyl Roels”; the later paper uses “Bodbyl-Roels.”

### Sicard et al. (2011)

Sicard, A., Stacey, N., Hermann, K., Dessoly, J., Neuffer, B., Bäurle, I., & Lenhard, M. (2011). Genetics, evolution, and adaptive significance of the selfing syndrome in the genus *Capsella*. *The Plant Cell*, **23**(9), 3156–3171. [DOI: 10.1105/tpc.111.088237](https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.111.088237). Author list checked against [publisher-deposited metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1105/tpc.111.088237).

**Supported:** Genetic and functional comparisons support adaptation for efficient self-pollination, especially reduced opening. The discussion predicts self-incompatibility breakdown before flower-size reduction under the reproductive-assurance hypothesis, while expressly leaving actual historical order unresolved. [Primary discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3203431/); [publisher abstract](https://academic.oup.com/plcell/article-abstract/23/9/3156/6097755).

**Unsupported extension:** Directly observed ancestral chronology, a universal order, or equivalence of self-incompatibility loss to a quantitative increase in Model 3 capacity. Flower-opening association is stronger evidence here than asserting every reduced floral dimension improves selfing.

**Metadata conflict:** Publisher and PMC records display acceptance in September 2010 despite receipt in June 2011. This apparent history-field error does not change the verified 2011 publication citation; do not silently use it as a biological or publication chronology.

### Sakai (1995)

Sakai, S. (1995). Evolutionarily stable selfing rates of hermaphroditic plants in competing and delayed selfing modes with allocation to attractive structures. *Evolution*, **49**(3), 557–564. [Publisher abstract and DOI: 10.1111/j.1558-5646.1995.tb02287.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/J.1558-5646.1995.TB02287.X).

**Supported at abstract level:** Resource allocation to attraction, competing/delayed selfing and inbreeding depression are jointly modeled. Stability of intermediate selfing depends on linear versus nonlinear constraints on flower number and size. Inbreeding depression changes optimal attraction allocation and selfing. Consequently, those conceptual ingredients alone are not new to Model 3.

**Unsupported extension:** A verified match to Model 3 equations, a temporal selfing-first prediction, isolation-dependent arrival dynamics, or a proof about finite-population trajectories. An evolutionarily stable allocation is not a measured sequence of threshold crossings. The paywalled PDF and all mathematical assumptions remain unverified in this check.

### Busch et al. (2022)

Busch, J. W., Bodbyl-Roels, S., Tusuubira, S., & Kelly, J. K. (2022). Pollinator loss causes rapid adaptive evolution of selfing and dramatically reduces genome-wide genetic variability. *Evolution*, **76**(9), 2130–2144. [DOI: 10.1111/evo.14572](https://doi.org/10.1111/evo.14572); pagination verified in the [publisher issue](https://academic.oup.com/evolut/issue/76/9).

**Supported:** This continues the earlier experiment for four additional generations, reaching nine generations, and examines genomic variation. Two replicate populations per treatment retained substantial census sizes. The authors combine genomic observations with individual-based simulations; a simple reduction in effective population size does not explain the genome-wide pattern, and selection interacting with linkage and inbreeding is implicated. [Primary full-text PDF, abstract and Methods, PDF pages 1–5](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10353587).

**Unsupported extension:** Treating genetic-variation loss as pure small-population drift, treating this as an independent nine-generation replication of the 2011 experiment, or citing it as proof of a floral-reduction chronology. It already provides precedent for stochastic/genetic consequences and individual-based modeling of pollinator loss. Model 3's fixed inbreeding depression does not by itself reproduce evolving genetic load.

## Consequences for the current manuscript

The active Discussion's rejection of a novel general selfing-first hypothesis is justified. Its phrase “reproductive assurance before floral reduction” should, when precision matters, be read as the specific Capsella hypothesis about self-incompatibility breakdown; it does not describe measured historical timing. The novelty audit's cautious separation of sequence interpretation from observed chronology is supported.

The current model's defensible comparison remains the linked separation of temporal threshold order, necessity of capacity evolution, and additional isolation divergence. That is a description of this manuscript's design, not certification that no earlier paper combined those ideas. Fixed nonzero capacity must remain distinct from no realized selfing. None of these four references validates Model 3's numerical results, island calibration, or unresolved positive-mutation comparison.

No contrary primary evidence was found within this narrow check that requires reversing the existing novelty audit. Remaining limits are partial access for the two 2011 papers, abstract-only verification for Sakai, and the absence of an exhaustive prior-art search. No “first,” universal selfing-first, or necessary-mediation claim is warranted.
