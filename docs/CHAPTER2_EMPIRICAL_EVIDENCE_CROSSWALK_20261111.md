# Empirical evidence for four steps in the island reproductive-assurance model

**11 November 2026.** This scoping search compared primary empirical studies against the [active manuscript](CHAPTER2_NEE_SUPPORTED_RESULTS_ARTICLE_EXTRACT_20261019.md) and its [source-ranked model findings](CHAPTER2_R1_R5_SOURCE_RECOVERY_AUDIT_20261022.md). Study designs, traits and fitness endpoints determine how far each comparison transfers. The search covered publisher and indexed primary articles returned by combinations of *pollinator loss, delayed selfing, reproductive assurance, emasculation, common garden, experimental evolution, paternity, display size, realized heritability, floral selection response, allele loss, plant extinction, founding event, island, Izu,* and the taxa below. We followed source papers identified in the [earlier mechanism review](CHAPTER2_ASSURANCE_ORDER_FLORAL_PRIOR_ART_20261111.md), sought opposite outcomes, and distinguished temporal data from among-population contrasts. Coverage is broad and purposive. It provides no estimate of publication frequency, no exhaustive database census and no pooled effect size.

## 1. What does early assurance secure?

| Primary study and design | Observation | Correspondence to the simulation |
|---|---|---|
| *Collinsia verna*, multiyear field study of mating and seed production under variable pollinator service. [Kalisz, Vogler & Hanley (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature02776) | Autonomous selfing increased seed production per flower when visits were infrequent while the species retained mixed mating. | Direct evidence for filling a reproductive shortfall. Timing of selfing in a flower and the time at which inherited assurance first increases across generations are different biological clocks. |
| *Aquilegia canadensis*, ten populations over two years with experimental and genetic estimates of reproductive costs. [Herlihy & Eckert (2002)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11907577/) | Seed production gained through selfing was outweighed by seed discounting and inbreeding depression under the studied conditions. | A positive maternal seed increment can coexist with lower net fitness. Count viable and recruited offspring before extending a seed result to demography. |
| *Clarkia xantiana*, experimental populations varying in size and presence of pollinator-sharing congeners. [Moeller & Geber (2005)](https://moellergroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/moellergeber2005.pdf) | Reduced herkogamy and protandry were favoured through female fitness particularly in small populations isolated from congeners. | Evidence of context-dependent selection on traits promoting selfing. The Model 3 intervention assigns the order of expression; the natural study measured selection on floral traits. |
| *Mimulus guttatus*, replicated populations exposed to contrasting pollinator treatments. [Bodbyl Roels & Kelly (2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21884055/) | Pollinator loss favoured changes in autonomous seed production and floral traits, with differences in the strength and timing of responses. | A multigeneration analogue for the temporal question. Populations were assigned pollination treatments, rather than assigned to acquire high assurance before or after changing attraction. |

**Gap.** None of these comparisons independently assigns assurance-first versus investment-first expression at matched founding states and estimates its subsequent occupancy effect. The model's +1.21-point exploratory average and registered environment-by-schedule interaction describe distinct simulation endpoints.

## 2. What does an additional visit contribute once assurance is available?

| Primary study and design | Observation | Correspondence to the simulation |
|---|---|---|
| *Tuberaria guttata*, intact, emasculated and supplemental outcross treatments in three natural populations. [Teixido & Aizen (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcz014) | Delayed selfing most strongly offset maternal selection for larger corollas at the least-visited site. Assurance produced a smaller average seed gain at the most-visited site. | Direct evidence for maternal compensation. Evolution of inherited allocation on the model's higher-service side and narrowing between island environments remain predictions. |
| *Mimulus ringens*, experimental floral displays with offspring paternity. [Karron & Mitchell (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcr193) | Outcross siring declined per flower as more flowers were displayed. Whole-plant outcross siring showed no detectable increase. | The display manipulation measures male reproductive success through outcross offspring. Return per flower and return per individual are different endpoints. |
| *Ipomoea purpurea*, manipulated display and genetic markers in arrays. [Lau et al. (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1086/588080) | Larger displays increased selfing, and selection through male function favoured smaller displays. | A direct counterexample to assuming that siring benefits necessarily maintain conspicuous displays. This experiment manipulated simultaneous flower number, not inherited investment per se. |
| Reduced visitation in *Lobelia siphilitica* with supplemental pollination controls; contrasting visitor treatments across generations in *Brassica rapa*. [Brown & Caruso (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.10706); [Gervasi & Schiestl (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms14691) | The *Lobelia* treatment produced maternal selection for taller inflorescences and more vibrant petals. *Brassica* lines differentiated in floral traits across pollinator treatments. | Visitor scarcity can favour greater attraction. Visitor identity can structure realized evolution. A selection gradient in one generation and a response over generations should be reported separately. |

**Gap.** The cross-environment policy effect would require a field comparison separating variation in pollination service from inherited assurance, floral allocation, flower number and flower size. A maternal seed assay and outcross paternity for the same individuals can distinguish the two components of return. The within-flower interaction supplies a hypothesis about a reproductive route, not the fraction of the policy contrast caused by that route.

## 3. When does selection become an inherited floral response?

| Primary study and design | Observation | Correspondence to the simulation |
|---|---|---|
| Primarily selfing Joy Road *M. guttatus* population, five generations of artificial selection on stigma–anther distance and population sequencing. [Selection experiment (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2024.0586) | Floral response was weak, with realized heritability estimates of 0.020–0.028. | Measures realized response in an extant population with a selfing history. Herkogamy is a mating-system trait; a weak response does not locate a missing investment allele or prove that selfing caused its loss. |
| *Phlox drummondii*, bidirectional artificial selection on corolla diameter over three generations from a wild population. [Lendvai & Levin (2003)](https://www.nature.com/articles/6800249) | Diameter responded rapidly in both directions, with estimated realized heritability 0.83. | Demonstrates genuine capacity to change when the starting genetic variance and imposed selection permit it. Species, trait and selection protocol differ from the monkeyflower experiment. Their estimates cannot be treated as a selfing effect size. |
| Wild *M. guttatus*, three field seasons comparing genotypes differing in flower size. [Mojica & Kelly (2010)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20462906/) | Genotypes producing larger flowers had lower survival to flowering. Selecting the sample at flowering omitted an important component of fitness. | Observing only reproducing plants can alter inference about floral selection. This is viability selection, a distinct explanation from omission of a favoured allele during finite offspring sampling. |
| Four Paris-region *Viola arvensis* populations, ancestral–descendant common gardens and pollinator behaviour. [Acoca-Pidolle et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.19422); [follow-up on pollen and ovules](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40898833/) | Corollas, nectar and bumblebee attraction changed in parallel with increased realized selfing. The follow-up found no comparable rapid change in pollen or ovule production. | The same population series exhibited recurrent change in some floral components and little change in others. Treat as complementary readouts rather than two independent replications. Historical pollinator abundance and the relative evolutionary order of traits were not assigned. |
| Twenty *M. guttatus* populations under imposed mating treatments over ten generations. [Tusuubira & Kelly (2024)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2024.1378568) | The gain in autogamy included adaptation; much of the reduction in flower size reflected inbreeding depression. | The same visible decline can have different causes. Model investment is heritable resource allocation. Direct correspondence to corolla measurements requires testing the genotype and inbreeding effects. |

**Gap.** None of these studies estimated a lower-investment allele's next-generation expectation from parental contributions and then counted its complete loss at that offspring census. The model's 315/1,238 is conditional on observed first losses. These empirical studies do not identify a drift fraction for the model or for island floral phenotypes.

## 4. Which populations and variants survive?

| Primary study and design | Observation | Correspondence to the simulation |
|---|---|---|
| *Clarkia concinna*, field pollination in isolated patches and observations of patch extinction. [Groom (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1086/286135) | Small isolated patches suffered failures of effective pollination; chronically poor reproduction and elevated extinction were observed in such patches. | Connects pollination to population fate beyond seed-set assays. The study did not assign evolving versus fixed assurance or score the alleles supporting a floral target at extinction. |
| Genome sequencing of *M. guttatus* lines evolved with and without bees. [Busch et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1111/evo.14572) | The no-bee populations evolved more selfing and lost genome-wide nucleotide diversity over nine generations. | Empirical link between pollinator loss, mating and genomic consequences. This work revisits the pollinator-loss experiment reported by Bodbyl Roels & Kelly; it must not double the independent evidence count. Genome-wide diversity is different from accessibility of one directional investment target. |
| Natural *Impatiens capensis* populations, demographic reconstruction and genomic comparisons after founding. [Schoen & Toczydlowski (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.70880) | The inferred severity of founding bottlenecks and duration of subsequent recovery were associated with genomic signatures and diversity in extant populations. | Shows that founding and recovery history matter for standing variation. Extant populations do not supply the denominator of all colonizing populations, including failures. |
| Jamaican and Brazilian *Eichhornia paniculata*, common glasshouse and regional genetic comparisons. [Barrett (1985)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1095-8312.1985.tb00385.x); [continental–island variation study (1987)](https://www.nature.com/articles/hdy198791) | Jamaican samples had smaller, less showy self-pollinating flowers; sampled populations differed in marker variation, associated with outcrossing rate. | An island analogue in which the classical syndrome occurs. Colonization, drift and mating-system effects remain entangled. The two studies concern the same species and regions; marker variation is not floral-target accessibility. |

**Gap.** A field test analogous to the registered comparison would follow populations from establishment, count failures, measure mating and resource regime, and assay segregating variants affecting a defined floral response. None of the sources located tests a survival interaction and a specified founder-relative high-investment allele-support contrast under matched initial expressed assurance. The model's joint sign change across budgets is exploratory and is not a field estimate.

## Geographic scope and alternative readings

In the Izu archipelago the *Campanula* field record directly relates visitors to reproduction. Observations found differing dominant bees across Honshu, Oshima and more distant islands, together with smaller island flowers. Breeding-system descriptions distinguish self-compatible populations that mostly outcross from populations that predominantly inbreed. This archipelago supplies a field reason to measure visitor identity, compatibility and realized mating separately. Historical evolution of Model 3 traits, the effect of assurance on an investment gap, and plant movement back to the mainland have not been demonstrated by those comparisons. [Inoue & Amano (1986)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-1984.1986.tb00018.x); [Inoue (1990)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-1984.1990.tb00192.x)

In introduced *Nicotiana glauca*, island populations tended toward greater self-pollination capacity, especially on the more recently colonized island. Hummingbird visitation was neither less frequent nor more variable there, and the field comparison detected no ongoing selection favouring selfing on the islands. Present capacity alone therefore cannot establish how it arose. [Schueller (2004)](https://doi.org/10.3732/ajb.91.5.672)

Broader comparisons delimit how far this example travels. A Caribbean Gesneriaceae study of 28 insular and 26 mainland species found no island–mainland difference in measured autofertility, whereas global comparisons find self-compatibility overrepresented in some island floras. The two results assess different endpoints. In Pacific comparisons of island–mainland relatives, flower size and total display also had different trajectories. Thus a replicated selfing *capacity* in the model, a species' compatibility status, autonomous fruit production, and a shift in measured corolla size require separate field tests. [Caribbean breeding and pollination comparison](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2745.12457); [Grossenbacher et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.14534); [Zell et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.20234); [Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1086/709018); [Ciarle et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcaf005)

## Use in the Article

Field and experimental evidence supports reproductive insurance and trait-dependent pollen returns. Observations and manipulations also show both marked and weak floral responses, and associations between reproductive history and surviving genetic variation. The manuscript's contribution is to compare assigned expression order and assurance policies, inspect allele transmission in finite offspring, and retain the denominator of starting populations for the joint persistence endpoint. Published empirical work supports parts of that interpretation; it does not validate the model's numerical effects, establish a single causal sequence or establish that species diversity is maintained on islands.
