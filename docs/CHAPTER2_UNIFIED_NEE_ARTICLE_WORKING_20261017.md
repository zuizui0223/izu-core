## Abstract — provisional, existing evidence only; NOT for submission

What makes evolution after island colonization predictable, and what options remain for later change? We used a finite diploid model to connect founding genetic state, reproductive assurance, floral investment and population persistence under pollinator limitation. Investment declined even when assurance expression was fixed. Allowing assurance evolution nevertheless modified environmental investment contrasts. Separate exploratory archives showed history-dependent floral responses and more repeatable assurance endpoints, without separating selection from fixation as causes. In a preregistered comparison crossing 160 founders with 160 visitor histories and matching initial expressed assurance, the policy-by-resource survival interaction was 92.71 percentage points (adjusted 97.5% interval 92.05–93.36). Where every population survived, assurance evolution increased loss of alleles compatible with a founder-relative high-investment target by 8.33 points (6.69–9.96). These results distinguish selection from finite realization, and persistence from retention of a specified floral option. Founding variation constrains accessible responses, but its variance contribution is not separated from all stochastic processes. Ancestral recovery, adaptation through other traits and recolonization success remain distinct outcomes. The findings motivate hypotheses about subsequent island divergence without demonstrating reproductive isolation or speciation.

## Introduction

Island colonists encounter pollination environments with only the genetic variation they carry. We ask how this starting state and pollinator limitation direct assurance and floral investment, how their responses interact in finite populations, and what these histories leave for persistence and subsequent adaptation. Three principal tests ask whether investment decline requires assurance evolution, whether assurance evolution improves persistence, and whether it preserves a specified floral genetic option.

Models connect selfing, allocation and plant–pollinator demography (Lepers et al., 2014), while selfing can rescue or imperil populations (Cheptou, 2019; Xu, 2023). Pollinator exclusion reduced genomic variation even in large populations (Busch et al., 2022). Resurrection studies documented floral reduction and increased selfing (Acoca-Pidolle et al., 2024), but experimental crosses show that inbreeding depression can contribute substantially to smaller flowers (Tusuubira and Kelly, 2024). Our investment variable represents allocation, not corolla size.

We test a narrower counterfactual: with initial expressed assurance matched, does allowing its evolution change retention of alleles compatible with a founder-relative investment target when every population survives, and improve persistence under harsher resources? Target support is necessary under the focal closed inheritance rule, but does not establish actual response or general evolvability. Selection-based irreversibility after pollinator restoration already has a mathematical precedent (Acoca-Pidolle, 2025, preprint); selfing need not uniformly impair adaptation (Hartfield and Glémin, 2024).

New pollination regimes arise through local changes or colonization; adaptation need not restore ancestral flowers. Colonization models already connect mating-system plasticity, persistence and adaptation (Peterson and Kay, 2015). Our bounded transfer distinguishes these outcomes without claiming natural island assembly or a general colonization advantage.

We use a finite diploid model with separately inherited investment I, visitor matching M and assurance A; fixed expressed A still permits selfing. The registered assurance-policy comparison supplies the principal genetic and demographic evidence; exploratory readbacks and transfers delimit interpretation. Inference units and evidential ranks remain separate.

### Q1 — Selection: floral investment can decline without evolving assurance

Before plant evolution, a fixed-state delayed-selfing diagnostic gave an investment-contribution gradient **+0.579 under higher visitor replenishment versus −0.700 under lower replenishment**: attraction was locally favoured in one prescribed visitor regime but disfavoured in the other. This local derivative does not guarantee the next generation's mean genetic change. Its reproductive interpretation includes female outcross, paternal pollen export, viable selfing and floral allocation cost ([original Q1](CHAPTER2_UNIFIED_EVOLUTION_PERSISTENCE_MANUSCRIPT_20261010.md#q1--thresholds-reproductive-returns-and-genetic-access)).

The original matched evolving-versus-fixed A campaign follows 64 independent synthetic visitor histories with eight nested demographic repeats in each of four reproductive settings. Floral investment is lower under low replenishment with expressed assurance A fixed at 0.5; this policy blocks capacity evolution **but does not prevent selfing**. Allowing assurance capacity to evolve compresses the near/far difference in four originally registered settings. When fresh founders are crossed with fresh visitor histories, compression transports in only **three of four** settings: the direct-A-cost interaction remains inconclusive. These counterfactuals reject a universal prerequisite of A evolution for the beginning of I decline **within the tested Model 3**. Both original near and far regimes were pollen limited; near is not a pollination-sufficient control. They do not identify natural island causes.

### Q2 — Sequence: precedence does not establish evolutionary necessity

Under delayed selfing and a direct assurance cost, A crossed the registered sustained-response threshold before I in **51/64** visitor histories, with 13 nearly simultaneous. Under prior selfing at the same primary threshold, only **30/64** were A-first. A separate registered intervention changing standing I-locus allele carriers in 384 paths found order-score difference +0.109375 with adjusted interval [−0.125,+0.34375] and I-event difference +0.1875 [−0.0625,+0.421875]; **both were inconclusive**. A-first cannot be promoted to a universal evolutionary clock or a cause of later irreversible loss. A separately registered **assigned phenotype-expression schedule**—not spontaneous inherited order—produced pooled far–near survival DID **−0.013589 [−0.022812, −0.004302]**, wholly within the predeclared ±0.05 practical-equivalence region ([original assigned-order readout](../results/chapter2/order_expression_full_cohort_20261009.json)).

### Q3 — Realization: functional visitor history and finite-population processes

The original prospective **128-visitor-history** bridge is independent of the later allele-closure cohorts. Natural far-minus-near investment change averaged **−0.4510 in a genotype-density approximation versus −0.1446 in the finite diploid model**; zero-deadband mixed-sign history labels were **0/128 versus 12/128**. Count-matching reversed both mean responses to **+0.0338 and +0.0333**, yet yielded **16/128 versus 68/128** mixed labels. Increasing finite plant capacity from 48 to 192 reduced mixed labels from 12/128 to 1/128. Count matching changes visitor identities as well as counts; capacity changes demography, and deterministic density is not the ABM's exact stochastic mean. These results show conditional differences in realized response, **not a measured drift fraction** ([frozen bridge](MODEL3_CH2_BRIDGE_PROSPECTIVE_RESULTS_20260927.md)). In a separate ten-context mutation comparison, two density contexts predicted A-first while only **3/32 and 2/32** finite histories did so despite complete occupancy; the genetic-access study below does not establish why those two contexts differ ([Q3 source map](CHAPTER2_Q3_FINITE_GENETIC_ACCESS_AND_REALIZATION_20261015.md)).

Archived fixed-A analyses show history-dependent floral structure: eight-repeat reliability increased from .621 to .825 with capacity, while single-trajectory ICC increased from .170 to .370. These are not natural-island chance fractions or isolated drift effects; the detailed qualifications are retained in Online Methods.

### Assurance endpoints were more repeatable under the sampled conditions

A post-outcome readback of an existing finite experiment compared evolving A
and I in the same surviving contexts: four mating settings, three initial
states, 128 visitor histories and four demographic repeats, with paired
near/far environments (12,288 arm trajectories). Within-context far-endpoint
variance, averaged over eligible contexts, was lower for A in all twelve
setting-by-start summaries. For central starts, all four repeats attained

agreement did not rank A higher in every noncentral setting.

This descriptive endpoint result does not establish deterministic evolution
or a uniformly positive response to pollinator decline: most unanimous A
far-minus-near contrasts were zero. The comparison conditions on survival
in all four paired repeats, uses five allele nodes per trait, fixes M,
and excludes mutation. Boundaries, inherited variation and convergence
can therefore contribute to repeatability. Event order and loss timing
cannot be reconstructed from these endpoint records. No new biological
updates were run for this audit.

### Inherited genetic accessibility can close before complete locus fixation

In a distinct 2,304-path experiment with no I-locus mutation or seed immigration, **703 histories** lost standing support for an additional founder-relative 0.05 **decrease** in I **before** single-allele fixation; 535 first became excluded at fixation. In 315 of the 1,238 selected first-loss transitions, low-I alleles disappeared despite favorable prior conditional parental weighting. This shows that expected favorable directional return and transmission of the alleles needed for the next response are different. It does **NOT** show loss of the **high-I** alleles relevant to post-restoration recovery; a separately marked, retrospective **read-only reverse-direction audit** tests that distinct upper-support boundary below.

### Q4 — Persistence: demographic effects do not prove a causal order-to-rescue path

The original conditional preparation-policy experiments yielded **+2.83 and +4.59 percentage-point** occupancy contrasts under two stated future challenges, with floor effects and competing interval conventions; neither identifies a survival benefit of the **naturally evolved A-first label**. The assigned-expression-order interaction was **−0.013589** (history-bootstrap 95% interval [−0.022812, −0.004302]), entirely within its preregistered **±0.05 practical-equivalence region**; a restricted-window confirmation failed. These imposed expression schedules are not naturally realized mutation-order effects. These negative and bounded results remain part of Q4 rather than being replaced by the newer policy-by-resource-budget effects ([original Q4](CHAPTER2_UNIFIED_EVOLUTION_PERSISTENCE_MANUSCRIPT_20261010.md#q4--persistence-consequences-of-reproductive-preparation)).

A no-evolution, fixed-visitor clonal model gave 80-update occupancy 0.0302 at K8 versus 0.8287 at K48 under budget 6; demographic conditions alone can generate large differences ([exact demographic null](CHAPTER2_Q4_EXACT_CLONAL_DEMOGRAPHIC_NULL_20261010.md)).

In a separate, pilot-informed, independently randomized 32-visitor-history stress study, the registered pooled assurance-evolvability by historical-replenishment occupancy interaction was **+0.1083984375**, 95% interval [0.0654296875,0.1513671875], averaged equally across four mating conditions; the prior-selfing interval included zero. Its delayed-control-specific interaction was +0.15234375, not the four-setting pooled effect. During the shock, A remained evolvable and carrying capacity, fecundity and pollen availability changed together; the result is **not** genetic legacy mediation, natural A-first rescue, or proof that a protected floral allele remained available. Separately assigned A-first/I-first expression schedules mostly did not establish a general survival effect.

The source reproductive ledger does contain a pollen-mediated benefit of one plant's floral investment to other conspecific mothers in selected states, but sustained individual-versus-group conflict sufficient to drive extinction was **not** established. "Private insurance versus public investment" is therefore a **testable mechanism framing**, not an already proven group-selection or tragedy-of-the-commons result.

## Discussion

The study connects inherited starting conditions, selection on assurance and investment, finite realization, and subsequent persistence and genetic options. Three conclusions anchor this account: investment declined without evolving assurance; assurance evolution improved persistence under harsh resources; and, where every population survived, it increased loss of alleles compatible with a specified high-investment target. The connection is an explanatory framework, not a demonstrated mediation chain.

### Founders constrain the possible before selection determines the favourable

Arrival can sample alleles stochastically, yet the inherited state constrains subsequent evolution. Under closed transmission, missing alleles can make a target unreachable; available alleles do not guarantee its realization. Starting-state comparisons therefore reveal a conditional deterministic role for founder history: it restricts possible responses and changes threshold outcomes, without uniquely determining trajectories. They do not quantify natural founder effects. The 160-by-160 design cannot separate founder-by-history interaction from stochastic residual, because it lacks independent repetitions within each treatment cell.

### Why assurance and investment differ in predictability

Cost-free delayed selfing without pollen discount has nonnegative local maternal return when outcross fertilization is incomplete. Investment balances receipt and export benefits against allocation costs in a particular visitor and population context. These mechanisms motivate different predictability, but nonnegative return is not proof of strong selection. Prior selfing still delivers individual benefits while pre-empting outcrossing; 51/64 versus 30/64 A-first outcomes measure order, not an isolated test of private benefit.

Assurance endpoints were often more repeatable, but selection and fixation may contribute successively. Concentration below the global upper bound does not exclude fixation at the boundary of standing variation. Floral responses contain both history-dependent structure and within-history dispersion. This is not evidence of phenotypic plasticity: inherited I changes differ from environmentally induced expression of the same genotype. Neither environmental responsiveness nor conditional dispersion alone identifies a pure drift fraction. The Methods audit documents what remains unidentifiable.

### Assurance changes floral evolution through potentially different routes

Fixing expressed A still permits selfing. Investment decline under that policy demonstrates that evolving assurance is unnecessary, while the narrowing of environmental investment differences shows that assurance can nevertheless modify the response. Compression transported in three of four independently founded settings. Reproductive compensation can change the marginal return on investment; genetic associations can also transmit correlated changes. These are distinct candidate routes. The [association-disruption experiments](CHAPTER2_HITCHHIKING_SECONDARY_EXPANSION_REVIEW_20261020.md) establish neither assurance-specific hitchhiking nor its absence. Baseline-pollen interactions likewise include inherited history rather than isolating instantaneous deficit.

### Finite populations need not realize the deterministic sequence

The [numerical comparison](atom_comparison_20261011/ATOM_ABM_NUMERICAL_VERDICT_20261011.md) uses discrete-update genotype density, not a full three-locus PDE or the finite model's exact stochastic expectation. All ten contexts passed adjacent-grid checks. In two contexts density predicted A-first, but only 3/32 and 2/32 finite histories realized that category despite complete survival; a smaller threshold changed the density classification. Thus favourable direction does not determine realized order. The discrepancy does not isolate drift, and separate genetic-support losses are not its demonstrated cause.

### Persistence and retained options are separate consequences

Selfing, allocation and demography are already linked theoretically (Lepers et al., 2014); reproductive rescue and genetic erosion also have precedents (Cheptou, 2019; Busch et al., 2022; Xu, 2023). Our initially expression-matched policy comparison separates demographic effects from retention of a directional floral option. At B8, complete survival excludes differential extinction as the explanation for increased support loss. At B4.5, assurance strongly improved survival. Different resource conditions do not establish survival purchased through genetic loss, or genetic loss causing survival. Retained target support is not general evolvability.

### Reversibility, mainland return and island diversification

A future mainland-like environment raises three questions: can descendants persist, regain ancestral traits, or adapt through different traits? These are separate outcomes. Late transfers distinguish support, polymorphism and realized response; some cohorts already met the ancestral target, and restricted I response can coexist with M-mediated improvement. Survivor-conditioned gains are not unconditional adaptation. Marker retention in small secondary-colonization experiments establishes no general assurance-mediated recolonization advantage. Closed-inheritance restrictions are not permanent irreversibility once mutation or immigration is allowed.

Islands may consequently change both which lineages persist and which responses remain available to them. This is a hypothesis about the prerequisites for subsequent divergence, not demonstrated speciation. Investment is not flower size or reproductive isolation; self-compatibility is not realized selfing. Goldberg et al. (2010) found higher net diversification in self-incompatible Solanaceae, but our model does not explain those rates. Pollinator-mediated isolation, radiation and natural mainland return remain untested. The [expanded story](CHAPTER2_FOUNDER_PREDICTABILITY_SPECIATION_STORY_20261021.md) separates observations, mechanism candidates and these evolutionary implications.

### Endpoint concentration does not identify the cause of repeatability

A source-verified exploratory readback of the four original matched archives found central-start far-arm A=0.75 in 418–494 of 512 endpoints per setting, with no endpoints at the model-wide bounds 0 or 1. Thus saturation at the global upper bound alone cannot explain this concentration. The value 0.75 may nevertheless be the upper limit of realized standing variation; endpoint means cannot establish allele fixation. Two separately archived genotype captures did show A becoming single-allelic at censuses 27 and 52, before I at 53 and 147, respectively. These two paths establish examples, not a population-wide frequency or a mediation effect.

For central-start far-arm I, within-history sums of squares comprised approximately 56–74% of total sums of squares across the four settings. This is a descriptive partition among sampled surviving contexts, not a causal drift fraction or the fraction of variation among natural islands due to chance. A had lower absolute within-history variance, but this did not imply a larger environment-explained proportion. The four archives retain endpoints and logs, not the trajectories needed to separate selection before fixation from convergence after fixation. ### Identifiability of founder and environmental contributions

The 160-by-160 confirmation crosses founder and visitor sources but has one realization per founder-by-history-by-policy-by-budget cell. It cannot separately identify founder-by-history interaction and stochastic residual. Policy or resource treatments are not within-cell replicates. Its crossed inference for registered policy contrasts remains valid under its declared convention; a four-component variance attribution does not follow from that design. Separate repeated-history experiments quantify conditional repeatability, but their estimates cannot be pooled with this confirmation into one variance budget. No such new decomposition was performed. Freezing a reanalysis after inspecting outcomes would not make it prospective confirmation.

### Historical model and inference ranks

The original Model 3 simulates finite diploid plants with separate additive visitor-matching, investment and autonomous-assurance loci. Exogenous functional visitors enter and disappear; encounter probabilities reflect floral affinity, matching optima, visitor effectiveness and pollen transfer. Maternal outcross, paternal export and viable autonomous-selfed offspring contribute to finite reproduction. Flowers incur allocation cost, and offspring alleles are inherited by Mendelian transmission with setting-specific mutation. The original Q1–Q4 and independent genotype-density experiments have separate registered inference units, horizons and source hashes; they are not pooled as one replicated island dataset. All original raw archives, readouts, negative/null outcomes and original EL-versioned figures remain unchanged.

The Q1 assurance-fixed policy sets actual expressed A=0.5; selfing remains possible. The 2,304-history structural-support experiment sets I mutation and seed immigration to zero. Its source restriction on **further decrease** is proved from nondecreasing minimum surviving I allele. The complementary **HIGH-I genetic support** restriction, evaluated in the read-only reanalysis and independently in a tiny later synthetic history, is that the maximum surviving I allele cannot increase under the same closed transmission rule. Thus a target I_star that exceeds this maximum is structurally unreachable in occupied descendants. The converse is not guaranteed by the theorem; selection, finite time and allelic pairing still matter.

### Model-derived functional insurance and evolutionary access are distinct

In source Model 3, cost-free delayed selfing acts as a buffer against failed outcross fertilization, not as a required replacement for outcrossing. For viable-self progeny factor v=1-delta, expressed assurance A, and outcross fertilization q, expected viable maternal seeds are Y=O[q+v*A*(1-q)]. At fixed inherited state, the fraction retained when q falls from one to zero is v*A, relative to that same state's original ovule budget. This is **functional pollen-failure tolerance**, not realized population persistence or long-term fitness. At fixed maternal ovule budget and assurance, the transmission of variation in annual outcross success to expected viable-seed production also follows Var(Y)=O²[1-(1-delta)A]² Var(q); insurance can attenuate the reproductive consequences of unreliable service. This is an algebraic source identity rather than an empirical estimate of stochastic extinction risk. With no direct assurance cost or pollen discount, an increase in A has nonnegative local return under delayed selfing; at perfect q=1 the return is neutral, not a genetic proof that A cannot decline.

Attraction I changes pollen receipt and paternal export while paying allocation cost. Under the closed inheritance rule, attaining a high-I target requires sufficient retained high-I allelic support. Selection and finite transmission affect whether and how rapidly that option is realized; favorable selection is not necessary for every realized increase. The mother's seed buffer does not guarantee retained support or subsequent response. Prior selfing, pollen discount and assurance resource cost can also make lowering A favorable after visitor return. Hence higher reproductive buffering may coexist with restricted recovery of high I, but the completed policy contrast below measures genetic support, not a causal tradeoff between buffering and realized recovery. The distinction is defined more precisely in the [source mechanism note](protocols/CHAPTER2_TWO_THRESHOLD_PRIVATE_SOCIAL_RECOVERY_SYNTHESIS_20261018.md).

### Unconditional recovery estimand and its conditional companion

For historical policy a in {A_evolvable, A_fixed}, check-point t, and identical imposed future visitor environment E0, define the binary outcome for a complete randomized history:

    Y_a(t) = 1{historical population occupied at t
               AND occupied at t+H
               AND mean I is >= I_star for L consecutive censuses
                   by the predetermined future deadline t+H}.

The policy estimand is E[Y_evolvable(t)] minus E[Y_fixed(t)] across independently crossed founder distributions and exogenous visitor histories, averaging appropriately nested demographic and future streams. Historical extinction is a recovery failure, NOT a zero-valued extinct trait. This is an unconditional policy difference in success of attaining/preserving the high-I state, not by itself a contrast in **regaining** it. Report original investment decline status separately. A secondary recovery analysis among actually declined surviving states is explicitly survivor- and post-treatment-selected, so cannot be equated with the randomized policy effect.

For an individual SAVED occupied state, repeat future restored stochastic streams to estimate a state-specific recovery probability. That conditional state-specific experiment addresses what can happen from a given genotype, not how many independent founding populations would have reached that genotype under each policy. Define the ecological selection-assay sign and the high-I support theorem on the same saved state so failure can be classified into an ecological return lock, genetic support closure, finite-time nonrealization or extinction. Mutational restoration of genetic access is a distinct policy, not a relabeling of restored visitation.

### Two clocks and trial design

Define permanent extirpation T_E by an explicit visitor-service removal/no-recolonization policy, not the first sampled zero-visitor year. Estimate the first robustly supported recovery-failure window T_R from predeclared uncertainty-adjusted probability curves and a sustained temporal rule; nonmonotonic recovery curves may have no unique T_R. T_R < T_E is the candidate "before pollinators disappeared" comparison only when high-I positive selection has been verified after restoration and the target was reachable at baseline.

Use independently drawn founder genotype blocks × independently drawn visitor histories. Predefine 1–2 primary contrasts, effect-size thresholds, family-wise confidence rules, full resource budget and sample-size planning independently of prospective outcomes. Follow the two-axis, source-variance/interaction-aware gate retained under PR #585; repeated future histories do not increase the number of independent founders. The new intervention is unregistered and unrun: these paragraphs are a **design proposal**, not a frozen protocol.
