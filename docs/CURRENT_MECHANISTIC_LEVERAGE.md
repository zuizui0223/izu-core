# Current mechanistic leverage

## Bottom line

The current Izu programme has moved beyond a generic statement that island flowers differ from mainland flowers, but it has **not** identified one historical cause for every channel.

The adopted evidence is best read at three levels:

1. **response shape established** — what changes across the frozen island series;
2. **mechanistic leverage** — whether explicit alternative axes can reproduce that response;
3. **causal attribution** — which remains blocked until independent lineage-level predictions and controls are available.

`data/predictive_meta/current_mechanistic_leverage.csv` is the machine-readable claim boundary.

## Campanula: split the continuous channels from the breakpoint channel

### Flower size

The focal literature supports island-series floral-size erosion, but the present island-side flower-length table is sparse (`n=4` with locked values). More importantly, source-native population-genetic evidence supports a north-to-south colonisation history in the same species complex. The flower-size cline therefore remains a strong descriptive pattern but a weak mechanism discriminator.

### Multilocus outcrossing

Outcrossing declines strongly along the island series. Simple climate PC1, great-circle mainland distance, island area/connectivity, and pre-1986 volcanic-recency axes do not reproduce that ordered erosion as well as island order.

However, island order is not a causal exposure. The allozyme literature reports progressive southward colonisation, while the RAPD literature does not support one universal diversity-by-distance axis and identifies Miyake as a multiple-immigration exception. The continuous mating-system pattern is therefore established without declaring whether pollinator turnover, demographic history, or both generated it.

### Autonomous reproductive capacity

This remains the strongest current breakpoint channel. The Oshima-to-Toshima transition is much sharper than the continuous morphology/outcrossing patterns and is not reproduced by the tested climate, distance, static geography, or pre-observation volcanic-history alternatives.

That gives the channel substantially more mechanistic leverage than the two continuous clines. It still does not prove a historical Bombus effect because an unmeasured breeding-system or demographic threshold could coincide with the same boundary.

## Independent lineages are already useful before quantitative meta-analysis

### Weigela

The primary abstract reports a gradual mainland-distance decline in corolla length. This is useful precisely because it does **not** supply a universal second-step response. The same lineage has independent genetic evidence of repeated founder effects and stepping-stone colonisation, making it a strong reminder that distance/order clines are mechanistically ambiguous.

The source-recovery audit currently reaches verified abstract/full-text access routes but not the population-level means, sample sizes and uncertainty required by the quantitative holdout gate. The missing original table is therefore an explicit access bottleneck; numbers are not reconstructed from the abstract.

### Ligustrum

The primary source describes the system as generalist and reports shorter Izu corolla tubes/stamens, with the strongest shortening on Hachijo. Therefore the negative-control prediction cannot be “generalists never change.” The relevant prediction remains that a generalist should not repeatedly show the same specialist-specific breakpoint after environmental/history alternatives are accounted for.

Accessible supporting-information metadata identifies a significance matrix and a pollinator table, but significance markers are not effect sizes. Population means, uncertainty, sample sizes and exact locality mapping remain required.

### Hosta

The primary abstract reports shorter corollas in southern Izu but a complicated, non-monotonic pattern in other floral dimensions. Hosta is therefore a prospective heterogeneity/falsification lineage rather than a convenient replication. Exact population and pollinator tables must be recovered before assigning a regime breakpoint or dependency class.

### Goodyera

The 2024 New Phytologist study provides a qualitatively different response. Mainland pure *G. henryi* is Bombus-dependent, whereas the Kozu plants initially identified as *G. henryi* are hybrids with *G. similis* and are pollinated by the same scoliid wasp that pollinates *G. similis* on the island. The hybrid viscidium is shorter than in mainland *G. henryi*.

This is strong mechanism context but not a same-lineage floral effect. The correct response mode is hybrid replacement plus interaction rewiring. The same study reports no discernible regional floral-morphology shift in wasp-pollinated *G. similis*, making it a valuable matched dependency-control direction, though not yet a formal equivalence test.

### Lilium

The source documents an alternative Lepidoptera timing mechanism and direct day/night reproductive-effectiveness evidence, but variety identity is confounded with geography. It prevents the comparative programme from becoming a universal Bombus story.

## What the paper can now argue

The defensible emerging claim is not:

> Bombus loss causes every southern Izu plant to shrink and self.

It is:

> Plant responses to simplified or altered pollination environments in the Izu Islands are heterogeneous across biological channels and lineages. In the focal Campanula system, continuous erosion in morphology and realized outcrossing is separated from a sharp autonomous-reproduction transition. Explicit static geography and pre-observation volcanic-history alternatives do not reproduce that multichannel pattern, but continuous island-order responses remain confounded with source-supported colonisation history. Independent lineages show different response modes, including gradual floral differentiation, complex morphology, matched nondependent stability, and hybrid-mediated interaction rewiring.

The pollinator-specific hypothesis therefore becomes a prediction about **which dependency classes show which response modes or shared breakpoints**, rather than a claim that all island plants show one syndrome.

## Decisive next evidence

The most valuable next gains are now:

1. population-level numerical morphology and locality mapping for Weigela, Ligustrum, or Hosta;
2. a predeclared dependency classification based on source-native pollination data;
3. a quantitative control/equivalence analysis for an appropriate nondependent lineage;
4. population-history covariates only when their original tables can be matched to the same focal populations;
5. additional independent cases of interaction rewiring, reproductive-assurance transition, or failure to persist across the regime boundary.

Until those gates are met, the causal-claim column in the leverage matrix remains `no` for every evidence unit.
