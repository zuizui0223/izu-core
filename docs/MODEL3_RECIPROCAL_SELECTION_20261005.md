# Reciprocal effects on local selection

Exploratory diagnosis, declared before calculation in `data/design/model3_reciprocal_selection_20261005.json`. No ecological or reproductive parameter was changed. The four existing rare-mutant settings and five controlled communities were evaluated at matching0.2,0.5,0.8 and49x49 interior investment/capacity states:60 fields,144,060 states. These are numerical grid points, not independent biological replicates or a prevalence sample.

Across the evaluated grid, both cross-derivatives were negative: increasing resident capacity reduced the investment selection gradient, while increasing resident investment reduced the capacity selection gradient. Conversely, investment reduction increased the gradient toward capacity. This is reciprocal reinforcement in these directions, conditional on the model and tested states; it is not proof of a globally self-sustaining loop, irreversible endpoint, or realized time order. Gradients can remain positive on both traits even when their cross-derivatives are negative.

A concrete example from the independently checked900-cell archive is delayed selfing with capacity cost0.5, matching0.5 and community left4. At investment0.5, raising capacity0.3 to0.7 lowers the investment gradient from0.53555 to0.08218, without making it negative. At capacity0.5, lowering investment0.7 to0.3 raises the capacity gradient from0.32283 to0.86753. The values are local log-invasion-fitness gradients, not offspring counts or per-generation trait changes.

Central differences at steps0.0001 and0.00005 had zero sign disagreements, maximum relative step discrepancy1.873e-5 and maximum absolute discrepancy0.0008281. Independent invasion-gradient checks at60 central states (two traits each) differed by at most2.729e-10. This is numerical validation over the declared grid, not an analytical global proof.

The three-page `outputs/model3_reciprocal_selection_20261005/selection_fields.pdf` retains every controlled setting/community, one matching value per page. Orange solid and blue dashed lines denote zero investment and capacity gradients. Arrows are normalized local selection directions, not evolution velocities; genetic covariance, finite sampling and dynamically varying visitors are absent from this diagnostic. No main-cohort trajectory was overlaid onto an unrelated fixed-community field.

Remaining question: these controlled fields are not a gradient of island isolation. They do not identify which selection gradient first crosses zero along a generated visitor-replenishment gradient or which finite population changes first. Those questions require the corresponding environment histories and inherited dynamics. The current result complements, rather than replaces, the completed temporal-order and capacity-fixed experiments.
