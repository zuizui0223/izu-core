# Chapter 2 — Q3: from selection predictions to finite inherited realization (2026-10-15)

**Role:** source-locked interpretation handoff for the single unified Ecology Letters manuscript, not a new experiment or a fresh biological history. The objective is to state a **positive finite-genetic mechanism** without rewriting the frozen original claims, blending incompatible cohorts, or treating the conditional density closure as the ABM stochastic expectation.

## The ecological question and its positive answer

**Question:** why can populations experiencing similar constraints on pollen delivery acquire different realized flower-investment and reproductive-assurance trajectories?

**Supported within Model 3:** pollination conditions shape the direction of reproductive returns; finite visitor histories and plant demography alter which investment differences appear; inherited allele support can be lost before complete fixation, such that a predeclared further investment response becomes impossible under the unchanged no-investment-mutation/no-immigration law. A favorable **conditional expected frequency change** of the needed lower-investment allele does **not** guarantee that the finite next generation retains it.

This is a concrete mechanism for **nonrealization of a threshold-defined evolutionary response**. It is **not** evidence that all source-matched density–ABM differences are caused by drift, that natural island differentiation has the measured frequency, or that the two sources of stochasticity have known attributable shares.

## Finite genetic accessibility: a more precise positive mechanism

**Original source:** [Supporting Information S14](CHAPTER2_UNIFIED_EL_SUPPORTING_INFORMATION_20261010.md#s14-fixed-access-ecological-order-boundary-and-absorbing-states). The separately frozen fixed-access ecological-order experiment admitted **2,304 original histories** (two fixed founder-genotype backgrounds × nine visitor contexts × 128 reused stochastic IDs; **not** 2,304 independent islands). Matching/attraction `I` had **standing template alleles but no new `I` mutation**, and there was no seed immigration. The response was **founder-relative I decrease ≥0.05**, sustained 20 occupied censuses with the original tie rule. It is a defined event, not an arbitrary smaller investment decline.

The unchanged Mendelian transmission law has a strict consequence. If the minimum remaining I allele exceeds the target `I0−0.05`, the specified event is structurally inaccessible to descendants; the minimum cannot decrease without mutation or immigration. **703 histories** first excluded the target **before a single I allele was fixed**, retaining nonzero investment-expression variance. A further **535** first exclusions coincided with fixation. These two numbers partition the **1,238 first-exclusion histories** across the 2,304 original paths. In one of the 703, the investment event had already been completed; therefore exclusion-before-event among missing responses counts **702**, not 703. The exclusion is conditional on the specified target, not a proof of evolutionary irreversibility or absence of any further smaller decline.

The distinct **1,238 first-exclusion transitions** were then audited using actual parental outcross/self seed ledgers. In **315/1,238** cases, the preceding **conditional expected** frequency of the needed low-investment alleles **increased** relative to the current unweighted parental frequency. Nevertheless, the realized finite offspring cohort retained none, excluding the prescribed threshold; the other **923/1,238** had negative expected change. This **outcome-selected accounting** includes finite parent sampling plus Mendelian transmission in the observed-minus-expected residual; it cannot identify pure drift or assign a per-island genetic-loss probability. These 315 transitions are **not** a subset definition of the 703 pre-fixation cases, and neither count is an independent island-system denominator.

Thus **favorable conditional parental weighting does not guarantee preservation of genetically accessible evolutionary responses in a finite offspring cohort**. The mechanism has been independently read back and numerically audited within S14, but **has not been experimentally isolated as the cause of the separate density–ABM discrepancy**.

## Implications for one-paper logic

- **Q1: what selection favours.** Fixed-state near/far return components and the evolutionary assurance-capacity intervention identify directional incentives within the model; they do not determine all finite reproductive histories.
- **Q2: which change appears first.** A-first is observed in only some cohorts and depends on event threshold, founder access and visitor ecology; threshold crossing is not unique causal necessity.
- **Q3: whether it can actually occur.** The **bridge** demonstrates sensitivity of realized direction to two compound finite-ecology/demography manipulations; the **original allele-state audit** supplies a specific, law-bounded way in which an expected favorable direction may become structurally inaccessible.
- **Q4: whether it persists.** Controlled assurance mean/preparation changes subsequent occupancy conditionally; the source studies **do not establish** a complete Q1→Q2→Q3→Q4 historical mediation chain or a natural-order survival advantage.

**Use in the main paper:** one compact paragraph in the Q3 Results and a limited interpretation sentence in Discussion; retain original 128, 320 and 2,304 source denominators and all 315/1,238/703 caveats here/S14. The exact classification deadband must accompany any mixed-history count. The source core, registered criteria, Q4 nulls, manuscript abstract and data/DOI status are unchanged.
