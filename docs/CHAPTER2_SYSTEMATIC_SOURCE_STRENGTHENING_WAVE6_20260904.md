# Chapter 2 systematic source-strengthening wave 6

Updated: 2026-09-04

## Decision

Wave 6 continues source strengthening after the wave-5 state of **34 targets requiring additional source work**.

Seven targets are re-reviewed:

- Saint Vincent and the Grenadines;
- Faroe Islands;
- Turks and Caicos;
- Norfolk Island;
- Cayman Islands;
- Dalmatian Islands;
- Bioko.

Two targets are cleared from the source-quality queue. Five remain open because direct local pollination or reproductive-process evidence is still absent or incomplete.

This wave does not modify the frozen 25-entry identifiability audit, the current 36-entry descriptive confrontation, or formal external prediction=`not_evaluable`.

## Cleared targets

### Saint Vincent and the Grenadines

Primary anchor: `10.1017/S0021859600088055`.

Corbet & Willmer's St Vincent study directly measured yellow-passionfruit pollination by the indigenous carpenter bee *Xylocopa mordax*. Flower-bagging experiments showed that fruit set depended on cross-pollination, exclusion of visitors prevented fruit set, and natural fruit set could be limited by insufficient pollination.

This is strong source-native pollination evidence. The focal plant is a commercial crop, however, and St Vincent sits within an already represented Caribbean geographic layer. The record therefore clears the literature-quality gap but is deliberately retained as a direct crop-pollination/process record rather than promoted as a new global-confrontation geography.

### Faroe Islands

Primary anchor: O. Hagerup (1951), *Pollination in the Faroes—in spite of rain and poverty in insects*, Biologiske Meddelelser 18(15):3–48; CiNii record `BA50238336`.

This 48-page source-native monograph is explicitly devoted to pollination under Faroese rain and insect scarcity. Later ecological syntheses repeatedly cite Hagerup's Faroese observations for widespread autonomous/self pollination and the spatial restriction of fly-mediated service around settlements.

The historical source is retained with an explicit mechanistic caveat: some species-level rain-pollination interpretations were challenged by later experimental work. The Faroe record therefore supports historical island pollination/reproductive-assurance breadth, not an unqualified claim that every proposed rain-pollination mechanism was correct.

It remains a possible new geographic breadth candidate, but still fails the full Chapter 2 transition contract.

## Targets that remain open

### Turks and Caicos

Primary anchor: `10.1017/S0030605311000251`.

The Oryx study of three endemic plants explicitly reports that pollination is unknown for *Argythamnia argentea* and *Encyclia caicensis*. The existence of attractive flowers or likely syndromes is not converted into direct pollination evidence.

This primary source therefore strengthens the **gap classification**, not the process evidence. Turks and Caicos remains open.

### Norfolk Island

Authoritative taxonomic anchor: *Flora of Australia*, Vol. 49, Oceanic Islands 1.

The flora identifies *Elatostema montanum* as monoecious and describes male and female inflorescences on the same plant. This resolves the earlier sex-system conflict created by a 2026 Parks Australia account that referred to 'male' and 'female' plants.

The taxonomic discrepancy is therefore narrowed, but no source-native controlled pollination experiment or direct pollination-mode study has been recovered. Norfolk remains open as a **pollination-mechanism** gap rather than a sex-system gap.

### Cayman Islands

Cayman cycad-associated *Rhopalotria* records and the regional *Zamia–Rhopalotria* obligate pollination literature are strong, and a Cayman ghost-orchid study identifies plausible hawkmoth pollinators. Targeted re-search still did not recover a direct Cayman plant-pollination event or reproductive-effect experiment.

Host association, regional mechanism and candidate-pollinator plausibility are therefore not treated as equivalent to a local process measurement. Cayman remains open.

### Dalmatian Islands

Targeted searches for island-endemic pollination, flower visitors, breeding systems, self-compatibility and reproductive experiments did not recover a qualifying Dalmatian-island-specific primary process study.

Mainland Croatian or generic Mediterranean evidence is not substituted for a local island gate.

### Bioko

The Bioko literature is rich in vertebrate, bushmeat and tropical-forest ecology. The current targeted search did not recover a qualifying source-native flowering-plant pollination or breeding-system experiment.

Frugivory, feeding records and general forest ecology are not converted into pollination evidence.

## Claim boundary

The exhaustive programme now distinguishes four different outcomes cleanly:

1. direct evidence sufficient to clear source quality;
2. direct evidence that remains geographically or biologically limited;
3. primary literature that explicitly documents a knowledge gap;
4. repeated search with no qualifying local source recovered.

None of these states automatically changes the current 36-entry manuscript confrontation. The frozen 25-entry audit and formal prediction boundary remain unchanged.
