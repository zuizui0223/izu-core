# Deep literature synthesis: what does the plant “island syndrome” actually mean?

Updated: 2026-08-24

## Purpose

The manuscript should not treat the plant **island syndrome** as one causal process. The literature is more coherent when island-associated patterns are separated into three layers:

1. **colonization / assembly filtering** — traits change the probability of arriving, establishing or persisting;
2. **in-situ evolution after colonization** — established island lineages diverge from mainland relatives;
3. **post-establishment ecological response / interaction reorganization** — established lineages respond to current changes in pollinator functional composition, partner context and reproductive buffering.

The current frozen ABM addresses the third layer. Its role is therefore not to deny aggregate island syndromes, but to explain why a common island-like pollinator-functional perturbation does not force all established lineages onto one trajectory.

---

## 1. Strongest assembly-level result: Baker's law is a filtering hypothesis

The most robust reproductive component of the plant island syndrome is the enrichment of **capacity for uniparental reproduction** among colonists.

Pannell et al. (2015; DOI `10.1111/nph.13539`) argue that Baker's law should be read narrowly: it predicts enrichment of self-fertility/uniparental reproductive capacity in colonizing situations, not uniformly high realized selfing rates after establishment. Pannell (2015; DOI `10.1111/mec.13087`) further separates dispersal, establishment and later demographic phases, each of which can impose different reproductive and dispersal sieves.

Grossenbacher et al. (2017; DOI `10.1111/nph.14534`) provide strong comparative support. Across >1500 species from Asteraceae, Brassicaceae and Solanaceae, 66% of island species were self-compatible versus 41% of mainland species. Their interpretation is explicitly a **colonization filter**.

Zell et al. (2025; DOI `10.1111/nph.20234`) broaden this with 3222 flowering plant species from 169 families. Self-compatibility was strongly associated with island occurrence; longer lifespan and generalized pollination syndrome also increased island occurrence in interaction with breeding system, while arrival opportunity and conserved taxonomic effects remained important.

### Consequence for the manuscript

A strong island reproductive syndrome can arise **before** post-establishment evolution is considered. Therefore:

> aggregate enrichment of self-compatible/generalized strategies does not imply that every established lineage subsequently evolves in the same direction.

This literature directly supports the manuscript's filtering-versus-response distinction.

---

## 2. The self-compatibility / dioecy tension already implies stage dependence

Island floras can show enrichment for self-compatible colonists while some island lineages or floras also show elevated gender dimorphism. This is not necessarily contradictory if the patterns arise at different stages.

Pannell et al. (2015) and Grossenbacher et al. (2017) both note that post-colonization selection can obscure an initial self-compatibility filter. Lord (2015; DOI `10.1093/aobpla/plv095`) reviewed 321 species across Southern Ocean islands: self-compatibility was extremely common among species with data, but anemophily and gender dimorphism were not clearly elevated relative to appropriate reference floras, and source flora/climate strongly complicated simple island predictions.

### Consequence

Even the best-known reproductive island syndrome is **stage-dependent and pathway-dependent**, which argues against a single universal lineage trajectory.

---

## 3. Floral morphology does not follow one universal directional syndrome

### 3.1 No general flower-size reduction across Pacific sister taxa

Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson (2020; DOI `10.1086/709018`) analysed 556 plant species representing 136 phylogenetically independent island-mainland sister contrasts. They found **no overall tendency for island flowers to be smaller**. Smaller island flowers appeared in particular archipelagos and taxonomic families, while other families showed larger flowers or no divergence.

Their interpretation is especially important: if island floras are enriched for small-flowered species, **ecological filtering may explain the aggregate pattern better than universal post-colonization dwarfing**.

### 3.2 Southwest Pacific: in-situ evolution is conditional on starting state and pollination mode

Ciarle, Burns & Mologni (2025; DOI `10.1093/aob/mcaf005`) analysed 129 independent colonization events across ten Southwest Pacific archipelagos. Animal-pollinated flowers followed an island-rule pattern, whereas wind-pollinated flowers showed gigantism. Results remained robust to breeding system, mainland source pool, taxonomic differentiation, family and island type.

Méndez (2025; DOI `10.1093/aob/mcaf053`) explicitly contrasts two mechanisms for an apparent island floral pattern: **environmental filtering of mainland variation** versus **in-situ evolution after colonization**.

### Consequence

The modern floral literature supports:

> island floral change depends on mainland starting state, pollination mode, clade and archipelago.

That is a close empirical analogue of the manuscript's state-dependent logic, without claiming that the ABM's synthetic coordinate has already been identified as flower size.

---

## 4. Network-level island syndrome: simplification plus reorganization, not monotonic collapse

Traveset et al. (2016; DOI `10.1111/geb.12362`) compared 52 quantitative plant-pollinator networks worldwide: 23 continental-island, 18 oceanic-island and 11 mainland networks. Oceanic island networks were generally smaller and topologically simplified, with lower interaction diversity and higher plant niche overlap. Yet many structural properties remained similar across geographical origins, and island traits affected network metrics differently.

This matters because “simpler island network” does **not** mean every network metric declines or every plant loses function in the same way.

Wang et al. (2020; DOI `10.1038/s41598-020-70954-7`) examined 55 plant species on Yongxing Island. Plants were visited by more pollinator species than classical pollination syndromes predicted, and five floral traits explained only 22.5% of visitor preference. Restrictive flowers were more specialized, but most floral traits did not uniquely predict ecological specialization.

Abe (2006; DOI `10.1093/aob/mcl081`) documented an Ogasawara pollination syndrome with relatively high dioecy, subdued floral colours and dominance of small solitary pollinators on less-invaded islands, while also finding specialized pollination niches and strong disruption by introduced honeybees.

### Consequence

Island interaction systems often become **depauperate, generalized and reorganized**, but specialized niches and compensatory rewiring can remain. This is consistent with the ABM result that local interaction context can **rescue or worsen** a lineage rather than act as a universal buffer.

---

## 5. Functional diversity is the strongest bridge from island syndrome to the current model

Hiraiwa & Ushimaru (2024; DOI `10.1111/1365-2435.14527`) provide the most direct empirical bridge. Across 40 spatiotemporally variable coastal plant-pollinator networks spanning Japanese continental and oceanic islands, reduced pollinator functional diversity—especially low abundance of long-tongued pollinators—reduced community-level functional specialization and flower-pollinator trait matching. Lower trait matching then reduced community-level pollination success. Pollinator species diversity itself was not the main predictor of trait matching or pollination success.

### Consequence

The manuscript should say **pollinator functional simplification/reorganization**, not merely “pollinator loss” or “lower pollinator richness”. The evidence-supported chain is:

```text
island isolation / faunal disharmony
        ↓
pollinator functional composition changes
        ↓
trait matching / interaction opportunity changes
        ↓
lineage-specific post-establishment response
```

not:

```text
island → fewer species → one floral response
```

---

## 6. Functional island biogeography already points away from a single phenotype

Schrader et al. (2021; DOI `10.1111/brv.12782`) advocate a functional island-biogeography framework and identify self-compatibility, clonal reproduction, flower accessibility and pollination generalism as traits relevant to island assembly.

Whittaker, Fernández-Palacios & Matthews (2023; DOI `10.1093/oso/9780198868569.003.0011`) review island evolutionary syndromes in plants across reproductive, morphological and interaction traits but emphasize that systematic comparative support is uneven among candidate syndrome components.

Ciarle & Burns (2024/2025; DOI `10.1080/0028825X.2024.2377418`) review the plant island syndrome in New Zealand's outlying islands and similarly show that some classical predictions are supported whereas others remain geographically contingent or poorly tested.

### Consequence

The literature is shifting from:

> “What is the island phenotype?”

toward:

> “Which filters, starting conditions and functional constraints generate each island-associated pattern?”

The present manuscript fits this transition.

## 7. Three-layer conceptual synthesis

```text
MAINLAND SOURCE POOL
    |
    | dispersal + arrival opportunity
    v
COLONIZATION / ASSEMBLY FILTER
    | self-compatibility / reproductive assurance capacity
    | lifespan / clonality
    | generalized accessibility
    v
ESTABLISHED ISLAND FLORA
    |
    | altered pollinator functional composition
    | interaction-network reorganization
    v
POST-ESTABLISHMENT RESPONSE
    |
    +--> pre-existing functional position -> branch potential
    |
    +--> local interaction context -> branch allocation / rescue / worsening
    |
    +--> reproductive assurance -> magnitude attenuation
    v
OBSERVED LINEAGE TRAJECTORIES

Repeated filtering + repeated conditional responses + aggregation across lineages
    -> macroecological island syndrome
```

This is the central literature-backed logic for the paper.

## 8. Evidence matrix

| Claim | Evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Self-compatibility is enriched on islands | strong: Grossenbacher 2017; Zell 2025 | assembly/filtering |
| Baker's law predicts uniformly high realized selfing | incorrect narrow reading | capacity for uniparental reproduction is the relevant trait |
| Generalized pollination/longevity can aid island occurrence | supported but interactive: Zell 2025 | non-random starting-state distribution |
| Island flowers universally become smaller | contradicted: Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson 2020 | filtering and context matter |
| Flower-size evolution can depend on starting state/pollination mode | regionally strong: Ciarle et al. 2025 | conditional in-situ evolution |
| Oceanic pollination networks are simpler | supported but metric-specific: Traveset et al. 2016 | network reorganization, not uniform collapse |
| Island pollination is always generalized | too strong | generalization is recurrent but specialist niches persist |
| Functional diversity loss can drive matching/function loss | strong in Izu: Hiraiwa & Ushimaru 2024 | direct upstream bridge to current ABM |
| All island-syndrome components share one mechanism | unsupported | preserve multi-layer causal structure |

---

## 9. Revised novelty statement

> **Plant island syndromes are composites of selective assembly, in-situ evolutionary change and reorganized biotic interactions. Existing comparative studies show that these components can be strong but conditional: self-compatibility is enriched through colonization filtering, flower-size evolution depends on starting size and pollination mode, and oceanic pollination networks simplify and reorganize without converging on one universal topological outcome. What remains less developed is a mechanistic account of why established lineages exposed to a comparable pollinator-functional perturbation occupy different response trajectories. Our frozen ABM addresses that post-establishment layer by separating branch generation by pre-existing functional state from branch allocation by local interaction context and downstream attenuation by reproductive assurance.**

This is stronger and safer than claiming that island syndromes are false.

## Claim boundary

This synthesis does not convert literature-level recurrence into evidence that all islands share one empirical causal mechanism. It sharpens the distinction among assembly filtering, in-situ evolution and post-establishment response, and therefore defines the current paper's contribution more precisely.
