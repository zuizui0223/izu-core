# Cover letter draft — Journal of Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, **“One perturbation, multiple island responses: state-dependent branching under pollinator functional simplification,”** for publication in *Journal of Ecology*.

Island ecology has long sought recurrent reproductive syndromes, yet those aggregate patterns do not imply that established plant lineages must follow one common trajectory after pollinator communities simplify or reorganize. Our manuscript addresses this gap by asking why a common island-associated change in pollinator function can produce divergent plant responses.

The study makes three linked contributions to plant ecology.

First, a frozen agent-based experiment identifies **pre-existing lineage functional position** as the replicated minimal tested generator of within-environment response-sign branching. Removing this heterogeneity eliminates mixed-sign branching in both the original and independently seeded blocks, whereas other residual single-factor removals retain branching.

Second, we separate the ecological roles of downstream context. **Local interaction context reallocates response branches bidirectionally**—it can rescue or worsen individual lineages—whereas **autonomous reproductive assurance mainly attenuates decline magnitude** and does not robustly reverse response sign. This distinction clarifies why superficially similar forms of “resilience” need not share one mechanism.

Third, we challenge the frozen response-state architecture against a source-audited set of 13 island plant–pollinator systems. Branching, same-direction propagation and buffering/alternative states recur across these systems without system-specific parameter tuning. We retain a reproductive-axis-decoupling constraint and a failed signed-position prediction rather than treating every external case as a success.

We believe the manuscript fits *Journal of Ecology* because plant ecology and plant–animal interactions are central, and the main contribution is a general ecological principle: **aggregate island syndromes can coexist with lineage-level branching because colonization and persistence filter which states arrive, whereas starting functional state and local interaction context determine how established lineages respond.** The study therefore connects island biogeography, plant reproductive ecology and ecological-network resilience through a common state-dependent response framework.

All primary numerical results are derived from frozen simulation outputs and source-audited literature challenges. No new unpublished field dataset is required for the primary claims.
