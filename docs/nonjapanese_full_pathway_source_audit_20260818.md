# Non-Japanese full-pathway source audit — 2026-08-18

## Target

Recover an independent non-Japanese system with all three source-native layers in one study/system:

1. geography / island constraint;
2. interaction architecture or trait matching;
3. realized reproductive performance.

This is the admission standard for a second **full pathway** replication of the current `constraint-mediated functional conservation` hypothesis.

## Result

No second non-Japanese full-pathway study was admitted in this audit.

### Caribbean Gesneriaceae — partial pathway

Martén-Rodríguez et al. (2015) compares 28 island and 26 mainland Gesneriaceae species and includes:
- effective visitation rates;
- quantitative pollination-network specialization;
- island/mainland differences in functional specialization;
- breeding treatments and autofertility/fruit-set measurements.

This is strong evidence for `geography -> architecture/reproductive-strategy response`, but the study does not supply a source-native direct coefficient or matched pathway from architecture/trait matching to realized reproductive success. It remains **partial**, not full.

### Global 52-network comparison — architecture only

Traveset et al. (2016) directly compares mainland, continental-island and oceanic-island network structure and supports a geography-linked shift in interaction opportunity space, but it does not attach matched reproductive performance to each network. It remains **architecture-level** evidence.

### Wanshan–Yongxing — architecture only

Wang et al. (2025) directly compares a continental and oceanic island and shows large differences in species composition, network structure and pollinator importance. The inspected source route does not provide a matched reproductive-performance endpoint needed for a full pathway.

### New Zealand trait matching — architecture only for this purpose

Biddick & Burns (2018) shows morphology predicts the topology of an insular plant–bird pollination network, but it lacks an island-mainland geography contrast and matched reproductive endpoint.

### Fragmentation island-mainland studies — informative but not admission-equivalent

Fragmentation studies can link isolation, visitation and fruit set, and some network studies compare habitat islands with a mainland reference. They do not automatically satisfy the oceanic-island biogeographic contrast or the same architecture/trait-matching definition, so they are not promoted as second full-pathway replications.

## Current evidence rank

- **Full pathway:** Hiraiwa et al. 2024, Japanese continental/oceanic island gradient.
- **Independent partial pathway:** Caribbean Gesneriaceae.
- **Independent geography -> architecture replication:** global 52-network comparison; Wanshan–Yongxing.
- **Independent architecture/trait-matching evidence:** New Zealand and other island systems.

## Consequence

The repository should not claim two independent full pathway demonstrations yet.

The current cross-region support is instead:

> the geography-to-architecture part repeats independently, while the complete `geography -> architecture/matching -> reproductive function` path is directly demonstrated in one regional natural experiment and partially corroborated elsewhere.

## Reopen condition

Reopen the full-pathway search when a source provides, within one non-Japanese island-mainland or isolation-gradient system:

- an explicit geography/isolation contrast;
- network architecture, functional diversity, or trait-matching measurement;
- reproductive performance (pollen deposition, fruit set, seed set, or equivalent);
- a source-native link between the architecture/matching measure and reproductive performance.
