## 1. Search/review protocol and what coverage means

- Search date: **2026-10-27**. Four prespecified *topic families*: (i) island colonization, breeding system and floral island phenotypes; (ii) selfing/pollinator loss and floral reproductive investment; (iii) functional matching and floral divergence; (iv) genetic variance, population rescue, allele retention, future evolvability and reversibility.
- Queries combined keywords equivalent to: \`island self compatibility floral size colonization filtering\`; \`pollinator loss self fertilization reproductive assurance floral investment selection\`; \`pollinator assemblage trait divergence matching pollination generalized\`; \`selfing standing genetic variation evolutionary rescue adaptation\`; \`mating system irreversibility pollen decline theoretical model\`; and author/DOI forward checks of Grossenbacher, Ciarle, Zell, Sakai, Lepers, Busch, Xu, Peterson, Opedal, Hartfield, Acoca-Pidolle and other directly adjacent sources.
- Discovery routes: current publisher pages, PubMed/PMC and targeted citation/DOI searches; original source papers and journal records prioritised over reviews and preprints. Primary abstracts/full-text passages independently checked where publicly accessible. A preprint remains a preprint; online-publication year may differ from volume year.
- **Limitation:** Not an exhaustive Web of Science/Scopus/Crossref/PubMed census, not forward/backward snowballing of every citation, not a PRISMA systematic review, no machine-verified all-date deduplication, no journal-independent novelty guarantee. More unseen or later publications may overlap.
- Comparison unit: *the estimand* (e.g. self-compatibility among extant island species, change in mean floral size, genome-wide diversity, loss of alleles sufficient for one founder-relative high-I target, population survival), not merely matching words such as “selfing”/“evolvability.”
- Evidence levels: **P** = published primary article, **R** = published review/primary-source synthesis, **W** = publicly available working preprint. These abbreviations describe literature identity, not causal/inferential strength of izu-core evidence.
- Natural associations, empirical selection gradients, phylogenetic reconstructions, evolution experiments and model interventions are **not exchangeable causal units**. In particular, Ch1 self-compatibility ≠ Model3 expressed or inherited A; flower-size/colour ≠ I or M; an exposed genotype distribution ≠ measured realized selfing; “evolutionary access” ≠ demonstrated adaptive recovery.

## 2. Island biogeography and the **non-universal** floral response

| Directly checked literature (DOI) | What is already known | Boundary for our claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Baker (1955)**, *Evolution*. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1955.tb01544.x (P) | Uniparental reproduction can favour establishment after long-distance dispersal (“Baker’s law”). | Mate/colonist limitation is not identical to effective pollinator shortage after colonization. |
| **Grossenbacher et al. (2017)**, *New Phytologist*. https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.14534 (P) | Islands had more self-compatible species than sampled mainland floras in three families (66% versus 41%); explicitly interprets much of the contrast as **colonization filtering**. | Do **not** claim novel recognition of recurrent island self-compatibility or independent post-colonization SC evolution. |
| **Zell et al. (2025)**, *New Phytologist*, online 2024-11-08. https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.20234 (P) | 3,222 species/169 families in assembled data; breeding system, lifespan, floral symmetry and arrival opportunity jointly influence island occurrence; SC advantage is known. | Closest added global island **functional-vs-form trait** comparator. Model3 does not newly discover the simple tripartite distinction. |
| **Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson (2020)**, *American Naturalist*. https://doi.org/10.1086/709018 (P) | 556 species/136 phylogenetically independent island–mainland comparisons; flowers were **not on average smaller** in their sampled Pacific systems. | Does not prove all island flowers are directionless in every region; absence of a common mean trend differs from absence of structured selection. |
| **Ciarle, Burns & Mologni (2025)**, *Annals of Botany*. https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcaf005 (P) | 129 colonization comparisons in ten Southwest-Pacific archipelagos: animal-pollinated flowers followed an island-size rule, wind-pollinated flowers showed enlargement; in situ flower-size change documented. | **Direct counterexample** to saying that floral form “never converges” or island flowers “lack predictable evolution.” Prefer pollination-mode- and lineage-dependent *directions*. |
| **Ciarle (2026)**, *Oikos*, e11782, online 2025-12-24. https://doi.org/10.1002/oik.11782 (P) | Pacific *Coprosma* study distinguishes biased colonization of large-leaved ancestors and subsequent in situ evolution of leaf size/stature. | **Not a flower or pollinator experiment**; appropriate precedent for separating assembly from within-lineage evolution only. |
| **Goldberg et al. (2010)**, *Science*. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1194513 (P) | Phylogenetic species-selection / long-run mating-system extinction implications existed long before this Model3. | Do not claim new species-level extinction or speciation estimates from a finite population ABM. |

### Additional island assemblage/turnover controls (not flower-trait evolution)

- **Schrader, Coleman, Zelený, Kreft & Westoby (2025)**, *Ecology Letters* 28:e70223. https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.70223 (**published primary, 132 repeatedly surveyed Australian islands**). Over six decades, trait-dependent colonization/extinction operated despite broadly stable mean community traits. This is direct evidence that **community trait structure and lineage-level trait evolution are not interchangeable**. It does not measure floral A/M/I adaptation or a genetic-target policy comparison.
- **Song et al. (2026)**, *New Phytologist*. https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.71040 (**published primary**). Thirteen tree carbon/water functional traits and associations were compared among 37 tree species on 35 Chinese islands and nearby mainland plots; several size-related hydraulic and anatomical island shifts were reported even when trait–trait associations remained relatively stable. This is **not a flower/pollination study** and is included to guard against turning one qualitative island-trait comparison into a universal ecological claim.

**Decision:** The island-level puzzle is *recurrent SC/assurance-associated establishment patterns versus context-dependent, sometimes predictable floral trajectories*. “Function deterministic, morphology random” is incorrect both for external studies and for present Model3 identifiability.

## 3. Reproductive assurance, allocation, selection and empirical selfing syndrome

| Study | Verified prior contribution | Model3 R1/R2 positioning |
|---|---|---|
| **Sakai (1995)**, *Evolution*. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1995.tb02287.x (P) | Theory jointly models prior/delayed selfing and allocations to attracting structures, with nonlinear flower size/number constraints and inbreeding depression. | Joint mating/investment theory **not new**. Model3’s separately manipulable expressed A is a specific intervention. |
| **Lepers, Dufay & Billiard (2014)**, *Evolution*. https://doi.org/10.1111/evo.12533 (P) | Pollination mutualism, plant and pollinator demography, selfing and investment under the selfing syndrome already co-modelled; possible mixed mating, complete selfing/outcrossing and evolutionary suicide. | The coupled ecological/evolutionary framework is existing; do not claim first theory of assurance–attraction interactions. |
| **Teixido & Aizen (2019)**, *Annals of Botany*. https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcz014 (P) | Natural *Tuberaria guttata* populations show delayed-selfing reproductive assurance can weaken pollinator-mediated selection favouring large flowers. | Strong **empirical antecedent of R2’s proposed mechanism**; Model3 policy-compression mechanism is not uniquely established as its cause. |
| **Bodbyl Roels & Kelly (2011)**, *Evolution*. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.2011.01326.x (P) | Five generations of experimental pollinator loss induced rapid change in mating-associated traits in *Mimulus*. | Trait change following pollinator loss already experimentally known. |
| **Acoca-Pidolle et al. (2024)**, *New Phytologist*. https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.19422 (P) | In four *Viola arvensis* populations, repeated selfing-syndrome evolution, floral reduction, nectar and attractiveness loss alongside higher selfing. | Real-world floral convergence **also exists**; multiple directions across settings are not evidence that convergence cannot occur. |
| **Tusuubira & Kelly (2024)**, *Frontiers in Plant Science*. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2024.1378568 (P) | Ten generations/20 large *Mimulus* experimental populations; floral-size decline under selfing partly reflects **inbreeding depression**, not purely adaptive flower allocation. | Prevent automatic interpretation of phenotypic shrinkage as selection for low I. |
| **Xu (2023)**, *American Naturalist*. https://doi.org/10.1086/725425 (P) | Eco-evolutionary selfing rescue under pollen shortage, with inbreeding depression and comparison of plastic versus evolved selfing; intermediate selfing may maximize rescue. | Model-based reproductive rescue and conditional demography already studied. |
| **Cheptou (2019)**, *Annals of Botany*. https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcy144 (R + original evolutionary-rescue model) | Short-term selfing-mediated rescue versus longer-run lineage/genomic disadvantage; evolutionary suicide can arise under some costs. | Do not describe “assurance saves populations but harms later prospects” as a first conceptual discovery. |

**Decision:** R1 demonstrates **non-necessity of A evolution for I decrease under fixed expressed A=.5** in tested conditions (selfing still permitted). R2 demonstrates A-policy modification of I contrast, with independent fresh-founder generality **3/4** and the four-setting registered decision **inconclusive**. The numerical interventions and delimiters matter more than an already-known verbal causal arrow.

| Study | Prior result | Implication for R3 |
|---|---|---|
| **Opedal (2019)**, *New Phytologist*. https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.15403 (R + genetic-variance synthesis) | Floral trait-specific genetic variances/evolvabilities and standing variation determine short-run capacity for pollinator shifts. | Genetic accessibility and floral evolvability already separate research targets; Model3 “allele support” ≠ all evolvability. |
| **Moir et al. (2025)**, *New Phytologist*. https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.20373 (P) | Seven *Brunsvigia* populations span bee, butterfly and long-proboscid fly-associated ecotypes; floral style lengths relate to local interacting pollinators. | Floral matching to pollinator morphology is demonstrably an existing explanation of geographic floral diversity. |
| **Torres-Vanegas et al. (2026)**, *Evolution*. https://doi.org/10.1093/evolut/qpag030 (P) | Local functional pollinator assemblage composition predicts floral/pollination trait differentiation in *Viscaria vulgaris* despite generalized pollination. | Strong **near-direct comparator** for the claim “visitor history predicts matching”; R3 M 40.146% is model-specific **known-source prediction error reduction**, not the first association in nature. |
| **Within-species floral adaptive walks (2025)**, *Nature Communications*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-57639-3 (P) | Floral colour changes and other morphology/reward components can form partially assembled suites during incipient pollinator shifts. | Abstract M is not literal floral colour/architecture, nor a real population's adaptive walk. |
| **Cui & Yuan (2026)**, *Annual Review of Plant Biology*. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-arplant-072125-082325 (R) | Reviews multidimensional pollination syndrome genetics, functional transitions, delayed selfing and the role of linkage/pleiotropy. | Trait matching is an integrated genetic problem, not synonymous with one cost-free scalar. |
| **Acoca-Pidolle, Wessinger & Hileman (2026)**, *New Phytologist*, first online 2026-09-29. https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.71605 (R) | Reviews genomic architecture of floral divergence and contrasts transitions to selfing versus pollinator shifts; evidence for nontrivial coordinated trait genetics. | **Important 2026 conceptual overlap**: “functional convergence but form-specific trajectories” is not an untouched theoretical idea. Three separately inherited Model3 loci are deliberately stylized—not observed floral QTL architecture. |

**Decision:** The R3 sources measure **different estimands and use different cohorts**: (1) survivor-conditioned endpoint A/I variance (12 summaries; central A exact agreement 66–111/128, I 7–20/128); (2) 160×160 known-source held-out error reduction for M change +40.146% and I change +0.359%; (3) a distinct A genetic fixation policy contrast at B8, 98.8555% versus 20.0977% at the highest **available** A=.75. Do **not** combine them into one experimentally identified intrinsic trait-determinism ranking, nor infer I 99.641% “luck/drift.” Cost-free M and a visitor schedule that directly moves matching optima can generate M's observed forecast advantage.

## 5. Genetic constraint, rescue, persistence and reversibility: direct threats to generic priority

| Study | Prior result | Novelty risk / distinction |
|---|---|---|
| **Peterson & Kay (2015)**, *American Naturalist*. https://doi.org/10.1086/679107 (P, individual-based modelling) | Colonizing plant populations, mating-system plasticity, population persistence, genetic variance and adaptation to a novel environment were already jointly simulated. | One of the **closest MODEL comparators**: no priority for linking selfing, future adaptation and persistence. This Article is distinguished only at the **specific policy/allelic-target contrast**, not the broad framework. |
| **Schoen & Toczydlowski (2026)**, *New Phytologist*. https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.70880 (P, 11 natural *Impatiens capensis* populations) | Population-genomic differences in bottleneck severity and postbottleneck recovery are associated with genetic diversity, inbreeding, linkage/recombination and spatial structure in an autonomously selfing species. | **Close 2026 empirical comparator:** apparent demographic recovery and genetic diversity can diverge; the study reconstructs natural histories, **not** a randomized A-expression policy, causal mediation or measured loss of one specified high-I flower-investment target. |
| **Busch et al. (2022)**, *Evolution*. https://doi.org/10.1111/evo.14572 (P) | Experimental pollinator loss caused increased selfing and genome-wide loss of variation despite large populations; selection, linkage and inbreeding, not simple drift alone, shape loss. | No novelty in selfing/genetic erosion or attributing change solely to neutral drift. Our high-I **target-compatible allele support** is more specific than neutral/genome-wide variability. |
| **Lande & Porcher (2015)**, *Genetics*. https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.115.176693 (P theory) | Quantitative genetic variance under partial selfing and consequences for evolutionary response already studied. | Model3 does not invent the variance/evolvability distinction. |
| **Hartfield & Glémin (2024)**, *PLoS Genetics*. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011312 (P theory and stochastic simulation) | Selfing can increase adaptive fitness under some models even while changing allele associations, selection interference and hitchhiking; its effects are **not uniformly deleterious**. | Explicit counterexample to a universal “selfing reduces all future evolutionary potential” claim. Model3 has no genomic architecture comparable to their multi-locus genome models. |
| **Bishop, Stinchcombe & Baucom (2026)**, *Evolution Letters*. https://doi.org/10.1093/evlett/qrag026 (P) | A resurrection study of *Ipomoea purpurea* found evolved genetic covariances among pollination/mating traits constraining predicted adaptive responses despite selection for change. | Real natural evolution and predicted response are already distinguished; Model3 target-support closure is a **different algebraic lower/upper genetic-access endpoint**, not the first study of evolving adaptive constraints. |
| **Acoca-Pidolle (2025)**, *EcoEvoRxiv* preprint. https://doi.org/10.32942/X2R93P (W; **not verified peer-reviewed**) | Mathematical model predicts mating-system evolutionary irreversibility under some absolute-trait selection and pollinator-restoration scenarios. | A more direct conceptual competitor for “restoration trap.” Model3 does **not** establish actual future adaptation failure or universal irreversibility; do not relabel this preprint as a journal article. |

| **Goldberg et al. (2010)**, *Science*. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1194513 (P) | Species-level mating-system selection and macroevolutionary asymmetry are known. | Our finite populations do not estimate lineage diversification rates. |

### Latest 2026 rescue theory and reviews — important competing conceptual ground

These works were independently checked from publisher/PubMed records on 27 October 2026 and further narrow any general claim that izu-core first distinguished demographic rescue from long-term adaptive potential.

| Literature | Existing finding | Appropriate separation from Model3 |
|---|---|---|
| **Christie, Fitzpatrick & Searle (2026)**, *Trends in Ecology & Evolution*, available online 9 Sep. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2026.08.010 (**published review**) | Explicitly distinguishes demographic, evolutionary and genetic rescue, while emphasizing non-exclusive mechanisms and short-term demographic benefit versus long-term adaptive potential. | **Strong priority warning:** general separation of persistence, genetic resource and future adaptation is known; this Article tests a particular randomized A-expression policy and *founder-relative high-I allelic target* outcome, not a first conceptual formulation. |
| **Balstad et al. (2026)**, *Trends in Ecology & Evolution*, 41:762–774. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2026.04.015 (**published review**) | Synthesizes 56 cases of evolutionary rescue in wild populations; empirical persistence and predicted evolutionary responses are already considered distinct. | Neither generic distinction between rescue mechanisms nor existence of future-adaptation uncertainty is novel. Its evidence consists of wild systems; Model3 is a synthetic policy/target-access contrast, not one of those empirical cases. |
| **Deraje & Uecker (2026)**, *Evolution*, 80:1733–1747. https://doi.org/10.1093/evolut/qpag095 (**published theoretical primary paper**) | Stochastic mate-finding Allee model studies evolutionary rescue, mating system incl. selfing, standing variation versus de novo change, and fecundity versus mate-finding trade-offs. | **Closest 2026 conceptual/mathematical comparator** for mate limitation, rescue and existing variation; does not report this Article's diploid A-policy effect on one floral I founder-target allele support when all populations survive. Do not claim first model to couple mating, demography and inherited accessibility. |

**Additional natural-history challenge:** Schoen & Toczydlowski (2026) directly associate historical bottleneck/recovery differences with selfer genomic variation; do not imply that the contrast between being alive and preserving adaptive variation is first observed in this Article. Its 11 observational populations and Model3's source-crossed targeted policy contrasts address different causal questions.

**Revised uniqueness claim:** Selfing-mediated demographic rescue, loss of genetic variation, distinct genetic/evolutionary rescue categories, and mate limitation during rescue are **pre-existing**. The targeted, source-registered contribution is **a specified initial-expression-matched A policy comparison for a necessary high-I allele-support endpoint at universal B8 survival plus a *separately registered* survival-resource interaction**. That within-model estimand differs from the closest papers reviewed, but a non-exhaustive search cannot establish worldwide priority or that NEE editors will consider it sufficiently broad.

**Decision:** Registered Model3 **R4 G/S** is the strongest distinctive result *within this particular model*: initially expressed A=.5 is matched across fixed-expression and evolving-expression policy, B8 all **25,600/25,600** crossed founder×history populations survive in either policy, yet loss of **founder-relative high-I allele support** is +**8.328125 percentage points** (adjusted 97.5% interval +6.693910 to +9.962340). Separately, B4.5/B8 survival policy×resource interaction is +**92.707031 pp** (97.5% interval +92.049592 to +93.364471). These are **two separate registered estimands**, not a demonstrated mediation chain or a new pre-registered joint endpoint. Complete B8 survival rules out *differential extinction* as the cause of the B8 support-loss contrast, **not** effects of different population-size trajectories among survivors.

R4 asks about loss of **necessary support for one specified founder-relative I target under closed inheritance**, not measured flower restoration, not probability of readaptation, not total quantitative genetic variance, not a complete genome-wide inference, and not universal loss of the capacity to respond via M or de novo mutation. The 160×160 crossed cells share 160 founder sources and 160 visitor histories; **25,600 are not independent natural islands or independent biological source units**.

The R5 transfer (I polymorphism **4/32** cohorts per policy, high-I support **24/32**) is **exploratory**. It specifically warns that support ≠ segregating variation ≠ realized post-transfer response. No proof of irreversibility, lineage speciation or successful mainland recolonization.

## 7. Manuscript changes justified by the source checks

### Introduction — claim correction

**Use:** “Self-compatibility is overrepresented among insular floras, consistent in part with colonization filtering; floral phenotypes do not show a single size-reduction direction across all lineages and pollination modes (Baker, 1955; Grossenbacher et al., 2017; Zell et al., 2025; Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson, 2020; Ciarle et al., 2025).”

**Avoid:** “Insular selection independently evolved SC everywhere”; “flowers do not show predictable change on islands”; “a single abstract M is a measured flower colour/shape phenotype.”

### Conceptual near-neighbour sentence

“Functional matching is an established explanation for geographically divergent floral traits (Moir et al., 2025; Torres-Vanegas et al., 2026). Our M-history forecast tests a specified *model-based known-source skill*, not a first demonstration of that association, and cannot separate history-by-founder interaction from finite stochastic realization for I.”

### Registered contribution — Discussion, prioritised over speculative diversification

“Previous studies have linked selfing to reproductive rescue, genomic diversity, and future adaptation (Peterson & Kay, 2015; Busch et al., 2022; Hartfield & Glémin, 2024). Our distinctive comparison holds *initial expressed assurance constant* and tests two independent policy effects: at B8, **support for a specified founder-relative high-investment target is lost more often despite universal occupancy**, while a separate resource interaction changes survival. It does not measure readaptation, isolate mediation, or generalise beyond this model.”

### Future empirical falsifier (not a retroactive result)

Track real founder/resident genomic variation for *specified* floral investment targets, functional matching response and population persistence across a documented pollination regime. Measure colonization filtering separately from in situ evolution; include visit effectiveness, maternal and paternal success, inbreeding depression, ecological/trait covariates and post-shift standing-genetic covariances. This is a proposal, **not** Ch1 validation or an izu-core completed experiment.
