## Abstract

Island, selfing and pollination syndromes summarize recurrent trait combinations, but long-standing debate already shows that syndrome categories do not uniquely identify the processes that generated them. We asked where repeatability is retained or lost as a recurrent pollination problem propagates toward floral evolution.

We used one ecologically explicit but system-uncalibrated plant–pollinator model linking functional matching and pollen transfer to mating, Mendelian inheritance and finite recruitment. Prospectively defined interventions tested state-dependent selection, reproductive context, genetic accessibility and finite-population realization. Functional matching generated opposite selection directions across starting floral states, and changing visitor functional composition at identical visitor number altered selection and inherited response. A proposed reproductive-assurance × investment-cost route failed a preregistered robustness test and was demoted from the headline mechanism. Restricting standing genetic variation selectively suppressed response on the constrained trait axis; empirically anchored mutation input narrowed, but did not reverse by year 800, the response gap between low- and high-standing-variation populations. In the original near–far isolation bridge, deterministic mean investment effects remained negative at inbreeding depression 0.25, 0.50 and 0.75 (-0.351, -0.451 and -0.414), but history-level uniformity was lost at 0.75: 116/128 histories remained negative-only, 11 were mixed across starting states and one was positive-only. Visitor-history and finite-demographic interventions further modified realized trajectories.

The contribution is therefore to locate where syndrome-component repeatability is retained, redirected or lost within one explicit causal pathway, rather than merely to show that the pathway can be partitioned into modules.

# Introduction

Syndromes are useful biological summaries because they identify trait combinations that recur across independent systems. Island syndromes describe repeated changes associated with isolation, selfing syndromes describe floral changes repeatedly associated with transitions toward autonomous reproduction, and pollination syndromes describe recurrent associations between floral phenotype and functional pollinator groups. Yet a syndrome is an outcome pattern, not a causal process. The same observed combination could arise because pollinator-mediated benefits changed, because reproductive assurance substituted for lost outcross service, because a different pollinator functional group imposed a new optimum, because only some traits possessed accessible genetic variation, or because finite populations filtered which responses persisted.

These distinctions are already partly developed in separate theoretical traditions. Fenster et al. (2004) defend pollination syndromes as functional-group hypotheses while identifying unresolved questions about selective factors, combined trait evolution and history. Ollerton et al. (2009) show that traditional discrete syndrome clusters often fit real flowers and their most frequent pollinators poorly and call for direct analysis of floral traits, pollinator traits, visitation and pollen transfer. Rosas-Guerrero et al. (2014), using effective pollinators, recover strong quantitative support for pollination-syndrome predictions, and Dellinger (2020) reframes the modern problem around system-specific constraints, pollinator-mediated selection and adaptive trade-offs. Mating-system models separately show how pollen limitation, pollen discounting and inbreeding depression can favour selfing through reproductive assurance (Porcher & Lande, 2005), while models of floral specialization show how pollinator abundance and effectiveness can alter the value of attraction and specialization (Sargent & Otto, 2006). Genetic work further shows that standing variation, major regulatory alleles, pleiotropy and developmental coupling can alter which transitions are accessible. The novelty pursued here is therefore not the statement that syndromes are imperfect causal explanations. It is to place these processes in one explicit eco-evolutionary pathway and intervene on the stages separately.

Island systems make that compositional problem especially clear. Isolation can simultaneously change visitor amount, visitor functional composition, opportunities for autonomous reproduction, plant gene flow and population size. Comparative island studies consequently report recurrent changes in pollination and breeding function without one universal floral endpoint. Chapter 1 of this dissertation formalizes that empirical tension: pollen limitation strengthens with isolation and reproductive assurance/accessibility recur, whereas detailed pollinator-facing display is less geographically repeatable. The unresolved problem is therefore not simply whether an island syndrome exists, but why repeatability can be strong at one functional layer and weak at another.

We address that problem with one shared biological model rather than separate syndrome-specific response rules. Plants carry diploid access/matching and floral-investment traits, with reproductive assurance fixed or inherited depending on the declared experiment. Visitor functional types determine finite compatible pollen transfer; outcrossing and delayed selfing produce viable offspring; Mendelian inheritance transmits trait variation; and recruitment, survival, immigration and finite population size determine which trajectories persist. The model is deliberately abstract: access is not literal corolla geometry and investment is not literal flower colour. This abstraction allows ecological selection, genetic accessibility and demographic realization to be manipulated separately before any molecular interpretation is imposed.

We ask five questions. First, can functional matching create different selection directions before inheritance or demography? Second, does the proposed reproductive-assurance × investment-cost reduction route remain negative across a declared envelope of pollination activity, inbreeding depression and annual versus perennial life histories? Third, is functional replacement of pollinators mechanistically distinct from simple visitor scarcity? Fourth, can unequal standing genetic variation make different syndrome components respond asynchronously under the same ecological selection, and does that ranking persist when de novo mutation input is scaled to an empirically anchored fraction of initial genetic variance and followed for 800 generations? Fifth, after these filters are separated, how do visitor history, plant-population finiteness, chronology and connectivity modify realized inherited trajectories? Natural island systems are used only for layer-specific plausibility and falsification; quantitative transfer to named islands is outside the present claim.

# Materials and Methods

## Ecological scope and claim boundary

The primary analysis is a frozen plant–pollinator eco-evolutionary model examined at three nested biological stages: reproductive selection, expected inherited change and finite-population realization. No empirical island outcome was used to tune the model or select the direction of any contrast. Trait values, time, distance and demographic frequencies are synthetic coordinates rather than calibrated natural estimates.

Natural evidence enters only after the synthetic objects and claim boundaries are defined. The confrontation layer combines a formal source audit of 25 research entries across 21 exact geographic labels, a broader source-verified descriptive programme that reached its geography-first stopping rule, source-native secondary reanalyses of compositional change, and existing Izu functional-network / pollen secondary analyses. The formal audit records whether each system directly observes comparable plant response, partner loss or arrival/replacement, realized community change and other mechanism-relevant coordinates without imputing missing axes from outcomes. The broader programme is used to assess biological vocabulary and search saturation, not to estimate natural prevalence.

The Izu secondary-data stress test is likewise deliberately asymmetric. We retain support when functional exposure predicts corrected trait matching, but also retain instability or failure when matching-to-pollen effects are not leave-one-island sign stable, historical signed-position projections fail null correction, or a bridge-state geographic contrast is not independently identified. The natural layer therefore constrains interpretation rather than selecting synthetic parameters.

The natural evidence is therefore a confrontation layer rather than a calibration layer. A future same-unit transition-linked study could directly test the full ecological chain, but current cross-sectional evidence cannot retrospectively identify the historical mechanism that generated a named island phenotype.

## Ecologically explicit reproductive and inheritance pathway

The model represents a focal plant population embedded in an externally supported visitor environment. Plants carry additive diploid loci for an abstract access/matching trait and floral investment, with reproductive assurance fixed or inherited depending on the declared experiment. Visitor functional types determine finite compatible pollen delivery. Outcrossing contributes maternal and paternal gametes; delayed selfing can fertilize remaining ovules; inbreeding depression reduces viable selfed offspring; and Mendelian inheritance precedes recruitment, density regulation and adult survival. No rule directly moves a floral trait toward an environmental optimum or toward the best visitor.

The island campaign crossed predeclared families for visitor-history transport, fixed-state reproductive assays, reproductive assurance, seed and pollinator connectivity, founding state, trait-grid representation, population scaling, life history, disturbance chronology and recovery. The completed campaign contains 19,968 audited cases across 80 production cells plus six held-out transport rows. All cases passed state/receipt audit and 80 predeclared deterministic replay checks. Extinct endpoints remained undefined rather than coded as zero.

Model 3 is interpreted at a bounded level. Its time step is a reproductive year, distances are standardized dispersal coordinates rather than kilometres, visitor types are functional agents rather than insect counts, and the floral-investment coordinate is not calibrated to colour, size or nectar-guide strength. Several numerical refinement contrasts remain outside the narrow prespecified tolerance, so qualitative directional contrasts and explicit survival outcomes receive greater inferential weight than exact effect magnitudes.

## Prospective syndrome-decomposition interventions

We added a sequence of prospective interventions without changing the existing Model 3 reproductive operator. Each design was committed before its corresponding outcome was inspected.

The first causal knockout separated pollinator scarcity, reproductive assurance and floral-investment cost. At a fixed starting access state and fixed four-type visitor composition, visitor activity was crossed at 0.05 and 0.4, fixed assurance at 0 and 0.5, and investment cost at 0 and 0.5. The primary endpoint was the fixed-state marginal total reproductive return to floral investment; deterministic inherited change, finite-population change and occupancy were secondary propagation endpoints. The preregistered signature of an assurance-by-cost route required a negative investment gradient under low service with assurance and cost present, together with upward movement of that gradient when either assurance or cost was removed.

A same-count rematching intervention then compared left-, centre- and right-shifted four-type visitor compositions at starting access states 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8, while activity, assurance, investment cost and visitor number were held fixed. This experiment distinguished change in functional composition from change in visitor amount.

A standing-variation pilot next changed only founder genetic variance. Access and investment founder means remained 0.5, mutation remained zero, and the ecological operator was unchanged. The equal-variation treatment used standing SD 0.15 on both axes; access-constrained and investment-constrained treatments reduced one axis to SD 0.03 while leaving the other at 0.15. We compared absolute terminal-minus-initial response on the constrained axis in deterministic genotype density and the finite ABM.

A subsequent mutation–pleiotropy experiment was prospectively frozen with depleted standing variation on both axes, trait-specific mutation-supply contrasts and positive mutational coupling over 400 reproductive years. Its preregistered endpoint was time to a sustained absolute change of 0.05 for 20 consecutive years. That test did not meet its mutation-accessibility or pleiotropy success criteria and was retained without lowering the threshold or extending the horizon.

Because the failed timing test left the investment axis almost completely censored, we designed a new prospective bottleneck diagnostic rather than re-tuning the failed experiment. This factorial crossed low versus restored investment standing variation (SD 0.03 versus 0.15), baseline versus elevated investment mutation supply, and plant capacity 48 versus 192 in left4 and right4 visitor environments. The primary endpoint was absolute terminal investment response at year 400. The decision tree separately evaluated the effects of standing variation, de novo mutation rescue and finite-population modulation of that rescue.

## Establishment robustness and mutation-input sensitivity

After the initial syndrome-decomposition experiments, we prospectively tested the two new headline candidates rather than treating their focal effect sizes as established.

For the assurance-by-cost route, a fixed-state response surface crossed visitor activity (0.025–0.4), assurance (0.25, 0.5, 0.75), investment cost (0.25, 0.5, 0.75) and inbreeding depression (0.25, 0.5, 0.75). The predeclared robustness rule required a non-isolated negative low-activity region, at least two adjacent negative activity levels at the central assurance/cost setting for every depression level, and negative inherited investment change at activity 0.05 across annual and two perennial sensitivity treatments. Adult survival 0.75 represented an expected multi-year life history; one perennial treatment retained the annual reproductive budget and the other scaled annual ovule and pollen budgets to 0.25 to approximate lifetime-budget matching. Failure of any component demoted the route from the headline mechanism.

For mutation input, Model 3 has no environmental variance and therefore cannot be calibrated to literal mutational heritability (V_M/V_E). We instead normalized per-generation mutation input to the founder investment allelic additive-variance proxy. Houle et al. (1996) report (V_G/V_M) on the order of 100 generations for morphological traits, motivating (V_M/V_{G,0}=0.01) as the central empirically anchored sensitivity; 0.001 and 0.03 were also tested. For a diploid trait mean (z=(a_1+a_2)/2) and rare independent mutation, the mutation-effect SD was chosen from (V_M \approx \mu\sigma_m^2/2). Low-standing populations (founder SD 0.03) were compared with a high-standing no-mutation reference (SD 0.15) at capacities 48 and 192 in left4 and right4 environments. Genetic-variance proxies and trait responses were followed to years 400 and 800. The predeclared drop rule removed the standing-variation dominance claim if the central mutation-input treatment exceeded the high-standing reference at year 400; year-800 and variance trajectories defined the time-horizon qualification.

## Prospective isolation-bridge controls

A 24,576-case bridge campaign was frozen before production outcomes were inspected. It crossed 128 independent visitor-history seeds, eight demographic repeats, three starting floral-investment states and four paired near-versus-far interventions: (1) natural isolation-driven visitor histories; (2) response-blind annual realized-richness matching by thinning both arms to the smaller annual count; (3) pooling eight independent visitor histories with count-scaled activity normalization; and (4) increasing plant capacity from 48 to 192 while retaining the natural visitor history. Each intervention was evaluated separately for the finite ABM and deterministic genotype-density counterpart.

The primary endpoint was far-minus-near terminal-minus-initial inherited investment. Directional branching was classified across the three starting states at fixed deadbands `0`, `0.01` and `0.05`, after averaging the eight demographic repeats within each independent history; repeat-specific and first-four versus last-four label instability were also retained. Mean uncertainty used 1,999 history-cluster bootstrap resamples. Extinct trait endpoints remained undefined rather than coded as zero.

Annual richness matching also changes visitor identity persistence, pooled histories alter environmental composition under a nonlinear reproductive operator, and increased plant capacity changes plant demographic stochasticity rather than visitor-community sampling. These are therefore orthogonal mechanism diagnostics, not literal field manipulations of species richness, island number or lifespan.

After the assurance-by-cost robustness test showed strong sensitivity to inbreeding depression, we prospectively propagated that sensitivity into the natural near–far deterministic backbone. We reran only the deterministic genotype-density counterpart at depression 0.25, 0.50 and 0.75, using the same 128 visitor histories, three starting investment states, founders, genotype grid and 200-year horizon as the frozen bridge. The preregistered robustness criterion required a negative overall mean, negative means for all three starting states, zero mixed histories and zero positive-only histories at every depression level. A negative mean with any mixed or positive-only histories was predefined as retention of the coarse negative backbone but failure of uniform one-directional repeatability.

## Layer-specific confrontation with real island systems

Natural evidence was not used to fit Model 3 parameters. Instead, already source-locked systems were classified by which nested model layer they could confront: **A**, functional composition / plant state / access / effectiveness and immediate reproductive response; **B**, an inherited longitudinal trajectory under a measured visitor regime; or **C**, finite-population and historical realization through assurance, chronology, connectivity, founding, recovery or persistence.

The formal source audit remains fixed at 25 research entries across 21 exact geographic labels, with 21/25 direct comparable plant responses, 2/25 direct partner arrival/replacement measurements and 0/25 full source-state → transition → realized-community → inherited-response contracts. The broader geography-first programme remains 42 research entries across 37 exact geographic labels. Those counts describe evidence breadth and identifiability, not independent island replicates or a denominator for natural branch frequencies.

For model-facing projection we used the previously source-locked propagation matrix of 14 biological system layers across 12 geographic clusters, supplemented by direct-history anchors recovered in the geography-first programme. We retained each source's original measurement scale and classified only the observed propagation structure: same-direction propagation, downstream branching, buffering/resilience, counterdirectional response, adjacent links only or unresolved missing links. No system was assigned a synthetic `k`, S/C/I regime, trait coordinate or Model 3 parameter cell.

Izu supplies the most resolved A-layer branching contrast: corrected matching is lower in all eight shared Oshima-to-post targets, while pollen response is four lower and four higher and floral-tube response is three shorter, four longer and one unchanged. Ogasawara *Psychotria* and Xisha *Cordia* provide stronger access/effectiveness-to-reproduction chains; Hawaii and Puerto Rico–Mona provide buffering examples; and the frozen Dominica *Heliconia* signed-position projection is retained as a counterdirectional falsifier. Surtsey, Tiritiri Matangi, New Zealand *Rhabdothamnus* and Mariana bird-loss systems are used as C-layer chronology, reintroduction/compensation or loss-response anchors. The current archive contains no clean natural B-layer analogue that jointly measures starting genetic/trait state, visitor regime and inherited longitudinal change while separating finite demography.

# Results

## Functional matching generates state-dependent reproductive selection

The prospective reduction audit crossed starting access states `0.20, 0.35, 0.50, 0.65, 0.80` with three four-type visitor compositions and a broad eight-type reference. Under each four-type composition, the fixed-state reproductive assay contained both positive and negative total investment gradients. For example, under `left4`, the gradient was `+1.5048` at starting access `0.20` but `-0.8720` at `0.80`; the signs reversed under `right4`.

Composition mattered at fixed count. The maximum left-versus-right difference in fixed-state total gradient was `2.3768`. By contrast, duplicating each `left4` functional type to produce eight visitor entries changed the fixed-state, deterministic and ABM operators by at most `1.78e-15` under fixed total activity. This is an operator control, not a claim that field species richness is irrelevant.

The deterministic genotype-density counterpart retained mixed positive and negative inherited investment changes in all three four-type visitor contexts, with a maximum left-versus-right endpoint difference of `0.1891`. Demographic stochasticity is therefore not necessary for response branching under these controlled visitor compositions.

The finite-population ABM also retained mixed signs in all three contexts. In this deliberately simple reduction audit, deterministic-density and mean-ABM response signs agreed in all evaluable cells. The larger island campaign nevertheless shows substantial ABM–density sign disagreement under chronology, assurance, life-history and recovery manipulations, showing that finite demography can strongly alter the realized response.

## Assurance-by-cost reduction is a conditional model mechanism

The initial focal knockout reproduced the expected negative gradient under low visitor activity, assurance and positive investment cost. The broader preregistered robustness surface showed that this was not an isolated numerical cell: 83.3% of the declared low-activity/high-assurance/high-cost region was negative. However, the location of that region depended strongly on inbreeding depression. At assurance 0.5 and investment cost 0.5, the estimated activity at which the gradient crossed zero shifted from approximately 0.182 at depression 0.25 to 0.096 at depression 0.50 and 0.040 at depression 0.75. Consequently, at the original activity 0.05 the gradient was already positive when depression was 0.75.

The inherited-response criterion also failed. Annual trajectories remained negative at depression 0.25 and 0.50 but became positive at 0.75. With adult survival 0.75 and the same annual reproductive budget, the finite-ABM mean was positive already at depression 0.50 and 0.75. The lifetime-budget-matched perennial sensitivity remained negative across all three depression levels, but that was insufficient to satisfy the preregistered all-life-history rule.

We therefore rejected the stronger interpretation of assurance-by-cost as a robust general adaptive-reduction route. The mechanism remains a coherent consequence of the declared reproductive-return and investment-cost functions over a substantial parameter region, but it is treated as a model-conditional sensitivity rather than a headline explanation of syndrome evolution.

## Pollinator replacement generates a distinct rematching route

Changing visitor functional composition at identical visitor number altered selection and inherited response. In the deliberately mirror-symmetric left4/right4 design, the left-minus-right fixed-state gradient contrast was +2.377 at starting access 0.2, approximately zero at the symmetric starting state 0.5 and -2.377 at 0.8; deterministic inherited-investment contrasts were +0.189, 0 and -0.189. The equal-and-opposite magnitudes and zero at 0.5 are consequences of the constructed symmetry and are not interpreted as discovered ecological thresholds.

The inferential result is narrower: at fixed visitor count, changing functional composition can redirect selection and inherited response for starting states away from the symmetry point. Functional rematching is therefore distinct from a simple change in visitor amount within the Model 3 operator.

## Standing genetic variation filters which syndrome components are reachable

Restricting standing variation in one trait axis selectively suppressed response on that axis while the ecological operator was held constant. Reducing access standing SD from 0.15 to 0.03 decreased its absolute deterministic response by 0.1136 on average across left4 and right4 environments; the finite-ABM difference was 0.0961. Reducing investment standing SD from 0.15 to 0.03 decreased absolute deterministic investment response by 0.1054 and finite-ABM response by 0.0830.

The within-environment contrasts show the selectivity of this filter. Under left4 visitors, equal-high standing variation produced deterministic changes of -0.150 in access and +0.119 in investment. Constraining access reduced access change to -0.0428 while investment remained +0.106; constraining investment left access at -0.148 while investment fell to +0.0104. The same ecological environment can therefore generate incomplete or asynchronous syndrome components because selection and accessible genetic variation are distinct stages.

## Standing variation leads over the tested finite horizon, but mutation narrows the gap

The standing-variation manipulation remained a strong genetic filter: constraining one trait axis suppressed response specifically on that axis while the ecological operator was unchanged. The first preregistered mutation–pleiotropy timing experiment did not meet its success criteria and remains a negative result.

The subsequent mutation-input sensitivity showed that the earlier effect-size comparison was too dependent on the chosen mutation parameters. The older (mu=10^{-3}, sigma_m=0.04) setting supplied only about 0.00194 of the low-standing initial additive-variance proxy per generation, roughly one fifth of the new central 0.01 anchor. Under the empirically anchored central input, mean absolute investment response in the high-standing no-mutation reference was 0.1968 at year 400 versus 0.0769 in the low-standing mutation treatment. At year 800 the corresponding values were 0.2015 and 0.1354. Thus the ranking did not reverse, but the high/low response ratio narrowed from about 2.56 to 1.49.

The genetic-variance trajectories also rejected an equilibrium interpretation. Most mutation-input cells were not near a variance plateau between years 600 and 800, and trajectories were strongly nonstationary. We therefore retain only a finite-horizon statement: pre-existing standing variation led the response over 400–800 generations under the central mutation-input sensitivity, while de novo mutation progressively narrowed the gap. The earlier 9.4% mutation-rescue/standing-variation ratio is not retained as a general effect size.

## Isolation assembly retains a negative mean backbone but not uniform history-level direction

The 24,576-case prospective bridge closed the original visitor-amount and finite-realization controls. At the original inbreeding depression 0.50, the finite ABM mean inherited-investment effect was -0.1446 (95% history-bootstrap interval -0.1588 to -0.1306) and the deterministic-density mean was -0.4510 (-0.4716 to -0.4301). The deterministic response was negative-only across all 128 histories at epsilon 0, whereas the finite ABM contained mixed histories.

The prospective depression propagation preserved the **mean** deterministic direction but not the stronger history-level uniformity claim. Overall far-minus-near deterministic effects were -0.3506, -0.4510 and -0.4142 at depression 0.25, 0.50 and 0.75, respectively; the three starting-state means were negative at every depression level. At depression 0.25 and 0.50, all 128 histories were negative-only across starting states. At depression 0.75, however, only 116/128 remained negative-only, 11/128 were mixed across starting states and 1/128 was positive-only. Thus higher inbreeding depression did not reverse the coarse mean isolation effect, but it erased the previously uniform deterministic direction for a subset of visitor histories before finite demographic stochasticity was introduced.

Annual response-blind visitor-count matching still changed the coarse regime at the original depression 0.50: the mean effect became positive in both finite ABM (+0.0333, 0.0245 to 0.0425) and deterministic density (+0.0338, 0.0236 to 0.0444). Finite-ABM mixed histories increased strongly, and deterministic density also became mixed near zero after matching. Visitor amount therefore shifts the mean regime, while reproductive context and visitor history determine whether that mean direction is repeated across histories.

Pooling eight independent visitor histories eliminated mixed labels in the original bridge, whereas increasing plant capacity from 48 to 192 moved the finite-population mean toward the deterministic counterpart and nearly removed finite-ABM mixed labels. These interventions remain mechanistically distinct and are not interpreted as estimates of a natural branch prevalence.

The resulting hierarchy is more specific than the original “directional backbone” wording: **the coarse deterministic mean is repeatable across the tested inbreeding-depression envelope, but deterministic history-level direction itself is conditional on reproductive context.**

## Reproductive assurance preserves trajectories while history changes inherited endpoints

Model 3 did not collapse conditional response into one inherited island phenotype. Under the declared chronology experiment, all compared populations experienced the same final 120-year environment, yet terminal investment differed by history: uninterrupted trajectories changed by +0.2115, early visitor absence by -0.1603, and late visitor absence by +0.0322. All 256 populations survived in each of these three arms. The result therefore demonstrates model-conditional historical contingency rather than a simple mapping from current environment to current trait.

Reproductive assurance changed whether an endpoint existed. Under the declared long visitor-absence schedule, fixed zero assurance yielded 0/256 terminal survivors, whereas fixed 0.5, fixed 0.9 and the corresponding evolving-assurance treatments retained 256/256. This survival contrast is conditional on the model's complete visitor absence, adult replacement schedule and lack of external seed rescue; it is not an empirical extinction probability.

Connectivity also separated into different routes. Increasing pollinator-distance while seed-distance was fixed could reverse the direction of investment change, whereas increasing seed-distance under a fixed pollinator regime altered the response differently. Seed immigration modifies demographic and genetic input; pollinator connectivity modifies the reproductive environment. A single geographic-isolation axis therefore need not represent both processes inside the model.

Transport tests further separated structural ordering from quantitative prediction. The descriptive S/C/I ordering C > I > S was retained in held-out transport cells, but same-regime marginal predictions had mean absolute error 0.0150 and 0.0106, whereas transport across disturbance regimes increased errors to 0.1581 and 0.1417. Similar determinant ordering therefore does not guarantee transport of the trait response itself.
## Numerical sensitivity and branch-identifiability limits

The numerical audit separated operator consistency from continuous-trait convergence. Re-embedding the same finite founder allele support on a finer grid reproduced individual trajectories exactly and density trajectories to machine precision. In contrast, all 16 comparisons that changed the representation of the initial allele distribution failed to establish ±0.01 equivalence. The signs of the main mean contrasts were retained across the frozen grid-sensitivity designs, but exact effect magnitudes and mixed-history counts were not treated as grid invariant. Mixed classifications were also unstable across demographic repeats, especially after richness matching. We therefore interpret mean directional contrasts and intervention responses more strongly than exact mixed fractions, and we do not infer latent branch prevalence or a continuous-trait PDE limit.

## Real islands occupy different stages of the same response architecture

The 14 source-locked biological system layers do not converge on one propagation pattern. Their descriptive states comprise one same-direction propagation case, two downstream-branching cases, three buffered/resilient cases, one counterdirectional case, four adjacent-link cases and three unresolved missing-link cases. These counts are not natural prevalence estimates; the rows are heterogeneous and not independent sampling units. Their value is structural: natural island evidence already contains propagation, branching, buffering and reversal rather than one universal downstream direction.

Izu provides the strongest current branching example. Corrected matching is lower in all eight shared Oshima-to-post targets, yet pollen receipt is lower in four and higher in four, while tube response is three shorter, four longer and one unchanged. Only two of eight targets show the complete matching-lower + tube-shorter + pollen-lower combination. Thus an upstream common functional shift need not propagate into one morphological or reproductive direction.

Other systems occupy different portions of the architecture. In Ogasawara *Psychotria*, morph-specific physical access is associated with directional pollen transfer and strong open-fruit-set asymmetry, providing a same-direction A-layer example. Xisha *Cordia* combines morph-state loss, very low visitation and lower natural fruit set on Dong with direct effectiveness/dependency measurements at Yongxing, although the measurements are asymmetric between islands. Hawaii lobelioids and Puerto Rico–Mona *Guaiacum* provide buffering examples in which altered interaction structure or visitor environment does not translate one-for-one into reproductive loss or mating-system change. The frozen Dominica *Heliconia* projection ran opposite to its declared signed-position prediction and is retained as evidence against a universal mapping.

Direct-history systems are most informative for the finite/history realization layer. Surtsey provides a dated empty-start chronology; Tiritiri Matangi provides documented pollinator reintroduction followed by compensatory function from other birds; and New Zealand *Rhabdothamnus* and Mariana bird-loss systems link partner loss to reproductive or recruitment consequences. None closes the full A → B → C chain on the same units.

The principal natural-data gap is therefore no longer generic 'validation'. A is partly confronted by several systems and C is partly confronted by direct-history systems, whereas B—the inherited deterministic trajectory under a measured visitor regime—is essentially unobserved in the current archive. The existing natural evidence supports the ecological vocabulary and supplies falsifiers without calibrating Model 3 or establishing the historical cause of any named island phenotype.
# Discussion

## Where repeatability is retained and lost

Pollination syndromes have long been debated as functional hypotheses rather than unique mechanistic explanations. Fenster et al. (2004) emphasize functional pollinator groups and differential selection, Ollerton et al. (2009) expose poor fit of many traditional discrete categories, Rosas-Guerrero et al. (2014) recover substantial predictive power when effective pollinators are considered, and Dellinger (2020) emphasizes evolutionary constraints, pollinator-mediated selection and trade-offs. Our contribution is therefore not the proposition that syndrome labels are outcomes rather than mechanisms, nor the architectural fact that Model 3 was built from separable modules.

The result is empirical within the numerical experiment: prospectively defined interventions locate **where repeatability is retained, redirected or lost**. The natural near–far deterministic mean remained negative across depression 0.25–0.75, so coarse directional repeatability survived a substantial change in reproductive context. Yet at depression 0.75, 12 of 128 histories no longer shared the negative-only direction across starting states, showing that history-level repeatability can fail within deterministic inheritance before demographic stochasticity is added. Controlled functional replacement redirects selection at fixed visitor number; standing variation filters how strongly a selected trait can respond; and finite visitor histories and plant demography further alter realized outcomes.

The relevant novelty is therefore a map of repeatability loss along the causal chain. Different stages do not merely exist as modules: the same perturbation is carried through them, and the analysis records whether a repeated direction survives each transition.

## Reproductive assurance affects persistence, but its adaptive-reduction route is conditional

The focal assurance-by-cost knockout is useful mechanistically because it distinguishes reduced pollinator-mediated benefit from a countervailing cost: within the declared equations, assurance can keep a reproductive route open while costly floral investment loses marginal value. The robustness analysis, however, shows why this mechanism should not be elevated to a general explanation of selfing-syndrome floral reduction. Its sign boundary moved strongly with inbreeding depression and did not propagate consistently across the declared perennial treatments.

Accordingly, the vNext uses reproductive assurance primarily for the result that is already robust in Model 3: it can determine whether an evolutionary endpoint exists under severe pollinator loss without prescribing one floral phenotype. The assurance-by-cost negative-gradient result remains a conditional sensitivity showing one way adaptive reduction can arise, not an empirical discovery that selfing generally causes reduced pollinator-facing investment.

## Functional rematching is different from pollinator loss

At fixed visitor number, changing functional visitor composition reversed the selection contrast across starting access states. A lineage already matched to one visitor environment and a lineage matched to another therefore need not respond in the same direction to pollinator replacement. This is a pollination-syndrome route rather than a simple service-loss route: the relevant quantity is not only how much visitation remains, but which visitor phenotypes deliver compatible pollen to which floral states.

That result also explains why the same decline in visitor richness can have different phenotypic consequences across islands. If the surviving visitor community differs functionally, selection can move toward a new match rather than simply reduce investment. Visitor scarcity and functional replacement should therefore be measured separately in empirical tests.

## Genetic accessibility can break phenotypic convergence after selection is shared

The standing-variation experiments demonstrate a downstream source of non-convergence: the same visitor environment and selection operator produced different response magnitudes when one trait axis had little available variation. Shared ecological selection therefore does not imply shared evolution if selectable variation differs among trait modules or lineages.

The mutation-input sensitivity narrows the stronger interpretation. Pre-existing variation led the investment response at both 400 and 800 generations under a central mutation input equal to 1% of initial additive variance per generation, but de novo mutation reduced the gap substantially through time and most variance trajectories had not stabilized by year 800. This is evidence for a finite-horizon accessibility filter, not a universal ranking of standing variation over mutation. It also confirms that the earlier order-of-magnitude effect-size contrast depended on a mutation input below the new central anchor.

This does not imply that flower colour is generally easy to evolve or floral architecture generally difficult. Different visible traits may differ in standing variation, mutational target size, effect-size distributions, regulatory architecture and developmental coupling. The model motivates those quantities as measurements; it does not assign a universal genetic hierarchy to colour and shape.

## Pollination ecology and realized evolution remain distinct biological stages

The expanded syndrome decomposition retains the original three-layer result. Functional matching can reverse the marginal reproductive return to floral investment before inheritance or demographic updating. Deterministic inheritance then shows which response is expected if demographic sampling is removed, and the finite ABM determines whether that response persists through recruitment, extinction, ancestry change and loss of variation. What is favoured, what is genetically reachable, what is expected to evolve and what is actually realized are therefore distinct biological objects.

Controlled visitor compositions demonstrated branch capacity, but isolation-driven visitor assembly produced a one-directional deterministic inherited response across all 128 visitor histories. Finite populations reintroduced realized sign heterogeneity in some histories. The existence of multiple potential routes therefore does not mean that every isolation regime will express them equally.

## A common pollination problem can produce repeatable function without repeatable form

The original isolation bridge remains the macroecological middle layer of this argument, but its interpretation is now more precise. Across inbreeding depression 0.25–0.75, the deterministic **mean** near–far effect remained negative and every starting-state mean remained negative. This supports a repeatable coarse response direction within the tested envelope. What failed at depression 0.75 was the stronger claim that every visitor history expresses that direction: 11 histories became mixed across starting states and one became positive-only.

Repeatability is therefore scale dependent even before finite demography. A common pollination problem can preserve a directional mean while losing lineage/history-level uniformity. The later filters compound that loss: functional replacement can redirect selection, available standing variation changes how much response is reachable, and visitor-history sampling plus finite plant demography change which inherited trajectories are realized.

This hierarchy provides the bridge to Chapter 1 without requiring a universal synthetic backbone. Reproductive assurance and accessibility can recur as broad functions while detailed display remains non-convergent because repeatability is progressively weakened by state, reproductive context, genetic accessibility, chronology and finite realization.

## Geographic isolation compresses distinct ecological connections

A single geographic-isolation coordinate can combine biologically different processes. Pollinator connectivity changes the functional reproductive environment; seed connectivity changes demographic and genetic input. In the 3 × 3 connectivity experiment, changing visitor distance and changing seed distance altered inherited floral investment through different routes and could move it in different directions.

This separation is especially relevant for island comparisons. A geographically remote population may experience poor effective pollination, restricted gene flow, both, or neither, depending on vector movement and source availability. Treating all of these as one isolation mechanism risks attributing a floral response to pollinator limitation when the relevant difference is demographic or genetic connectivity, or vice versa. Model 3 does not resolve those channels in existing observational datasets, but it makes their distinct measurements explicit.

## Natural systems provide confrontation, not quantitative transfer

The source-audited island evidence supports the biological plausibility of several modeled stages and supplies adversarial cases without calibrating Model 3. Izu shows a common upstream decline in corrected matching with divergent downstream pollen and tube responses. Ogasawara provides a more coherent access-to-pollen-to-reproduction example, while Hawaii and Puerto Rico–Mona show buffering and Dominica provides a counterdirectional case. Direct-history systems document founding, partner loss and reintroduction effects.

Quantitative transfer is outside the present claim. The formal audit contains no complete same-unit source-state -> measured visitor transition -> inherited longitudinal response -> finite-demographic realization contract (0/25), and existing systems differ in response scale, exposure definition and independent unit. Assigning them to synthetic parameter cells would therefore imply a calibration that the evidence does not support.

This is treated as a limitation rather than unfinished analysis. The model-derived stage decomposition can be confronted qualitatively now; quantitative transfer requires future same-population longitudinal data linking visitor transition, inherited response and demographic realization.

## Ecological validity and limits

The model is ecologically explicit but not system calibrated. Its strengths come from preserving the causal order of pollination, mating, inheritance and demography, from separating seed and pollinator connectivity, and from keeping extinction distinct from trait change. Its limitations are equally important. Visitor types are functional agents rather than measured species abundances; background flora supports visitors rather than being coevolved explicitly; floral investment and access are abstract traits rather than named colours, corolla dimensions or nectar guides; inbreeding depression is fixed rather than dynamically purged; and time and distance are model coordinates rather than years and kilometres for a particular archipelago.

These limitations define the inference. The model can identify which ecological processes are sufficient to change selection, expected inherited response and finite-population realization. It cannot estimate natural evolutionary rates, reconstruct a named historical island transition or predict the exact floral phenotype expected in a given region. The useful generalization is therefore structural: ecological function can be more repeatable than phenotypic form because the pathway from pollination to realized evolution contains multiple biologically distinct filters.

# Conclusion

A recurrent pollination problem can yield recurrent functional responses without a recurrent detailed floral phenotype because repeatability can persist in the coarse mean response while being redirected or lost by state-dependent selection, reproductive context, genetic accessibility and finite-population realization.

The strongest vNext evidence is not simply that these stages are separable. It is that the same pollination perturbation retains or loses repeatability differently as it passes through them. The natural near–far deterministic mean remained negative across inbreeding depression 0.25–0.75, but the previously uniform history-level deterministic direction failed at depression 0.75. Functional matching and pollinator replacement can redirect selection; reproductive assurance can determine persistence without prescribing one endpoint; standing variation can limit the magnitude of a selected response while de novo mutation narrows that limitation through time; and visitor history, plant demography, chronology and connectivity further filter realized inherited outcomes.

Two stronger candidate conclusions remain excluded from the headline claim. The assurance-by-cost negative-gradient route did not survive its declared robustness envelope, and the earlier order-of-magnitude standing-variation versus mutation effect-size ratio was input-dependent. The new depression propagation also retires the unqualified statement that natural isolation produces a uniformly one-directional deterministic response across all histories. Retaining these failures and qualifications identifies the actual scale at which repeatability survives.

Chapter 1 identifies which broad syndrome components recur in nature. Chapter 2 identifies where that repeatability is retained, redirected or lost in a numerical causal chain without assigning synthetic effect sizes to named islands. Quantitative natural transfer remains outside scope until longitudinal inherited-response data connect the same ecological transition units across the model's stages.
