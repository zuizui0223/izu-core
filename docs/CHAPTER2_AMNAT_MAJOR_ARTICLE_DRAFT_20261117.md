# Reproductive assurance can evolve first while floral investment varies in an island plant model

**Article type:** Major Article — anonymous author-review manuscript

**Running head:** Assurance and floral evolution

**Keywords:** reproductive assurance; mating system; floral investment; genetic accessibility; evolutionary predictability; island syndrome

**Main-text word count (including legends, excluding Literature Cited and Abstract):** 5159

## Abstract

Classical island syndrome predictions pair selfing with reduced floral display. We asked whether selection for autonomous seed production also predicts declining investment in attraction. In a finite diploid model, delayed selfing filled ovules missed by outcross pollen. When plants gained seeds according to their own assurance, assurance rose before investment fell in 17/32 populations, compared with 1/32 when filling probabilities were pooled at the same expected seed total for a given parental state. In the other 15 populations with individual returns, only assurance crossed the sustained response threshold. Attraction still affected pollen receipt and export and cost resources otherwise available for ovules. Favourable alleles could disappear during finite transmission. Investment could decline with assurance fixed. Evolving assurance narrowed investment differences between visitor environments in three of four fresh-founder settings but widened them at both resource budgets with a persistent visitor. Registered experiments showed improved survival under harsh resources and greater loss of alleles supporting ancestral investment where all populations survived. Similar floral displays on different islands could therefore conceal inherited differences in reproductive assurance.

## Introduction

Self-compatible plants are overrepresented on islands, consistent partly with colonization filtering (Grossenbacher et al., 2017; Zell et al., 2025). Floral size shows a less uniform pattern: some sister-taxon comparisons found no average reduction, while others found pollination-mode-dependent trends (Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson, 2020; Ciarle et al., 2025). Why might reproductive assurance recur without a corresponding reduction in attraction? In a plant with both sexual functions, delayed selfing can fill ovules left unfertilized by outcross pollen. Attracting a visitor can also deliver pollen to another plant. These routes raise two distinct questions about evolution after arrival: must assurance evolve for investment in attraction to decline, and does allowing assurance to evolve change how investment responds to pollinator limitation?

Selection during island colonization need not be equivalent to evolution after establishment: phylogenetic analyses of island *Coprosma* distinguish biased colonization from in situ morphological change (Ciarle, 2026). Colonists carry a sample of ancestral variation, so the traits favoured after arrival may differ from the changes genetically available to them. Visitor communities can also differ in both abundance and functional identity. A flower may become better matched to the remaining visitors without increasing its total investment in attraction. Conversely, a population may sustain seed production through selfing while losing variants required for a later floral response.

Classical genetic models already address selfing and inbreeding depression (Lande & Schemske, 1985). Other models link selfing to floral allocation and plant–pollinator demography (Sakai, 1995; Lepers et al., 2014), and reproductive assurance can rescue pollen-limited populations (Cheptou, 2019; Xu, 2023). Replicate monkeyflower populations evolved higher autonomous seed production after pollinator exclusion (Bodbyl Roels & Kelly, 2011); sequencing from that experiment revealed genome-wide loss of diversity (Busch et al., 2022). Floral reduction can accompany changing pollination and mating, with both selection and inbreeding contributing (Acoca-Pidolle et al., 2024; Tusuubira & Kelly, 2024). Partial selfing alters quantitative genetic variation (Lande & Porcher, 2015), while trait covariances can constrain adaptive responses despite selection (Bishop et al., 2026). On the Izu Islands, self-compatible *Campanula* largely outcrossed on Toshima and Niijima but predominantly inbred on Miyake and Hachijo (Inoue, 1990). Compatibility permits selfing; its realized contribution depends on the population.

Removing pollinators changes both the opportunity for outcrossing and the value of selfing. The monkeyflower experiments therefore leave open a narrower question. Given the same pollen receipts and the same expected number of seeds in a parental population, does it matter for evolutionary order which plants receive the extra seeds? We compare two rules for filling unfertilized ovules, then ask whether investment must follow assurance and whether the investment gap between visitor environments grows or shrinks. Finally, we follow transmission into offspring and compare the populations that survive with those retaining alleles for higher investment. Whereas genome-wide diversity summarizes variation across loci, this comparison asks whether a living population retains alleles sufficient for one specified direction of floral change.

## Material and Methods

### Model traits, reproductive accounting and finite inheritance

We analyzed a single mechanistic Model 3 family of finite diploid plant populations. Three inherited components were treated separately: autonomous reproductive assurance A, functional matching to visitors M and resource investment in attraction I. Visitor encounters depended on imposed visitor identities, optima and effectiveness; flowers gained maternal pollen receipt and paternal pollen export at an allocation cost that reduced available ovules. Selfing could fill ovules left unfertilized by outcross pollen, subject to the specified timing, inbreeding depression, costs and pollen-discount rules. Each census updated recruited diploid descendants through Mendelian transmission and finite population regulation. These are synthetic trait coordinates: A is not a field-measured self-compatibility category, M is not flower color or shape, and I is not corolla diameter. Visitor communities did not coevolve. Cohorts had different budgets, capacities, horizons and mutation settings; their raw units were not pooled as replicated natural islands (Supplementary Table S1).

For the cost-free delayed-selfing state, let O denote available ovules, q the fraction outcross-fertilized, δ the viability penalty of selfing and A the assurance fraction. Expected viable maternal seeds at that fixed state are Y = O[q + (1−δ)A(1−q)]. The local maternal benefit of raising A is O(1−δ)(1−q), which is nonnegative where viable selfing fills an otherwise unused ovule. This is not the whole lifetime fitness contribution: paternal pollen export, costs, inheritance, recruitment and time to fixation must also be evaluated. When A expression was fixed at 0.5, plants could still self and transmit latent A alleles; the intervention restricted expressed assurance evolution, not self-fertilization.

### Individually retained versus pooled assurance returns

The prospectively specified private-return diagnostic paired 32 founding, visitor and demographic-seed blocks, each initially containing 48 balanced diploids with expressed A=0.5. Native cost-free delayed selfing assigned every mother her own filling probability. In the counterfactual, each mother received the unfilled-ovule-weighted mean filling probability at that parental state; the two arms thus had the same expected total viable selfed seeds and outcross matrix **at a common parental state**, while eliminating the association of own A with the within-state filling benefit. Once histories diverged, their later seed totals and demographic conditions were not constrained equal. Both arms retained genotype-dependent transmission. The model used budget B=8, one persistent visitor, no pollen discount, mutation or immigration, and 200 updates.

We classified a genetic change as sustained when A rose or I fell by at least 0.05 from its actual founder mean for 20 consecutive censuses, with a five-update window for near simultaneity. The primary endpoint was the fraction of paired blocks in which A crossed before a subsequent I decline; a two-sided exact paired binomial test used discordant blocks. Any sustained A increase was secondary. An A-only trajectory was not counted as A-first followed by investment decline. This is one mating regime, not a general measured frequency of island evolution.

### Fixed versus evolving A and the resource-dependent investment gap

The original R1/R2 experiment compared A-expression-fixed and A-evolving policies under higher and lower visitor replenishment in four mating settings, using 64 independent visitor histories with eight nested demographic repeats. A distinct fresh-founder campaign crossed eight independent founder genotypes and eight visitor histories, four mating settings, two environments and two A policies (1,024 main paths, plus 64 zero-mutation technical controls); main paths ran 1,000 updates. Paired source identities and founder-by-visitor resampling were preserved. The registered conjunctive decision across all four settings remained inconclusive even though three setting-specific comparisons supported compression. Both original near and far regimes were pollen limited.

In a separate prospective descriptive bridge, the 32 founding blocks from the private-return diagnostic were reused, not independently resampled. We crossed A-expression policy, one persistent visitor at activity 0.4 versus 1.6, and B=4.5 versus B=8, following 256 source histories over 80 updates. For each complete source block we computed the high-minus-low investment gap under fixed A minus the corresponding gap under evolving A. At the endpoint we measured genetic means of A, M and I, expression, occupied high-I support, I polymorphism, realized selfed recruitment and survival from all starts. Whole occupied populations were moved intact to a common visitor destination (optimum 0.7, activity 1.6, B=8) for 40 updates; transfer thereby omitted dispersal or founding bottlenecks.

### Repeatability and predictions across archived sources

Exploratory matched histories compared far-environment A and I endpoints within four mating settings × three founding states × 128 visitor histories × four demographic repeats and two environments (12,288 arm trajectories through 200 updates). Variance and four-repeat agreement were conditioned on complete survival of eligible paired histories. A different B8 cohort crossed 160 founder IDs with 160 visitor-history IDs at census 80. Held-out prediction removed one crossed founder–history cell but retained other cells bearing its already observed source IDs; squared-error reduction for M or I was compared with an intercept-only predictor. Each crossed cell had one finite realization, so residual error cannot isolate stochastic drift from founder-by-history interaction. Visitor optima varied under a common count history and M carried no separate allocation cost.

The distinct deterministic genotype-density audit matched two founding backgrounds and five visitor settings to 32 finite ABM histories per context, but the density model is a discrete expected-density approximation rather than the exact ensemble mean of the finite simulation. It tests agreement of threshold categories under specified source conditions, not a quantitative drift fraction.

### Registered genetic support and demographic persistence

In the preregistered R4 comparison, 160 founder-source seeds × 160 visitor-history seeds yielded 25,600 crossed source cells, each exposed to fixed or evolving A expression, with the same founding genotype and expressed A=0.5, and B8 or B4.5 resource budgets. The capacity was 48, with delayed selfing, fixed inbreeding depression 0.5, no adult survival, mutation or seed immigration, and 80 updates. Six visitors initially declined to one in updates 5–34 and zero from update 35. The fixed-expression arm could still genetically fix latent A variants.

Two primary endpoints were declared separately: G, the B8 policy difference in loss of *alleles compatible with one founder-relative high-I investment target*, and S, the A-policy-by-budget interaction in all-start survival. Once the necessary standing alleles are gone under closed inheritance, the target is inaccessible; their presence does not establish realized recovery. Intervals were adjusted to 97.5% under the original crossed-source convention. The 25,600 cells shared only 160 founding and 160 visitor source identities: they are neither 25,600 independent founders nor natural islands. A single realization per source cell prevents separate estimates of stochastic residual and source interaction. Retrospective joint survival-plus-support and five target-tolerance readouts were not promoted to new registered endpoints.

### Exploratory later transfers and inferential boundaries

R5 sampled 32 B8 donors from four predefined founder indices × four visitor indices × two historical assurance policies. Two seed cohorts per donor, five imposed destination settings and 32 futures per cohort yielded 10,240 futures. Each transfer had at most 12 seedlings; all viable seeds were assumed to establish at the destination. Destination A could evolve regardless of source policy. We distinguished all-start occupancy, founder-relative high-I support and I polymorphism from survivor-conditioned investment and matching responses. These are nested, exploratory trajectories, not new confirmation of G or S.

Across all cohorts, original negative and inconclusive registered outcomes remain intact, alongside exploratory and numerical controls. Original random-stream identities, source-unit denominators, exact interventions, code versions and raw-archive receipts are inventoried for the supplementary source ledger; they are not independently verified external deposits.

## Results

The five results distinguish intervention evidence, exploratory repeatability and conditional transfer. Original Q1–Q4 outcomes, including null results, are indexed in Supplementary Table S1, including contrary results and their denominators. Cohorts are not pooled as independent islands.

### Private assurance returns and investment decline without assurance evolution

In the paired cost-free delayed-selfing pilot, pooling filling probabilities across unfilled ovules preserved total expected selfed seeds at a given parental state. Assurance rose first and investment later fell in 17/32 native versus 1/32 pooled histories (17 native-only versus one pooled-only discordant pair; two-sided exact paired P=0.000145). All 32 pairs remained occupied at update 200 (Fig. 1). In the other 15 native histories only assurance crossed its sustained threshold. Thus the individual seed return increased the frequency of the complete sequence, while almost half of the native populations crossed only the assurance threshold. Failure to cross the threshold allows smaller or transient declines. Paternal returns to attraction and loss of alleles during finite inheritance are candidate explanations for the absence of a sustained decline. Their contributions in those 15 histories remain unresolved.

With expressed assurance fixed at A=0.5, floral investment declined under low visitor replenishment in four reproductive settings. Fixed assurance still permits selfing: the intervention blocks evolution of its capacity, not self-fertilization. In an independent eight-founder × eight-visitor study, fixed-A investment change from the actual founder was negative for all eight founder-averaged outcomes in each of four mating settings (descriptive across-cell means −0.217 to −0.287). This supports the non-necessity of A evolution beyond the original shared founder. A fixed-state investment gradient changed from +0.579 under higher visitor replenishment to −0.700 under lower replenishment, demonstrating context-dependent local returns rather than a guaranteed genetic response. Assurance and investment therefore need not evolve as successive steps of one syndrome (Fig. 2; Supplementary Table S1).

With assurance held constant, investment still trades pollen receipt and export against ovule production. Low investment relative to another environment need not mean decline from founding: in the persistent-visitor comparison, mean fixed-policy investment rose at both activities but rose less at low activity.

### Assurance policy changes investment contrasts across environments

Allowing A evolution compressed the near/far investment difference in all four original settings. This effect transported to three of four independent fresh-founder settings; the prospectively registered four-setting decision remained inconclusive because the direct-assurance-cost interaction interval included zero (Fig. 3). Within those fresh data, evolving-minus-fixed near-side I was negative in all four settings, but far-side I was also negative in two: compression is not universal protection of investment under severe limitation. Both original visitor regimes were pollen limited, so this is not a mainland-versus-island contrast. The interventions show that assurance can modify floral evolution without being required for its initiation.

The effect is compatible with altered reproductive returns, but does not identify its entire mechanism. Association-disruption extensions established neither assurance-specific hitchhiking nor its absence. In a separate exploratory 384-path baseline-pollen factorial, additional visitor decline produced negative I contrasts under strong baseline limitation but positive contrasts relative to maintained-activity controls under weak limitation; additional A response was also greater after weak limitation. Both inherited history and absolute pollen delivery differed, so this does not isolate a pure evolutionary-memory mechanism. These results separate a demonstrated policy effect from unresolved physiological and genetic mediation.

In a separate comparison reusing the 32 founders from the private-return pilot, each founder experienced two activity levels of a persistent visitor. In these paired populations, the high-minus-low investment gap at B8 widened from 0.068 with assurance fixed to 0.104 with assurance evolving; it also widened at B4.5. Delayed selfing reduces the maternal value of additional outcross pollen, while export and ovule costs remain. The policy contrasts identify a change in the gap; the designs differ in several respects and do not identify the cause of its reversal.

### Repeatability, visitor matching and finite genetic realization

An exploratory readback of matched repeated histories found lower within-context far-endpoint variance for A than I in all twelve setting-by-start summaries. For central starts, four repeats reached identical A endpoints in 66–111 of 128 histories, versus 7–20 for I. This endpoint concentration is not proof of consistently strong selection: fixation and standing-variation boundaries can contribute (Fig. 4a; Supplementary Table S1). In central delayed-control starts, 103/128 contexts had unanimous A far-minus-near contrast signs but all 103 were zero contrasts. In a distinct B8 crossed cohort, 25,307/25,600 (98.9%) evolving-policy populations fixed genetically at the highest available A value (0.75), versus 5,145/25,600 (20.1%) under fixed expression, which still transmits latent A alleles. Across all A values, 25,582 and 15,664 populations fixed, respectively. The descriptive policy contrast supports a non-neutral direction in this model, but does not isolate selection from absorbing fixation or estimate their independent contributions (Supplementary Table S1).

In a separate 160-founder by 160-visitor-history cohort, held-out prediction of I change at B8 gained little from source identities. With evolving assurance, founder-only skill was −0.46%, history-only +0.36%, and additive skill −0.09%; the fixed-A comparison was similarly weak. Secondary history-only prediction of M change improved held-out squared error by 40.15% (Fig. 4b), versus +0.36% for history-only I change. These are improvements over a held-out intercept for unobserved pairings of known sources, not a fraction of flower morphology explained, an estimate for new founders or a fraction of I change caused by chance. Visitor histories varied matching optima under a shared count schedule; all visitors disappeared after update 35. Thus the result describes trait-specific predictability of historical effects, not universal environmental predictability.

The 97.622% combined residual for evolving-I change contains founder-by-history interaction and stochastic realization, which a single trajectory per crossed source cell cannot separate. It cannot be called a drift or chance fraction. Separate fixed-A repeated-history analyses found environmental structure in I, with eight-repeat reliability increasing from .621 to .825 at larger capacity. Density predictions likewise did not determine finite realization: two A-first density contexts produced that category in only 3/32 and 2/32 finite histories. These distinct designs show why weak main-effect prediction does not imply intrinsically unpredictable floral evolution (Supplementary Table S1).

### Persistence and founder-relative genetic options

The preregistered 160-by-160 comparison matched initial expressed A at 0.5. At B8, all populations survived to census80; founder-relative high-I support was lost in 16,000/25,600 evolving-policy cells versus 13,868/25,600 fixed-policy cells. The difference was +8.33 percentage points (adjusted 97.5% interval 6.69–9.96). Under harsh resources, survival was 25,118/25,600 versus 1,385/25,600; the registered policy-by-resource survival interaction was +92.71 points (92.05–93.36; Fig. 5).

These are different resource conditions, not evidence that survival caused genetic erosion or was purchased through it. The genetic outcome concerns alleles compatible with one investment target, not general evolvability. All five prespecified descriptive high-I target tolerances retained positive B8 policy differences (8.33–8.60 points). A separate post-outcome all-start joint outcome, being alive with high-I support, differed by −8.33 points at B8 but +35.57 at B4.5; the latter largely reflects survival differences. Complete sealed endpoint readback reproduced the 25,600 paired cells. Exploratory founder/visitor-crossed 97.5% t intervals were −9.96 to −6.69 and +32.58 to +38.55 points, respectively; the between-budget policy contrast was +43.89 points (40.59 to 47.20). At B4.5 the marginal effect was positive for all 160 founders and all 160 visitor histories; 995 matched cells changed from a negative B8 policy effect to a positive B4.5 effect, versus six in the reverse direction. These are descriptive, post-outcome findings, not extra registered endpoints or independent sign tests (Supplementary Table S1). Exploratory polymorphism clocks also mix fixation and extinction under harsh resources. The registered intervention nevertheless shows that persistence and retention of a specified floral option are distinct consequences of assurance evolution. Supplementary Table S1.

In the persistent-visitor comparison, all 32 source populations in each arm survived. At low activity and B8, 19/32 evolving versus 24/32 fixed-policy populations retained support for the high-investment target. Thus the bridge follows loss of a particular option among living populations; its complete source survival supplies no contrast for demographic rescue.

### Transfer separates support from realized response

A bounded exploratory transfer used 64 seed cohorts nested within 32 donors from four founders, four visitor histories and two assurance policies, with five destinations and 32 futures each. All 32 evolving-history cohorts entered fixed at A=0.75 and retained it in 5,120 futures. I was polymorphic in only 4/32 cohorts per policy; these supplied all I changes in visitor destinations. Yet 24/32 cohorts in each policy retained founder-relative high-I support, so support alone did not imply a capacity for further change (Fig. 6). Even without visitors, I increased in 27/1,024 fixed-history and 29/1,024 evolving-history future trajectories; such changes cannot be labelled adaptation to returning pollinators.

All evolving-history futures remained occupied, whereas fixed-history survival varied with destination. Restricted I response coexisted with changes in M and survivor-conditioned performance. Some cohorts already met the ancestral I target, and conditional performance gains do not establish an unconditional adaptation benefit. In a separate exploratory distant resident-mainland-like recipient, selected source markers remained in 2/8 fixed-history versus 4/8 evolving-history futures, whereas migrant genealogical descendants remained in 4/8 versus 6/8; resident community occupancy was 8/8 in both. Marker loss differs from lineage extinction, and neither proves successful independent mainland establishment or destination adaptation. These results distinguish persistence, ancestral recovery and response through other traits without proving a general recolonization advantage (Supplementary Table S1).

The bridge followed these same populations intact into a common destination for 40 updates. Across all eight source arms, mean investment fell among survivors while mean matching moved towards the new visitor optimum. These transfers preserve the source population at entry; the separate seed transfers in Fig. 6 include a founding bottleneck. The relevant comparisons distinguish survival, retention of a particular ancestral option and response through another trait.

## Discussion

Why should assurance recur while floral investment follows different paths? With cost-free delayed selfing, each additional viable selfed seed fills an ovule that would otherwise be lost. The size of that gain varies with pollen receipt, but its sign is shared wherever a shortfall remains. This buffers seed output against variation in pollen receipt. In *Collinsia verna*, autonomous selfing increased seed output per flower when visits were infrequent (Kalisz et al., 2004). In *Aquilegia canadensis*, it increased seed production, yet seed discounting and inbreeding depression outweighed that gain (Herlihy & Eckert, 2002). Earlier assurance can secure seeds during an intervening shortfall. The order comparison adds a distinction to this seed-stage evidence: at the same parental state, changing who receives the seed gain alters how often assurance rises before investment falls. The demographic value of expressing assurance early also depends on the outcrossed offspring forgone and on recruitment. When assurance expression preceded investment expression by assignment, initial pollen export was lower than under the reverse schedule. Over 80 updates, more selfed and fewer outcrossed offspring entered the population. These counts also depend on time alive. In this exploratory comparison terminal occupancy averaged 1.21 percentage points higher across near and far histories; the registered far–near difference in the schedule effect was −1.36 points. That interaction fell within the prespecified ±5-point equivalence range.

Across 136 island–mainland contrasts, flower size showed no uniform reduction (Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson, 2020). Introduced island populations of *Nicotiana glauca* tended toward greater self-pollination capacity, despite similar hummingbird visitation and no detected current selection for selfing (Schueller, 2004). Across fresh founders, assurance reduced investment on the higher-service side in all four settings. To test whether similar island displays conceal different pollination service, compare allocation in common-grown families from populations differing in assurance, alongside field measurements of pollen receipt and seed gains from supplemental outcross pollen.

### Founder variation and the private assurance benefit

Founders constrain the possible response. Without mutation or immigration, losing an allele can make a target unreachable; retaining it does not ensure that its carriers leave offspring. Weak founder-only prediction of investment change illustrates the difference between bounding accessible values and predicting an endpoint. Five generations of artificial selection on stigma–anther distance in a primarily selfing *Mimulus guttatus* population elicited little change (Tisinai & Busch, 2024). This demonstrates limited response in a mating trait; it does not identify loss of an investment allele.

### Assurance does not determine one floral trajectory

Delayed selfing reduces the maternal seed gain from a visit that can still export pollen. Reducing investment saves ovules but can cost pollen receipt and export; its realization requires transmissible variation. Assurance narrowed investment differences in three fresh-founder settings, yet widened them under both budgets with a persistent visitor. Similar investment sometimes concealed differences in assurance capacity within a service level. The direction of the environmental gap and the mating differences behind similar displays are distinct predictions. Matching has a visitor-defined optimum with no separate M allocation cost, and visitor history predicted matching better than investment. Floral evolvability varies among traits (Opedal, 2019), and local visitors help explain floral differences in nature (Moir et al., 2025; Torres-Vanegas et al., 2026).

In three natural populations of the mixed-mating annual *Tuberaria guttata*, seed production in intact, emasculated and hand-crossed flowers showed that delayed selfing most strongly offset maternal selection for larger corollas at the least-visited site (Teixido & Aizen, 2019). In a separate manipulation, reduced pollination in *Lobelia siphilitica* favoured taller inflorescences and more vibrant petals through female fitness (Brown & Caruso, 2023). Together the experiments show how the maternal return on floral traits varies with context. Whether assurance reduces inherited investment on the higher-service side sufficiently to narrow the difference between island populations remains a field prediction. We propose a field hypothesis: where selfing compensates for poor pollen receipt, attraction may still be favoured if additional visits increase the number of offspring a plant sires on other plants. Paternal returns need not increase in proportion to display: in Mimulus ringens, siring success per flower fell as display grew (Karron & Mitchell, 2012). The model contrast does not identify its paternal contribution. Separate association-disruption experiments likewise establish neither assurance-specific hitchhiking nor its absence (Supplementary Table S1).

### From favorable reproductive returns to inherited change

Founders can express identical assurance yet transmit different variants. Widening the founding A allele range advanced sustained assurance increase: 701/768 paired histories started by update 25, versus 134/768 with the narrower range. With mutation, assurance crossed the response threshold first in 51/64 histories under costly delayed selfing and 30/64 under cost-free prior selfing; cost and timing varied together. At low supply of matched visitors, the deterministic genotype-density approximation predicted both changes, assurance first, for two founders (Supplementary Table S1). The finite individual-based model reproduced that order in 3/32 and 2/32 histories; assurance alone crossed the threshold in another 15/32 and 7/32. All survived. At a smaller threshold the density trajectories were nearly simultaneous. The comparison isolates neither drift nor a survival benefit of early assurance.

Among 1,238 first losses of a lower-I allele, 315 occurred when its expected frequency was increasing: no offspring inherited a copy. These counts condition on first losses. The registered comparison instead followed alleles supporting higher investment.

### Population persistence and genetic options

Mating-system effects on rescue and genomic erosion are established (Peterson & Kay, 2015; Busch et al., 2022; Schoen & Toczydlowski, 2026; Deraje & Uecker, 2026); recent reviews distinguish demographic, evolutionary and genetic rescue (Balstad et al., 2026; Christie et al., 2026). Selfing need not impair later adaptation (Hartfield & Glémin, 2024). In 11 extant populations of *Impatiens capensis*, genomic data linked founding history and present variation (Schoen & Toczydlowski, 2026). Such samples omit failed populations. We instead followed all starting populations and measured retention of alleles compatible with one founder-relative high-I target, under initially matched expressed A. At B8, complete survival excludes differential extinction, but not different within-survivor census histories, as the explanation for increased support loss. At B4.5, assurance strongly improved survival. Counting all founding populations in a post-outcome joint analysis, assurance evolution reduced the fraction alive with high-I support by 8.33 percentage points at B8 and increased it by 35.57 at B4.5, chiefly because more populations survived there. Retained target support is not general evolvability.

### Subsequent environments and empirical tests

After transfer, some cohorts already met the ancestral investment target, while matching could improve with little investment response. Resurrection studies illustrate trait-specific change: across four *Viola arvensis* populations, corollas became smaller and nectar declined, while pollen and ovule production showed no detectable temporal change (Acoca-Pidolle et al., 2024, 2025). Survivor-conditioned gains are not unconditional adaptation. Marker retention in small secondary-colonization experiments establishes no general assurance-mediated recolonization advantage. A restoration-trap preprint exists (Acoca-Pidolle, 2025); our closed-inheritance support loss cannot establish irreversibility after mutation or immigration.

Lineage-level consequences of mating systems have precedents (Goldberg et al., 2010), but the present experiment measures only population persistence and target-specific genetic access, not natural speciation, mainland re-entry or diversification rates.

The two investment contrasts suggest a field test. Across populations differing in pollination service, compare inherited allocation in common-grown families. Within populations, vary display across assurance levels and measure both viable seed production and the outcross offspring each focal plant sires. This would show whether a similar investment conceals different maternal and paternal returns.

The model isolates processes relevant to island colonization using imposed visitor communities, fixed inbreeding depression and three separately inherited abstract traits; real floral architecture can involve many loci, pleiotropy and genetic covariances (Acoca-Pidolle et al., 2026). Its focal support, prediction and transfer cohorts exclude mutation and immigration, while some historical comparison experiments include mutation; I is allocation rather than flower size, and no expression plasticity is modelled. Founders come from restricted synthetic distributions. Prediction varies visitor identity under a common count schedule, with no independent repeat within each founder–history cell; interaction and stochastic residual remain confounded. M has no separate allocation cost, so the M/I skill contrast is design-dependent. Endpoint convergence does not separate selection from fixation, and assurance-specific hitchhiking remains unresolved. Original near/far regimes were both pollen limited. Transfer performance is survivor-conditioned, and natural recolonization, reproductive isolation and speciation were not measured.

The capacity to set seed autonomously changes what another visit can contribute; finite inheritance determines whether a population retains the variants needed for its next response. To test this account, follow populations from establishment, measuring autonomous seed set, investment and segregating variation, and retain extinct populations in the original denominator. Two islands with similar floral displays could then be distinguished by how their plants reproduce now and which floral changes remain possible.
