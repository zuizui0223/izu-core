# Q3 identification review — results-neutral companion

This note uses only the frozen protocol (SHA256 `e549464de272e5a792b45e61ea68f14af02ddf46ed1287a2a81249d0791f48e9`) and its intervention definitions. No current-run outcome, native update, new seed, or GitHub mutation was used. The examples below are mathematical identification illustrations, not simulated findings or proposed changes to the running experiment.

## What the experiment can answer

For each fixed original trigger state i, let T_i = 200 − trigger_i. The experiment compares two complete policies over that same remaining window: unchanged I fixation (h = 0), versus one mean-preserving restoration to allele pairs [v_i − h, v_i + h] with h = 1/16. Under the specified fresh-future, common-RNG coupling, define S_i as survival of both arms at census 200. The target is

`theta_i = E[I_native(200) − I_restored(200) | S_i, fixed state i, fixed T_i, fixed coupling]`.

The all64 and two block targets average these state-specific quantities with original-slot weights and three policy zeros. They do not weight states by survival probability. The all-pairs-survive gate must pass before the three primary mean inferences are evaluated; otherwise they are `not_evaluable`. Technical incompleteness instead remains HOLD.

Both block intervals wholly on the same side of zero establish directional replication **for these two fixed-state block averages under this conditional target**. Opposed intervals establish opposed conditional expected directions. Neither result identifies each state's direction, new-founder replication, an unconditional survival benefit, or a natural-population response. An interval inside ±.01 concerns the conventionally chosen operational margin for an average, not demonstrated ecological equivalence. Common-RNG coupling matters because conditioning on joint survival depends on the joint arm distribution, not just its marginals.

## Why support width and variance are not separated

The restoration simultaneously changes allele support from {v_i} to {v_i − h, v_i + h}, allele variance from 0 to h², and within-individual heterozygosity. Initially every individual's expressed I remains v_i: **expressed among-individual variance is still zero at restoration**. Subsequent segregation and population dynamics jointly determine its emergence and loss. Thus “adding variance” must distinguish allele variance from expressed variation.

Only the two combinations (support half-width, allele variance) = (0, 0) and (h, h²) are assigned. For any admissible endpoint contrast d, the hypothetical response laws `f_1(w) = d*w/h` and `f_2(V) = d*V/h²` give identical responses at both assigned policies: 0 and d. They differ at untested combinations. The observations therefore cannot uniquely label the contrast a support-width effect versus a variance effect. Even an h-series would keep V = h² for this symmetric construction; separating those attributes requires additional distributional interventions or explicit structural assumptions. Such work is outside this run.

## Why refixation is not an identified mediator effect

Refixation is a downstream event generated jointly with drift, selection, demographic history and terminal I. It was neither assigned nor prevented. Comparing paths that refix with paths that do not conditions on a post-intervention variable and does not reproduce an intervention on refixation. Moreover, fixation to v_i − h and fixation to v_i + h have the same “refixed” status but terminal contrasts +h and −h when the native arm survives. Refixation status alone cannot explain direction.

A general counterexample makes the mediation problem explicit. If an unobserved path variable U determines a recorded mediator M, the models `M = U, Y = a*M` and `M = U, Y = a*U` have identical observed (M,Y) distributions. Intervening on M changes Y only in the first. Observing more paths estimates their joint distribution more precisely without distinguishing those intervention meanings. Here, a well-defined manipulation of refixation—while specifying what happens to genotypes, reproduction and demography—is needed before a mediated contribution can be assigned. Complete ancestry records support descriptive/pathwise accounting; they do not by themselves identify that causal partition.

## Why remaining duration is not a separate contribution

Each source state has one assigned remaining window T_i. Trigger time was generated by the original history, so cross-state associations between terminal contrasts and T_i also mix genotype composition, demography and history. Algebraically, observed state contrasts can be written `theta_i = a_i + beta*T_i` for any chosen beta by setting `a_i = theta_i − beta*T_i`. With unrestricted state effects, those observations do not select a time contribution.

Saved histories can describe when changes occur within each observed window. A separately declared analysis could define earlier-horizon contrasts from prefixes, with its survival conditioning specified; it would not automatically partition the registered terminal contrast into time, support and refixation components, nor reveal futures beyond census 200. Longer duration also gives refixation and selection more opportunity, so their contributions need not be additive. A duration-specific causal question requires an explicit counterfactual and comparison design, not merely a regression across original trigger times.

## Why old mixed signs are not uniquely “noise”

Fresh replication reduces Monte Carlo uncertainty about the new conditional targets. If both blocks now have a common expected direction, it can show that the old point-sign opposition is not reproduced as opposed expected directions under this target. That is compatible with finite-future variability; it does not uniquely establish it as the historical explanation. The old primary and the new fixed-state paired-survivor conditional target differ in their inferential unit and conditioning; the follow-up is outcome-informed, holds the original states fixed, and uses its declared fresh-stream coupling. Block averages can also conceal state heterogeneity.

Conversely, opposed new block intervals would establish persistence of opposed conditional expectations in these blocks, without assigning the difference to support, refixation, duration or founder history. Unresolved intervals establish neither equality nor absence of heterogeneity. The original primary remains `unresolved`; this follow-up cannot retroactively replace it.

The native equations can support mechanistic reasoning, but a known simulator does not turn this two-policy experiment into a unique causal decomposition without defining the additional interventions or assumptions. The defensible result is the bounded conditional policy contrast and its declared decisions, followed by explicit remaining identification limits.
