## Figure alt text — ready for submission

### Figure 1

Conceptual diagram following an island-like pollination problem through four biological levels: immediate reproductive selection, conditional deterministic propagation, genetic accessibility and finite-population realization. The diagram separates directional response from reproducibility of history-specific effect magnitude and marks the deterministic closure as a mechanistic comparator rather than the stochastic mean of the finite model.

### Figure 2

Model-based comparison showing that changing pollinator functional composition at a fixed visitor number redirects floral-investment selection across starting plant states. A duplication control leaves the response unchanged when visitor entries are doubled but total activity and composition are held constant, separating functional composition from bookkeeping visitor count.

### Figure 3

Three-condition comparison of natural finite demography, pooled visitor histories and larger plant capacity. Visitor pooling and larger capacity both make directional signs more uniform, but independent new-history validation shows opposite history reliability: lowest after pooling, intermediate under natural demography and highest at capacity 192. History-structured and demographic residual variances show the same mechanistic contrast.

### Figure 4

Schematic and empirical-boundary figure showing how chronology, reproductive assurance, connectivity, demographic sampling and extinction alter realized evolutionary outcomes.
