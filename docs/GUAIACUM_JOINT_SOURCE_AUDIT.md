# Guaiacum sanctum joint source audit

## Decision

Fumero-Cabán et al. (2022) is a strong **population-level joint candidate**, not a harmonized second replicate for the Izu dependency × functional-exposure test.

The same species was studied in two Puerto Rican island populations:

- Guánica on the main island of Puerto Rico, where flower visits were overwhelmingly dominated by introduced *Apis mellifera*;
- Mona Island, where *A. mellifera* was absent and the visitor assemblage was richer and more even.

The same two populations also received a blocked breeding-system experiment with selfing, outcrossing, autogamy, agamospermy and open pollination.

## Source-native exposure contrast

The paper observed 4–8 focal trees per site/year over 2008–2010, with 180 observation hours divided into 732 15-minute segments. Visitor identity, flower number and visit counts were recorded and visitation was analysed as visits per flower per hour.

Table 1 contains:

| source contrast | Guánica | Mona |
|---|---:|---:|
| reported visitor species richness | 8 | 17 |
| total Table-1 visits, 2008–2010 | 9,443 | 3,358 |
| *Apis mellifera* visits | 9,282 | 0 |
| *Apis* share of Table-1 visits | 0.983 | 0 |
| count-derived Shannon | 0.113 | 1.998 |
| count-derived Gini–Simpson | 0.034 | 0.813 |

The source reports mean visitation rate in Guánica as approximately twice the Mona rate, with a significant island effect (`P < 0.001`) and no detected year effect (`P = 0.06`).

These are visitor-assemblage and visitation contrasts. They are **not** relabelled as Izu FDQ.

## Source-native reproductive contrast

For each island, 10 trees received five treatments with five flowers per treatment in a blocked design:

1. selfing;
2. outcrossing with pollen from 2–3 individuals at least 10 m away;
3. bagged autogamy;
4. agamospermy;
5. open pollination.

Agamospermy produced no mature fruit. Autogamy produced only three mature fruits in Guánica and two on Mona, leading the authors to conclude that a pollen vector is required for effective reproduction.

Selfing had lower fruit and seed production than outcrossing. The source-reported ISI (`self seed-set / outcross seed-set`) was:

- Guánica: **0.60**;
- Mona: **0.63**;
- between-island comparison: `t = -0.29`, `df = 18`, `P = 0.77`.

Thus a large pollinator-assemblage contrast does **not** coincide with a detectable difference in this breeding-system index.

The source also reports multiplicative fitness:

| treatment | Guánica | Mona |
|---|---:|---:|
| outcross | 1.50 | 0.90 |
| open | 1.60 | 0.97 |
| self | 0.43 | 0.40 |

## Why this matters

This system is useful because it directly demonstrates why the programme must keep **pollinator exposure** and **plant reproductive dependency** separate.

```text
pollinator assemblage / visitation
    changes strongly between populations

breeding-system index
    remains similar between populations
```

Therefore an exposure axis cannot simply be substituted for a dependency axis.

## Why this still does not open the moderation model

Three gates remain closed:

1. the paper does not report that the exact visitor-observation trees are the exact breeding-experiment trees;
2. richness / species-count diversity is not a prespecified functional-exposure metric harmonized with Izu FDQ;
3. the text does not provide numeric open/outcross fruit-set values needed to construct an autonomous-to-supported total-dependency ratio without digitizing Figure 4.

The study also lacks single-visit effectiveness / pollen-function measurements and a numeric floral-response transition.

Accordingly the matrix status is `partial` for functional exposure, total dependency and same-population joint context.

Search for lawful source-native tree-level data or tabulated treatment means. Until those are recovered, retain Guaiacum as a strong population-level joint architecture and do not fit a cross-lineage dependency × functional-exposure slope.
