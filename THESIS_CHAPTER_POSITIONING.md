# Thesis positioning — Chapter 2

## Role in the dissertation

This repository is the **Chapter 2 / mechanistic-identification** component of the dissertation.

The shared dissertation-level question is:

> **How does geographic isolation alter plant reproduction through changes in ecological interactions, and why do those changes produce different floral outcomes across islands and lineages?**

The companion repository [`zuizui0223/island`](https://github.com/zuizui0223/island) is Chapter 1 and addresses the macroecological boundary condition: where and under what biogeographic conditions island isolation is associated with floral and reproductive assemblage filtering.

`izu-core` addresses the mechanistic follow-up:

> **How is a changed pollination environment translated into reproductive, interaction, and floral responses within lineages, and why can the downstream response branch rather than converge on one island syndrome?**

## Chapter 2 framework

The core causal-identification framework remains:

\[
W(z)=F(z)E(z),
\]

where:

- `F(z)` is local reproductive contribution under a focal trait or interaction state;
- `E(z)` is establishment / reachability conditional on viable reproduction;
- `W(z)` is the observed island pattern.

The purpose of Chapter 2 is not to redescribe a floral pattern found in Chapter 1. It is to determine which intermediate mechanisms are actually identified by field, source-locked, or independent-system evidence.

## Relationship to the Chapter 1 channel-gated hypothesis

Chapter 1 (`island`) tests the **Channel-gated island assembly hypothesis** at a global comparative scale:

```text
geographic isolation
-> whether a regionally available pollination channel is retained or disrupted
-> floral / reproductive assemblage filtering
```

In that framework, Bombus is a useful regional channel indicator where it is biogeographically applicable. Structural Bombus absence is explicitly distinct from expected-but-deficient Bombus state.

Chapter 2 deliberately applies a stricter mechanistic standard. A Bombus-presence/absence contrast, visitor identity, island position, or floral phenotype is not sufficient to establish that Bombus-mediated reproductive function caused a response. Direct or source-resolved evidence about effective pollination, reproductive dependency, pollen transfer, treatment response, or lineage-specific response is required before a causal claim is admitted.

## Chapter 2 prediction: response branching

The central mechanistic expectation is not that every lineage exposed to a simplified pollination regime must converge on the same floral trait.

Instead:

> **A shared change in pollination function can generate different biological response modes depending on reproductive dependency, interaction structure, lineage history, and the network state in which the plant is embedded.**

The response-mode architecture therefore allows at least:

1. continuous within-lineage trait or mating-system erosion;
2. threshold transition into reproductive assurance;
3. interaction rewiring or hybrid / lineage replacement;
4. no shared specialist-like response in an appropriate dependency control;
5. alternative-guild specialization; and
6. occupancy / persistence filtering as a separate ecological outcome rather than within-lineage evolution.

These outcomes are not pooled into one island-adaptation score.

## Current mechanistic interpretation

The current Izu evidence supports a useful distinction between upstream and downstream mechanisms:

```text
pollinator functional diversity
-> trait matching                     [comparatively robust contemporary link]
-> pollen receipt                     [directional but network-state uncertain]
-> morphology / reproductive response [branching, lineage dependent]
```

Thus a binary Bombus label is intentionally not treated as the complete contemporary mechanism. Bombus can define a biologically meaningful regional boundary or candidate dependency, but functional diversity, trait matching, effective service, reproductive dependency, and lineage-specific response determine how that boundary is translated biologically.

## Chapter 2 falsification logic

The mechanistic hypothesis is made difficult to satisfy on purpose.

A convincing pollinator-mediated explanation should show that:

- a source-resolved specialist or dependent lineage responds at the relevant functional boundary;
- a matched nondependent or alternative-guild lineage does not repeatedly show the same specialist-specific response merely because it shares island geography;
- climate, area, isolation order, volcanic/history variables, and observation structure do not reproduce the same response equally well;
- interaction rewiring, hybrid replacement, and occupancy filtering are not mislabeled as within-lineage adaptation; and
- effective-pollinator identity and reproductive dependency are measured or independently supported rather than inferred from floral form alone.

## What Chapter 2 identifies

Chapter 2 is the mechanistic-resolution layer of the dissertation. It can evaluate:

- effective pollinator / legitimate-contact structure;
- single-visit pollen deposition and associated measurement reliability;
- reproductive dependency and pollination-treatment response;
- fruit, seed, outcrossing, and autonomous reproductive assurance;
- within-lineage response shape (cline, threshold, no response, alternative mode);
- matched dependency controls; and
- whether observed differences are more compatible with local reproductive function `F(z)` or with establishment / reachability `E(z)`.

Where these links remain unmeasured, the mechanism remains unresolved rather than being filled by proxy.

## Relationship to Chapter 1 (`island`)

| Chapter 1 — `island` | Chapter 2 — `izu-core` |
| --- | --- |
| global / multi-island comparative scale | Izu / lineage / plant / network scale |
| asks **where and when** filtering appears | asks **how and why** the response is generated |

| Bombus is a regional channel indicator / natural experiment | Bombus identity alone is not causal evidence |
| structural absence vs expected deficit is a key comparison | effective service / dependency / response mode are key comparisons |
| alternative bird/Lepidoptera-like phenotypes may support ecological interpretation | alternative guilds require source-resolved or direct mechanistic evidence for admission |

The thesis-level handoff is:

```text
Chapter 1: Geography -> channel regime -> assemblage filtering
                         |
                         v
Chapter 2: channel regime -> plant-specific functional/reproductive response
```

Chapter 1 establishes that a universal island floral syndrome is not expected and identifies the conditions under which a classical syndrome may emerge. Chapter 2 explains why even within a shared degraded pollination environment the downstream biological response can branch across lineages.

## Claim boundary

This repository should not claim that:

- historical Bombus loss is identified from island position or visitor records alone;
- every lineage should reproduce the same Oshima-to-post-Oshima breakpoint;
- pollinator occurrence equals effectiveness;
- floral form identifies effective-pollinator dependency;
- one functional decline must yield one floral response direction; or
- occupancy / lineage replacement is equivalent to within-lineage floral evolution.

The Chapter 2 contribution is narrower and stronger: **to identify the reproductive and interaction mechanisms that translate pollination-regime change into lineage-specific biological responses.**
