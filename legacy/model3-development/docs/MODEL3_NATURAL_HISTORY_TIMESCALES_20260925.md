# Model 3: natural-history timescales and prospective horizons

Date: 2026-09-25. Status: literature-grounded design recommendation, not a frozen campaign or a simulation result. No campaign was run for this note. Code inspected: `scripts/model3_evolution.py`, `simulate` and `next_population`.

## Decision

Interpret this model as **local population response from standing genetic variation during a bounded interval after establishment or environmental change**. Island geological age supplies historical context and possible disturbance limits; it does not specify how many reproductive seasons this local population has experienced. Extending an 80-year run to geological ages would not supply missing mutation, plant immigration, juvenile development, succession, or changing source communities.

Use explicit reproductive-season and demographic timescales, with common-calendar and common-replacement-time comparisons. The numerical horizon grid below is a prospective scenario design informed by the kinds of timescale present in natural history; none of the cited studies estimates universal island arrival, extinction, survival, or evolutionary rates.

These sources motivate separation of processes. They do not establish a typical Izu island lifetime, time since founding of an Izu plant population, or Izu pollinator turnover. A claim about a named Izu population would require its own geological/disturbance history, establishment evidence, demography, and repeated occupancy observations.

## What the existing numbers mean mathematically

The code labels each step a reproductive year and runs two pollination episodes inside it. Those episodes are not independent years or biological replicates.

* With constant adult survival `s`, expected reproductive-state persistence is `tau_P = 1/(1-s)` steps: 1 at `s=0`, 4 at `s=.75`. This is a **replacement-time proxy**, not an empirically measured generation interval. Equating it with mean parental age additionally needs a stable age distribution, immediate maturity, and age-independent reproduction. Actual recruitment limitation can alter turnover; report realized recruitment and persistence too.
* Each resident visitor type independently disappears after a season with probability `p`. Its expected residence is `tau_V=1/p`: 20 steps for `.05`, 6.67 for `.15`. This is a model residence time for a functional type; calling it species extinction would add an unsupported biological identification.
* Immigration is one Bernoulli opportunity per year for a new type drawn from a uniform trait source. Mean interval is `tau_A=1/a`: 3.33 steps for `.3`, 10 for `.1`. It is not a per-absent-species recolonization rate.
* The exact expectation satisfies `E[K_(t+1)] = (1-p) E[K_t] + a`, with limiting mean `a/p`: 6 for the `.3/.05` case and 0.667 for `.1/.15`. Initial counts are 9 and 4. Thus the present contrast changes both turnover and long-run richness and includes a transient decline. Horizon comparisons must not disguise this transient as adaptation.

Seasonal inactivity concerns visit intensity within an occupied population. Resident loss concerns disappearance across years. New immigration concerns dispersal and establishment. Keep these three meanings separate; a seasonal non-detection is not evidence of extinction. The current model implements idealized type presence and pollen activity, not an observation model for detecting resident species.

## Concrete prospective duration design

Before observing outcomes, declare the following as scenario choices rather than fitted natural-history values:

1. Use replacement-time checkpoints `H/tau_P = {10, 50, 100}`. With current survivals this gives annual checkpoints **10, 50, 100 seasons** and perennial checkpoints **40, 200, 400 seasons**. The first checkpoint asks about initial response on a tens-of-generations scale; later checkpoints test persistence or loss of standing variation and demographic failure. The experiment above establishes plausibility of early response only, not adequacy of any checkpoint.
2. Also retain common-calendar checkpoints **10, 50, 100 seasons** for both life histories. Label these a separate comparison: equal exposure duration and equal replacement opportunity answer different questions. A single longest trajectory per seed can supply nested checkpoints; checkpoints are not independent replications.
3. Report the ratios `tau_V/tau_P` and `tau_A/tau_P`. Current values are respectively `(20, 3.33)` and `(6.67, 10)` for annual non-island/island cases, and `(5, .833)` and `(1.67, 2.5)` for perennial cases. These expose whether environmental replacement is fast or slow relative to plant replacement.
4. If rates are later parameterized, preregister a mechanistic grid such as `tau_V/tau_P = {1, 5, 20}`, while setting expected richness independently through `a = K_target*p` where the Bernoulli-arrival constraint `a <= 1` permits it. Infeasible combinations must be omitted prospectively or require a separately reviewed immigration process; do not silently clip probabilities. This optional extension is not implemented here.
5. Treat 200–400-season runs as **stationary-environment stress scenarios**, not reconstructions of Surtsey, Krakatau, or Izu succession. If a named population has a documented shorter undisturbed window, that window bounds the empirical interpretation. Do not increase duration until a desired trait response appears.

The horizon is fixed even when a population goes extinct or a locus loses variation. Record extinction as absorbing, preserve undefined traits after extinction, and report variation loss explicitly. No mutation means lost alleles cannot reappear: apparent late stability may be exhausted variation, not adaptation or evolutionary equilibrium. Capacity 48 is a census ceiling, not measured effective population size. Plant recolonization, speciation, and island-wide macroevolution are outside the present model.

## Replication is a precision decision

Natural history determines interpretation of a trajectory, not the number of Monte Carlo seeds. Define an independent replicate as one seed-level founder/community/demographic history, preserving paired controls. Choose seed count before inspecting biological outcomes. For a single binomial probability, the worst-case normal-approximation 95% half-width is `1.96/(2*sqrt(R))`: approximately **385 seeds for 0.05** or **1,068 for 0.03**. These are illustrative precision targets, not guarantees for simultaneous contrasts, rare events, paired differences, or survivor-conditioned trait means. The final design must name the estimand and precision target; use paired seed-level uncertainty for contrasts and report unconditional extinction separately from survivor-conditional traits. Runtime-only pilot measurements can choose the feasible fixed budget without selecting seeds or horizons on scientific outcomes.

## Claim ceiling

Permitted: model-conditional local response, extinction risk, and sensitivity to environmental versus demographic timescales under declared standing-variation assumptions. Not permitted: an inferred real island evolution rate, universal island extinction rate, proof that 100 or 400 years is sufficient for nature, or a historical explanation of island floral syndromes. Neither a long run nor many replicates repairs missing natural-history calibration.
