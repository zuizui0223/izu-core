# Cover letter — Nature Ecology & Evolution candidate V0.1

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Ecological determinant rankings change across breadth–synchrony regimes,”** as an Article for *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.

Ecological studies routinely ask which determinant matters most and often treat the resulting ranking as a portable property of the system. Our manuscript shows that this ranking can instead be a property of the ecological regime in which the system is observed.

The study combines four layers that were kept prospectively separated during analysis.

First, we derive an exact null in which a variance-equivalent effective number is sufficient: additive community variation and state-by-community interaction variation share the same second-moment multiplier and cannot reverse rank. We then show analytically why this sufficiency is generically limited to second order. An exact quadratic state–community expansion identifies the mixed-response curvature condition under which higher moments alter determinant ordering.

Second, two structurally distinct nonlinear ecological models cross this analytical boundary. We retain a prespecified scalar-curvature prediction that failed rather than optimizing it away. Guided by that failure and by the exact mixed-response result, we froze a new feedback intervention before running six unused simulation seeds. Active state adjustment produced 52 community-to-interaction rank reversals across 108 paired comparisons; the feedback knockout produced none. The intervention changes phase topology rather than simply amplifying one variance component.

Third, before extracting natural data we froze an independent breadth–synchrony admission and routing protocol. The final regime plane contains 42 island interaction systems from six source studies and five island or archipelago groups. Source-balanced breadth and synchrony both span the predeclared ranges, the two axes are not strongly collinear, and the interior of the plane is occupied. All numerical dispersion criteria remain satisfied after removing the entire largest study (19 Mallorca systems). The frozen route therefore classifies the manuscript as an NEE candidate. Candidate hunting was stopped immediately under the predeclared stop rule once this result and leave-one-source-out robustness were available.

Finally, a separate frozen audit of 25 island research entries finds 21/25 with comparable plant responses, only 2/25 with direct partner arrival or replacement, and 0/25 with the complete outcome-independent determinant–response contract. We therefore do not claim that the synthetic determinant crossover has already been observed in nature. Instead, the natural regime analysis demonstrates that the context dimensions required by the theory are real and broadly occupied, while the audit identifies why determinant rankings cannot currently be transported across those regimes from existing island studies.

We believe the manuscript is suited to *Nature Ecology & Evolution* because the central issue is not specific to island pollination or to one modelling framework. It concerns a widespread inferential practice across community, population and ecosystem ecology: compressing structured ecological context and then interpreting a determinant ranking as globally meaningful. Our results provide an exact sufficiency boundary, a prospective mechanistic intervention, an external natural-regime test and a concrete measurement prescription.

The manuscript does **not** equate natural Hill diversity with the synthetic effective-number parameter, infer a universal natural threshold, or use the natural data to retune the models. The 25-entry identifiability denominator and its 0/25 full-contract result were frozen independently and remain unchanged. All source admission, effort normalization, regime metrics, journal-routing thresholds and stop rules were specified before the corresponding natural coordinates were opened.

This work is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. A companion island-focused manuscript surface developed earlier in the project is scientifically distinct in scope; the present manuscript uses the previously frozen island evidence only within explicit overlap and claim-boundary rules, and the active repository routing now treats the combined theory–regime paper as the primary submission surface.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
