# Ecology Letters cover letter — Lane B submission draft

**Status:** submission-ready scientific draft; author metadata and declarations remain to be completed before use.

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Letter, **“Effective independence alone does not determine nonlinear ecological response,”** for publication in *Ecology Letters*.

Ecologists often compress nominal replication and synchrony into an effective number of independent units. That variance correction is useful and well established: spatial synchrony can reduce the effective number of independently fluctuating subpopulations, and recent metacommunity theory shows that aggregation and stochastic dispersal can alter synchrony and stability. Our manuscript asks a different question: **if two ecological systems have exactly the same variance-equivalent effective independence, must they have the same nonlinear response geometry?**

We show that the answer is no. In an exactly solvable bilinear model, aggregation and synchrony collapse onto one coordinate and the interaction-to-community variance ratio is fixed. Two nonlinear ecological response classes break that reduction. A frozen plant–pollinator model shows a finite-scale reversal between community and state-by-community variance and an interaction-dominated intermediate regime. A structurally separate adaptive consumer–resource model reproduces the same qualitative phase across a broad parameter grid. Most directly, along an exact equal-`k_eff` contour the continuous state/community/interaction decomposition changes substantially while the expected support of realized community trajectories increases. The mismatch persists under a second correlation construction in which partner identities remain independently generated.

The general contribution is therefore not that synchrony reduces effective replication, nor simply that averaging can generate crossover. It is that **variance-equivalent effective independence is not, by itself, a sufficient descriptor of nonlinear ecological response**. Aggregation or support breadth and synchrony or shared stochasticity can act through distinct response geometry even when they produce the same variance-equivalent effective independence. This gives a concrete reason to retain those context axes separately in ecological theory and study design.

We believe the work is well suited to *Ecology Letters* because it combines an exact analytical baseline, two structurally different nonlinear ecological response models, an exact equal-effective-independence counterexample and an explicit empirical implication. It addresses a general problem in ecology—how averaging, dependence and context determine which source of variation appears dominant—rather than a system-specific mechanism.

### Novelty relative to related and recent work

The manuscript explicitly positions the known pieces rather than claiming them as new. Keitt et al. (2002) recognized that spatial synchrony can reduce the effective number of independently fluctuating subpopulations; nonlinear averaging is a long-standing consequence of Jensen’s inequality; and Townsend et al. (2025) showed that aggregated, temporally stochastic dispersal can alter synchrony and stability in a metacommunity. Our contribution starts after those results: **we test whether a single variance-equivalent effective-independence coordinate uniquely determines a nonlinear ecological response decomposition and show that it does not.**

Relative to the separate plant–pollinator analysis from which the frozen response model was drawn, this Letter is not an extension of that paper’s ecological interpretation. The present manuscript develops the exact bilinear benchmark, the general determinant-order argument, a structurally separate consumer–resource response class, exact equal-`k_eff` contour tests, and a second correlation implementation specifically to address the general reduction question. The second nonlinear system and equal-`k_eff` diagnostics were developed after observing the original plant–pollinator pattern and are labelled transparently as post-hoc structural generalization and mechanism diagnostics rather than prospective confirmations.
