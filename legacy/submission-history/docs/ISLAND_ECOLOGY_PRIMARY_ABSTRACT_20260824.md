# Primary island-ecology title and abstract

## Preferred title

**One perturbation, multiple island responses: state-dependent branching under pollinator functional simplification**

## Alternative question title

**Why do island plants diverge under pollinator simplification? Functional starting state shapes branching and local context shapes propagation**

## Abstract

Islands repeatedly alter the abundance, identity and functional composition of pollinators, and broad comparative studies have identified recurrent island-associated shifts in plant reproductive traits. Yet established island plant lineages do not respond uniformly to apparently similar simplification or reorganization of pollinator function. Some show directional reproductive decline, others diverge among lineages or traits, and still others maintain reproduction despite altered interactions. We asked whether this diversity requires a different explanation for every island, or whether it can emerge from a common ecological architecture whose outcome depends on lineage state and local interaction context.

We used a frozen agent-based model in which plant lineages experienced a common change in pollinator functional opportunity while differing in pre-existing functional position and downstream interaction or reproductive context. We decomposed the model with factorial and residual ablations and then challenged the already-frozen response-state vocabulary against independent island plant–pollinator systems identified from a global literature screen. No external island outcome was used to tune parameters, choose random seeds or add mechanisms.

A common perturbation generated multiple downstream response states. Mixed-sign branching occurred in 0.4167 of matched runs in both the original and independently seeded blocks, but disappeared completely when pre-existing lineage functional-position heterogeneity was removed. Other tested residual single-factor removals retained branching. Local support strongly reallocated branch identity, changing 105 of 288 paired lineage response signs when removed, and network context was bidirectional: it rescued reproductive sign in 16 of 96 eligible declines but worsened 11 of 96. Autonomous assurance mainly attenuated propagation, reducing decline magnitude in 207 of 216 cases while producing no sign rescues in the independent block and none across a broadened 525-contrast envelope.

The global screen retained 54 geographic/system units, of which 13 entered a strict external challenge. These comprised three branching systems, six same-direction propagation systems, two buffering or alternative systems, one reproductive-axis-decoupling constraint and one retained falsification. All 11 generative challenges were covered or sign-compatible with response classes already generated by the frozen model. We therefore propose a **state-dependent view of island pollination responses**: island-associated biotic simplification does not imply a universal post-establishment trajectory. Instead, pre-existing lineage state determines the potential for response branching, local interaction context governs how perturbations propagate or are redirected, and reproductive filters can attenuate downstream consequences. Broad island syndromes may still emerge through colonization and persistence filters or aggregation across lineages, but they should not be interpreted as evidence that every established lineage follows the same ecological path. External state compatibility supports the generality of this response architecture, not a claim that the same empirical causal mechanism operates on every island.

## Core take-home sentence

**Island pollinator simplification is better viewed as a common perturbation acting on different ecological starting states than as a force imposing one universal plant trajectory.**
