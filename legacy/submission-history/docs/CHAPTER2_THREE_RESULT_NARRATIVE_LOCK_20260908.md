# Chapter 2 three-result narrative lock

Updated: 2026-09-08
Status: active journal-facing narrative contract after exact realized-richness reframe

## One-sentence paper

Pollinator-community reorganization generates a hierarchical plant-response architecture: realized richness helps place the coarse response regime, realized community composition interacting with plant starting state determines response branches, real island systems undergo composition change that is not reducible to richness loss, and in Izu contemporary functional community structure maps onto plant–pollinator matching while downstream floral and pollen responses branch.

## The paper is one three-step argument

The Results are not three independent evidence inventories. Each result supplies the premise needed by the next:

1. **Result 1 — mechanistic prediction.** Define what kind of community change can generate heterogeneous plant responses and separate coarse regime placement from branch identity.
2. **Result 2 — real-world exposure.** Test whether real island systems actually undergo the compositional reorganization required by Result 1, rather than only uniform richness loss.
3. **Result 3 — biological consequence.** In one source-linked island system, test whether contemporary functional community structure maps onto the plant interaction state and whether that signal propagates uniformly or branches downstream.

The inferential route is therefore:

**mechanistic prediction → real-world compositional exposure → biological consequence**

The identifiability audit remains in the paper only as a claim-boundary and robustness layer. It is not a fourth scientific objective and it is not the explanation for why the study was done.

## Result 1 — Community reorganization separates coarse regime placement from branch identity

The synthetic model defines the mechanism, not natural prevalence.

The exact realized-richness hard control sharpens the previous relational result:

- baseline: 41/96 community realizations contain both positive and negative plant responses;
- equal initial richness alone: 53/96 remain mixed, but subsequent richness trajectories are not equalized;
- exact stepwise realized-richness matching: the ensemble mean is all-positive in all 6 prespecified matching seeds;
- nevertheless 51–65/96 individual community realizations remain mixed-sign;
- state × community non-additivity remains 42.72–48.51%;
- community-realization additive share remains 50.04–55.92%;
- starting-position additive share remains only 0.94–2.21%.

The supported hierarchy is:

`realized richness / turnover -> coarse regime placement`

`starting state × realized community composition -> branch identity`

Partner loss and arrival remain the strongest sign-stable regime associations on the frozen parameter surface. Local filtering and autonomous assurance remain downstream modifiers; they do not replace the richness/composition hierarchy.

### Result 1 claim ceiling

Allowed:

- realized richness materially influences ensemble mean-regime placement in the declared model;
- branch identity remains strongly contingent on starting state evaluated against realized community composition;
- community composition cannot be reduced to an additive starting-state effect.

Not allowed:

- richness is irrelevant;
- the synthetic percentages are natural frequencies;
- the exact matching control is an empirical richness manipulation;
- the synthetic coordinate is a calibrated floral trait.

## Result 2 — Real island systems undergo compositional reorganization beyond richness loss

Result 1 matters in nature only if island interaction systems actually experience changes in partner identity/composition that are not equivalent to a simple richness decrease. Two source-native external reanalyses now provide that exposure check.

### Wanshan–Yongxing

For the matched seven-plant subnetwork, pollinator assemblage turnover is 0.9796 (Morisita–Horn turnover; exact bootstrap 95% interval 0.9443–1.0000), while the pollinator-richness log response ratio is -0.1054 with a 95% interval of -1.3218 to +0.2877. The source-native contrast therefore contains near-complete partner replacement without a correspondingly decisive richness contrast.

### Ogasawara

Within the source-defined Anijima anole presence/absence context, matched-plant pollinator turnover is 0.6817 (95% interval 0.4975–0.9653), whereas the pollinator-richness log response ratio is -0.3146 with a 95% interval of -0.8755 to +0.4055. The context is spatially structured rather than randomized, but it independently shows substantial rewiring that cannot be summarized by richness change alone.

Together these two systems establish the ecological premise needed by Result 1: **composition change beyond richness loss is a real island exposure, not a synthetic convenience.** They are independent context examples, not exchangeable replicates and not a pooled island effect.

### Result 2 boundary — identifiability is a limit, not the objective

The frozen 25-entry source audit and the geography-first saturation exercise remain useful only to bound stronger historical claims. They show that outcome measurements are common but complete matched source-state → partner transition → realized community → plant-response chains remain absent. This prevents the paper from claiming that the same historical transition has been causally tracked across archipelagos. It does not weaken the directly measured Result 2 exposure statement above.

### Result 2 claim ceiling

Allowed:

- partner turnover/rewiring can dominate over a decisive richness contrast in real island interaction systems;
- at least two independent source-native contexts show this qualitative exposure recurrence;
- the broader literature contains branching, buffering and axis-decoupled outcomes consistent with the need for a richer response vocabulary.

Not allowed:

- a universal island rewiring coefficient;
- geological island origin as the cause of the Wanshan–Yongxing contrast;
- invasive anoles as a randomized causal treatment in Ogasawara;
- formal cross-system meta-analysis from the two context examples;
- synthetic regime labels assigned to empirical systems from observed outcomes.

## Result 3 — Functional community structure in Izu propagates into divergent plant responses

Result 3 asks whether the type of community variation established in Result 2 is biologically consequential for plants and whether its effect propagates uniformly downstream.

### Contemporary functional structure → plant matching

In the source-native 2024 Izu networks, pollinator functional diversity (FDQ) positively predicts corrected trait matching after site and season fixed effects:

- Izu five-island coefficient: +1.943;
- post-Oshima four-island coefficient: +2.059;
- all leave-one-island coefficients remain positive in both scopes.

This is the positive empirical bridge: continuous contemporary pollinator functional structure carries information about the interaction state experienced by plants beyond a binary sampled-Bombus label.

### Matching → downstream responses

Corrected matching is positively associated with pollen receipt on average (+0.0353 across five islands; +0.0342 post-Oshima), but leave-one-island ranges cross zero. Downstream propagation is therefore weaker and more network-state dependent than the FDQ-to-matching link.

Across the eight source-defined Oshima-to-post plant targets, corrected matching is lower for all 8/8, but floral-tube responses split 3 shorter / 4 longer / 1 unchanged and pollen responses split 4 lower / 4 higher. Only 2/8 follow the complete matching-lower + tube-shorter + pollen-lower combination.

This closes the three-result argument: **real functional community variation maps onto plant interaction matching, but a shared upstream shift does not impose one downstream floral or reproductive syndrome.**

### Historical boundary check

Historical signed-position analyses remain in the paper as adversarial checks, not as the centre of Result 3. Raw matching contains source-state plus broad composition information, whereas the historical predictor does not explain null-corrected matching and the Oshima-source bridge is unsupported. Present-day FDQ-to-matching therefore must not be rewritten as proof of historical Bombus loss or a dated causal transition.

### Result 3 claim ceiling

Allowed:

- contemporary pollinator functional structure is robustly associated with corrected plant–pollinator matching in Izu;
- downstream matching-to-pollen propagation is weaker;
- plants sharing lower corrected matching branch into different floral and pollen outcomes.

Not allowed:

- FDQ causally changes matching;
- matching causally changes pollen receipt;
- historical Bombus loss caused the contemporary pattern;
- the synthetic branch coordinate has been calibrated to an Izu floral trait.

## Role of the former identifiability act

Identifiability is now deliberately demoted from a coequal Result to three supporting roles:

1. prevent historical-causal overclaiming;
2. explain why external context examples are not pooled as a formal mechanism test;
3. specify the future matched transition measurement that would raise the claim ceiling.

The 0/25 full-contract result belongs in a compact Result 2 boundary paragraph and Supporting Information, not in the paper's headline conclusion.

## Discussion route

The Discussion should mirror the same three results:

1. **Richness sets the coarse opportunity regime; composition × state retains branch contingency.**
2. **The required compositional exposure exists in real island systems and is not equivalent to richness loss.**
3. **In Izu, functional community structure reaches plant matching, but downstream biological responses propagate contingently.**

The conclusion is not “why previous studies could not identify the mechanism.” The conclusion is that a single island-response syndrome collapses two different ecological operations—coarse regime placement and branch allocation—and then incorrectly assumes uniform downstream propagation.

## Final paper claim

The paper may claim a **hierarchical response architecture under community reorganization** with bounded empirical recurrence:

> realized richness helps position the coarse response regime; partner composition interacting with plant state retains substantial branch contingency; real island systems exhibit compositional reorganization beyond simple richness loss; and in Izu contemporary functional community structure maps onto plant matching while downstream floral and reproductive responses branch.

The paper may not claim a complete historical causal chain or a universal quantitative island effect.
