## The five claims that close Chapter 2

### C1 — richness places the coarse regime but does not determine branch identity

The exact stepwise realized-richness control is the key separation test. Matching realized richness at every step moves the ensemble mean geometry to all-positive in all six prespecified matching seeds, while 51–65/96 individual realizations remain mixed and state × community non-additivity remains 42.72–48.51%.

Therefore richness is part of coarse regime placement, but it does not exhaust branch contingency.

### C2 — response is relational

Across the finite-community simulations, branch identity depends on plant starting state evaluated against the realized community. Neither starting state alone nor community identity alone provides a fixed universal response rule.

This is the central HOW result of the chapter.

### C3 — determinant ordering is regime dependent

Under active plant adjustment, median starting-position share increases from 2.55% at `k=1` to 55.84% at `k=16`, while median community-realization share falls from 72.98% to 12.72%. Starting position exceeds community realization in all six prespecified seeds from `k=4` onward, while mixed branching persists at finite `k`.

The numerical crossover is model-specific. The transferable claim is only that determinant ordering can change as realization-driven variance is reduced.

### C4 — downstream modifiers do not replace the upstream mechanism

Local filtering can reallocate branches, while reproductive assurance changes response magnitude without sign rescue in the declared envelope. They therefore occupy downstream positions in the response architecture rather than providing alternative explanations for the upstream state × realized-community contingency.

### C5 — metadata bound reality and identifiability

The metadata layer answers a different question from the simulation: how far can the synthetic mechanism be confronted with existing natural evidence without inventing missing links?

The frozen formal audit contains 25 research entries across 21 exact geographic labels. Comparable plant response is available in 21/25, direct partner arrival/replacement in 2/25, and no entry supplies the full outcome-independent transition contract. Formal external prediction is therefore `not_evaluable`, not negative and not validated.

The broader descriptive programme reached 42 research entries across 37 exact geographic labels and the declared geography-first saturation rule. Further cross-sectional searching is not a Chapter 2 completion requirement.

Two source-native external examples show large partner turnover without a precise, universal richness-collapse signature: Wanshan–Yongxing partner turnover is ~0.980 and Ogasawara Anijima-context turnover is ~0.682. These are compositional plausibility examples, not pooled causal validation.

Existing Izu secondary data add an adversarial local stress test. Functional exposure predicts corrected matching robustly, but the matching-to-pollen link is positive on average without leave-one-island sign stability. Eight shared targets with lower corrected matching branch into 3 shorter, 4 longer and 1 unchanged tube responses, and into 4 lower versus 4 higher pollen responses. The null-corrected historical signed-position projection is unsupported, and the causal Oshima/post-Oshima boundary is not identified.

This is exactly the evidence pattern expected from a useful metadata layer: support, failure and non-identifiability are all retained.

## What metadata are allowed to do

Metadata may support:

- biological plausibility of partner turnover, buffering, branching and axis decoupling;
- adversarial examples against simple richness-only or one-direction island narratives;
- existing-data demonstrations that relational functional structure can coexist with heterogeneous downstream response;
- explicit alternative mechanisms and failed projections;
- an empirical measurement/identifiability ceiling.

Metadata may not support:

- natural prevalence estimates from the synthetic branch frequencies;
- a universal natural threshold near synthetic `k≈4`;
- visitor richness or Hill diversity as literal synthetic `k`;
- historical *Bombus* causation;
- a causal Oshima–post-Oshima boundary;
- the statement that the entire synthetic mechanism has already been validated in one natural system.

## Chapter architecture

The dissertation chapter should therefore be written as four main acts, not as a simulation chapter followed by a missing-field-data apology.

```text
1. Conditional response geometry
   common reorganization -> multiple branches

2. Separation and robustness
   exact richness control -> branch contingency remains
   finite-community audit -> determinant hierarchy changes
   mean-field limit -> asymptotic boundary

3. Downstream architecture
   filtering reallocates; assurance attenuates

4. Metadata confrontation
   natural ingredients are plausible
   simple alternatives fail in some systems
   full transition contracts are absent
   claim ceiling is therefore explicit
```

The chapter conclusion is reached at the end of Act 4.

## HOW / WHY closure

**HOW is closed.** The chapter identifies a response architecture in which coarse regime placement, starting state and realized community occupy different inferential positions.

**Proximal WHY is closed.** The same broad reorganization need not produce one response because branch identity is relational and the dominant variance source itself can change across finite-community regimes.

**Ultimate WHY remains out of scope.** Colonization, assembly, historical partner loss, evolutionary origin and persistence are upstream processes. Their absence is a scope boundary, not an unfinished Chapter 2 analysis.
