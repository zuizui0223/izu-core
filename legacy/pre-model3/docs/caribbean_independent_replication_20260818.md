# Caribbean independent replication — 2026-08-18

## Question

Does the refined `constraint-mediated functional conservation` framework recur outside the Japanese island gradient?

## Independent Caribbean comparison

Martén-Rodríguez et al. (2015, Journal of Ecology; DOI 10.1111/1365-2745.12457) compared 28 insular and 26 mainland Caribbean Gesneriaceae species using field pollinator observations and breeding-system experiments.

### Geography -> architecture

- All mainland species were functionally specialized, whereas 71% of insular species were functionally specialized and the remaining insular species had mixed pollination systems.
- Island species had more pollinator partners than mainland species.
- For hummingbird-pollinated species, visitation averaged 0.07 visits flower^-1 h^-1 on islands versus 0.24 on the mainland (P = 0.021).

This is an independent Atlantic/Caribbean replication that pollinator limitation can coincide with a shift in feasible interaction architecture rather than a simple loss of all pollination service.

### Architecture / assurance response

Autofertility did not differ between island and mainland species. Thus the response to insular pollinator constraint was not a universal increase in autonomous selfing. The authors instead identify generalized pollination as a plausible alternative reproductive-assurance strategy in the island lineage.

## What this adds to the repository hypothesis

The Caribbean comparison independently supports the conditional part of the framework:

> oceanic/insular pollinator constraint can change interaction architecture, while the compensating reproductive strategy does not have to converge on selfing.

This agrees with the Japanese Izu gradient at the level of `geography -> constrained pollinator opportunity -> architectural response`, despite different flora and pollinator guilds.

## What it does not prove

The study does not directly estimate a causal effect of generalized architecture on realized reproductive success. Therefore it is **not** a second full `geography -> architecture -> reproductive function` pathway demonstration.

Current evidence tiers are therefore:

1. **Direct full pathway:** Japanese continental/oceanic island gradient (Hiraiwa et al. 2024): geography-linked functional-diversity/trait-matching change and trait matching linked to pollination success.
2. **Independent partial pathway:** Caribbean Gesneriaceae (Martén-Rodríguez et al. 2015): geography-linked interaction architecture and experimentally measured reproductive assurance, but no direct architecture -> reproductive-success coefficient.
3. **Independent architecture replication:** global 52-network comparison (Traveset et al. 2016) and Wanshan-Yongxing comparison (Wang et al. 2025).

## Refined inference

The strongest defensible statement is now:

> **Geographically distant island systems repeatedly show geography-associated changes in the interaction architectures available to plants. The resulting reproductive response is conditional rather than a universal shift to selfing or one floral syndrome. A direct architecture-to-function pathway is demonstrated regionally in Japan and is independently corroborated, but not yet fully replicated, in the Caribbean.**

The next decisive target remains a non-Japanese system with source-native network/trait-matching metrics and a directly linked reproductive-performance endpoint.
