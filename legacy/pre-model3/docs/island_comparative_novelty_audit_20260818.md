# Island comparative novelty audit — 2026-08-18

## Purpose
Separate results already established in the island-pollination literature from synthesis claims that arise from the current `izu-core` comparative evidence architecture and world-replication screen.

This document is deliberately conservative. A point is marked **repository-derived** only when it is produced by combining source-locked evidence already represented in this repository. It is not claimed as literature-novel unless the contrast below survives further exhaustive review.

## What prior studies already established

The following are **not** novelty claims of this repository:

1. **Island pollination networks are often generalized and can contain super-generalists.**
   - Waser et al. 1996 established that pollination systems are frequently more generalized and dynamic than classical syndromes imply.
   - Olesen & Eskildsen 2002 showed super-generalist structure and integration of introduced species in Azores and Mauritius.
   - Traveset et al. 2016 found oceanic-island pollination networks to be smaller and topologically simplified, with lower interaction diversity and higher plant niche overlap.
   - Hervías-Parejo et al. 2020 showed that realized visitors on an oceanic island exceeded classical pollination-syndrome predictions.

2. **Functional diversity and trait matching can matter more than pollinator species counts.**
   - Hiraiwa & Ushimaru 2017 showed niche shifts after loss of long-tongued pollinators and incomplete compensation in plant reproduction.
   - Hiraiwa et al. 2024 showed that lower pollinator functional diversity reduces community-wide trait matching and pollination function.

3. **Pollinator replacement and redundancy can buffer or fail to buffer reproduction.**
   - Kaiser-Bunbury et al. and related network-resilience studies linked generalization/redundancy to robustness.
   - New Zealand, Ogasawara, Hawaii, Seychelles and other individual systems already document strong dependence, partial replacement, ineffective replacement, or restoration-mediated resilience.

4. **Classical island floral syndromes are not universally directional.**
   - Reviews of island plant evolution already treat self-compatibility, floral-size shifts, colour shifts, pollination-mode shifts and generalism as variable rather than universal.
   - Lord 2015 and later island-biogeography syntheses document mixed support for simple predictions such as universal autonomous selfing or universally reduced floral display.

Therefore, `izu-core` should not claim discovery of island generalization, pollinator replacement, functional redundancy, trait matching, or non-universal floral syndromes themselves.

## Repository-derived results that are stronger than any single included study

### R1. Functional recurrence with architectural divergence

Across the current geographically distant replication screen, **7 of 8 independent island systems have direct or explicit reproductive linkage to pollination state/service**, while one retained counterexample (Azores) shows stable reproduction under a redundant generalist complex.

At the same time, the eight systems occupy four distinct interaction-architecture macroclasses:

- concentrated dependency;
- complementary/redundant generalism;
- species-specific mosaic;
- novel-partner replacement.

The important result is not that any one architecture is new. The repository-derived result is that **reproductive dependence/maintenance recurs while the architecture supplying that function repeatedly differs**.

Current status: **supported as a cross-system replication pattern, not a prevalence estimate**.

### R2. The repeated object is function, not guild identity or a single island syndrome

The same reproductive function is maintained by taxonomically and structurally different partners across systems: insects, birds, reptiles, native generalists, introduced visitors, and mixed assemblages. The cross-system invariant is therefore better represented as **maintenance of viable pollination function** than as persistence of a particular pollinator guild.

This is a repository-level synthesis because no single source can establish the invariant across the geographically separated systems represented here.

Current status: **strong synthesis candidate**.

### R3. Floral phenotype is conditionally coupled to local architecture, not globally convergent

The morphology corroboration screen independently shows:

- local visitor–trait matching in some systems;
- mixed/transitional rather than classical syndrome phenotypes in Galápagos;
- specialist exceptions to generalized small/subdued accessibility patterns in Ogasawara;
- no universal smaller-flower effect across Pacific sister-taxon contrasts;
- joint effects of island context, climate and trait identity in Malesia;
- poor correspondence between classical syndrome labels and realized visitor breadth in Xisha.

Together with R1, this yields a stronger conditional statement:

> **Pollination function can recur across islands without requiring global convergence of the floral phenotype that realizes it.**

Current status: **supported at the replication-screen level; not yet a formal common-effect model**.

### R4. Counterexamples improve rather than break the framework

The Azores no-current-limitation case is not coded as failure or missingness. It occupies the complementary/redundant-generalism architecture and shows that successful function can be maintained without current pollinator limitation.

Likewise, ineffective replacement in Ogasawara and effective/partial replacement elsewhere are distinct outcomes inside the same framework.

This means the proposed comparison does not require all islands to move in one direction. It predicts **conditional divergence in how function is maintained**.

Current status: **framework-level insight supported by retained counterexamples**.

## Candidate central novelty

The strongest defensible candidate novelty from the repository is:

> **A cross-archipelago shift from trait- or guild-centred island syndromes to a function-centred comparative framework: viable pollination function repeatedly matters across geographically distant islands, but the interaction architecture and floral phenotype that realize that function are contingent and can diverge through concentration, redundancy, mosaic assembly, or partner replacement.**

Short form:

> **functional recurrence + architectural contingency**

or, more cautiously,

> **recurrent pollination function without a universal interaction or floral syndrome**.

## How this differs from adjacent prior frameworks

| Prior framework | What it asks | What this repository adds |
|---|---|---|
| island syndrome / Baker-style predictions | Do island plants repeatedly become self-compatible, smaller, less showy, etc.? | treats those traits as possible responses, not the invariant itself |
| pollination syndrome | Which floral phenotype predicts a pollinator guild? | allows different guilds/architectures to realize the same reproductive function and tests realized interactions rather than syndrome labels alone |
| island network generalization | Are island networks more generalized / nested / simplified? | distinguishes multiple architectures, including concentrated dependency and failed/partial replacement, and connects them to reproductive function |
| functional-diversity / trait-matching studies | Does loss of functional diversity reduce matching and reproduction? | asks whether this functional consequence recurs across remote island systems even when the compensating architecture differs |
| resilience / redundancy theory | Does redundancy buffer function under disturbance? | places redundancy as only one of several recurrent island solutions, alongside concentrated dependency, mosaics and partner replacement |

## Claims that are still premature

Do **not** yet claim:

- a global prevalence of the four architecture classes;
- that 7/8 is an unbiased global frequency;
- that island systems uniquely show this pattern relative to mainland systems;
- that architecture causes floral divergence;
- that one universal latent variable already explains all systems;
- that visual-signal evolution has been demonstrated across the current matrix;
- that the framework is literature-novel until a broader systematic novelty search is complete.

## Next falsification tests

1. Add geographically distant systems selected without regard to support direction.
2. Add explicit mainland / continental-island controls to test whether the pattern is island-specific or general mutualism biology.
3. Admit morphology and visual-signal data only when source-native measurements can be linked to the same system.
4. Predeclare architecture classification rules before adding the next systems.
5. Test whether architecture class predicts which response axis changes (morphology, selfing/assurance, signal, network, reproductive outcome) without pooling incompatible effect sizes.

## Bottom line

The repository's current scientific contribution is **not** another claim that islands are generalized, pollinator-poor, selfing-prone, or morphologically simplified. Its strongest current contribution is a comparative reorganization of those observations:

**the reproducibly conserved level appears to be pollination function, whereas the interaction architecture and floral phenotype that maintain it are contingent.**

That distinction is the working novelty to test harder, not yet a final literature-priority claim.
