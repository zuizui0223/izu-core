# Joint identifiability of functional exposure and reproductive dependency

## Question

Can the current real-data panels already test the hypothesis that plant reproductive dependency moderates the downstream response to pollinator functional change?

## Result

Not yet.

The repository contains exact source-native evidence for both sides of the proposed moderation:

- Izu panels contain exact continuous functional-exposure axes (FDQ/RBLP) and downstream matching/pollen or reproductive-response information.
- Balearic *Malva arborea* and Canary *Lotus maculatus* contain exact direct total reproductive-dependency contrasts on their own source scales.

But these exact channels do not overlap in the same plant populations. Therefore there is currently no exact panel in which a harmonized continuous functional-exposure measure and a direct total reproductive-dependency estimand are jointly observed.

## Why no cross-study regression is run

A regression that combines Izu FDQ with Balearic or Canary dependency ratios would create a synthetic pairing of measurements from different plants and systems. It would answer no identified biological estimand. Partial evidence from Seychelles, Cneorum and Galapagos is retained as partial rather than promoted to an exact total-dependency estimate.

## Current interpretation

The real-data programme now supports three separate claims:

1. Pollinator functional structure is associated with interaction matching within Izu, including post-Oshima islands.
2. Downstream reproductive responses are heterogeneous rather than a universal one-direction cascade.
3. Direct reproductive dependency varies greatly among real island plant populations.

Together these observations motivate, but do not yet identify, the moderation hypothesis:

> plant reproductive dependency may determine how strongly a change in pollinator functional structure propagates into reproductive and ultimately floral response.
