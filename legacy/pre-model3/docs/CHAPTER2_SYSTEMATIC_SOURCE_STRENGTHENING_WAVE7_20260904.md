## Cleared targets

### Austral Islands

Primary anchor: `10.1098/rsbl.2011.0771`.

The 17-island *Glochidion–Epicephala* survey collected *Glochidion* fruit in the Austral archipelago, reared *Epicephala* and reported the first published moth records from the archipelago. Female moths examined in the study carried the characteristic proboscis hairs associated with pollinating *Epicephala* lineages.

The paper's direct flower observations occurred in the Society Islands, so the Austral result is described as **source-native specialized-mutualist presence**, not as a direct local pollination-effectiveness experiment.

Because this is the same research source already used for the French Polynesia/Marquesas process layer, Austral is resolved inside that existing research layer rather than counted as a new entry.

### Tuamotu Archipelago

Primary anchor: `10.1098/rsbl.2011.0771`.

The same 17-island survey explicitly includes Tuamotu *Glochidion* collections and reports the first published *Epicephala* records from the archipelago. This replaces the first-pass crop/germplasm-only source state with native specialized-mutualist evidence.

As with Austral, the source does not justify splitting Tuamotu into a separate research entry or claiming local pollination effectiveness that was not observed directly. It is resolved as nested source-native coverage within the existing French Polynesia research layer.

## Targets that remain open

### Gambier Islands

Primary anchor: `10.1098/rspb.2013.0361`.

The southeastern Polynesian co-radiation study directly includes *Phyllanthus/Glochidion wilderi* from Mangareva and shows that the Gambier host lineage represents a distinct colonization history. The accessible source does not isolate an equally explicit Gambier flower-pollination observation or reproductive-effect experiment.

Host/mutualism phylogeography is therefore not converted into local pollination effectiveness.

### Wallis and Futuna

The same 2013 co-radiation study includes *Glochidion* taxa from Wallis. Its additional *Epicephala* specimen list explicitly includes American Samoa, Fiji and New Caledonia, but does not establish a direct Wallis/Futuna pollinator observation in the accessible source.

Source-native host sampling is insufficient to close the local pollinator/process gate.

### Niue

Repeated targeted searches did not recover a qualifying Niue-specific native plant-pollination, flower-visitor or breeding-system primary study. Regional Polynesian or crop evidence is not substituted.

### Kiribati / Gilbert Islands

The recovered literature is dominated by atoll flora, biodiversity and crop records. No qualifying source-native flowering-plant pollination or breeding-system experiment was recovered.

### Tuvalu

Coconut/germplasm and general atoll biodiversity material is available, but no qualifying native flowering-plant pollination or breeding-system primary study was recovered. The target remains an explicit natural-process source gap.

## Claim boundary

Geographic source resolution and research-entry count are separate quantities. A multi-archipelago paper can resolve multiple geographic targets without creating multiple independent research entries.

Austral and Tuamotu therefore improve source-native geographic coverage but do not alter the current 36-entry descriptive confrontation. The frozen 25-entry identifiability audit and formal prediction boundary remain unchanged.
