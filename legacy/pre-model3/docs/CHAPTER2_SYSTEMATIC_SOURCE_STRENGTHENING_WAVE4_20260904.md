# Chapter 2 systematic source-strengthening wave 4

Updated: 2026-09-04

## Decision

Wave 4 starts from the post-wave-3 state of **39 targets requiring additional source work** and re-reviews eight Atlantic/Pacific targets:

- São Tomé and Príncipe;
- Ascension Island;
- Tristan da Cunha;
- Palau;
- Marshall Islands;
- Solomon Islands;
- Tonga;
- Rapa Nui / Easter Island.

Only **Rapa Nui** is cleared from the source-quality queue. The other seven remain open because targeted source strengthening still does not recover a qualifying direct local flowering-plant pollination or breeding-system result at the required geographic/process level.

This wave does not change the frozen 25-entry identifiability audit, the current 36-entry descriptive confrontation, or formal prediction=`not_evaluable`.

## Rapa Nui: breeding-system evidence resolved, extant mechanism unresolved

Primary anchor: Püschel et al. 2014, `10.1371/journal.pone.0115548`, supplemented by direct conservation reproductive work reported in *Rapa Nui Journal* 27(2).

*Sophora toromiro* has been extinct in the wild on Rapa Nui for more than half a century. Peer-reviewed ex-situ lineage work documents that surviving progenies derive through self-pollination, while conservation breeding work reports controlled self-pollination and seed production.

This resolves the first-pass question of whether a direct reproductive/breeding-system source exists. It does **not** recover an extant Rapa Nui plant–pollinator community, a historical partner transition, or a contemporary island reproductive response. Rapa Nui is therefore retained as an ex-situ breeding-system search record and is not promoted to the global confrontation.

## Seven targets remain open

### São Tomé and Príncipe

The endemic flora and orchid diversity are source-verified, including the São Tomé endemic *Angraecopsis thomensis* (`10.2307/4111077`) and the modern endemic-plant Red List data book. The recovered sources remain taxonomic/floristic rather than direct pollination or breeding-system studies. Orchid form is not converted into a pollination claim.

### Ascension Island

The classic endemic-flora conservation paper (`10.1016/0006-3207(80)90056-7`) directly documents extinction, survival and introduced-plant effects, but it contains no direct flowering-plant pollination or breeding-system measurement. Ecological disturbance context is not equivalent to a pollination-process source.

### Tristan da Cunha

Repeated targeted searches for floral biology, visitors, breeding system and self-compatibility still did not recover a qualifying Tristan-specific primary source. This is recorded as a literature/source gap, not as evidence that pollination biology is absent.

### Palau

Primary anchor: Costion & Plunkett 2016, `10.3897/phytokeys.58.5292`.

The Palau *Osmoxylon* revision discusses an inflorescence morphology that could fit bird pollination, but explicitly states that no published pollination observations were available and calls for direct observation. This source is useful precisely because it prevents syndrome-based overinterpretation. Palau remains unresolved.

### Marshall Islands

Plant/insect sources recovered multi-atoll crop-pest and biological-control surveys, but no qualifying direct native flowering-plant pollination or breeding-system study. General insect-on-plant records are not treated as flower-visitation evidence.

### Solomon Islands

Primary context: the direct Fiji *Dillenia biflora* bat-pollination study, `10.1371/journal.pone.0262985`.

That study identifies several Solomon Islands *Dillenia* species as plausible candidates for similar corolla-removal/bat-pollination mechanisms based on floral morphology and historical descriptions. It does not observe pollination in the Solomon Islands. Regional mechanistic plausibility is therefore not promoted to source-native evidence.

### Tonga

The same *Dillenia* paper notes that the nectar bat *Notopteris macdonaldi* was historically present on ʻEua but was extirpated, while explicitly stating that it is unknown whether *D. biflora* occurred there. Historical bat presence without a matched host and flower-pollination observation is insufficient.

## Interpretation

The systematic programme is increasingly distinguishing three different forms of incompleteness:

1. **no direct source recovered** despite targeted search (e.g. Tristan);
2. **a primary source explicitly says direct pollination observations are lacking** (Palau);
3. **a related-region mechanism is plausible but not source-native** (Solomon, Tonga).

These states should not be collapsed into one generic `missing` category. They have different evidential meanings, but none currently closes the Chapter 2 full contract.

The source-strengthening queue has now moved from **48 after the Andaman correction → 43 after wave 2 → 39 after wave 3 → 38 after wave 4** while preserving the frozen scientific boundaries.
