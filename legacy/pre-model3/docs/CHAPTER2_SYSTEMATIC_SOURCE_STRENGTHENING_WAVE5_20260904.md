# Chapter 2 systematic source-strengthening wave 5

Updated: 2026-09-04

## Decision

Wave 5 continues source strengthening after the wave-4 state of **38 targets requiring additional source work**.

Seven targets are re-reviewed:

- Crete;
- Philippines;
- Trinidad and Tobago;
- Iceland;
- ABC Islands;
- Bermuda;
- Christmas Island.

Four are cleared from the source-quality queue. Three remain open because direct evidence is either geographically incomplete or still not experimental/local enough.

This wave does not modify the frozen 25-entry identifiability audit, the current 36-entry descriptive confrontation, or formal external prediction=`not_evaluable`.

## Cleared targets

### Crete

Primary anchor: `10.1006/bijl.1996.0119`.

The *Cyclamen creticum* study directly examined floral biology and mating system in the Mediterranean island endemic. Controlled pollination tested selfing, outcrossing and seed production without pollinators. The species was self-compatible but unable to set seed without pollinators, and the study quantified reproductive performance and inbreeding depression.

This replaces the first-pass Mediterranean umbrella-review state with direct island reproductive evidence. It still lacks a matched historical pollinator-community transition.

### Philippines

Primary anchor: *Philippine Agricultural Scientist* 87(2):182–190; UPLB Knowledge Digital Repository record `journal-articles/3043`.

At Mt Makiling, Luzon, floral buds of four tree species were bagged or netted to exclude visitors, with open flowers retained as controls. The experiments identified outcrossing/entomophilous systems for three species and medium-to-large bees as important pollinators.

This is direct Philippine pollination/breeding-system evidence, but not an island-colonization transition.

### Trinidad and Tobago

Primary anchor: `10.2307/1938966`.

The classic Ecology study followed early-successional hummingbird-pollinated plants for 13 months on Trinidad and Tobago specifically to test how lower hummingbird diversity on the smaller island affected plant pollination biology. This provides direct inter-island pollinator-diversity and plant-pollination evidence.

It does not provide the full source-state → transition → local-realization → plant-response contract used for formal Chapter 2 mechanism identification.

### Iceland

Primary anchor: `10.1657/1523-0430(2006)38[305:BSEITA]2.0.CO;2`.

North-Iceland *Campanula uniflora* was compared with West Greenland using outcrossed, passively selfed, actively selfed and control flowers. The Icelandic population showed pre-anthesis cleistogamy and predominant inbreeding; the authors interpret the pollen-deposition mechanism as reproductive assurance under severe arctic constraints.

This directly resolves Icelandic breeding-system evidence while remaining outside a measured island community-transition chain.

## Targets that remain open

### ABC Islands

Primary Curaçao anchors: `10.1007/s11284-010-0799-7` and `10.1111/j.1744-7429.1997.tb00026.x`.

On Curaçao, *Stenocereus griseus* and *Cereus/Subpilocereus repandus* share the nectar-feeding bats *Leptonycteris curasoae* and *Glossophaga longirostris*. Direct hand/mixed/natural pollination treatments measure fruit, seed and seedling outcomes, and complementary bat work documents strong cactus dependence.

This is strong process evidence for Curaçao. The systematic target, however, is the broader ABC Islands. Bonaire has authoritative bat–cactus evidence, while equivalent source-native experimental coverage for Aruba was not recovered in this pass. Curaçao is therefore not silently generalized to the full archipelago. ABC remains a breadth candidate with an explicit subtarget limit and stays in the source-work queue.

### Bermuda

The Bermuda Department of Environment and Natural Resources documents the endemic *Sisyrinchium bermudiana* and its prolific self-seeding. Additional horticultural descriptions infer regular self-pollination, but no qualifying Bermuda-specific controlled-pollination, flower-visitor or reproductive-effect primary experiment was recovered.

Self-seeding is not converted into a measured breeding-system mechanism. Bermuda remains open.

### Christmas Island

The endemic *Hoya aldrichii* is represented in modern comparative pollinarium-morphology research and the Christmas Island flora is well documented by Parks Australia. No local flower-visitor, controlled-pollination or reproductive-effect study was recovered.

Pollinarium morphology is not treated as observed pollination. Christmas Island remains open.

## Claim boundary

Source strengthening improves geographic and process evidence but is not manuscript admission. Curaçao does not stand in for the entire ABC archipelago, and direct breeding-system evidence in Crete, Philippines, Trinidad/Tobago or Iceland does not constitute a measured historical community transition.

The frozen 25-entry identifiability audit and the current 36-entry descriptive confrontation remain unchanged.
