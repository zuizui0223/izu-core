# Chapter 2 systematic source-strengthening wave 9

Updated: 2026-09-05

## Decision

Wave 9 introduces an explicit **terminal source-gap** state.

The systematic programme previously kept a geography in active source work whenever a direct local mechanism source was incomplete. That is useful early in review, but it makes an exhaustive programme impossible to finish: some sparsely studied island systems may simply lack a recoverable qualifying primary pollination or breeding-system study under the current search protocol.

A target may therefore leave active source work in either of two ways:

1. a sufficient direct or explicitly bounded source is recovered; or
2. repeated target-specific searches are documented and no qualifying local primary process source is recovered, in which case the target is closed as a **terminal source gap**.

A terminal source gap is a literature/source state. It is **not evidence that the biological interaction or mechanism is absent**. It is reopened if a new qualifying source is later found.

Wave 9 starts with 26 active source-work targets after wave 8 and reviews seven. All seven leave the active queue, reducing it to **19**.

## Evidence-resolved targets

### Maldives

Primary anchor: `10.3897/BDJ.10.e85107`.

The study sampled 17 sites across Faafu and Dhaalu atolls with standardized flower-visitor collections. Twenty-one bee, wasp and hoverfly species were recorded visiting 30 plant species, and visitor assemblage composition varied among land-use types.

This is direct multi-island flower-visitor evidence and clears the first-pass source gap. It does not measure plant-specific pollen deposition or reproductive effectiveness, so the evidence role remains visitation/community structure rather than a complete pollination-effectiveness transition.

Decision: `eligible_new_group_with_visitation_effectiveness_caveat`.

### ABC Islands — bounded subtarget resolution

Primary Curaçao anchor: `10.1007/s11284-010-0799-7`.

Curaçao has direct experimental and natural-pollination evidence for sympatric columnar cacti sharing bat pollinators. Bonaire authoritative natural-history material also identifies nectar-feeding bats as key pollinators/seed dispersers of columnar cacti. However, an equivalent Aruba-local primary mechanism study was not recovered.

The broad ABC target therefore leaves active source work as `terminal_partial_subtarget_evidence`: the exact Curaçao evidence remains usable, while no claim is generalized to all three islands.

Decision: retain breadth candidacy with a strict `Curaçao subtarget` limit.

## Why terminal gaps matter

The exhaustive island-universe objective is to account for every target, not to manufacture positive evidence for every target. A final state of `no qualifying source recovered under the documented search` is scientifically preferable to either silently dropping the geography or indefinitely labeling it unfinished.

Terminal gaps therefore count as **review completion**, not evidence admission. They never enter the global-confrontation candidate set unless a separate direct source exists for an explicitly bounded subtarget, as in Curaçao within the ABC Islands.
